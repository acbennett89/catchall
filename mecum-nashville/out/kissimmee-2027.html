How the expected price is made. Prior Mecum sales of the same year, make and model (tightening to trim where possible, loosening to ±2 or ±5 years when needed) from the last three years, matched on Mecum's own lot grade — a General-run lot is compared with past General-run lots, a Feature lot with Feature lots. Measured on this sale's first day, General-grade cars hammer around the lower quartile of their model's Mecum history, so that is what "expected" reports for them; graded lots use the median. The range is the middle half (25th–75th percentile). Every figure Mecum publishes — this sale's results and the comps — is the sale price including the 10% buyer's premium (a $6,050 result is a $5,500 hammer). By default this sheet strips that back out and shows hammer prices — the numbers the auctioneer actually calls; untick "Hammer prices" to see what a car costs with the premium. Condition, options, documentation and provenance are not in the comps — a numbers-matching car will beat its expected figure, a driver-quality car will trail it.

Lots you hide, watch and your notes are saved only in this browser. Results shown as "Sold" were captured when this sheet was built.
