**Decision Assessment Document**

***Larson v. McDonough*, 10 F.4th 1325 (August 26, 2021)**

**United States Court of Appeals, Federal Circuit**

**What the case is about:**

The Veteran appealed a decision of the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (CAVC) holding that it lacked jurisdiction to review a Board of Veterans Appeals (Board) determination of what constitutes a disability under 38 U.S.C. § 1110. Because the Federal Circuit Court of Appeals (Federal Circuit) had previously held that the CAVC has jurisdiction to review a Board determination that a claimed condition did not constitute a disability for purposes of § 1110, the Federal Circuit reversed the CAVC’s jurisdictional finding and remanded the case.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Federal Circuit’s decision may be referenced in the Manual M21-1 at V.ii.3.C.3.a.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The Veteran served on active duty for training in the United States Navy Reserves in 1988 and on active duty in the Navy from 1989 to 1993. He gained a substantial amount of weight before, during, and after his active service. In 2009, he filed a claim for service connection for multiple conditions, including the two conditions at issue in this appeal, obesity and dysmetabolic syndrome (DMS). VA denied the claims in 2010 and the Board affirmed that denial in 2016, finding that neither DMS nor obesity was a disability because neither condition is ratable under the VA Schedule of Rating Disabilities (rating schedule).

On appeal the CAVC affirmed the Board’s denial of service connection for DMS and obesity, holding that it lacked jurisdiction to review a Board determination of what constitutes a disability under § 1110. Relying on Federal Circuit precedents CAVC reasoned that such inquiry amounted to a review of the ratings schedule, which is prohibited by 38 U.S.C. § 7252(b).

On appeal to the Federal Circuit the Veteran argued that the CAVC erred when it held that the prohibition against review of the rating schedule deprived it of jurisdiction to review the Board’s determination that DMS and obesity were not disabilities for § 1110 purposes. Specifically, he asserted that in *Saunders v. Wilkie*, 886 F.3d 1356 (Fed. Cir. 2018), the Federal Circuit considered the legal standard governing what constitutes a disability under § 1110 and held that the Veterans Court has jurisdiction to review a Board determination of what constitutes a disability. The Federal Circuit determined that the question before it was narrow, and characterized that issues as whether a Board decision concerning what constitutes a disability under § 1110 necessarily implicates the content of the rating schedule and thus triggers § 7252(b)’s restriction on the CAVC’s jurisdiction.

Upon review of relevant statutes and case law and consideration of the arguments of the parties, the Federal Circuit found no statutory requirement stating that if the VA (or the Board or the CAVC) determines that a condition not listed on the rating schedule as a disability under § 1110, VA must modify the rating schedule. Hence the Federal Circuit reasoned that reviewing a determination by the Board that a claimed condition does not constitute a disability under § 1110, by itself, leaves the rating schedule undisturbed.

The Federal Circuit noted that in *Saunders v. Wilkie* it specifically held that the CAVC can review what constitutes a disability and held that the CAVC had applied the wrong legal standard in determining that, for an ailment to constitute a disability under § 1110, it must be accompanied by a “diagnosis or identifiable condition.” In *Saunders* the Federal Circuit  held that the legal definition governing the term “disability” as used in § 1110 was “the functional impairment of earning capacity, not the underlying cause of said disability.” *Saunders* articulated a definition of “disability” for § 1110 purposes that is distinct from and not coextensive with disabilities listed on the rating schedule. The Federal Circuit noted that while DMS and obesity can relate to ratable disabilities, they do not appear as independent disabilities on the rating schedule. Therefore, the Veteran in this case (like the Veteran in *Saunders)* sought only to establish service connection for his conditions under § 1110 and was not asking the Veterans Court to invalidate or revise any portion of the rating schedule.

Because *Saunders* controlled the outcome of this appeal, the Federal Circuit held that the CAVC erred when it determined that it lacked jurisdiction to review the Board’s denial of the Veteran’s claim for service connection for DMS and obesity. The Federal Circuit determined that § 7252(b)’s restriction of the CAVC’s jurisdiction is not implicated where, as here, a veteran seeks only to establish that conditions are service-connected disabilities for § 1110 purposes. The Veteran in this case did not ask the CAVC to invalidate or modify a portion of the rating schedule, nor did he ask the CAVC to order the VA to assign him a rating for a condition in a manner inconsistent with the rating schedule. Thus, the Federal Circuit found that the CAVC is not prohibited from reviewing the Veteran’s appeal of the Board’s determination that DMS and obesity were not disabilities under § 1110.

The Federal Circuit reversed the ruling of the CAVC and remanded the case.
