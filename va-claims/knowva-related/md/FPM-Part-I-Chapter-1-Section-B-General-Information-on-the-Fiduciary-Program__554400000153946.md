**I.1.B.1.a . Definition: Due Process Period**

The ***due process period*** is the control period allotted for the beneficiary to submit additional evidence or request a hearing.  The minimum due process period is 60 days, unless waived by the beneficiary.  When a waiver is not received, finalization of the determination occurs after the 65th day to allow time for evidence to reach the fiduciary hub (hub).

***References***:  For more information on

* determinations of incompetency, see [38 CFR 3.353](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=7e44776bb9178bfb1cdd3970c7d9c0ab&mc=true&node=se38.1.3_1353&rgn=div8), and
* due process, see [38 CFR 3.103](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=7e44776bb9178bfb1cdd3970c7d9c0ab&mc=true&node=se38.1.3_1103&rgn=div8).

**I.1.B.1.b . Due Process for Final Determinations of Inability to Manage Financial Affairs**

When the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) makes a proposed determination of a beneficiary’s inability to manage VA benefits, the station of jurisdiction (SOJ) must notify the beneficiary of the proposed adverse action and its ramifications, and provide the beneficiary with the opportunity to

* submit additional evidence to contest the action, and
* hold a hearing before VA.

Upon completion of all actions associated with the proposal, the SOJ will

* establish an end product (EP) 590, if not automatically established by the system
* add any necessary claim attributes (e.g., claim label, corporate flash, or special issue), and
* transfer the EP to the hub of jurisdiction, if National Work Queue (NWQ) routing does not occur.

**I.1.B.1.f . Additional Evidence or Hearing Requests Received During the Due Process Period**

If VA receives a request for a hearing or additional evidence during the due process period that relates to the issue of inability to manage affairs, and such evidence may affect the outcome of the rating decision, the hub must review the evidence prior to finalizing the rating.

Upon review of the evidence, the Fiduciary Service Representative (FSR) must determine if the additional evidence is duplicative.  Duplicate evidence is any evidence that was reviewed in conjunction with the proposed rating and is described in the proposed rating decision and/or the beneficiary notification letter.

If the evidence is not duplicative, the FSR must then consider whether the evidence conflicts with the proposal of incompetency.  New evidence that is not duplicative and also conflicts with the proposed decision of the beneficiary’s inability to manage their VA benefit must be carefully reviewed.  The FSR will determine if the proposed rating should be overturned, and the beneficiary rated as able to manage their affairs without limitation.

***References***:  For more information on

* principles of reviewing and weighing evidence, see [M21-1, Part V, Subpart ii, 1.A](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014383/)

**I.1.B.1.h . a Beneficiary’s Rights and Representation**

Per [38 CFR 13.30](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=0fc93475f73d92e7dcf3f350363035ee&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_130&rgn=div8), beneficiaries within the Fiduciary Program have specific rights in addition to the standard rights applicable to all VA beneficiaries.  For example, the beneficiary is entitled to be informed of the fiduciary’s contact information.

***Important***:  Hub personnel must always ensure these rights are upheld for all beneficiaries within the Fiduciary Program.

Per [38 CFR 13.40](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=b373873dc4f2ebd65fd08461a7729037&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_140&rgn=div8), beneficiaries within the Fiduciary Program have specific requirements to be followed by those who represent them before VA.  For example, reasonable fees are authorized to the fiduciary when regulatory criteria are met.

***References***:  For more information on

* the specific rights of beneficiaries in the Fiduciary Program, see [38 CFR 13.30](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=0fc93475f73d92e7dcf3f350363035ee&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_130&rgn=div8)
* authorizing fees for fiduciaries, see [FPM, Part II, 1.C.4](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153966/), and

**I.1.B.2.a . Fiduciary Adjustments**

The hub may receive a request for appointment of a fiduciary for a beneficiary who

* VA has determined unable to manage VA benefits
* a court of jurisdiction declared unable to manage affairs, or
* is a minor.

The hub may receive notification of the requirement for a fiduciary following a determination that a beneficiary is unable to manage their financial affairs from either

* an SOJ providing a [*VA Form 21-592, Request for Appointment of a Fiduciary, Custodian or Guardian*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21-592-ARE.pdf)
* the fiduciary promulgation team as described in [FPM, Part I, 1.A.1.b](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153945/), or
* a court having jurisdiction as described in [FPM, Part I, 1.B.2.c](#2c).

The hub may also receive requests from the RO rating activity to remove a fiduciary and pay the beneficiary directly due to final rating determination of a beneficiary’s restored ability to manage financial affairs.

---

**I.1.B.2.b . Controlling EPs**

All fiduciary functional processes must be controlled using the appropriate EP and claim label.

The date of claim for the initial appointment (IA) is the date the *EP 590 Due Process for Incompetency*/600 finalizing the finding of incompetency is authorized, or the date the hub receives the request for a fiduciary appointment.

The date of claim for the fiduciary removal is the date of VA’s receipt of the evidence restoring ability to manage financial affairs, or the date of the Field Examiner’s (FE’s) *Supervised Direct Pay Assessment*.

***References***:  For more information on

* basic functions of a hub, see [FPM, Part I, 1.A.1.a](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153945/), and

**I.1.B.2.c . Judicial Determination of a Beneficiary’s Inability to Manage Affairs**

Judicial findings regarding a beneficiary’s inability to manage their affairs are not binding on the RO rating activity.  If court documentation is received which declares a Veteran unable to manage their financial affairs, rating action is required to determine if VA concurs with the court’s finding.  In this circumstance, it is not necessary to propose a rating of an inability to manage VA benefits.  Instead, the RO rating activity will review the evidence available and issue a rating based on that evidence.

---

**I.1.B.2.d . Responsibilities of SOO for Beneficiaries With Judicial Determinations**

Upon receipt of a judicial determination of a beneficiary’s inability to manage affairs, the station of origination (SOO)

* completes a final rating on the beneficiary’s inability to manage VA benefits
* ensures the judicial documents are available in the eFolder
* establishes an *EP 290, FID-Fiduciary Adjustment*, and
* notifies the hub of jurisdiction using [*VA Form 21-592*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21-592-ARE.pdf).

It is unnecessary for the SOO to

* prepare a proposed rating, or
* provide advance notice of a rating.

***Note***:  Upon establishment of the EP 290, the NWQ will route the EP to the appropriate hub for action.

***References***:  For more information on

* sending [*VA Form 21-592*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21-592-ARE.pdf) to the hub, see [M21-1, Part X, Subpart ii, 6.C.1.c](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000177976/), and

**I.1.B.2.e . Responsibilities of the Hub Regarding Judicial Determinations**

Upon receipt of notification from the SOO of the judicial determination, the hub will

* confirm receipt of valid court documentation
* follow all procedures to appoint a fiduciary, and
* release retroactive benefits to the fiduciary, if appropriate.

**I.1.B.2.f . Guidelines for Evaluating Competency**

In the absence of clear and convincing evidence to the contrary, presume that a person is competent.

A finding of incompetency cannot be made without a definite expression by a responsible medical authority unless the medical evidence of record is

* clear
* convincing, and
* leaves no doubt as to the beneficiary’s incompetency.

***Important***:  The question of a child’s permanent incapacity is one of fact for determination by the rating activity based on competent evidence of record per [M21-1, Part X, Subpart ii, 6.B.2.c](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000177975/).  The RO rating activity is responsible for reviewing the evidence and completing a rating decision to determine whether a child beneficiary is permanently incapable of self-support and competent to manage their financial affairs as described in [M21-1, Part X, Subpart ii, 6.D.2.h](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000177977/) and [M21-1, Part X, Subpart ii, 6.A.2.c](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000177974/).

***Note***:  If competency of a beneficiary is raised without relevant medical evidence or statement from a responsible medical authority, development must be undertaken for medical evidence before the case can be forwarded for a decision.

**I.1.B.3.a . Purpose of the Fiduciary Program**

The Fiduciary Program provides oversight of VA’s most vulnerable beneficiaries who are unable to manage their VA benefits due to

* injury
* disease
* the infirmities of advanced age, or
* being less than the age of majority.

 VA appoints fiduciaries who manage VA benefits for these beneficiaries and conducts oversight of VA-appointed fiduciaries to ensure that they are meeting the needs of the beneficiaries they serve.

***Important***:  A ***beneficiary*** is an individual entitled to receive VA benefits, including proceeds of VA insurance.  Beneficiaries are classified as

* minors
* Veterans, and
* other adults, including

  + helpless adults
  + surviving spouses
  + dependent parents, and
  + insurance proceeds recipients.

A ***fiduciary*** is a person or legal entity (such as a bank) charged with the duty of managing the estate of an incompetent beneficiary.

A ***court-appointed fiduciary*** is a person or legal entity appointed by a State or foreign court to supervise an incompetent beneficiary and/or that person’s estate.  Courts may appoint financial institutions, State agencies, corporations, fiduciary services, or individuals to serve.

The statutory title of a court-appointed fiduciary may vary from State to State.  The most commonly used titles are

* guardian
* conservator
* committee
* trustee, or
* curator.

***Notes***:

* The appropriate title for court-appointed fiduciaries should be used, as State law requires.
* In some cases, VA may appoint a fiduciary other than the court-appointed fiduciary to serve as a VA fiduciary.

**I.1.B.3.b . Assessing the Competence and Personal Well-Being of Beneficiaries**

Program responsibilities include assessing the competence and personal well-being of beneficiaries.  This assessment is for the purpose of evaluating whether the beneficiary needs assistance in managing their VA benefits.

It is VA’s policy that every beneficiary has the right to manage their VA benefits.  However, in cases where medical evidence indicates that the beneficiary cannot manage their VA benefits, VA may decide that the beneficiary needs the assistance of a fiduciary.  This determination may also be made based upon notice that a court has appointed a guardian for the beneficiary.  VA’s decision that a beneficiary needs a fiduciary may be appealed to the Board of Veterans’ Appeals.

***Reference***:  For more information on the fiduciary appeals authority, see [FPM, Part III, 1.A.1](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153977/).

**I.1.B.3.c . Selecting a Qualified Fiduciary**

Program responsibilities include selecting a qualified fiduciary best suited to meet the needs and situation of the beneficiary.

Prior to appointment of a fiduciary, VA will assess the well-being and needs of the beneficiary and the beneficiary’s dependents in light of their funds.  This assessment is based, in part, upon a personal interview with the beneficiary.

In deciding who should act as fiduciary for a beneficiary, VA will always select the most effective and least restrictive fiduciary arrangement.  If during the fiduciary appointment process the beneficiary shows sufficient indication of being able to manage their funds, the beneficiary may be placed under supervised direct payment (SDP) for a temporary period.  If a fiduciary is required, VA will consider the choice of the beneficiary, then the beneficiary’s spouse, family members, friends, then caregivers who are qualified and willing to provide fiduciary services for the beneficiary without a fee.  As a last resort, VA will consider appointment of a paid fiduciary.

An assessment of the qualification of a proposed fiduciary may include, but is not limited to

* the willingness to serve and fulfill the responsibilities of a fiduciary
* an interview with the prospective fiduciary
* a credit history check, and
* a criminal background inquiry.

After the IA of a fiduciary, VA conducts follow-up oversight with beneficiaries and fiduciaries to monitor the beneficiary’s well-being and fiduciary’s performance.  If VA identifies unmet needs or adverse conditions, VA may remove the fiduciary and appoint a new fiduciary for the beneficiary.

***References***:  For more information on the

* beneficiary’s fiduciary preference, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.4.b](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153950/), and

**I.1.B.3.m . Consent and Routine Use of Information Under the Privacy Act**

The Privacy Act prevents disclosure of information from a claimant’s or beneficiary’s VA records to any third party unless the

* claimant or beneficiary consents to the disclosure, or
* disclosure has been published as a routine use in the Federal Register.

---

**I.1.B.3.n . Access to VA Records Under the Privacy Act**

The stipulations regarding access to VA records under the Privacy Act are listed below.

* Any material contained in a claimant’s or beneficiary’s VA record, when that record is part of any VA system of records, may be released to that individual unless to do so would be injurious to the claimant or beneficiary’s physical or mental health.
* Sensitive medical information may be disclosed only through special procedures as specified by VA regulations.
* A person rated incompetent for VA purposes

  + may have access to their own records
  + may consent to having a fiduciary have access to their records, and
  + should be encouraged to request the records through the VA-appointed fiduciary, although, if the individual insists upon access to their own records, those records cannot be withheld.

---

**I.1.B.3.o . Exceptions to VA Records Access Under the Privacy Act**

Only the individual by whose name the records are retrievable may have access to those records except under the following circumstances:

* a person holding a properly executed and timely POA from a competent individual may exercise the Privacy Act rights of the individual within the grant of authority contained in the POA
* the parent of a minor may have access to the minor’s records, and
* a court-appointed guardian of an individual declared to be incompetent due to physical or mental incapacity or age may have access to that individual’s records.

***Note***:  A VA fiduciary who is not a court-appointed guardian may not have access to a beneficiary’s records unless authorized by the beneficiary.

**I.1.B.3.q . Responsibilities of VHA and Hub**

Social workers or other case managers at VA medical center (VAMC) facilities and hub personnel are frequently involved in cases of mutual concern.

With respect to the primary responsibility of each agency/activity

* Veterans Health Administration (VHA) has primary responsibility for the coordination of all services to Veterans enrolled in the contract nursing home and Residential Care Home programs, and
* hub personnel have primary responsibility for protecting the VA-derived income of incompetent Veterans.

**I.1.B.3.r . Collaborating in Cases of Mutual Concern**

There must be an understanding by personnel in each program of the other’s goals and priorities to

* provide the best possible service to beneficiaries and their dependents, and
* prevent duplication of effort.

There must also be the recognition of the need for joint cooperation and consultation in areas of mutual concern.  It is especially important that there be discussion between hub personnel and VHA staffs concerning any planned revision in

* rates charged for the care of specific Veterans, and
* the amount of funds allocated for the incompetent Veteran’s personal use.

It is equally important to impress on VHA personnel that the appointment of fiduciaries for payment of VA benefits is a Veterans Benefit Administration responsibility and that VHA should not solicit for the court appointment of fiduciaries.

**I.1.B.3.s . Meetings With VHA Personnel**

Each fiduciary activity supervisor should meet with the appropriate personnel from each VAMC in their jurisdiction at least once a year to discuss services to incompetent Veterans.  These meetings may be conducted by meeting with the appropriate individuals separately or as a group.

These meetings should be an opportunity for further clarification of joint responsibilities in the delivery of services to incompetent Veterans.  The guidelines regarding meeting objectives include establishing guidelines or joint planning initiatives

* that will protect the interests of incompetent Veterans and sustain or improve their quality of life, and
* for sharing reports and other information of mutual concern regarding Veterans.

Some social work activities may want copies of field examinations and others will not.

***Note***:  The fiduciary activity supervisor and VHA personnel must establish the desired level of interaction between their respective staffs.

**I.1.B.3.t . Facilities With VHA Supervision**

The fiduciary activity supervisor should solicit a list of all facilities at which VHA supervises cases.  Preparation of field examinations (or alternate forms of supervision) for Veterans in these programs may include one or more of the following:

* a review of the last VHA supervisory report
* a discussion with the appropriate social worker, or other case worker, prior to conducting the field examination, or
* calling or sending a letter.

This should help to prevent unnecessary duplication of contacts and also afford VHA and hub personnel the opportunity to share relevant information.

* scheduling fund usage reviews, see [FPM, Part I, 6.B.3](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000223126/), and
* overview of fund usage review critical dates, see [FPM, Part I, 6.A.1.d](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000223127/).

---

**I.1.B.3.v . Considering the Impact of a Catastrophic Event**

When attempting to complete a Fiduciary Program oversight activity, hub personnel must consider the potential impact of a catastrophic event.

When a catastrophic event occurs, fiduciaries and beneficiaries may lose access to possessions, including personal documents and records, and face challenges

* obtaining or submitting documents to support fiduciary oversight
* responding to contact requests or being present for scheduled interviews, and/or
* providing evidence at the time of VA’s request.

---

**I.1.B.3.w . Determining a Catastrophic Event and Good Cause**

Both beneficiaries and fiduciaries may be negatively impacted by catastrophic natural, man-made, or environmental events.

A ***catastrophic event*** is characterized by

* a high number of deaths or injuries
* immense damage or destruction of property to facilities that provide basic services, such as

  + water
  + sewer, and
  + electrical services
* disruption to the economy
* severe weather (e.g., natural disasters or extreme temperatures that impact a beneficiary’s and/or fiduciary’s ability to safely travel), and/or
* severe effects to State, local, tribal, and private sector capabilities to begin and sustain response activities in a designated area.

The table below provides examples of catastrophic events.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Category of Catastrophic Event** | **Examples** |
| natural disasters | * hurricanes * tornados * floods * fires * severe weather (e.g., excessive heat or cold), and * earthquakes. |
| man-made or environmental disasters | * chemical spills * fires * structural failures, and * terrorism. |

***Important***:  Hubs must use the [Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) Disaster Declarations for States and Counties website](https://www.fema.gov/data-visualization/disaster-declarations-states-and-counties) to verify that a catastrophic event has occurred.  If the incident verification is not possible through the FEMA website, hubs must attempt to verify the event using reputable local government or news sources from the impacted area.

When a beneficiary or fiduciary resides in an area where a catastrophic event occurs 5 days before or 30 days after a scheduled oversight activity (e.g., an accounting report or field examination), the hub must presume good cause.

***Presuming good cause*** means that it is very likely that the fiduciary or beneficiary was impacted by a catastrophic event, and as a result, the hub will show flexibility in conducting the required oversight.

***Example***:  A catastrophic earthquake occurs on May 5, 2021.  The fiduciary resides in the impacted area verified by review of the system and the [FEMA Disaster Declarations for States and Counties website](https://www.fema.gov/data-visualization/disaster-declarations-states-and-counties).  The fiduciary’s fund usage review was due on May 1, 2021.  Presume good cause due to the fiduciary residing in the impacted area.

***References***:  For more information on

* rescheduling field examinations due to safety concerns, to include catastrophic events, see [FPM, Part I, 2.B.2.j](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153948/)
* good cause to extend an accounting submission, see [FPM, Part I, 3.A.3.h](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153954/)
* presuming good cause when accounting information is due, see [FPM, Part I, 3.A.3.i](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153954/)
* considering the impact of a catastrophic event when suspending benefits, see [FPM, Part I, 4.A.1.b and f](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153961/), and
* disaster declarations for States and counties, see the [FEMA Disaster Declarations for States and Counties website](https://www.fema.gov/data-visualization/disaster-declarations-states-and-counties).

## 4.  Fiduciary Responsibilities

---

**Introduction**

This topic contains information on a fiduciary’s responsibilities within the VA Fiduciary Program, including

* [general responsibilities](#4a)
* [financial responsibilities](#4b)
* [non-financial responsibilities](#4c), and
* [responsibilities to VA](#4d).

---

**Change Date**

February 4, 2022

---

**I.1.B.4.a . General Responsibilities**

Fiduciaries appointed by VA to manage the VA funds of a beneficiary are also responsible for monitoring the beneficiary's well-being and using available funds to ensure that the beneficiary's needs are met.  Fiduciaries owe VA and beneficiaries the duties of good faith and candor and must administer a beneficiary's FUM in accordance with [38 CFR 13.140(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=da5369517c8c5425850f21b02eae895b&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1140&rgn=div8).

In all cases, the fiduciary ***must*** disburse or otherwise manage funds according to the best interests of the beneficiary and the beneficiary's dependents and in light of the beneficiary's unique

* circumstances
* needs
* desires
* beliefs, and
* values.

Fiduciaries must also take reasonable precautions to protect the beneficiary’s private information contained in the fiduciary’s paper and electronic records.  Reasonable precautions are defined as actions taken by the fiduciary to protect the beneficiary’s private information against unauthorized access that would result in substantial harm or inconvenience to the beneficiary.

***Reference***:  For more information on the responsibilities of a fiduciary, see [38 CFR 13.140](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=da5369517c8c5425850f21b02eae895b&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1140&rgn=div8).

---

**I.1.B.4.b . Financial Responsibilities**

Due to their enhanced access to funds, VA fiduciaries have specific financial responsibilities to all beneficiaries they serve as outlined in [38 CFR 13.140(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=da5369517c8c5425850f21b02eae895b&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1140&rgn=div8).  Failure to uphold these responsibilities could result in their removal as fiduciary.

***Reference***:  For more information on the specific financial responsibilities of a fiduciary, see [38 CFR 13.140(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=da5369517c8c5425850f21b02eae895b&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1140&rgn=div8).

---

**I.1.B.4.c . Non-Financial Responsibilities**

Per [38 CFR 13.140(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=da5369517c8c5425850f21b02eae895b&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1140&rgn=div8), VA fiduciaries have additional responsibilities unrelated to finances.  These responsibilities must be maintained the same as the financial responsibilities listed in [38 CFR 13.140(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=da5369517c8c5425850f21b02eae895b&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1140&rgn=div8), and failure to uphold these responsibilities could result in their removal as fiduciary.

***Reference***:  For more information on the non-financial responsibilities of a fiduciary, see [38 CFR 13.140(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=da5369517c8c5425850f21b02eae895b&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1140&rgn=div8).

---

**I.1.B.4.d . Responsibilities to VA**

Any fiduciary who has VA benefit FUM on behalf of a beneficiary in the Fiduciary Program must

* notify the hub regarding any change in the beneficiary's circumstances, to include

  + the beneficiary's

    - relocation, or
    - serious illness, or
  + any other significant change in the beneficiary's circumstances which might adversely impact the beneficiary's well-being
* provide documentation or verification of any records concerning the beneficiary or matters relating to the fiduciary's responsibilities within 30 days of a VA request, unless otherwise directed by the Fiduciary Hub Manager
* appear before VA for face-to-face meetings when necessary, and
* comply with the policies and procedures prescribed in [38 CFR Part 13](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/retrieveECFR?gp=&SID=da5369517c8c5425850f21b02eae895b&mc=true&n=pt38.1.13&r=PART&ty=HTML).

***Notes***:

* If the fiduciary is also appointed by a court, they must annually provide a certified copy of the court accounting(s) to the hub or facilitate the hub's receipt of such accountings.
* A ***certified copy*** is a true copy authenticated by the signature and seal of the custodian of record.  Certification by a notary public is not sufficient.

## 5.  FE Responsibilities

**Change Date**

---

**I.1.B.5.a . Duty to Represent and Protect**

In carrying out assigned responsibilities, the FE represents the VA and the Secretary.

Authority is given for on-site decision making and unilateral initiation of action to protect the rights of

* VA beneficiaries, and
* the Federal Government.

---

**I.1.B.5.b . General Responsibilities of the FE**

A beneficiary’s disability often seriously hampers their ability to seek information or to comprehend responses.  Therefore, it is important that the FE explain to the fiduciary and/or the beneficiary’s dependents the full range of benefits.

FEs should inform the beneficiary and fiduciary of how to apply for applicable benefits.  If the beneficiary is receiving benefits for which the individual does not appear to be entitled, the FE must inform the beneficiary and fiduciary of the possibility of reduction in benefits.

The FE must document any applicable discussions about benefit changes in the field examination.

***References***:  For more information on

* possible changes to benefit entitlement, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.3.d and j](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153950/), and
* Federal benefits for Veterans, dependents, and survivors, see the [VA Survivors and Burial Benefits Kit](https://benefits.va.gov/BENEFITS/docs/VASurvivorsKit.pdf).

---

**I.1.B.5.c . FE Determinations**

The FE is responsible for

* developing facts to determine the extent to which the beneficiary has the capacity to handle benefits payable, in adult beneficiary cases
* establishing a method of payment appropriate to the beneficiary’s situation, in minor and adult beneficiary cases, and
* determining

  + the type of payment method best suited to the beneficiary’s situation
  + a prospective fiduciary’s qualifications, when a fiduciary is appointed, and
  + the frequency and timing of field examinations with minor and adult beneficiaries.

---

**I.1.B.5.d . Appointing and Instructing a Fiduciary**

The FE is responsible for

* appointing or securing the appointment of a fiduciary, which may include recognizing an existing fiduciary
* instructing the fiduciary in their duties
* authorizing use of VA benefit payments, and
* negotiating a fee with a fiduciary, when appropriate.

---

**I.1.B.5.e . Developing Facts and Taking Necessary Action**

The FE is responsible for developing facts and taking necessary action on matters of

* verifying the custody of a minor beneficiary
* fund usage
* the beneficiary’s well-being
* the needs of dependents, and
* other benefits to which the beneficiary or their dependents may be entitled.

***Notes***:

* If in the course of a field examination the FE determines the need for written correspondence, the FE will generate that correspondence within VBMS.
* When the FE becomes aware of facts which conflict with the beneficiary profile in VBMS, the FE must update the beneficiary profile.

---

**I.1.B.5.f . Ensuring Proper Use of Funds and Well-Being of Beneficiary**

The FE is responsible for taking independent corrective action immediately, when appropriate, to ensure the

* proper use of VA/beneficiary funds, and
* well-being of the beneficiary.

***Note***:  This may include

* a change of fiduciaries
* referrals to other divisions or agencies, and
* other actions within the scope of the FE’s duties.

---

**I.1.B.5.g . Investigating Allegations of Misuse**

The FE is responsible for investigating allegations of misuse made against a fiduciary.  These allegations may be made by the beneficiary, any other interested party, or through VA discovery.  Evidence must be thoroughly developed and documented to facilitate an official determination as to whether misuse has occurred.

---

**I.1.B.5.h . Conducting Onsite Reviews**

The FE is responsible for periodic onsite reviews of certain fiduciaries serving multiple VA beneficiaries.  The purpose of the review is to ensure these fiduciaries are performing their duties satisfactorily and to protect beneficiaries from misuse of their benefits by the fiduciaries.

---

**I.1.B.5.i . Investigating Non-Fiduciary Matters and Making Judgments Regarding Evidence**

The FE is responsible for

* investigating other matters arising under laws administered by or affecting VA, and
* making a judgment as to the evidence needed by the requesting authority to make its decision, and obtaining and providing that evidence.

---

**I.1.B.5.j . Administering Oaths**

When necessary, in the performance of duty, the FE is responsible for

* administering oaths, and
* taking sworn statements.

***Note***:  A ***sworn statement*** is the testimony of a witness subscribed and sworn to, or affirmed before, an FE under the authority of [38 U.S.C. 5711(a)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5711).

---

**I.1.B.5.k . Determining the Necessity of Scheduling an Interview**

When determining whether to make an appointment for a field examination and selecting a location for the meeting, the FE must consider the facts known about the case.

***Example***:  The FE should take into consideration whether the beneficiary

* is likely to be violent
* may accuse the FE of improper conduct, or
* may be influenced by others if a scheduled appointment is made.

---

**I.1.B.5.l . Refraining From Expressing Personal Opinions During an Interview**

The FE must not express to claimants or witnesses personal opinion on the issues or outcome of any matter that they are not personally authorized to resolve.

Hub personnel must not allow their personal biases or feelings to intrude in the course of their duties.

***Important***:  Fairness and courtesy must at all times be shown.  When, after careful consideration of all procurable and assembled data, a reasonable doubt arises regarding the degree of capacity to manage their financial affairs, such doubt will be resolved in favor of the beneficiary after careful consideration of all procurable and assembled data.

***Reference***:  For more information on reviewing a beneficiary’s capacity to manage funds, see [FPM, Part I, 5.A.1](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000223123/).

## 6.  LIE Responsibilities

---

**Introduction**

This topic contains information on the responsibilities of the LIE, including

* [shared responsibilities](#6a)
* [general monetary responsibilities](#6b)
* [protecting and advising on issues surrounding benefits](#6c)
* [additional benefit responsibilities](#6d), and
* [handling issues surrounding the death of a beneficiary](#6e).

---

**Change Date**

November 9, 2020

---

**I.1.B.6.a . Shared Responsibilities**

The Legal Instruments Examiner (LIE) shares responsibility with supervisors, FEs, and other fiduciary staff for making administrative and quasi-legal determinations involving the overall supervision of beneficiary VA FUM and the protection of rights to benefits.

***Note***:  There is considerable crossover of interests and mutual support between LIEs and FEs.

---

**I.1.B.6.b . General Monetary Responsibilities**

The LIE is responsible for

* reviewing the monetary needs of the beneficiary
* authorizing the

  + liquidation of bonds, and
  + release of funds from restricted accounts, after consultation with other fiduciary staff when in order, and
* monitoring personal funds of patient accounts held at Hines Benefits Delivery Center.

***Note***:  Consultation with other fiduciary staff may be required.

---

**I.1.B.6.c . Protecting and Advising on Issues Surrounding Benefits**

The LIE is responsible for acting to protect insurance and other rights to VA benefits, and advising fiduciaries of other Federal and State benefits to which the beneficiary may be entitled.  This includes

* reviewing records to ensure that all beneficiaries and dependents are accounted for in award actions (such as dependent parents and children living with other relatives)
* identifying situations where there is a possibility for additional benefits (for example, aid and attendance or housebound benefits, or benefits for a child over age 18 who remains in school)
* ensuring that possible entitlement to medical benefits is discussed with the fiduciary and dependents
* making certain that the spouse and children are aware of possible entitlement to Dependents’ Educational Assistance
* ensuring that a request for waiver of insurance premium is submitted for National Service Life Insurance policies or application for reinstatement and waiver of lapsed policies, when indicated
* following up on diaries when there is a need for the beneficiary and/or the fiduciary to supply information necessary for continuation of benefits, such as income information, and
* ensuring that beneficiaries are aware of possible entitlement to State and local benefit programs, education benefits, and other community services.

---

**I.1.B.6.d . Additional Benefit Responsibilities**

The LIE is responsible for

* taking prompt action on allegations of fiduciary misuse received by mail, telephone, or as a result of a personal interview
* initiating action to have payments adjusted, suspended, resumed, or recovered, when appropriate
* ensuring all information within the claims processing system is both accurate and current, at the time of any record maintenance or updating
* taking follow-up or corrective action required in any case
* initiating and responding to correspondence pertaining to individual VA FUM supervision
* identifying legal problems for action by the District Counsel
* developing missing Veteran cases
* requesting

  + the transfer of institutional award funds to VA for disposition when the Veteran dies, and
  + field examinations for estate administration problems and benefit issues
* communicating and/or coordinating with the Veterans Service Center (VSC), insurance, or finance activities to arrange prompt release of funds when financial hardship exists, and
* executing the CHANGE OF FIDUCIARY command to update and correct the master record, when assigned.

***Example 1***:  Requesting a possible successor fiduciary when the current fiduciary does not account properly or there is evidence of unauthorized expenditures or misappropriation of the beneficiary’s funds.

***Example 2***:  Notifying the VSC of

* a change in income
* a change in dependents
* death
* hospitalization or incarceration of the Veteran, or
* other evidence of fraud, waste, or abuse.

***Note***:  When suspension of payments may result in hardship to the beneficiary, every available alternative must be pursued before initiating suspense action.

---

**I.1.B.6.e . Handling Issues Surrounding the Death of a Beneficiary**

The LIE is responsible for

* identifying those cases in which the Federal escheat statutes may apply upon the death of a beneficiary, and
* initiating action to recover funds from those estates to which the government is entitled.

***Reference***:  For more information on escheat statutes and recovering funds to which the government is entitled, see [FPM, Part II, 1.C.3](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153966/).
