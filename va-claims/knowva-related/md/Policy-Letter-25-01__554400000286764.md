Subject:Presumptive Service Connection for Bladder, Ureter, and Related Genitourinary Cancers Due to Exposure to Fine Particulate Matter 2.5.

**1. Purpose**

Claims processors in ROs, PMCs, and DROCs must apply this guidance when processing claims and appeals for presumptive service connection for bladder, ureter, and related genitourinary (GU) cancers due to exposure to fine particulate matter 2.5 (PM2.5).

**2. Background**

[Section 202](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/3967/text#H361F65BF2276473291F7B30BF1FCAE25) of the Sergeant First Class Heath Robinson Promise to Address Comprehensive Toxics (PACT) Act of 2022, provided a process for the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) to establish presumptions of service connection based on toxic exposures. At the direction of the Secretary of Veterans Affairs (SECVA), a formal evaluation was initiated on February 26, 2024, to assess if cancers of the ureter, urinary bladder, and related GU organs are associated with exposure to PM2.5. A work group operating under the Military Environmental Exposure Sub-Council conducted a formal evaluation which found that these cancers are linked to exposure to PM2.5. Based upon that finding, SECVA authorized presumptive service connection for bladder, ureter, and related GU cancers for certain Veterans and directed VA to commence rulemaking for the issue. On January 2, 2025,VA issued an [interim final rule (IFR)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/02/2024-31220/presumptive-service-connection-for-bladder-ureter-and-related-genitourinary-cancers-due-to-exposure) to establish presumptive service connection for urinary bladder, ureter, and related GU cancers due to exposure to PM2.5 and VA added [38 C.F.R. § 3.320a](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFR39056aee4e9ff13/section-3.320a).

On January 2, 2025, VA issued immediate guidance to the field directing claims processors to hold impacted claims which were ready for decision if they could not otherwise be granted under an existing authority pending finalization of this policy letter. This policy letter provides interim guidance regarding the new regulation and claims processing. With the release of this policy letter, the stay on claims directed by the January 2, 2025, immediate guidance is officially lifted and claims processors shall begin processing the previously held claims.

**3. Eligibility**

For purposes of administering benefits under Title 38 United States Code, Chapter 11 (Compensation) and Chapter 13 (Dependency and Indemnity Compensation), claims processors shall grant service connection for any Veteran who otherwise meets the basic entitlement requirements under such chapters, and where the exceptions listed in [38 C.F.R. § 3.320a(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/part-3/section-3.320a#p-3.320a(d)) are not present as defined in [M21-1 V.ii.2.B.1.d - Burden of Proof Required to Rebut Presumptive Service Connection](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000180482/M21-1-Part-V-Subpart-ii-Chapter-2-Section-B-Presumptive-Service-Connection-SC), and who:

* was diagnosed with urinary bladder cancer, over-lapping sites of the bladder, and/or cancer of the ureter, ureteric orifice, and/or urachus at any time after separation from service, and
* served on active military, naval, air, or space service in any of the following locations:

1) Somalia or the Southwest Asia theater of operations (which includes Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, the neutral zone between Iraq and Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, Oman, the Gulf of Aden, the Gulf of Oman, the Persian Gulf, the Arabian Sea, the Red Sea, and the airspace above these locations) during the Persian Gulf War on or after August 2, 1990; and/or

2) Afghanistan, Syria, Djibouti, Uzbekistan, Egypt, Jordan, Lebanon, Yemen, and the airspace above these locations during the Gulf War on or after September 11, 2001.

* At this time, no end date for the Gulf War has been established by Congress or the President per [38 C.F.R. § 3.2(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/part-3/section-3.2#p-3.2(i)).
* The minimum active-duty service requirement as defined in [38 C.F.R. § 3.12a](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/section-3.12a) applies.

*Note*: Service in the above locations and timeframes defines a “covered Veteran” in the *Presumptive Service Connection Based on Exposure to Burn Pits and Other Toxins, Including Fine Particulate Matter (BPOT)* section of the [PACT Act Implementation SOP](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/21/PACT%20Act%20Implementation%20SOP.pdf).

For survivors, claims processors should apply this guidance for any claims related to the new presumptive conditions where a:

* survivor is claiming that the principal or contributory cause of the Veteran’s death is, or is secondary to, urinary bladder cancer, over-lapping sites of the bladder, and/or cancer of the ureter, ureteric orifice, and/or urachus; or,
* claimant has a specific allegation of eligibility under the new policy.

This eligibility applies to new claims and appeals, pending claims and claims pending in a VBA decision review lane (e.g., higher-level reviews (HLRs) and supplemental claims).

**4. Training**

The creation of these presumptions does not change the general principles of claim development and evaluating disabilities. To ensure claims processors are properly addressing the new presumptive conditions, Compensation Service will assign a training course to inform claims processors of the updated policy changes.

**5. Claim Processing**

***Claim Establishment and Data Tracking***

Claims processors must follow the steps directed in [M21-1 VIII.ii.2.B.1.b - Tracking Fine Particulate Matter Claims](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000177442/M21-1-Part-VIII-Subpart-ii-Chapter-2-Section-B-Developing-Claims-Based-On-Fine-Particulate-Matter-Exposure#1b) to ensure that any new claims have the *Gulf War Presumptive – Airborne Hazards* special issue (SI) indicator properly added to the relevant contention(s).

*Important:* For supplemental claims, consider this guidance as both *new* evidence sufficient to render the supplemental claim complete and *relevant* evidence sufficient to trigger a duty to assist under [38 U.S.C. § 5103A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5103A) and issue a merit-based decision.

*Note*: If the claim was held under the prior immediate guidance, claims processors must:

* remove the *VACO SI**5* from the impacted presumptive contention(s),
* ensure that the *Gulf War Presumptive – Airborne Hazards* SI is added to the impacted presumptive contention(s),
* ensure all development is complete, and
* make the claim ready for decision (RFD).

***Development Guidance***

While these new presumptive conditions are not listed under [38 U.S.C. § 1120](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1120), which was created by the PACT Act, they do follow the same procedures found in the topic for *Presumptive Service Connection Based on Exposure to Burn Pits and Other Toxins, Including Fine Particulate Matter (BPOT)* in the [PACT Act Implementation SOP](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/21/PACT%20Act%20Implementation%20SOP.pdf). For development purposes, follow the BPOT procedures in the PACT Act Implementation SOP. The terms “particulate matter” and “BPOT” are synonymous and fall under the same category of "covered Veterans" for presumptive purposes.

Please refer to [M21-1 III.i.2.B - Duty to Notify Under 38 U.S.C. 5103](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000014101/M21-1-Part-III-Subpart-i-Chapter-2-Section-B-Duty-to-Notify-Under-38-USC-5103) on procedures for duty to notify 5103 notice requirements.

***Examinations***

For claims based on PM2.5, follow the examination guidelines noted in [M21-1 IV.i.1.B.1.g - Presumptive SC Claims](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000180495/M21-1-Part-IV-Subpart-i-Chapter-1-Section-B-Evidentiary-Standards-for-Finding-an-Examination-or-Opinion-Necessary).

Follow the guidelines below for the presumptive conditions:

* In most cases, confirming a diagnosis for a new presumptive GU cancer requires a bladder exam using a cystoscope and a bladder tissue biopsy. Additionally, determining the stage of the cancer may require diagnostic testing.
* Request a medical opinion if the evidence of record is unclear as to whether the Veteran is diagnosed with one of the presumptive cancers or to help determine the originating site of the cancer.
  + Do not request that the Veteran be sedated and/or undergo an invasive procedure, such as a biopsy.
* If requesting a medical opinion, identify any evidence supporting a diagnosis to allow the examiner to state why a biopsy was not conducted. A medical opinion is typically not required to establish a nexus for presumptive conditions.
* A unique disability benefits questionnaire (DBQ) will not be created to address the presumptive cancers.
  + The Genitourinary DBQs should be utilized, depending on the location of the cancer(s): Male Reproductive Organ (Including Prostate Cancer) or Urinary Tract (Including Bladder and Urethra) Conditions. Other DBQs may be needed if medical evidence suggests the cancer metastasized or there are residuals in other body systems.
  + The Urinary Tract (Including Bladder and Urethra) Conditions DBQ would be used for diagnoses involving the bladder or urethra. The Male Reproductive Organ Conditions (including Prostate Cancer) DBQ would be used if the condition involves the male reproductive system (e.g., malignant genitourinary tumors occurring in the prostate gland, penis, and/or testes).
* Generally, do not request that a Veteran report for an examination for cancer when the disease is currently active, undergoing treatment, or otherwise warrants a 100-percent evaluation under the applicable diagnostic code (DC) as directed in [M21-1 IV.i.1.B.1.a - Using Medical Evidence in Lieu of Examination](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000180495/M21-1-Part-IV-Subpart-i-Chapter-1-Section-B-Evidentiary-Standards-for-Finding-an-Examination-or-Opinion-Necessary). Applicable DCs with active disease and treatment descriptions can be found in [38 C.F.R. § 4.115b - Schedule of ratings—genitourinary system](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/section-4.115b).
* This guidance should also be followed for dependency and indemnity compensation claims.

***General Rating Guidance***

As noted above, creation of these presumptions does not change the general principles of claims development and evaluating disabilities. Regular rating principles apply when evaluating these new presumptive conditions.

In establishing a presumption of service connection for conditions related to exposure to PM2.5, follow the general presumptive service connection policies outlined in [M21-1 V.ii.2.B - Presumptive Service Connection](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000180482/M21-1-Part-V-Subpart-ii-Chapter-2-Section-B-Presumptive-Service-Connection-SC).

*Reminder*: Service connection on a presumptive basis for cancer associated with an in-service event may only be established if the cancer in consideration is the primary or originating site of the cancer. See [M21-1 V.ii.2.B.1.b - Considering Presumptive Service Connection for Metastatic Cancer](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000180482/M21-1-Part-V-Subpart-ii-Chapter-2-Section-B-Presumptive-Service-Connection-SC?query=cancer#1b), which discusses how to determine whether a cancer is primary or metastatic, and when presumptive service connection may be established.

Evidence may show surgical intervention is required. As applicable, claims processors should consider temporary, total evaluations addressed in [M21-1 VIII.iv.8.D - Other Temporary Total Evaluations](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000177543/M21-1-Part-VIII-Subpart-iv-Chapter-8-Section-D-Other-Temporary-Total-Evaluations?query=surgery).

***Rating Guidance*****: *Diagnostic Codes (DC)***

Refer to [38 C.F.R. Part 4 - Schedule for Rating Disabilities](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-4) and assign the appropriate DC based on the location of the cancer and the diagnosed condition. This may require analogous codes. Use DC 7528 for the impacted presumptive cancers.

*Reminder*: Under [38 C.F.R. § 4.14](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-4/subpart-A/section-4.14), only one DC may be assigned for any active cancers or secondary cancers in the same body system, with the exception of cancers of the gynecological system. For more information, see [M21-1, V.iii.8.A.2.c - Malignant Neoplasms of the Gynecological System](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000180511/M21-1-Part-V-Subpart-iii-Chapter-8-Section-A-Disabilities-of-the-Gynecological-System#2c).

Review the medical evidence for a diagnosis. A cystoscopy may be used to confirm the diagnosis of malignant neoplasms of the urinary bladder. In some instances, it may require a bladder tissue biopsy, abdominal ultrasound and computed tomography scan, or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) to determine the stage of the cancer.

Refer to the following Manual sections for additional information:

* [M21-1 V.ii.3.D.5.a - Evaluating Malignant Neoplasms and Associated Impairments](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000180489/M21-1-Part-V-Subpart-ii-Chapter-3-Section-D-Evaluating-Disabilities)
* [M21-1 V.ii.3.A.2.c - Considering Complications of an Expressly Claimed Issue](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000180486/M21-1-Part-V-Subpart-ii-Chapter-3-Section-A-Determining-the-Issues#2c)

***Rating Guidance*****: *VBMS-R***

When needed, claims processors should properly edit the analysis language. Rating Glossary fragments addressing this guidance were updated and are available to use. The *Gulf War Presumptive 3.320* SI must be added to each issue, with “*Particulate Matter*” as the special issue basis.

***Effective Dates***

The guidance in this policy letter is effective January 2, 2025, the publication date of the IFR. The effective date for service-connected death or service connection of these conditions based on exposure to PM2.5 cannot be granted earlier than the effective date of the act or administrative issue, in accordance with [38 C.F.R. § 3.114](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/section-3.114). Apply the effective date provisions under [38 U.S.C. § 5110(g)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section5110&num=0&edition=prelim) and [38 C.F.R. § 3.400(p)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/part-3/section-3.400#p-3.400(p)), and [38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(h)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/part-3/section-3.2500#p-3.2500(h)) for supplemental claims. See [M21-1 V.ii.4.A](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000180492/M21-1-Part-V-Subpart-ii-Chapter-4-Section-A-Effective-Dates) for more information on effective dates. **As the IFR was published on January 2, 2025, the effective date cannot be prior to that date when granting benefits on a presumptive basis**. For intent to file and claims received after the date of the regulation change, ensure all criteria was met on or before the regulation change before applying a retroactive effective date under [38 C.F.R. § 3.114](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/section-3.114).

*Reminder: Claims submitted prior to the January 2, 2025, law regulation change*

For any impacted claims with an intent to file date or date of claim before the January 2, 2025, regulation change, consider whether further development is necessary under the [PACT Act Implementation SOP](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/21/PACT%20Act%20Implementation%20SOP.pdf), Topic 2, “*Toxic Exposure Risk Activity (TERA) Procedures for Non-Presumptive Claims*.” When qualifying service and diagnosis are established on or before the regulation change, and an examination and TERA medical opinion have not already been requested, it is generally required to obtain such an examination and opinion.

* When a grant of benefits on a presumptive basis is warranted, but the intent to file or claim was received before the regulation change and additional development is required to make a determination regarding a potential earlier effective date, complete a partial grant effective the date of the regulation change. Indicate in the decision narrative that a determination regarding a potential earlier effective date is deferred for additional development. Code the condition as a *deferred evaluation* in the VBMS-R Master Record. Defer the issue in VBMS-C for development consistent with Topic 2 of the [PACT Act Implementation SOP](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/21/PACT%20Act%20Implementation%20SOP.pdf). Continue the end product at authorization in VBMS-A.
* If the medical opinion with supporting rationale and all relevant medical evidence establishes that a grant based on TERA participation is warranted,
  + establish service connection on a direct basis per PACT Act guidance, and
  + assign an earlier effective date consistent with the PACT Act Implementation SOP content titled, *Establishing an Effective Date for TERA Claims*.
* If the medical opinion with supporting rationale and all relevant medical evidence establishes that a denial based on TERA participation is warranted, deny entitlement to an earlier effective date prior to the date of the regulation change. Supportive explanation must be provided in the Reasons for Decision section of the rating decision.

***Processing HLRs, AMA Board Remands, and Legacy Appeals***

For HLRs, follow the table below:

Processing Higher Level Reviews

| **If ...** | **Then ...** |
| --- | --- |
| the evidence of record is sufficient to grant the maximum benefit under the new regulation, even if the evidentiary record closed prior to January 2, 2025, | grant entitlement – see paragraphs above titled *Rating Guidance*and *Effective Dates.* |
| the evidence of record suggests there may be entitlement, but additional development is needed, | return the issue as a Duty-to-Assist (DTA) error for development. |
| no eligibility exists, despite the change in regulation (e.g., VA completed all appropriate development, but VA still cannot confirm a qualifying location of service), | deny service connection under usual HLR procedures unless there is a DTA error or Difference of Opinion unrelated to the new theory of presumptive service connection. |

For legacy remands, claims processors will review those appeals prior to certification to the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) for any potential development that may be required by this policy guidance, in accordance with the procedures outlined in [M21-5 7.F.3.c - When to Certify an Appeal](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000139892/M21-5-Chapter-7-Section-F-Docketing-Certification-and-Claims-Folder-Transfer-to-the-Board-of-Veterans-Appeals#3c). In these cases, no request for review from the claimant or Board remand instructions will be necessary to initiate development. Legacy appeals awaiting a *de novo* review and/or the issuance of a Statement of the Case will follow the same guidance.

For AMA Board remands regarding issues impacted by the new presumptive service connection, no request for review from the claimant or Board remand instructions are necessary to initiate development. VBA will complete the development required to adequately address the claim based on the newly authorized theory of presumptive service connection prior to issuing a decision.
