Decision Assessment Document
 Kilpatrick v. Principi, May 7, 2003, 327 F.3d 1375
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about (CAVC)?**

The CAVC invalidated a portion of [38 CFR 3.809(b)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=d2cf07f5f4e4e3ac029a35603951a906&node=se38.1.3_1809&rgn=div8) and [GC opinion 24-97](http://www.va.gov/ogc/docs/1997/Prc24-97.doc), which had held that a person entitled to compensation pursuant to [38 U.S.C. 1151](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1151) is not eligible for a special housing adaptation grant for that disability.  Also, the Court vacated VA’s decision denying the claim for automobile or adaptive equipment benefits based upon a section-1151 disability and remanded the claim for further development and adequate reasons or bases.  *See Kilpatrick v. Principi*, 16 Vet. App. 1 (2002).

**What the case is about (**Federal Circuit)**?**

The Federal Circuit affirmed the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (CAVC) holding that the veteran, who is in receipt of compensation pursuant to 38 U.S.C 1151, is entitled to specially adaptive housing (SAH) benefits provided under [38 U.S.C. 2102](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/2102).  See *Kilpatrick v. Principi*, 327 F.3d 1375 (Fed. Cir. 2003).

**Impact on VBA:**

Significant impact.  A portion of 38 C.F.R. 3.809(b) was invalidated by the CAVC opinion and should be amended.  Also, certain sections of M21-1 should be amended to show that it is possible for a person in receipt of compensation for a section 1151 disability to obtain a special housing adaptation grant including Part IV, paragraphs. 23.08.  ***Regulations and laws referenced***:  38 CFR 3.809. [38 USC 2101](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/2101).

**Summary of the facts and Court's reasons:**

As a result of VA medical treatment, the veteran in this case could not walk or use his legs and lost bowel and bladder control.  He was evaluated as 100% disabled and was in receipt of compensation benefits pursuant to [section 1151](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1151).  VA denied the veteran’s claim for specially adapted housing (chapter 21) and for automobile or adaptive equipment (chapter 39) because the veteran was in receipt of compensation for a section 1151 disability.  The CAVC reversed VA’s decision relating to chapter 21 and vacated and remanded the decision relating to chapter 39.  The CAVC invalidated [VA General Counsel Precedent Opinion 24-97](http://www.va.gov/ogc/docs/1997/Prc24-97.doc) and *the portion of [section 3.809(b)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=d2cf07f5f4e4e3ac029a35603951a906&node=se38.1.3_1809&rgn=div8) that requires that the disability must have been incurred or aggravated as the result of active service.*

According to the CAVC, it is chapters 21 and 39, and not section 1151, that determines the eligibility of a section 1151 beneficiary for benefits under those chapters.  The Court found that the regulation, 38 C.F.R. 3.809, which requires that the disability must have been incurred or aggravated as the result of active service, improperly limits the class of beneficiaries entitled to the housing benefit in 38 U.S.C. 2101.  As a result, the Court invalidated the regulation.  Similarly, GC opinion 24-97 was found invalid since it purports to bar a section-1151 beneficiary from receiving the housing benefit.

Although the CAVC did not reverse VA’s decision denying automobile benefits under [38 U.S.C. 3901](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/3901) and [3902](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/3902), the Court vacated that part of VA’s decision because of the lack of an adequate statement of reasons or bases for its decision.  Although section 3901(a) defines an eligible person as a person whose disability is the result of an injury incurred or disease contracted in or aggravated by active service, the Court found that there was a discrepancy within the statute and, as a result, implied that section 1151 beneficiaries might be entitled to automobile or adaptive automobile benefits.

On appeal to the Federal Circuit VA argued that the plain language of [section 2101(a)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/2101) contains two requirements: (1) the veteran must have a disability that qualifies for compensation under chapter 11; and (2) the veteran’s disability must be service-connected.  According to the veteran, section 2101(a) means that chapter 21 benefits are available to all persons who are compensated under chapter 11 in the same manner as veterans with permanent and total service connected disabilities.

The Federal Circuit found, contrary to the submissions of both parties, that the plain language of section 2101(a) does not answer this case. The Federal Circuit examined the historical background of section 2101(a), and of section 1151, and concluded that Congress directly addressed the issue present in this case and intended for persons disabled by VA medical or surgical treatment, such as this veteran, to be eligible for SAH benefits under that section.
