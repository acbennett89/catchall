**Decision Assessment Document**

**Stern v. McDonough, April 20, 2021, 34 Vet.App. 51**

**United States Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)**

**What the case is about:**

The Veteran appealed a Board of Veterans Appeals (Board) decision that found reductions in disability ratings for bilateral polyneuropathy of the arms and legs were proper. The Court addressed the appropriate remedy when the Board, in reducing a non-protected rating, fails to consider whether an improvement in the disability reflects an actual improvement in the ability to function under the ordinary conditions of life and work, as required by Brown v. Brown, 5 Vet.App. 413 (1993).

The Court found that where the Board entirely fails to address whether the improvement in the disability reflects an improvement in the ability to function under the ordinary conditions of life and work, as required by Brown, reversal of the rating reduction is required. Although §3.344(a) did not apply in this instance because the ratings were not protected, Brown established that two factual findings must be made for a valid reduction of a non-protected disability rating: (1) actual improvement in the disability and (2) that improvement reflects “improvement in the . . . ability to function under ordinary conditions of life and work.”

The Court concluded that “the Board’s total failure to make the finding required by Brown is the type of error requiring reversal.”

**Impact on VBA:**

M21-1 Part X.ii.4.A.2.b. will be updated to reference this case.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The Veteran served on active duty in the U.S. Army from August 1988 to March 1989. He appealed a Board decision that found reductions in disability ratings for bilateral arm and leg polyneuropathy were proper.

The Court noted review of rating reduction cases generally revolve around 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.105(e), 3.343, and 3.344 and the protections afforded therein. In Brown, the Court held that "in any rating-reduction case not only must it be determined that an improvement in a disability has actually occurred but also that that improvement actually reflects an improvement in the veteran's ability to function under the ordinary conditions of life and work." 5 Vet. App. at 421.

In this case both the Veteran and VA agreed that the Board failed to consider specific evidence and explicitly determine whether there was an improvement in the Veteran’s ability to function under the ordinary conditions of life and work. The Court noted the Board began the analysis prescribed in Brown, but stopped short by failing to "fully apply" it.

Having determined that the Board committed legal error, the Court concluded that the Board's failure to address whether the improvement in disability reflects an improvement in the ability to function under the ordinary conditions of life and work, also required reversal of the rating reduction in this case.

The Court DISMISSED and REVERSED the Board’s decision that the reductions in disability ratings for bilateral arm and leg polyneuropathy were proper, reinstated the disability evaluations previously assigned for those conditions, and REMANDED the case for further proceedings consistent with its decision.
