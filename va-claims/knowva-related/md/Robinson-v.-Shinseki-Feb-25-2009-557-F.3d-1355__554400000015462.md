Decision Assessment Document
 Robinson v. Shinseki, Feb 25, 2009, 557 F.3d 1355
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) must liberally read all filings, regardless of whether the appellant is represented by counsel, in the direct appeal phase of proceedings before the Board because this obligation is expressly imposed by VA’s regulations.  Where the claimant has raised an issue of service connection, the evidence in the record must be reviewed to determine the scope of that claim.  However, the Board is not required to consider all “theories of recovery,” as only theories with evidentiary support need to be considered.

The Federal Circuit also noted that the requirement to liberally read filings does not apply to filings by counsel involving claims based on clear and unmistakable error (CUE).

**I**mpact on** VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran filed a claim in December 1998 for service connection for heart and thyroid conditions as secondary to his service-connected peptic ulcer disease.  The regional office (RO) denied the claim in September 1999.  The veteran’s attorney filed a Notice of Disagreement concerning the RO’s decision denying the claims for entitlement to service connection for heart disease and hyperthyroid condition “as secondary” to the service-connected peptic ulcer condition.

The subsequent Statement of the Case (SOC) characterized the issues as service connection for the heart disease and hyperthyroid condition as secondary to the service-connected peptic ulcer disease.  In the decision section, the SOC indicated:  “service connection for heart disease is denied” and “service connection for hyperthyroid condition is denied.”

The Board denied the appeal in May 2004, finding no relationship between the claimed conditions and service-connected peptic ulcer disease.  The Board stated that the veteran contended that the cardiovascular disease and/or a thyroid disorder as were secondary to a service-connected peptic ulcer disease, rather than due directly to any incident of active service.  Thus, the Board concluded that it would focus solely on secondary service connection for those disorders.

The veteran argued before the Veterans Court that the Board erred in failing to address service connection on a direct basis.  The Veterans Court held that it had jurisdiction because direct service connection was not a different claim for secondary service connection; it was instead another argument under the same claim.  The Veterans Court concluded that neither the veteran nor the record raised the theory of entitlement to service connection on a direct basis; thus, the Board did not err in failing to discuss that theory.

The Federal Circuit, in holding that the Board must liberally read filings by counsel on direct appeal, stated that the requirement did not extend to filings by counsel in the CUE context.  The Federal Circuit explained that CUE collateral proceedings are fundamentally different from direct appeals.  No regulation requires VA to liberally construe CUE claims; the burden is on the claimant to specifically raise each issue.  Furthermore, each new CUE theory is independent for res judicata purposes, so a narrow reading of what CUE theories were raised would work no disadvantage to the claimant, as any unraised theories could be raised in a new CUE action.

Although VA is obligated to determine all potential claims raised by the evidence, claims which have no support in the record need not be considered by the Board.  The Federal Circuit noted that the regulations clearly oblige the claimant to raise issues in the first instance, including in direct appeals.  See [38 C.F.R. § 20.202](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=2023d80326108dd2b839a57dc3321bc8&node=se38.2.20_1202&rgn=div8).  The Federal Circuit stated that the Board, in the present case, was obligated to consider direct as well as secondary service connection if raised by the record since the “basic issue” of service connection had been raised.

The Federal Circuit nevertheless affirmed the Veterans Court decision because the Veterans Court had found that the record did not raise any issue of direct service connection.
