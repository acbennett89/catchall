Decision Assessment Document
 Robinson v. Peake, Jan 29, 2008, 21 Vet.App. 545
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court, rejecting the each-theory-equals-a-distinct-claim concept, held that separate theories in support of a claim for benefits for a particular disability does not equate to separate claims for benefits for that disability.

The Court also held that the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) is not required *sua sponte* to raise and reject “all possible” theories of entitlement.  The Board is only required to discuss a theory of entitlement that was raised either by the appellant or by the evidence of record. Note: sua sponte means to voluntarily; on its own motion; without prompting.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran filed a claim in December 1998 for service connection for heart and thyroid conditions as secondary to his service-connected peptic ulcer disease.  The Board remanded the case in October 2001 for additional development to include a VA examination that addressed the etiology of the claimed conditions and provided an opinion as to whether the heart and thyroid conditions were aggravated by or related to the service-connected peptic ulcer disease.  A VA examination undertaken in November 2002 included an opinion that there was no relationship between the heart and thyroid conditions and service-connected peptic ulcer disease.

The Board denied the appeal in May 2004, finding no relationship between the claimed conditions and service-connected peptic ulcer disease.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board violated *Stegall v. West,* 11 Vet.App. 268 (1998) by failing to ensure compliance with the prior remand order as the examiner failed to discuss etiology.  He argued that this rendered the examination inadequate.  Also, the veteran contended that failing to adjudicate whether he was entitled to service connection for heart and thyroid conditions on a direct basis violated the holding of [*Schroeder v.* *West*, 212 F.3d 1265 (Fed.Cir.2000)](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/212/212.F3d.1265.99-7103.html).

The Court, in rejecting the veteran’s argument, held that the Federal Circuit’s holding in *Schroeder*, as well as other case law, repudiates the each-theory-equals-a-distinct claim concept.  The Court determined that because the veteran’s new argument in support of disability benefits on a direct basis does not constitute a separate claim (neither the veteran nor counsel had raised the theory previously before the RO or Board), it had jurisdiction over the matter.

The Court concluded that there was nothing in the veteran’s appeal to the Board, prepared by his counsel, that could have been expected to be liberally construed or read sympathetically in order to determine that the veteran was seeking to have his claim adjudicated on a theory of direct service connection.  The Court concluded that the Board did not err by failing to adjudicate a claim on the basis of direct service connection as such theory was not contained in the Substantive Appeal or any other document before the Board.

The Court also determined that there was no evidence cited by the veteran’s attorney that should have compelled the Board to adjudicate the claim on a direct service connection theory.  There was no medical or lay evidence or continuity of symptomatology in the record that linked the thyroid or heart conditions to peptic ulcer disease.  The Court indicated that it would be impractical for the Board to explicitly mention every prior medical record noting any type of symptom and state that there is no evidence that the symptom is directly connected to the current condition.  The Court presumes that the Board considered this evidence and found it too scant to warrant comment.

The Court also rejected the veteran’s arguments under *Stegall* as it found the 2002 VA examination compliant with the Board’s previous remand order.

The Court affirmed the Board’s May 2004 decision.
