Decision Assessment Document
 Hill v. McDonald, Oct 7, 2016, 28 Vet.App. 243 (2016)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that if VA determines that a claimant has established veteran status for a period of active duty for training (ACDUTRA) by establishing service connection for one disability, the claimant may take advantage of the presumption of aggravation for other preexisting disabilities claim to have been aggravated during the same period of ACDUTRA.

The Court also held that it is not necessary for a claimant to have undergone an entrance examination prior to the period of ACDUTRA for the presumption of aggravation to be applied if the baseline severity of the preexisting condition can be determined through other contemporaneous evidence.

(The Court held that their holdings also apply to claimants whose claims are based on inactive duty for training).

**Impact on VBA:**

The Manual will be revised under III.ii.6.A.2.i and IV.ii.2.B.4 in accordance with the Court’s opinion.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who was in the Army National Guard from 1980 to 2002, served a period of ACDUTRA from June 7 to June 21, 1997.  Service treatment and private medical records showed treatment for depression and posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) prior to and during the Jun 1997 ACDUTRA.  Private medical records also showed treatment for a back condition due to a 1994 workplace injury.

During the June 1997 ACDUTRA, the veteran was standing in close proximity to a tree that was struck by lightning, which caused him to fall to the ground.  The veteran immediately sought medical attention for knee and back pain.  A line of duty (LOD) investigation determined that the knee injury was incurred in the line of duty and the chronic low back pain was “in line of duty –[existed prior to service]-aggravation.”  The investigator indicated that there was a history of low back pain; however, the force of being thrown to the ground may have aggravated the condition.  Evidence was later incorporated into the record indicating worsening of physical and psychiatric symptoms after the lightning strike.

The veteran filed a claim for service connection for multiple conditions in June 2002 to include the low back and knee conditions, as well as memory loss, alleging that these conditions were secondary to the lightning strike during ACDUTRA.  The regional office (RO) granted service connection for the knee condition as a result of the lightning strike, but denied the low back and memory loss claims.

In September 2008, the veteran sought to reopen the claims for the low back disability and short-term memory loss with PTSD, which the RO denied.  He was later afforded a VA psychiatric examination in March 2010 in which the examiner opined that the veteran’s depression was not worsened by the lightning strike, but the lightning strike had worsened the low back pain, which aggravated the depression beyond its normal progression.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in April 2014, determined that new and material evidence had not been submitted to reopen the low back claim and denied service connection for an acquired psychiatric disability, to include PTSD.  While acknowledging that veteran status was in order by establishing service connection for the knee disability, the Board, with respect to the low back claim, found that the Internet articles submitted were immaterial and dismissed his testimony that the lightning strike caused him to hit a tree as patently incredible.  The Board relied on the March 2010 VA opinion to deny the psychiatric disability claim.

The veteran argued before the Court that he is entitled to the presumption of aggravation for both disabilities on appeal because he had established that he injured his right knee during a period of ACDUTRA.  The Secretary agreed with the veteran, espousing that the Court’s precedent, beginning with Biggins v. Derwinski, 1 Vet.App. 474 (1991), and continuing in Paulson v. Brown, 7 Vet.App. 466 (1995), supports his position.  The Secretary also asserted that an entrance examination was unnecessary to establish the baseline severity of a preexisting condition if relevant medical evidence existed contemporaneous to entry into the relevant period of ACDUTRA that was sufficient to establish a baseline as to the severity of the injury or disease for purposes of determining whether such injury or disease was aggravated during ACDUTRA.

The Court held that since the veteran is service connected for a right knee disability incurred during the relevant period of ACDUTRA, he is also a veteran for the purposes of his claim for the psychiatric disorder and low back disability alleged to have worsened during the same period of service.  The Court further held that it necessarily follows that where VA concludes that a claimant that has established veteran status as a result of one service-connected disability incurred during a period of ACDUTRA, that claimant is then entitled to the presumption of aggravation for a different, preexisting disability shown to have worsened during the same period of ACDUTRA.

The Court also concluded that the parties’ agreed upon interpretation that no entrance examination is necessary when there is contemporaneous evidence of the baseline severity of the preexisting condition is the proper interpretation of 38 U.S.C. § 1153 for claims based on a period of ACDUTRA where a claimant is able to establish veteran status by virtue of one service-connected disability and attempts to service connect a second disability under the presumption of aggravation.  The Court noted that because service personnel entering into periods of ACDUTRA are not routinely provided entrance examinations, interpreting the statute as requiring an entrance examination as a prerequisite to the application of the presumption of aggravation would render meaningless it’s holding that the presumption of aggravation applies under the circumstances of the case.

The Court held that if a claimant submits documentation that, together with the rest of the record, evidences both the baseline severity of the preexisting condition prior to the period of ACDUTRA and a permanent increase in disability during a period of ACDUTRA, the claimant may take advantage of the presumption of aggravation.

The Court, in analyzing the merits of the case, concluded that the Board clearly erred in determining that the Internet articles were not material because they did not tend to show that the veteran’s current low back disability was incurred in or aggravated by  his period of ACDUTRA.  The Court noted that one of the articles addressed the missing element of nexus in stating that a lightning strike might affect the musculoskeletal system, which was material to whether the lightning strike could have worsened the preexisting back condition.

The Court also held that the Board’s determination that the veteran’s testimony of the effect of the lightning strike to be patently incredible as clearly erroneous due to the Board having weighed the evidence, which is prohibited.  VA is required to presume the credibility of newly submitted evidence for the purposes of determining whether it is material and sufficient to reopen a previously denied claim.  The Court reversed the Board’s finding and remanded the matter with directions to the Board to reopen the claim for a back disability.

Regarding the psychiatric disability appeal, the Court agreed with the Secretary’s concession that the Board provided an inadequate statement of reasons or bases for relying on the March 2010 VA psychiatric examination as evidence against the veteran’s claim.  The Court indicated that it was unclear as to why the examiner described the veteran’s alcohol use immediately following the lightning strike as a compounding factor causing an increase in depression, rather than as favorable evidence of aggravation related to the lightning strike.  The Court directed that, on remand, the Board must seek clarification from the March 2010 VA examiner or provide the veteran a new examination that specifically addresses the issue.

The Court further instructed the Board that if determines that there was a permanent increase in severity of the low back or psychiatric condition during ACDUTRA, it must afford the veteran the presumption of aggravation for the condition.
