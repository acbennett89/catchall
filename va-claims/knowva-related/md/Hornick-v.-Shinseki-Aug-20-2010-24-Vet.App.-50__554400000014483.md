Decision Assessment Document
 Hornick v. Shinseki, Aug 20, 2010, 24 Vet.App. 50
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that protection, under [38 U.S.C. § 1159](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1159), from severance of awards of service connection that have been in effect for 10 or more years also extends to awards of compensation under [38 U.S.C. § 1151](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1151) that have been in effect for 10 or more years.

The Court rejected [VA Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion (GC Prec.Op.) 13-96](http://www.va.gov/ogc/docs/1996/Prc13-96.doc), which determined that the section 1159 protection does not apply to awards of compensation under section 1151.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.  The Office of General Counsel will withdraw Prec.Op. 13-96.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, while waiting outside a VAMC dental clinic in March 1991, injured his low back when the bench he was sitting on broke.  The regional office (RO), in May 1996, awarded compensation benefits under section 1151, effective September 1994.  In February 1997, the RO, finding the veteran to be totally disabled as a result of his March 1991 low back injury, increased his disability rating to 100 percent, effective September 1994.

In January 2006, the RO severed benefits under section 1151 on the basis that the original grant was clearly and unmistakably erroneous because the injury did not result from examination, hospital care, or treatment (citing *Sweitzer v. Brown*, 5 Vet.App. 503 (1993)).  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in September 2008, affirmed the RO’s severance action.  Relying upon GC. Prec. Op. 13-96, the Board found that section 1159 protection did not extend to awards of compensation benefits under section 1151; thus, the RO was not prohibited from severing section 1151 compensation benefits.

The veteran argued before the Court that since section [1151](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1151) requires VA to treat disabilities compensated under that section “as if” they were service connected, section [1159](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1159) prevents VA from severing that award of benefits once it has been in effect for 10 years.

The Court indicated that the term “service connection” in section 1159 would seem to exclude section 1151 compensation recipients from its protection, because that statute provides only compensation, not service connection.  However, the Court stated that Congressional intent was clear that beneficiaries of section 1151 compensation are to have their disabilities treated “as if” they were service connected.

The Court held that because both sections 1151 and 1159 are contained in title 38 under the subchapter dealing with general compensation provisions, it would violate proper statutory construction to conclude that these sections are not part of a harmonious whole and that the protection afforded by section 1159 would not apply to those individuals receiving section 1151 compensation benefits.  The Court stated that to conclude otherwise would be inconsistent with the statutory scheme of chapter 11, the purpose of which is to provide disabled veterans with compensation and, under certain circumstances, to protect the basis for that compensation.  The Court also noted that the Supreme Court held in !@#$%*Brown v. Gardner*, 513 U.S. 115 (1994) that if a statute is ambiguous, “interpretive doubt is to be resolved in the veteran’s favor.”

The Court, observing that while the Board is bound by VA General Counsel opinion, it is not, rejected the determination contained in GC Prec.Op. 13-96.

The Court reversed the Board’s September 2008 decision and remanded the case for the Board to reinstate the veteran’s benefits under section 1151.
