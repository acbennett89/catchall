Decision Assessment Document
 Winn v. Brown, Jan 30, 1996, 8 Vet.App. 510 (1996)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the Secretary's interpretation or 38 U.S.C. 1111, as codified at 38 C.F.R. 3.303(c) is entitled to deference as it is reasonable and plausible.  Therefore, personality disorders are not diseases or injury-related defects within the meaning of veteran's benefits law, and the presumption of soundness under section 1111 does not apply to personality disorders.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran served on active duty from August 1962 to June 1968.  At enlistment, he denied any history of psychiatric problems.  After several years, he had disciplinary problems and was separated from service with a diagnosis of immature personality with passive aggressive features.  It was subsequently determined that he had been diagnosed with paranoid schizophrenia as a teenager in 1959.  Preservice treatment records denoted that he had been diagnosed with personality disorder, paranoid or depressive neurosis, and schizophrenia.  His claim for service connection has been denied on more than a dozen occasions at the regional office level and on six occasions by the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA).  On appeal to the Court in 1991, the appeal was remanded for the BVA to locate missing medical records from a VAMC between 1968 and 1973.  The BVA returned the appeal to the regional office for a search to locate the records, but the search was not completed.  In an August 1993 BVA decision, it was conceded that the veteran had submitted new and material evidence, but the claim was again denied on the merits.  The Board also relied on a medical advisor opinion which did not favor the appellant.
