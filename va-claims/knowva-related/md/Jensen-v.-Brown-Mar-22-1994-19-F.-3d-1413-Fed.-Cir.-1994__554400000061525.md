Decision Assessment Document
 Jensen v. Brown, Mar 22, 1994, 19 F. 3d 1413 (Fed. Cir. 1994)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that reading 38 U.S.C. 1154(b) and 38 CFR 3.306(b)(2) together, an entirely consistent picture for analyzing a claim of aggravation is readily apparent.  The veteran has the opportunity to establish a rebuttable presumption of aggravation by providing the kind of evidence one engaged in combat or held as a prisoner of war is most likely to have available, lay testimony or other informal evidence of symptomatic manifestations, temporary or otherwise, of incurrence or aggravation.  Once this showing has been made, the government has the burden to rebut by clear and convincing proof that there has been no increase in the severity of the preexisting condition, thereby establishing lack of a statutory requirement, or that any increase was the result of natural progression.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims, in Jensen v. Brown, 4 Vet.App. 304 (1993), invalidated the second sentence of 3.306(b)(2), holding that temporary exacerbations are insufficient to establish service aggravation, and concluded that the regulatory provision was invalid because 38 U.S.C. 1154(b) does not confer any additional substantive rights to combat veterans over non-combat veterans.
