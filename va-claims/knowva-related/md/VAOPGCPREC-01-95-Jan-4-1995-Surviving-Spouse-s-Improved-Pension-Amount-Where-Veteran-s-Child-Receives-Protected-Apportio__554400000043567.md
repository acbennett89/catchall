Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 01-95, Jan 4, 1995, Surviving Spouse's Improved Pension Amount Where Veteran's Child Receives Protected Apportionment of Section 306 Pension
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Is the Department of Veterans Affairs Adjudication Procedure Manual M21-1, part IV, 20.46b., inconsistent with applicable law and regulation insofar as the manual directs that a surviving spouse's improved-pension award shall reflect the dependency of a child who is not in the surviving spouse's custody, but who receives a protected apportionment of the surviving spouse's pension under section 306 of Public Law No. 95-588?  If the manual provision is consistent with the law and regulations, must it be applied uniformly regardless of whether it is to the surviving spouse's advantage?

**Impact on VBA**

The provision in M21-1, Part IV, par. 20.46b., requiring payment of increased improved pension to a surviving spouse when a veteran's child not in the spouse's custody receives a protected apportionment, is inconsistent with the provisions of 38 U.S.C. § 1541(b) and (c) which authorize payment of the increased rate only when the veteran's child is in the surviving spouse's custody.

**Summary on facts and findings**

Under the provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.701(a), an election of improved pension under the provisions of Pub. L. 95-588 by a surviving spouse controls the claims of all children, however, such election shall not prejudice the rights of any child entitled to receive an apportionment under the prior law in effect on December 31, 1978.  The cited manual provision provides that the award to a surviving spouse is to be paid at the higher rate under section 1541(c) where there is a child of the veteran who is not in the surviving spouse's custody, but who has a protected entitlement to an apportionment.  The General Counsel determined that the statute is unambiguous and that, once a surviving spouse elects to receive improved pension, pension must be paid in accordance with the current provisions of section 1541, and VA lacks authority to pay the increased statutory rate under section 1541(c) when the veteran's child is not in the surviving spouse's custody.  General Counsel held that there is nothing in the applicable statutes or regulations which clearly indicates that the pension benefit payable to the child must be paid through withholding of the surviving spouse's improved pension award, rather than as a distinct entitlement payable under prior law and protected under the Pension Improvement Act.  Therefore, a child's protected pension award could be paid as a separate payment under the old law, rather than as a portion of the surviving spouse's improved pension award.  General Counsel also noted that nothing in the statutes or regulations precludes an apportionment of a surviving spouse's pension to a child out of custody when the spouse is being paid at the rate for a spouse alone under section 1541(b).

Because par. 20.46b has been determined to be inconsistent with the statute, it must be revised.  It is recommended that this revision take the form of the first option presented; that is, the surviving spouse be paid pension under the provisions of section 1541(b), and the child be awarded a separate payment under the old law, exclusive of the surviving spouse's improved pension award.  This change will necessitate a review of other manual provisions, such as Part IV, par. 20.47(a)(3) EXAMPLE, and Part V, chs. 4 and 6.  Additionally, computer audits of separate awards under the same claim number should be reviewed for possible revisions.
