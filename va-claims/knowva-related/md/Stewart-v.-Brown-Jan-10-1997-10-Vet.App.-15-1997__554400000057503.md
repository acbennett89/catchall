Decision Assessment Document
 Stewart v. Brown, Jan 10, 1997, 10 Vet.App. 15 (1997)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

Under 38 C.F.R. 3.151(a) VA “may” treat a compensation claim as a claim for pension and vice versa.  The Secretary is not automatically required to treat every compensation claim as being for pension or vice versa.  Rather, the Secretary has to exercise his discretion under the regulation in accordance with the contents of the application and the evidence in support of it.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran filed a claim for disability benefits in August 1970, and was awarded nonservice-connected disability pension based on a nervous disorder with epilepsy.  In December 1988, he filed a claim for service connection for post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD).  After initially being denied because there was no diagnosis of PTSD, service connection was established for this disorder with an effective date of December 28, 1988.  The veteran appealed the effective date on the basis that he had filed a claim for a nervous condition in 1970, did not know the difference between compensation and pension, and could not have been expected to know what PTSD was at that time.  On remand from the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA), the regional office reviewed additional medical records showing a diagnosis of personality disorder and psychosis with epilepsy in 1970 and 1972.  There was no indication of PTSD.  The denial of an earlier effective date was continued and affirmed by the BVA.

In a decision dated 10/15/96, the Court affirmed the decision of the BVA.  The appellant filed a motion for reconsideration alleging that the panel had erred by holding that VA properly exercised its discretion in treating his claim as a claim for pension, and by factually finding that he was mentally competent at the time his claim was filed so that VA had no heightened duty to assist.  The Court agreed that it had erred in making a factual determination that the appellant was competent at the time he filed his claim, and granted the motion for reconsideration.  The Court held, however, that the factual determination did not alter the result in the original opinion.

The Court reviewed the applicable statutes and regulations concerning effective dates and noted that, generally, benefits are effective from the date of receipt of a claim or the date entitlement arose, whichever is later.  The Court also noted that under the provisions of 38 C.F.R. 3.151(a), a claim for compensation may be considered a claim for pension and vice versa.  The use of the word "may" in the regulation means the Secretary is not automatically required to treat every compensation claim as also being a pension claim and vice versa, but must exercise his discretion in accordance with the contents of the application and the evidence in support of it.  In this case, the initial claim was for nonservice-connected pension.  There was no evidence submitted with the application, or in the claims file, which could have been construed as a claim for compensation, and VA, therefore, was no obligated to consider the claim for pension as one for compensation as well.  The Court further noted that even if the appellant had filed a claim for compensation in 1970, VA could not have awarded an earlier effective date under governing statutes and regulations, because evidence necessary to support a factual finding of the presence of PTSD was not in the record prior to December 1988.  The appellant also argued that he did not know the difference between compensation and pension when he filed the claim.  The Court noted, however, that he had applied for, and been denied, service connection for flat feet in 1957, thereby evidencing some understanding of the application process.  Appellant's counsel argued that VA had a heightened duty to assist the appellant because he was mentally incompetent when he signed the 1970 application.  The Court rejected this argument as being unsupported by statute or existing caselaw.
