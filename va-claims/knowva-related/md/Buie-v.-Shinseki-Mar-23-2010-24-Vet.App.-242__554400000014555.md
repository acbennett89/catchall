Decision Assessment Document
 Buie v. Shinseki, Mar 23, 2010, 24 Vet.App. 242
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that a total disability rating due to individual unemployability (TDIU) that is based on more than one underlying disability does not satisfy the first requirement of [38 U.S.C. § 1114(s)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1114) that a veteran have “a service-connected disability rated as total” for the purpose of establishing entitlement to special monthly compensation (SMC) at the (s) rate.

Note: 38 U.S.C. § 1114(s) provides for payment of SMC at the housebound rate if a veteran has a service-connected disability rated as total and additional service-connected disability or disabilities independently ratable at 60 percent or more.

The Court also held that if such a situation arises, VA’s duty to maximize a claimant’s benefits requires it to assess all of the veteran’s service-connected disabilities without regard to the order in which service connection was awarded to determine whether any combination of the disabilities establishes entitlement to SMC under section 1114(s).  This duty extends to total disability ratings established on a schedular or extraschedular basis.

If after such assessment, VA determines that the veteran is entitled to SMC, the effective date of the award of SMC will be the effective date assigned for the award of benefits for the final disability that forms the relevant combination of disabilities.

**Impact on VBA:**

No regulatory revision or manual change is required.  However, this decision may impose an additional review requirement subsequent to an initial grant of a total disability rating.  If a veteran who has a total disability rating based on multiple disabilities and who is subsequently awarded or is currently claiming service connection for an additional disability or disabilities or an increased rating seeking SMC under section 1114(s), the case should be reviewed to determine if the evidence shows that a new grant of service connection or an increased evaluation of an existing service connected single disability, standing alone, is sufficient to warrant a TDIU grant for the purpose of assessing entitlement to SMC at the (s) rate.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who had been service connected and rated 10 percent for a low back disability since 1971, filed a claim for increased evaluation for the disability in November 1995.  The regional office (RO) denied the claim in May 1996.  He submitted a statement in February 1997 requesting reopening of the claim.  In the statement, he indicated that his back condition had worsened and was receiving ongoing treatment from VA.  The RO, in July 1997, increased the evaluation to 20 percent for the low back disability.  In an August 1997 statement, the veteran again requested an increased rating for his low back disability.  In March 1998, the RO increased the evaluation for the low back disability to 40 percent.  The veteran wrote to the RO in April 1998, requesting a 60 percent disability evaluation for his low back condition.  The following month, he again requested reevaluation of his low back disability and sought to reopen previously denied claims for service connection for bilateral knee disabilities.  Note: The RO eventually granted service connection for traumatic arthritis of both knees, awarding separate 10 percent evaluations, effective May 29, 1998.

The RO, in July 2001, granted a 60 percent evaluation for the low back disability, effective May 29, 1998.  A TDIU evaluation based on his service-connected knee and back disabilities was also assigned from the same date.  The veteran contested the effective date of the TDIU evaluation.

In May 2004, the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) granted service connection for posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD).  The RO assigned a 30 percent evaluation from August 28, 1997, and a 70 percent evaluation from January 26, 2001.

The Board, in May 2008, granted an effective date of April 16, 1998, for the TDIU evaluation and the 60 percent evaluation for the low back disability.  The Board concluded that the veteran’s February 1997, August 1997, April 1998, and May 1998 statements were not notices of disagreement (NODs); thus, the RO had not erred in treating those statements as new claims for increased evaluation for the low back disorder.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board failed to consider [38 C.F.R. § 3.156(b)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=3fdbeb8453049e144df08e5e7c10f252&node=se38.1.3_1156&rgn=div8) for the purpose of determining whether his 1997 and 1998 statements abated the finality of the RO decisions.  He also contended that the Board erred in not awarding him SMC at the (s) rate, despite the fact that he met the statutory requirements for such award.  The veteran claimed that because, the RO in July 2001, already granted him entitlement to TDIU based on multiple disabilities from May 1998 (prior to the Board’s grant of an earlier effective for the TDIU grant), he was entitled to receive SMC effective January 26, 2001, the effective date VA assigned for the award of the 70 percent evaluation for PTSD.

The Court concluded that based upon the plain and unambiguous language of section [1114(s)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1114) demonstrating Congressional intent not to permit combined ratings to satisfy the statute’s first requirement of a “service-connected disability rated as total,” and the Secretary’s reasonable interpretation of the statute under [38 C.F.R. § 3.350(i)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=3fdbeb8453049e144df08e5e7c10f252&node=se38.1.3_1350&rgn=div8), it could not discern any rational basis to permit a “total” rating – schedular, extraschedular, or TDIU – to satisfy the statute when the total rating is based on more than one service-connected disability.  Although a TDIU rating may satisfy the “rated as total” element of section 1114(s), the Court held that a TDIU rating based on multiple underlying disabilities cannot satisfy the section 1114(s) requirement of “a service-connected disability” because that requirement must be met by a single disability.

The Court, in holding that VA’s duty to maximize a claimant’s benefits requires it to assess all of the veteran’s service-connected disabilities without regard to the order in which service connection was awarded to determine whether any combination of the disabilities establishes entitlement to SMC at the (s) rate, envisioned a scenario in which a veteran receives a TDIU rating based on multiple underlying disabilities and then later receives a schedular disability rating for a single, separate disability that would, by itself, create the basis for a TDIU award. In such a scenario, if the order in which the veteran received disability awards was determinative, he would be ineligible to receive SMC.  However, if the veteran had been awarded service connection for these disabilities in the reverse order, he would be eligible to receive SMC.  The Court concluded that Congress did not intend such an absurd result.

The Court vacated and remanded the Board’s decision.  The Court instructed the Board to determine whether the veteran’s PTSD alone entitled him to a TDIU rating.  If so, he would be entitled to receive SMC at the (s) rate because of his other “disability or disabilities independently ratable at 60 percent.”

The Court also determined that the veteran’s 1997 and 1998 statements were not NODs; however, the Court held that the Board erred in failing to determine whether any of those statements, which were received within one year of the RO decisions, included the submission of new and material evidence, as § 3.156(b) requires that any subsequent decision based on such evidence relate back to the original claim.  The Court directed the Board to discuss § 3.156(b) to determine if any of the veteran’s statements submitted within a year of a RO decision included the submission of new and material evidence.
