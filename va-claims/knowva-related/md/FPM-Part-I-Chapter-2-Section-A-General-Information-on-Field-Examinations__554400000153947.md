## 1.  Authority and Purpose of Field Examinations

---

**Introduction**

This topic contains general information on the authority and purpose of field examinations, including

* [purpose of conducting field examinations](#1c)
* [actions which do not constitute a field examination](#1d), and
* [responsibility for determining a fiduciary](#1e).

---

**Change Date**

---

**I.2.A.1.a . Authority to Conduct Field Examinations**

[38 U.S.C. 5507](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5507) requires the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) to conduct an inquiry or investigation regarding the fitness of a person to serve as fiduciary for a beneficiary.

[38 U.S.C. 5711](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5711) authorizes VA to conduct investigations and examine witnesses upon any matter within the jurisdiction of VA, require the production of books, papers, documents, and other evidence; and take affidavits and administer oaths and affirmations.

---

**I.2.A.1.b . Reasons for Conducting Field Examinations**

In accordance with [38 U.S.C. 5502](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5502), VA makes benefit payments directly to adult beneficiaries unless VA determines payment to a duly recognized fiduciary is in the best interest of the beneficiary.  When the payment method is anything other than direct and unsupervised, the fiduciary hub (hub) must conduct periodic field examinations or other approved means of supervision to continually assess the beneficiary’s status and integrity of services provided by the fiduciary.

The reasons for conducting a field examination include

* determining whether benefits should be paid directly to the beneficiary or continue to be made to the fiduciary, and
* ensuring the well-being of a beneficiary and the beneficiary’s dependents.

***Reference***:  For more information on field examination types, see [FPM, Part I, 2.A.2](#2).

---

**I.2.A.1.c . Purpose of Conducting Field Examinations**

With respect to fiduciary appointments, VA will conduct field examinations for the purpose of performing oversight of beneficiaries in the Fiduciary Program and supervision of fiduciaries appointed to manage their VA benefits.

---

**I.2.A.1.d . Actions Which Do Not Constitute a Field Examination**

To be considered a field examination, it is essential that the elements of inquiry, search, or investigation are present.

The following actions do *not* constitute Fiduciary Program field examinations of any type and do not fulfill VA’s responsibility to perform oversight:

* merely visiting a home
* dropping off VA information
* completing eligibility verification reports
* collecting routine documents
* conducting an away-from-office general interview on benefits
* taking a claim for benefits, or
* delivering letters, subpoenas, or similar documents without gathering and documenting facts, or developing evidence.

***Reference***:  For more information on due diligence, see [FPM, Part I, 2.B.2.i](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153948/).

---

**I.2.A.1.e . Responsibility for Determining a Fiduciary**

If the beneficiary is under the age of majority, rated unable to manage their funds, or under a court appointed guardianship, the Field Examiner (FE) is responsible for determining the type of fiduciary relationship that will best serve the needs of the beneficiary.  Benefit payments may be made

* directly to a supervised direct payment beneficiary
* to a VA-appointed fiduciary
* to a spouse fiduciary, or
* in extenuating circumstances only, to a court-appointed fiduciary (VA recognized).

***References***:  For more information on

* appointment and fiduciary types, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.4.a](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153950/), and
* determining a fiduciary for a beneficiary with a previously established court appointment, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.4.g](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153950/).

**I.2.A.2.a . IA Field Examinations**

An initial appointment (IA) field examination involves the need for and, when necessary, the qualification and appointment of a fiduciary to receive VA benefits or insurance payments.  The appointment is made on behalf of a beneficiary who is determined to be unable to manage their benefits, has not reached age of majority, or is under a court-appointed general law or plenary guardianship or Uniform Veterans Guardianship Act guardianship.

IA field examinations are completed to

* assess the

  + needs and well-being of the beneficiary and their dependents
  + ability of an adult beneficiary to manage funds, and
  + suitability of the proposed fiduciary
* identify a proposed fiduciary
* document income and expenditures for the beneficiary
* ensure VA funds are properly protected, if required, and
* provide advice and assistance with benefits and needs.

---

**I.2.A.2.b . Types of IA Field Examinations**

There are three types of IA field examinations:

* IA
* successor, and
* expedited.

***IA***:  A face-to-face interview or telephone interview, when applicable, and investigation activity conducted by the designated hub or other qualified VA personnel for the purpose of qualifying and appointing a fiduciary for an individual who is not currently under hub supervision.

***Successor IA (SIA)***:  A face-to-face inquiry or telephone interview, when applicable, and investigation activity conducted by the designated hub or other qualified VA personnel for the purpose of qualifying and appointing a replacement fiduciary for the beneficiary.

***Expedited IA (EIA)***:  A telephone interview and investigation activity conducted by the designated hub or other qualified VA personnel for the purpose of expediting the qualification and appointment of a fiduciary.

***Note***:  EIAs will occur if

* the proposed fiduciary is the beneficiary's

  + spouse, or
  + parent (natural, adopted, or stepparent),and the beneficiary is under the age of majority, or
* the annual amount of VA benefits the proposed fiduciary would manage for the beneficiary does not exceed the amount specified in [38 U.S.C. 5507(c)(2)(D)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5507), as adjusted by VA pursuant to [38 U.S.C. 5312](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5312).

***Exception***:  For situations where the criteria above is met but the EIA is not appropriate, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.6.b](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153950/).

***Reference***:  For more information on the current annual adjusted benefit rate for the purpose of EIAs, see [FPM, Part I, 2.A.2.j](#2j).

**I.2.A.2.d . Follow-up Field Examinations**

Follow-up field examinations are performed subsequent to an IA field examination.  These examinations may include the need to

* reassess the

  + needs of the beneficiary and dependents, and
  + adult beneficiary’s ability to manage funds
* assess the fiduciary relationship
* uncover any irregularities, and
* provide advice and assistance in relation to VA or other benefits based on the beneficiary’s needs.

A follow-up field examination that results in a change of fiduciary is considered an SIA field examination.  The VBMS EP must be changed to reflect the appropriate examination type.

***Reference***:  For more information on scheduling future oversight actions, see [FPM, Part I, 2.D.1](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153952/).

---

**I.2.A.2.e . Types of Follow-up Field Examinations**

The four types of follow-up field examinations are listed below.

***Scheduled follow-up field examinations***:  The inquiry and monitoring activity conducted for the purpose of protecting the beneficiary and their VA benefits through face-to-face contact with the beneficiary.

***Telephone follow-up field examinations***:  The inquiry and monitoring activity conducted for the purpose of monitoring the beneficiary and their VA benefits through telephone contact with the beneficiary.

***Fund usage field examinations***:  The specific inquiry into the fiduciary’s use of the beneficiary’s funds for the purpose of reviewing an issue affecting the fiduciary’s management of VA benefits, which was identified or reported to the hub, and determining if the fiduciary’s appointment should continue.  Appropriate reasons for fund usage field examination requests could include, but are not limited to

* accounting assistance issues, or
* fund usage review documentation issues.

***Unscheduled follow-up field examinations***:  The inquiry conducted for the purpose of reviewing a specific issue affecting the *beneficiary’s well-being*, which was identified by or reported to the hub.  Appropriate reasons for unscheduled follow-up field examination requests could include, but are not limited to

* adverse conditions, or
* a well-being check.

**I.2.A.2.g . Non-Fiduciary Program Field Examinations**

A non-fiduciary program field examination is a field examination that does not involve Fiduciary Program issues.  This type of exam is conducted to develop evidence to enable the requesting VA business line to resolve outstanding issue(s).

Non-fiduciary program field examinations are grouped into the following areas of investigation:

* debt collection
* bankruptcy
* fraud
* tort
* equal opportunity violations, and
* other.

The *other* category includes requests for facts or documentation involving claims for benefits such as compensation or pension.

Evaluating the information being requested, the hub employee conducting the non-fiduciary program field examination must determine the most appropriate method to conduct the examination.  For example, if the hub is being asked to assist with obtaining information relating to a bankruptcy, the hub employee must determine if the information may be obtained through telephone contact and through review of available records.  If the hub employee finds the information can be obtained without face-to-face contact, an in-person meeting should not occur.

***References***:  For more information on

* content requirements for non-fiduciary program field examination requests, see [FPM, Part I, 2.A.3.b](#3b), and
* field examination documentation, see [FPM, Part I, 2.E](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153953/).

**I.2.A.2.h . Special Considerations for a Beneficiary Under the Age of Majority**

Direct payment is made to a beneficiary under the age of majority when the beneficiary

* is in, or was discharged from, military service, or
* receives benefits as the surviving spouse of a Veteran.

When any of the above criteria are met, hub supervision is not required.

***Note***:  Direct payment is made to a beneficiary when the beneficiary attains the age of majority under State law, unless the child is rated unable to manage funds or is under a court-appointed guardianship.

When direct payment to a beneficiary under the age of majority is authorized, complete a [*VA Form 21P-555, Certificate of Legal Capacity to Receive and Disburse Benefits and Fee Authorization*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-555-ARE.pdf), annotate the reason the beneficiary is paid directly and refer [*VA Form 21P-555*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-555-ARE.pdf) to the VA Insurance Center in Philadelphia, by email at [VAVBAPHI/IC/295](mailto:295.VBAINS@va.gov) for action.  If the beneficiary is in military service, the report should include the beneficiary’s last known address in military service.

Insurance payments due to a beneficiary under the age of majority are considered VA benefits.  VA insurance policy requires that these payments are supervised by the hub.  In this case, the Philadelphia Insurance Center will request that the hub appoint and supervise a fiduciary using a [*VA Form 21-592*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21-592-ARE.pdf).  The hub will conduct an IA field examination and take all necessary actions.

The supervision and future actions for VA benefits and insurance payments for beneficiaries under the age of majority living in foreign countries is accomplished through annual written contact.

***References***:  For more information on

* direct payment to a beneficiary under the age of majority, see

  + [38 CFR 13.100](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=37dc4787212c848633ff20581222cabc&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1100&rgn=div8), and
  + [38 U.S.C. 5502](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5502), and

**I.2.A.2.i . When Fiduciary Supervision Is Required for Beneficiaries Under the Age of Majority**

Fiduciary supervision for beneficiaries under the age of majority is required when

* the beneficiary under the age of majority is in receipt of

  + VA benefits, or
  + an apportioned award, or
* a VA insurance payment is due to a beneficiary under the age of majority.

**I.2.A.3.c . Review of Fiduciary Program Field Examination Requests**

The hub employee conducting the field examination must review the field examination request for adequacy and need.  Additionally, the hub employee conducting a follow-up field examination must determine the most appropriate type of field examination based on the beneficiary’s situation on the date of review.

If the hub employee conducting the field examination determines that there is evidence in the eFolder prior to the interview or during contact with the beneficiary that the beneficiary no longer meets the criteria for the current beneficiary follow-up contact type, the individual conducting the field examination must

* document the

  + evidence, and

**I.2.A.3.d . FHM Review of Non-Fiduciary Program Field Examination Requests**

The Fiduciary Hub Manager (FHM) or designee must review non-fiduciary program requests for adequacy and need.  The hub must return requests containing insufficient information or seeking information that can be obtained without a field examination to the originator noting the reason for return.

**I.2.A.3.e . IA Requests for Beneficiaries in Foreign Countries**

Requests for IA field examinations for beneficiaries in foreign countries are under the jurisdiction of hubs based upon the beneficiary’s location.

The table below describes the process for forwarding IA requests for beneficiaries living in foreign countries.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Requests concerning beneficiaries residing in …** | **Are forwarded to the …** |
| South and Central America, Mexico, and the Caribbean | Lincoln Fiduciary Hub    [VAVBALIN/RO/FIDUCIARY/PROMULGATION](mailto:Promulgation.VBALIN@va.gov). |
| all other foreign countries including the Philippines | Indianapolis Fiduciary Hub    [VAVBAIND/RO/FIDUCIARY/PROMULGATION](mailto:PROMULGATION.VBAINDY@va.gov). |

The hub will transmit requests for IA field examinations for beneficiaries in foreign countries to the appropriate foreign service post.

***Reference***:  For additional information on annual written contact, see [FPM, Part II, 2.D.1](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000154889/).

**I.2.A.3.g . Oversight of Beneficiaries in Foreign Countries**

Foreign beneficiaries will be afforded the least restrictive method of oversight.  For oversight following the IA, hubs will typically follow the annual written contact guidelines.

If additional oversight is required for a foreign beneficiary, hubs must first attempt to perform the required field examinations via telephone or virtual methods.  When face-to-face field examinations are required in foreign countries, they are normally conducted by U.S. Embassy or Consulate personnel.  In Canada, field examinations are conducted by personnel of Veterans Affairs Canada.
