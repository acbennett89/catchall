Decision Assessment Document
 Anderson v. Shinseki, Mar 13, 2009, 22 Vet.App. 423
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that when a case is submitted for extra-schedular consideration under [38 C.F.R. § 3.321(b)(1)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=7195f8a162ef31156f52e59a958b54c7&node=se38.1.3_1321&rgn=div8),  the analysis performed by the regional office (RO) or Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) is done for the limited purpose of determining whether *referral* of the matter for extra-schedular consideration is warranted. The actual review of the matter and approval of an extra-schedular rating is done in the first instance by the Director of Compensation and Pension (C&P) Service.

The Court clarified that the “steps” as used in [*Thun v. Peake*, 22 Vet.App. 111 (2008)](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014690/), are elements that must be established before an extra-schedular rating can be awarded. Note: The Court held in Thun that determining whether a claimant is entitled to an extra-schedular rating under § 3.321(b)(1) is a three-step inquiry to be undertaken by the RO or Board: (1) If an exceptional disability picture that renders available schedular evaluations for the service-connected disability as inadequate is shown, and (2) it is determined that an exceptional disability picture exhibits other related factors such as marked interference with employability or frequent periods of hospitalization”); then (3) the case must be referred to C& P Service to determine whether or not to assign an extra-schedular rating.

The Court, in affirming *Floyd v. Brown*, 9 Vet.App 88 (1996) , held that although the Board is precluded from initially assigning an extra schedular rating, there is no restriction on the Board’s ability to review the denial of an extra schedular rating on appeal.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who served honorably during World War II and the Korean War, was awarded service connection for hearing loss with a 10-percent evaluation in 2002. He disagreed with the decision and appeared before a decision review officer (DRO) in an informal conference in June 2004. The DRO discussed the applicability of an extra-schedular evaluation, and the veteran agreed to provide a statement detailing how his hearing loss caused marked interference with employment for submittal to “VA Central Office for extra-schedular evaluation.” In a subsequent letter, the veteran stated that his hearing loss caused him to retire early from his job with a telephone company with less than full pension in 1977 and adversely affected his work as a real estate agent from 1978 to 1984. The RO referred the claim to the Director of C&P Service for consideration of an extra-schedular rating. C&P Service, in March 2005, concluding that the veteran’s hearing loss was not so severe as to interfere with his ability to be gainfully employed, denied the extra-schedular evaluation.

The Board, in affirming the 10-percent schedular evaluation in April 2006, also discussed the C&P decision denying an extra-schedular evaluation and found that there was no evidence that the hearing loss caused marked interference with employment.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board exceeded its jurisdiction by overturning DRO findings that were favorable to his claim.

The Court, in rejecting the veteran’s argument, indicated that [38 U.S.C. § 7104(a)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/7104) contains no limitations on the Board’s ability to review favorable findings and that the statute permits it to review the entirety of proceedings below. The Court stated that the findings made by the DRO, implied by the act of referring the claim for extra-schedular consideration, were not binding upon C&P Service or the Board.

The Court explained that the Director of C&P is authorized to approve an extra-schedular evaluation by [§ 3.321(b)(1)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=7195f8a162ef31156f52e59a958b54c7&node=se38.1.3_1321&rgn=div8) and the analysis performed under the regulation by either the RO or the Board is done for the limited purpose of determining whether referral of extra-schedular consideration is warranted. Although the Board is precluded from initially assigning an extra-schedular evaluation, there is no restriction on the Board’s ability to review the denial of an extra-schedular rating on appeal under the *Thun* elements. Thus, the Court held that the Board did not exceed its jurisdiction in denying an extra-schedular evaluation for hearing loss.

The Court, in analyzing the Board’s reasons and bases, found that it lacked an articulation of the actual symptoms and functional impairments alleged by the veteran, such as his statements throughout the record that he had difficulty in communicating with others in work and social conversations. Because the Board’s description of the veteran’s disability picture presented by the record was incomplete, the Court held that its analysis of whether that disability picture is adequately contemplated by the rating scheduled is flawed.

The Court vacated and remanded the Board’s decision for readjudication.
