## 1.  **Interview Logistics and Contacts**

---

**Introduction**

This topic contains required information that the Field Examiner (FE) must gather and document in preparation for and during IA and SIA field examinations for both adult and minor beneficiaries, unless otherwise specified, including

* [documenting the interview logistics](#1a)
* contact with

  + [the beneficiary](#1b)
  + [dependents](#1c)
  + [child dependents and minor beneficiaries](#1d), and
  + [the fiduciary](#1e)
* [fiduciary training during the field examination](#1f)
* [when a face-to-face interview with the fiduciary will not be conducted](#1g)
* [when the minor beneficiary’s caregiver is not the fiduciary](#1h)
* [other contacts](#1i)
* [report that a beneficiary is a danger to self or others](#1j), and
* [Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) and Privacy Act requirements](#1k).

**I.2.C.1.a . Documenting the Interview Logistics**

The Field Examiner (FE) must provide information in the field examination that adequately details the interview, including

* date and method of contact of each interview
* identity and relationship of each person interviewed, and
* directions to the address of the fiduciary or beneficiary if it is not accessible by global positioning system or mapping technology.

---

**I.2.C.1.b . Contact With the Beneficiary**

During the field examination process, the FE must collect or verify the following information:

* full name
* date of birth
* Social Security number (SSN)
* telephone number
* mailing address, and
* physical address.

If the above information was collected and verified at the time of scheduling but the FE identifies changes during the field examination, the FE must update the beneficiary's record with the correct information.

If the identity of a beneficiary is in question, the FE must verify the identity by viewing one of the following documents:

* State-issued driver’s license or identification card
* Department of Defense (DoD) identification card
* passport or passport card
* Social Security card
* birth certificate, or
* government issued identification card.

If custody of a minor is in question, the FE must view the court documents awarding custody.

The table below describes the required contact with adult and minor beneficiaries, as specified, for initial appointment (IA) and successor IA (SIA) field examinations.

| **Beneficiary Type** | **Required Contact** |
| --- | --- |
| adult | Generally, the FE must conduct a face-to-face interview with the beneficiary.    ***Exception***:  A face-to-face interview with the beneficiary ***will not*** be conducted when   * the case qualifies for an expedited IA (EIA) as outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.6](#6) * the beneficiary is    + incarcerated, or   + a patient in a hospital with restrictive entry rules, such as a facility for the criminally insane that requires warden approval, or * there is a documented safety concern as outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.B.1.b](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153948/) that has not been resolved.   The FE must fully document when a face-to-face interview with the beneficiary is not conducted in the field examination to include   * the reason the face-to-face interview was not practicable, as determined on a case-by-case basis * applicable information found in the review of Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) systems as outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.B.1.a](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153948/), and * contact with the beneficiary via telephone to obtain the beneficiary information outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.2](#2)-[4](#4).  If any of the information cannot be obtained, the FE must explain the attempts at obtaining the information.   If the beneficiary cannot be reached by telephone, the FE must   * fully document the attempts, and * contact the facility where the beneficiary resides to verify the beneficiary’s status. |
| minor | Follow the guidance in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1.d](#1d) to determine   * when it is appropriate to contact the minor beneficiary directly, and * the most appropriate method for contacting the minor beneficiary, when applicable.   The field examination will document the method of contact or, if applicable, explain the reason the minor beneficiary was not contacted.    ***Note***:  A ***minor beneficiary*** is any beneficiary under the age of majority.  A beneficiary under the age of majority, is also considered a *child*.    ***Reference***:  For more information on the definition of a child, see [38 CFR 3.57](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFR2f2f66751230c72/section-3.57). |

***Notes***:

* In situations where the FE is unable to complete the interview with an adult beneficiary as scheduled (for example, the beneficiary was absent), due diligence must be fully documented when attempting to reestablish contact with the beneficiary.  Repeat the steps for scheduling an interview as outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.B.2.f](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153948/) except for the steps involving sending the *Bank Letter Beneficiary*.  These steps must be completed even if executed during initial scheduling as the beneficiary could have changed locations in the interim.
* If there is no new contact information and good cause is not shown for the beneficiary’s absenteeism, proceed with recommending suspension of benefits per [FPM, Part I, 4.A.1.e](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153961/).
* If safety concerns with the beneficiary are identified, follow the guidance in [FPM, Part I, 2.B.3.k-m](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153948/) to ensure proper application of the HOSTILE indicator in the system, if required.

***Important***:

* When completion of the interview is not possible, update the suspense date to reflect the due date of the next action coming due.
* When determining if a face-to-face interview is impracticable, consider the specific facts and circumstances of the interview, including the effort, cost, safety, time, and resources required to conduct it.

**I.2.C.1.c . Contact With Dependents**

FEs must attempt to contact each adult beneficiary’s dependents, or the custodian of child dependents under the age of 12, and document the contact in the field examination.  If the FE is unable to contact a dependent and the FE is otherwise unable to decide that the dependent’s needs are met, the field examination must document the FEs attempt to contact the dependent and provide an explanation as to why contact did not take place.

Where direct contact with a beneficiary’s adult dependents is warranted as indicated in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.2.d](#2d), but the dependents are unavailable for a face-to-face visit during the beneficiary interview, telephone contact is sufficient.

***References***:  For more information on

* contact with child dependents, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1.d](#1d)
* the definition of a dependent, see [38 CFR 13.20](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-13/section-13.20), and
* documenting dependents and their needs, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.2.d](#2d).

**I.2.C.1.d . Contact With Child Dependents and Minor Beneficiaries**

FEs will minimize direct contact with

* beneficiaries’ child dependents, and
* minor beneficiaries who are in the Fiduciary Program by reason of being less than the age of majority.

Use the table below to determine the appropriate method of

* obtaining financial information as described in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.3](#3), and
* assessing a dependent child or minor beneficiary’s situation as required by [FPM, Part I, 2.C.2.b](#2b)-[e](#2e).

| **If the individual is …** | **Then attempt to contact the …** |
| --- | --- |
| age 12 or older | dependent child or minor beneficiary directly utilizing the most efficient method of communication based on the availability of the child.    ***Notes***:   * If the FE is unable to contact the individual, request the information from the    + individual’s custodian and/or guardian   + beneficiary, or   + proposed fiduciary. * If the information is not available after all attempts, follow the guidance in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1.c](#1c). |
| younger than 12 years of age | custodian and/or guardian of the child dependent or minor beneficiary, using the most efficient method of communication.    ***Important***:  Requesting telephone or face-to-face contact should be avoided and only completed in rare circumstances. |

***Important***:

* If there is a well-being concern for a beneficiary or dependent who is under the age of majority, report the issue as directed in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.2.e](#2e).
* If the child dependent or minor beneficiary is in the custody of a court or under the supervision of a social service agency through court orders, a report from a social service agency or court will suffice for contact when the

  + court or agency specifically requests that the minor not be interviewed, and
  + purpose of the field examination can otherwise be fulfilled.

***References***:  For more information on

  + minor beneficiary, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1.b](#1b), and

**I.2.C.1.e . Contact With the Fiduciary**

During the field examination process the FE must collect or verify the following information:

* full name
* date of birth
* SSN
* relationship to the beneficiary
* telephone number
* mailing address
* physical address, and
* e-mail address, if available.

This information may have been collected and verified at the time of scheduling; however, if the FE identifies changes in this information during the field examination, the FE must update the record with the correct information.

For an IA and SIA, other than the situations noted in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1.g](#1g) or [FPM, Part I, 2.C.6.a](#6a), the FE ***must*** conduct a face-to-face interview with the fiduciary to complete the appointment process.

***References***:  For more information on scheduling field examinations for

* prospective fiduciaries, see [FPM, Part I, 2.B.2.g](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153948/), and

**I.2.C.1.g . When a Face-to-Face Interview With the Fiduciary Will Not Be Conducted**

During IA and SIA field examinations, when the proposed fiduciary is available to meet at the same time and location of the beneficiary interview, the FE should schedule the interviews together.  When the interviews cannot be scheduled together, a face-to-face interview with the proposed fiduciary ***will not*** be conducted when the proposed fiduciary is

* an entity, such as the trust department of a bank that provides fiduciary services per [38 CFR 13.100(f)(4)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9ff96e3f2643e55c565956dbe139896d&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1100&rgn=div8)
* residing outside of the conducting FE’s territory and it is necessary to transfer the field examination work item to another FE creating unnecessary delays in processing, or
* performing satisfactorily as VA fiduciary for at least one other VA beneficiary and is determined to be suitable in accordance with all applicable elements of [FPM, Part I, 2.C.4.d](#4d), including up-to-date submissions of accountings.

***Exception***:  A face-to-face interview with the proposed fiduciaries noted above may be conducted when the proposed fiduciary has telephone accessibility issues (for example, deaf or telephone connection issues) and the accessibility issues are documented in the field examination.

When the face-to-face interview with the fiduciary is not conducted, the FE must

* obtain and review the criminal background inquiry (CBI) through CLEAR, unless the proposed fiduciary is an entity, prior to contacting the proposed fiduciary
* contact the proposed fiduciary via telephone or secure videoconferencing through approved methods to ensure the fiduciary understands the responsibilities of a VA fiduciary
* utilize the CBI to conduct identification protocol verifying the proposed fiduciary’s

  + first, middle, and last name
  + last four digits of their SSN, and
  + date of birth
* obtain and upload a signed [*VA Form 21P-4703*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf), through a secure method such as

  + an encrypted e-mail
  + an approved VA claims submission service website, or
  + by mail in accordance with [FPM, Part II, 1.A.1](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153964/)
* determine the suitability and type of fiduciary as outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.4.d](#4d)
* investigate the proposed fiduciary as outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5](#5), and
* document in the field examination the case-specific reason why a face-to-face interview with the proposed fiduciary in this scenario has been determined to not be practicable and reference this manual citation.

***Important***:

* For adult beneficiaries, the order of preference outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.4.b](#4b) must be followed.
* To determine if a face-to-face interview is impracticable, the FE must consider the specific facts and circumstances of the interview.  This includes evaluating the efforts, cost, safety, time, and resources required to conduct the interview.

***Notes***:

* Do not conduct the interview with the proposed fiduciary if the CBI indicates a bar to service as outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.b](#5b).
* A [*VA Form 21P-4703*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf) is not required prior to conducting the CBI.

***References***:  For more information on

* listing multiple beneficiaries on *[VA Form 21P-4703](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf)*, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.4.e](#4e)
* notification of fiduciary training requirements, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1.f](#1f)
* review of the CBI, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.k](#5k), and
* inquiry, investigations, and qualification of fiduciaries, see [38 U.S.C. 5507](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5507).

**I.2.C.1.h . When the Minor Beneficiary’s Caregiver Is Not the Fiduciary**

If the minor beneficiary’s caregiver is not the fiduciary, effective communication must be established between the minor’s caregiver and their fiduciary so that they know whom to

* contact with questions or problems, and
* notify about changes to the

  + personal relationship
  + fiduciary relationship, and/or
  + benefit entitlement.

---

**I.2.C.1.i . Other Contacts**

The FE must document the name and contact information (if available) of people considered to be the next of kin, caregiver, and/or friend to the beneficiary in the beneficiary profile.  This information may be used in

* managing the case if there is need for someone to assist with obtaining medical care for the beneficiary
* making forfeiture determinations upon death of the beneficiary, and
* contacting the beneficiary or fiduciary.

***Note***:  ***Next of kin*** refers to a relationship resulting from a biological relationship, marriage, or from adoption proceedings.  An ex-spouse, stepparent, sister-in-law, etc., are not next of kin, but can be listed as a friend and/or caregiver.

***Important***:  When scheduling additional interviews in the future, update the suspense date to reflect the due date of the next interview.

***Reference***:  For more information on updating the beneficiary profile, see the *Veterans Benefits Management System (VBMS) Core User Guide*.

---

**I.2.C.1.j . Report That a Beneficiary is a Danger to Self or Others**

FEs may receive a report that a beneficiary is a danger to self or others.  The role of the FE is to document written or verbal receipt of the report and direct the individual providing the report to contact local law enforcement.

When an FE receives a report that a beneficiary is a danger to self or others, the FE must:

* direct the individual providing the report to contact local law enforcement and make a referral to Adult Protective Services, and
* document the conversation on [*VA Form 27-0820*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-27-0820-ARE.pdf) and upload to the electronic claims folder (eFolder).

The FE may also provide the following resources:

* Veterans Crisis Line telephone number

  + 1-800-273-8225, Press 1, or
  + 988, Press 1, or
* [National Adult Protective Services Association](https://www.napsa-now.org/help-in-your-area/).

***Note***:  The FE must follow Privacy Act/Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) procedures for reportable incidents listed in that guidance.

---

**I.2.C.1.k . FOIA and Privacy Act Requirements**

The FE must attempt to discuss the FOIA and Privacy Act information outlined in [FPM, Part I, 1.B.3.l-p](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153946/) with the beneficiary.  If the beneficiary is not capable of hearing and/or understanding the conversation, the FE may discuss the FOIA and Privacy Act rights with the fiduciary.

**I.2.C.2.a . General Information on Beneficiary Well-Being**

In accordance with [38 CFR 13.120(b)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9d20c899337f0dbd33d8f08300fb37e1&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1120&rgn=div8), the FE must, to the extent possible, evaluate the beneficiary’s

* physical and mental well-being, and
* environmental and social conditions.

For VA purposes, assessing the beneficiary’s well-being means considering the beneficiary’s physical, mental, environmental and social challenges/needs and the resources the beneficiary has available to meet those needs, even if the beneficiary chooses not to employ available resources.

Consistent with [38 CFR 13.120(b)(4) and (5)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9d20c899337f0dbd33d8f08300fb37e1&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1120&rgn=div8), while assessing the beneficiary’s well-being, the FE must consider

* how VA benefits could help meet the needs of the beneficiary, and
* if the beneficiary’s physical, mental, environmental and social challenges/needs must be addressed by other State or Federal agencies.

***References***:  For more information on

* developing facts and taking necessary action, see [FPM, Part I, 1.B.5.e](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153946/), and
* general responsibilities of the FE, see [FPM, Part I, 1.B.5.b](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153946/).

---

**I.2.C.2.b . Evaluating Physical and Mental Well-Being**

The FE must evaluate the beneficiary’s physical and mental well-being during the field examination.

For adult beneficiaries, the FE must ask probing questions about the beneficiary’s

* access to medical care
* physical limitations, including what conditions or circumstances impact the ability to manage their funds, and
* apparent orientation to time, place, and events.

The FE should look for issues or conditions that indicate the beneficiary requires assistance with issues affecting current or additional VA benefits, claims, and non-VA matters that may affect or conflict with VA benefits.

For minor beneficiaries, the FE must evaluate the following physical and medical information by speaking with the proposed fiduciary and the appropriate individual as described in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1.d](#1d).  The FE must ask probing questions about the beneficiary’s

* access to

  + medical care and medications, if needed
  + food, clothing, and shelter, and
  + education, and
* any physical limitations.

Additional probing questions may be required if there is an indication of an issue reported by the beneficiary.

***Reference***:  For more information on additional VA benefits, see

* [FPM, Part I, 4.B](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153962/), and
* [Veterans Benefits Administration (VBA) Fact Sheets](https://benefits.va.gov/BENEFITS/factsheets.asp).

---

**I.2.C.2.c . Evaluating and Documenting Environmental and Social Conditions**

The FE must evaluate the beneficiary’s social and physical environment during the field examination.

For adult beneficiaries, the FE must ask probing questions about the

* type of place where the beneficiary resides
* living arrangement
* way the beneficiary spends their time
* beneficiary’s social relationships, and
* non-dependent(s) living in the beneficiary’s home including

  + the relationship with the beneficiary, and
  + any monthly contribution toward expenses, or non-monetary services provided, if non-dependent.

For minor beneficiaries, the FE must evaluate the following by speaking with both the proposed fiduciary and the appropriate individual as described in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1.d](#1d):

* the type of place where the minor beneficiary resides
* with whom the minor beneficiary lives
* if the residence is adequate
* current school grade
* if the minor beneficiary’s needs are met, and
* any adverse conditions.

The FE must document the following information regarding the beneficiary’s environmental and social conditions within the field examination:

* beneficiary’s residence type

**I.2.C.2.e . Documenting Beneficiary Well-Being**

Examples of potential findings include report of

* abuse such as physical, sexual, or emotional, and
* neglect such as

  + withholding access of medical care or equipment, or
  + failure to provide

    - necessary food, clothing, and/or shelter, or
    - education (for example, the child is not enrolled in school).

* call the local law enforcement agency or 911, if the situation is a life-threatening emergency

**I.2.C.3.a . Financial Responsibilities of the Fiduciary**

**I.2.C.3.e . Documenting and Verifying Liquid Assets**

The FE must review and document each liquid asset identified during all IA and SIA field examinations.  A liquid asset is an item that may easily be converted into cash.

Visual verification of liquid assets is necessary.  Acceptable methods of asset verification include viewing one of the following items:

* an original, photocopied, or computer-generated financial institution document which does not exhibit any signs of alteration, not older than 90 days from the date of the field examination
* an online statement viewed from the financial institution's website, or
* a [*VA Form 21P-4718a, Certificate of Balance on Deposit and Authorization to Disclose Financial Records*](https://www.vba.va.gov/pubs/forms/VBA-21P-4718a-ARE.pdf), obtained from the financial institutions.

During SIA field examinations, the FE must verify liquid assets listed in the previous field examination by reviewing and documenting the amount, sale, or distribution of assets identified in the previous field examination.  If the beneficiary no longer holds an asset (for example, sold property or closed an account) or has exhausted an asset that was documented in the previous field examination, the FE must explain the condition of the assets.

***Important***:  If the FE cannot visually verify liquid assets during the field examination

* complete the interview
* inform the fiduciary that they must submit the required verification to the fiduciary hub (hub) using a secure communication method (for example, fax, mail)
* establish the appropriate development activity
* allow 14 days for receipt of the information, and
* keep the field examination open until the 14-day suspense expires.

If the proposed fiduciary does not submit the asset verification within the requested timeframe, the FE must proceed with appointing the next most qualified fiduciary, in accordance with [FPM, Part I, 2.C.4.b](#4b).

***Notes***:

* If the fiduciary has an authorized commingled financial account per [FPM, Part I, 3.C.2.g](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153957/), the FE must verify that the financial information provided clearly identifies the income and expenditures of the specific beneficiary.
* The FE is not required to *verify* non-VA assets during an EIA field examination.

| Financial accounts such as   * checking * savings * money market accounts * cash on hand * short-term investments approved by VA, and * other items that may be easily converted to cash.   ***Note***:  This includes savings and/or checking accounts where VA benefits are deposited. | * Account type * account description * verification date * verification method * by whom the account is managed * name of financial institution * date of financial institution statement * statement ending balance * if the account is properly titled * whether the account houses VA derived funds, and * corrective action taken, if applicable.   ***Important***:  When adding an asset, enter the information in the appropriate fields. **I.2.C.3.f . Documenting Recurring Income**

As indicated in [FPM, Part I, 2.B.1.a](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153948/), prior to the field examination, the FE must review beneficiary information in authorized VA systems.  During this review, the FE must identify if the beneficiary and their dependents are recipients of the following benefit types and the amount of the payment:

* Social Security
* military retired pay, and/or
* VA benefits (for example, education, compensation, pension, or vocational rehabilitation).

If the amount of income identified in authorized VA systems from the three benefits listed above differs from the amount identified during the field examination, the FE must

* determine the actual amount received by viewing

  + an original, photocopied, or computer-generated financial institution document, which does not show any signs of alteration, or
  + the check or check stub, and
* document in the field examination the

  + actual amount received
  + method used to determine the actual amount received, and
  + reason for the difference between the amount shown in authorized VA systems and in the field examination.

The FE must also review the award and rating information in VA systems prior to the field examination to determine all income that was previously reported to VA as part of a claim for benefits.  This will assist the FE in ensuring that the fiduciary is aware of all recurring income and identifying any potential changes in benefit entitlement in accordance with [FPM, Part I, 2.E.3.d](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153953/) and [FPM, Part I, 2.C.3.j](#3j).

During the field examination, the FE must request *all* beneficiary and dependent recurring income sources and amounts.

The FE must review and document the following in the field examination:

* all recurring income sources and amounts for the beneficiary and their dependents
* the frequency of income (the FE must calculate and document as a monthly amount of recurring income amount), and
* to whom that income is paid (beneficiary, dependent or the name and relationship of any other party, if known).

For minor beneficiaries, the field examination must document the total income and FUM of each minor.  The FE must also document the need for an annual accounting or protection of the FUM, if required.

***Important***:  The INCOME section is intended for recording recurring income, except for VA insurance payments.  Within the INCOME section, utilize the VA OTHER INCOME type and enter the following within the BENEFICIARY’S INCOME field:  *One-time VA Life Insurance Payment*.

**I.2.C.3.h . Documenting Expenses**

Documenting expense information during the IA or SIA assists with oversight of the use of beneficiary funds during follow-up field examinations, accounting and fund usage reviews, and other VA fiduciary oversight activities.  Therefore, FEs must make efforts to clearly document individual expenses and avoid listing expenses as personal allowance when individual expense types are known.

During each field examination, the FE must request the beneficiary’s recurring expenses and the amounts of those expenses.  Recurring expenses are those that the beneficiary can expect to pay on a regular basis (for example, monthly, quarterly, or annually).

Expenses that must be documented include, but are not limited to monthly payments on services, and debts or loans.  The field examination must include the

* expense type (for example, mortgage, including a mortgage backed by VA Home Loan, groceries, vehicle, electric, water, insurance, etc., using the company name, when available), and
* monthly expense amounts.

If the frequency of the expense payment is other than monthly (for example, quarterly, or annually), the FE must calculate and document the monthly amount in the field examination.

If the beneficiary or fiduciary reports expenses but does not report ownership of the asset associated with expenses reported, the FE must explain the situation in the COMMENT AND OBSERVATIONS section of the associated expense within the field examination.

***Example***:  The fiduciary reports expenses for car maintenance and car fuel.  The FE must determine and document if the beneficiary owns or leases a car associated with the car and fuel expenses.

* If the beneficiary owns, or makes payments towards ownership or the lease of the vehicle, the FE must document the vehicle as an asset in the ASSETS section of the field examination.
* If the beneficiary does not own the vehicle, the FE must explain why the beneficiary is paying expenses for the car that the beneficiary does not own or is not shown to make regular payments for their use/benefit (for example, the beneficiary’s child or caregiver owns the vehicle but only drives it to meet beneficiary’s needs).

All expenses documented in the field examination must be included in the fiduciary and beneficiary notification letters.

***Note***:  If there are changes in expenses, such as unreimbursed medical expenses, that may change the beneficiary’s entitlement to VA benefits, follow the guidance in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.3.j](#3j).

**I.2.C.3.i . Expense Requirements for Minor Beneficiaries**

In accordance with [38 CFR 13.240](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-13/section-13.240), the FE must inform the fiduciary that VA benefits should only be relied upon to the extent that the person or persons who have custody of the minor beneficiary, and are responsible for their needs, are unable to provide those needs.

***Exception***:  A fiduciary who receives VA education benefits on behalf of a beneficiary who is less than the age of majority may use the benefits for the beneficiary’s education, regardless of the ability of the custodian(s) to pay for the beneficiary’s education.

***Note***:  There is no requirement for the FE to document this discussion within the field examination.

Use the table below to determine the expense information that must be documented in the field examination for a minor beneficiary.

| **If the person or persons who have custody of the minor beneficiary and who are responsible for their needs are ...** | **Then the FE must document the ...** |
| --- | --- |
| able to provide for the beneficiary’s needs | * proposed fiduciary’s plans for the VA funds * beneficiary’s expenses and amounts, if applicable, and * justification for any documented recurring expenses, as the custodian(s) can provide for the beneficiary’s needs. |
| unable to provide for the beneficiary’s needs | * proposed fiduciary’s plans for the VA funds * source(s) of income of the minor beneficiary’s custodian(s) * list of individuals in the household dependent on the custodian(s) for support, and * beneficiary’s expenses and amounts. |

***Important***:

* If the fiduciary indicates that funds will be saved, no expenses should be documented.
* When documenting expenses for a minor beneficiary, matters for consideration include, but are not limited to, the minor’s total income, reasonable use of the minor’s funds, and the fiduciary’s responsibility for the minor.
* All documentation requirements must be reflected in the field examination within VBMS.  FEs may utilize the COMMENTS AND OBSERVATIONS field within the field examination.

**I.2.C.3.k . Advising the Fiduciary of VA Debts**

Use the table below to determine the VA FUM and documentation requirements based on field examination type.

| **If the IIf the field examination type is an ...** | **Then the VA FUM is …** |
| --- | --- |
| IA/EIA | the retroactive payment due to the beneficiary, if any, upon release to the fiduciary.  If a retroactive payment is pending, the retroactive payment is not documented as VA FUM at the time of the field examination conducted by the FE.  The Fiduciary Service Representative will update VA FUM within the beneficiary profile to reflect the amount of the retroactive payment released to the fiduciary immediately upon authorization of the award as outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.E.3.g](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153953/).    ***Important***:  When VA appoints a beneficiary under supervised direct pay (SDP), retroactive funds are withheld until the beneficiary is rated competent or a fiduciary is appointed. | SIA | the amount of funds to be transferred to the appointed successor fiduciary or beneficiary with an SDP appointment. The field examination may not be submitted for closure without documenting the status of the funds transfer.    ***Important***:   * Funds managed directly by a beneficiary under an SDP appointment are not considered VA FUM. Any determination of VA FUM following an IA/EIA shall include any VA benefits deposited within a properly titled account managed by an existing or previous fiduciary.

**I.2.C.4.a . General Policy on Appointment and Fiduciary Types**

Under [38 U.S.C. 5502(a)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5502), VA is authorized to make payments

* directly to the beneficiary under SDP in adult beneficiary cases, or
* to a fiduciary on behalf of the beneficiary.

When VA determines that payments will be made to a

* beneficiary who is under SDP, as described in [FPM, Part I, 5.A.2.a](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000223123/), the payments will be under the fiduciary type of SDP, or
* fiduciary on behalf of the beneficiary, the payments will be made under one of the following types:
  + VA-appointed fiduciary
  + spouse fiduciary, or
  + temporary.

A ***VA-appointed fiduciary*** refers to a person or legal entity appointed by VA to serve as fiduciary for VA benefits for a beneficiary who is unable to manage their VA funds.

A ***spouse fiduciary*** refers to a husband or wife whose marriage meets the requirements of [38 U.S.C. 103(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/103), including common law marriage and same-sex marriage, who is appointed by the VA.  Hubs may consider the spouse as valid for appointment as a spouse fiduciary when the spouse has been verified on the award as a dependent spouse within the last eight years and since that time was not removed due to death, divorce, or the beneficiary’s request.

***Important***:  Determining a spouse for VA purposes is an adjudication decision.  Hubs will not make this determination except as outlined under [FPM, Part I, 2.E.3.k](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153953/).

A ***temporary fiduciary*** refers to an individual or entity that is appointed by VA to serve as a fiduciary for a beneficiary for not more than 120 days who also meets the responsibilities and criteria described in [FPM, Part I, 5.B.1](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000223124/).

***Notes***:

* Court-appointed fiduciaries whom VA appointed before August 13, 2018, the effective date of updated fiduciary regulations under [38 CFR 13](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=51e838d21f7b4c5935d578188e552e72&mc=true&node=pt38.1.13&rgn=div5), may maintain their appointment type until the first follow-up field examination on or after August 13, 2018.

  + The first follow-up field examination must be converted to an SIA to change the fiduciary type and to instruct the court-appointed fiduciary regarding the requirements of a VA-appointed fiduciary.
  + For follow-up field examinations, the received date of the SIA is

    - August 13, 2018, if received on this date, or
    - the received date, if received after August 13, 2018.
* Legacy fiduciary types, such as legal custodian, must not be used.  Hubs must update the fiduciary type to the appropriate category as described in this block.  Changes in fiduciary type that impose different requirements regarding the qualification and/or oversight of the fiduciary must be conducted as an SIA and include all elements of an SIA field examination.

**I.2.C.4.b . Beneficiary's Fiduciary Preference**

When selecting a fiduciary, the FE must consider individuals and entities for appointment in the following order of preference, provided that the proposed fiduciary is qualified, willing to serve, and the appointment would serve the beneficiary's interest:

* the beneficiary's preference
* the beneficiary's spouse
* a relative who has care or custody of the beneficiary or their funds
* any other relative of the beneficiary
* any friend, acquaintance, or other person who is willing to serve as fiduciary for the beneficiary without a fee
* the chief officer of a public or private institution in which the beneficiary receives care or which has custody of the beneficiary
* the bonded officer of an Indian reservation, if applicable
* an individual or entity who

  + has been appointed by a court with jurisdiction to handle the beneficiary’s affairs, or
  + is not willing to serve without a fee, or
* a temporary fiduciary, if necessary.

If the beneficiary has a legal guardian appointed to handle the beneficiary’s affairs, the FE will presume that the beneficiary does not have the capacity to state a preference and will consider individuals and entities in the order of preference.

For minor beneficiaries 12 years or older at the time of an EIA, the FE must ask the minor whom they would like to serve as their fiduciary to manage their funds.  A minor beneficiary’s request to have a fiduciary other than the parent must be documented and considered per [38 CFR 13.100(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=12463a63279488836b91afe9aa2f8ba9&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1100&rgn=div8).

***References***:  For more information on

* the order of preference in appointing a fiduciary, see [38 CFR 13.100(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=12463a63279488836b91afe9aa2f8ba9&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1100&rgn=div8), and
* handling situations where contact with a minor beneficiary over the age of 12 is not possible in an EIA, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.6.f](#6f).

**I.2.C.4.c . Documenting the Beneficiary’s Fiduciary Preference**

During the field examination, the FE must document the following information regarding the beneficiary’s fiduciary preference if the beneficiary had the capacity to state a preference:

* full name of the preferred fiduciary, and
* whether or not the fiduciary was appointed.

If the preferred fiduciary was not appointed, the FE must also document the reason for non-selection.  Appropriate justifications for non-selection include

* a bar to service was identified after investigation
* the appointment was not in the best interest of the beneficiary after investigation or during the interview process, or
* the preferred fiduciary

  + was unwilling or unable to provide VA with the information to protect the beneficiary’s VA funds
  + did not agree to serve, or
  + is currently not performing satisfactorily.

If a fiduciary preference is not indicated, the FE must document the reason why the beneficiary did not provide a preference.  Reasons may include

* the beneficiary was

  + unable to respond to VA’s request for information
  + unwilling to provide a preference, or
  + a minor under the age of 12, or
* other (will need to specify).

***Reference***:  For information on documenting the rationale for recommending the approval of a fiduciary fee, see [FPM, Part II, 1.C.4.d](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153966/).

**I.2.C.4.d . Fiduciary Suitability and Type**

For all IA and SIA field examinations, the FE must

* assess the suitability of the proposed fiduciary, and
* determine whether the payment type is in the beneficiary’s best interest.

***Best interest*** refers to the decision to appoint a fiduciary based on an investigation and analysis that the fiduciary is the most suitable person to serve the beneficiary.  The fiduciary is expected to ensure the beneficiary has all their needs and wants met within their means.

***Important***:  For a minor beneficiary, each field examination must indicate whether the proposed fiduciary appears suitable to administer the minor’s current and future financial affairs.

If the proposed fiduciary already serves as a fiduciary to other VA beneficiaries of the Fiduciary Program, the FE must

* ensure the fiduciary is compliant with all responsibilities of a fiduciary with respect to all beneficiaries the fiduciary currently serves
* verify accountings for current beneficiaries are not past due, and
* determine that an appointment of an additional beneficiary will not degrade service for any other beneficiary under the fiduciary’s appointment.

If all three requirements are not met, the FE will not assign an additional beneficiary to the fiduciary.

VA policy is to use the least restrictive payment method to meet the beneficiary’s needs and afford adequate protection of VA funds.  VA must select the most suitable fiduciary with consideration to the beneficiary’s preference, regardless of any prior designation of a fiduciary.  This includes any court designation of a guardian appointed under the authority of the State’s Uniform Veterans Guardianship Act.

If the beneficiary’s FUM are expected to exceed $10,000, the FE must consider a fiduciary that is prepared to manage all responsibilities associated with service, to include the ability to

* make financially sound decisions, to include timely bill payment and minimal overdrafts
* complete annual accountings
* protect the beneficiary’s funds, and
* obtain a bond, if necessary.

The proposed fiduciary should be

* willing to complete VA forms as necessary
* agreeable to a CBI review and credit history check (if applicable)
* able to secure a bond when applicable
* agreeable to complete accountings when required
* willing to administer funds in accordance with VA regulations and policies and State and/or Federal laws, and
* capable of understanding their responsibility as a fiduciary.

***Notes***:

* Spouse fiduciaries will generally be exempt from accounting and surety bond requirements.  Should the FE determine that VA FUM require oversight not required of a spouse fiduciary, a different fiduciary must be appointed.
* ***Individual serving as a fiduciary*** refers to any one person, who is serving under their own SSN, who is a spouse or VA-appointed fiduciary.
* ***Corporation serving as a fiduciary*** refers to any entity or person, which serves using a tax identification number as a VA-appointed fiduciary.  This may include, but is not limited to a law firm, bank, or professional fiduciary service.
* Special care must be taken to ensure that individuals affiliated with a corporation serving as fiduciary are not appointed as a fiduciary in their own right.  Ensure the fiduciary record indicates the organization when establishing the fiduciary record.  ***Example***:  VA would not appoint John Jones, an attorney, as an individual using an SSN and also appoint a corporation serving as fiduciary as the principle of John Jones Law Firm using a tax identification number.
* The ***Uniform Veterans Guardianship Act*** refers to a court-appointed guardian, conservator, trustee, committee, or other term used to denote a court-appointed responsibility over one’s VA benefits.  These guardianships were established based on documentation provided to the court which indicated a person in receipt of and incapable of managing their VA benefits.  These guardianships do not revoke any rights of the beneficiary and do not afford the guardian the right to enter into contracts or exercise any authority over the beneficiary’s person.  VA will not consider this type of guardian as one who will speak for the beneficiary in all matters.

***Reference***:  For more information on fiduciary appointments, see [38 CFR 13.100](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=12463a63279488836b91afe9aa2f8ba9&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1100&rgn=div8).

**I.2.C.4.e . Selecting a VA-Appointed Fiduciary or Spouse Fiduciary**

The decision to appoint a VA-appointed fiduciary or spouse fiduciary must be based on

* the beneficiary being

  + incapable of handling their VA funds without supervision, or
  + under the age of majority
* an interview with the proposed fiduciary
* a credit report dated within 30 days prior to the appointment, if applicable
* a CBI within one year prior to the appointment, if applicable
* the fiduciary’s ability to complete accountings and obtain protection of the FUM, if required
* the least restrictive payment method to meet the beneficiary’s needs and afford adequate protection of VA funds
* a determination of the fiduciary’s suitability, and
* the understanding that any remaining assets in the case of a minor beneficiary are to be turned over to the minor when they reach the age of majority, if applicable.

The fiduciary must

* be a suitable candidate for this position of trust
* consent to act as fiduciary, and
* agree to the fiduciary responsibilities outlined on the *[VA Form 21P-4703](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf)*.

When appointing a fiduciary, consideration should be given to the

* preference of the beneficiary, for adult and minor beneficiaries age 12 or older
* amount of VA benefits payable
* amount of accumulated FUM, if any
* other resources and income of the beneficiary, and
* the beneficiary’s special needs and environmental surroundings.

***Notes***:

* The signature on the *[VA Form 21P-4703](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf)* may be an electronic or digital signature if it meets the requirements specified in [M21-1, Part II, Subpart i, 2.B.2.g](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000174859/).
* Multiple beneficiaries may be listed on the *[VA Form 21P-4703](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf)* if they share an eFolder (for example, multiple related minor beneficiaries with the same fiduciary).  If they do not share an eFolder, a separate *[VA Form 21P-4703](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf)* is required for each beneficiary.
* The FE will waive the face-to-face interview and credit history review when a fiduciary is appointed under the EIA process in accordance with [FPM, Part I, 2.C.6](#6).

**I.2.C.4.f . Bypassing an Existing Court Fiduciary in Favor of a VA-Appointed Fiduciary**

When considering appointing a fiduciary other than the existing court appointed fiduciary or court guardian, the FE must consider the following factors:

* the overall situation of the beneficiary
* the amount of

  + VA FUM, and
  + total assets
* whether the fiduciary is appointed as a general or plenary guardian, or by authority of the State’s Uniform Veterans Guardianship Act, and
* the views of the State court regarding the guardian’s ability to serve.

If VA decides to appoint an individual or entity that is not the existing court appointed fiduciary or court guardian, VA must

* upload all correspondence associated with the court appointment to the eFolder and document if the court appointment remains in effect for other non-VA assets during every subsequent field examination, and
* notify the court-appointed fiduciary or guardian

  + of the benefits payable to be managed by the VA-appointed fiduciary, and
  + that they will be provided a copy of all future notices related to the beneficiary’s case.

In minor beneficiary cases where the FE determines that the VA-appointed fiduciary who is also a court-appointed fiduciary or court guardian is not serving satisfactorily, the FE must

* provide a detailed explanation of the reason(s) for recommendation of a successor fiduciary
* document the qualifications of the recommended successor VA-appointed fiduciary, and
* complete the field examination and recommend the successor fiduciary identified.

***Important***:  If there are any difficulties while engaging with a court-appointed fiduciary (for example, obtaining accounting documentation, transfer of funds to the successor fiduciary), contact the District Counsel for further guidance by

* accessing the [Offices of Chief Counsel in the Districts website](https://www.va.gov/OGC/DistrictOffices.asp)
* determining, by State, which of the five districts is responsible, and
* emailing the appropriate district as follows

  + Continental District at [OGCContinentalWestAL@va.gov](mailto:OGCContinentalWestAL@va.gov)
  + North Atlantic District at [VAOGCNorthAtlanticCaseNotification@va.gov](mailto:VAOGCNorthAtlanticCaseNotification@va.gov)
  + Pacific District the Chief Counsel of the district
  + Southeast District the Chief Counsel of the district, or
  + Midwest District the Chief Counsel of the district.

**I.2.C.4.g . Determining a Fiduciary for a Beneficiary With a Previously Established Court Appointment**

VA appoints the least restrictive, most effective fiduciary, regardless of whether a State court has appointed a fiduciary for the beneficiary.  Under certain circumstances, appointing the fiduciary appointed by the court to serve as the VA-appointed fiduciary may be in the best interest of the beneficiary since this allows the same fiduciary to manage all funds.

In cases where a fiduciary is appointed by a State court and appointed by VA as a VA-appointed fiduciary, the fiduciary must follow VA Fiduciary Program guidance as outlined in [FPM, Part I, 3.B.2](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153955/) regarding certified court accountings.

For all fiduciaries appointed by a State court, the FE must obtain a certified or original court file-stamped copy of the appointment letter for inclusion in the eFolder and facilitate the inclusion of this information into the VBMS record.  Additionally, photocopies of any existing bond, the conservator’s inventory, and any orders pertaining to expenditure of funds must be obtained.

***Note***:  When the VA fiduciary is also court-appointed and required to account to the court but not to VA, the FE must follow the guidance in [FPM, Part I, 3.B.2.b](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153955/) to request and control for the certified copy of the court accounting during the IA process.

**I.2.C.5.a . Duty to Investigate Proposed Fiduciaries**

[38 U.S.C. 5507](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5507) makes clear the VA’s duty to thoroughly investigate proposed fiduciaries prior to appointment.  VA’s failure to properly investigate a proposed fiduciary may result in a finding of negligence.

In accordance with [38 CFR 13.100(f)(4)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=f370b6ba7bc808cb25e0a320f80375a5&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1100&rgn=div8), VA will not conduct the investigation prescribed in this section if the proposed fiduciary is an entity, such as the trust department of a bank that provides fiduciary services.  This includes the face-to-face interview, credit report, CBI, and proof of identity.

***References***:  For more information on the

* investigation of a proposed fiduciary, see [38 CFR 13.100(f)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=f370b6ba7bc808cb25e0a320f80375a5&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1100&rgn=div8), and
* EIA protocol, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.6.a](#6a).

**I.2.C.5.b . Bars to Serving as a Fiduciary**

An individual or entity may not serve as a fiduciary for a VA beneficiary if the individual or entity

* misused or misappropriated a beneficiary's VA benefits while serving as a fiduciary
* refuses or neglects to provide the authorization for VA disclosure of information
* is unable to manage their own Federal or State benefits and is in a Federal or State agency's fiduciary, representative payment, or similar program
* has been adjudicated by a court with jurisdiction as being unable to manage their own financial affairs
* is incarcerated in a Federal, State, local, or other penal institution or correctional facility, sentenced to home confinement, released from incarceration to a half-way house, or on house arrest or in custody in any facility awaiting trial on pending criminal charges
* has felony charges pending
* has been removed as legal guardian by a state court for misconduct
* is under the age of majority
* knowingly violates or refuses to comply with VA policies, or
* fails to satisfy the conditions of appointment based on CBI results as described in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.k](#5k).

***Important***:  If the fiduciary is barred from service, hub employees must select the DO NOT APPOINT flag on the fiduciary profile.  When a hub employee determines that a bar to service no longer exists, the DO NOT APPOINT flag must be removed by following the guidance in [FPM, Part II, 1.D.4.d](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153967/).

***References***:  For more information on

* felony convictions, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.k](#5k)
* bars to serving as a fiduciary, see [38 CFR 13.130](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=8a473d5cf11b75a29586518f05e5a4a4&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1130&rgn=div8), and
* managing the DO NOT APPOINT flag on the fiduciary profile, see FPM, Part II, 1.D.4.d.

**I.2.C.5.c . Verifying Proof of the Proposed Fiduciary’s Identity**

The FE must verify the proposed fiduciary's identity.

During face-to-face interviews, proper forms of identification may include the following:

* State-issued driver’s license or identification card
* DoD identification card
* passport or passport card
* Social Security card, or
* birth certificate.

During telephone interviews, FEs will utilize the CBI to conduct the identification protocol verifying the proposed fiduciary’s

* first, middle, and last name
* last four digits of their SSN, and
* date of birth.

If the proposed fiduciary’s identity cannot be verified, they must not be appointed.

***Note***:  Foreign field examinations (to include the Philippines) will use a universal identification card if the documents listed above are not available.

***Reference***:  For more information on verifying the proposed fiduciary’s identity during an EIA, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.6.d](#6d).

**I.2.C.5.d . Obtaining a Credit Report During an IA**

[38 U.S.C. 5507](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5507) provides authority for VA to obtain credit reports from prospective fiduciaries.

The FE’s decision to recognize a fiduciary must be based, in part, on a review of a credit report issued within 30 days prior to the proposed appointment.

***Exception***:  Credit reports are *not* required for

* an entity, such as a trust department in a bank that provides trust services
* a beneficiary appointed as SDP, or
* a fiduciary appointed under the EIA process outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.6](#6).

Any person considered as a prospective fiduciary for any VA beneficiary must sign a [*VA Form 21P-4703*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf), acknowledging that VA may investigate their credit.  Upon receipt of the proposed fiduciary’s consent, hub personnel will secure a credit report.

The credit report must be uploaded to the eFolder regardless of whether the subject of the report is appointed as fiduciary.

***Note***:  The signature on the [*VA Form 21P-4703*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf) may be an electronic or digital signature if it meets the requirements specified in M21-1, Part II, Subpart i, 2.B.2.g.

***Reference***:  For more information on listing multiple beneficiaries on *[VA Form 21P-4703](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf)*, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.4.e](#4e).

**I.2.C.5.e . Review of Credit Reports During an IA**

For IAs, the FE must evaluate all information from the proposed fiduciary’s credit report to include the credit history and the amount of debt as noted in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.f](#5f).

A proposed fiduciary with an unsatisfactory credit report will not qualify as a VA fiduciary without the FE documentation of mitigating circumstances and decision to appoint.  Mitigating circumstances are justifications provided by the proposed fiduciary for negative findings on at least one of the areas of review.  The FE should evaluate the information, weigh it against the credit report, and determine if the justification(s) are sufficient to demonstrate suitability for appointment.  If the FE cannot document mitigating circumstances, the proposed fiduciary will not be appointed.

---

**I.2.C.5.f . Analyzing the Credit Report**

If the credit report contains negative information, such as late payments, collections, foreclosures, and/or bankruptcies, the FE must consider the facts and circumstances surrounding the issue and how that information relates to the prospective fiduciary’s ability to manage the beneficiary’s funds.

The payment history and amount of debt are areas of the credit report that can display higher risk where the FE must not appoint the proposed fiduciary without mitigating circumstances.

Use the table below to help analyze a credit report.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Area of Review** | **Criteria** |
| payment history | * No more than    + eight instances of late payments within the past year, and/or   + three accounts currently in collections. * No    + bankruptcies in the past seven years   + judgments in the past five years   + charge-offs in the past five years, and/or   + State/Federal tax liens in the past five years.   ***Note***:  Late payments, collections, charge-offs, and bankruptcies are areas of concern. |
| amount of debt | Debt to income (DTI) ratio can be no more than 75 percent of the monthly household income.    ***Note***:  The DTI used in the initial calculation is a modified form of back-end DTI.  Back-end DTI is typically calculated by comparing all monthly obligations to the gross monthly income.  For VA purposes, the FE compares the total of all monthly obligations found on the credit report to the monthly income.  The monthly income is calculated by dividing the approximate annual income listed on [*VA Form 21P-4703*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf) by 12.  Having a high debt amount and low available income is an area of concern, as it increases the likelihood that the potential fiduciary will use VA benefits improperly. |

***Important***:

* Prospective fiduciaries with negative entries on their credit report that are above the maximum negative entry threshold cannot be denied the opportunity to serve based solely on credit history.
* As a part of a comprehensive analysis, the FE must attempt to clarify information relating to situations exceeding the maximum negative entries as shown above.  If necessary, contact the proposed fiduciary by telephone to develop situational details.  If situational details indicate the negative entries are justifiable, the FE will annotate the situational details and the satisfactory credit report review.
* If a proposed fiduciary is disqualified from appointment based even in part on information contained in their credit report, hubs must mail the *Bad Credit Letter* to the disqualified fiduciary.

***Notes***:

* The FE must document the findings in the ADDITIONAL COMMENTS CREDIT REVIEW field within the CREDIT AND CRIMINAL BACKGROUND INQUIRY section of the field examination.  Should more space be necessary, utilize the COMMENTS AND OBSERVATION field at the end of the field examination.  If the proposed fiduciary provides sufficient mitigating circumstances, the FE has the discretion to appoint.
* In certain, rare situations, the preferred fiduciary’s credit report will return no results.  This can occur when the proposed fiduciary resides outside the U.S. or when the proposed fiduciary’s credit is frozen due to identity theft.  If one or both of these situations are the cause of a no credit report result, hubs may still appoint the preferred fiduciary if the fiduciary is able to provide proof of income and their ability to pay their own expenses.  If the proposed fiduciary is unable or unwilling to provide sufficient evidence to verify their income and ability to pay expenses, the hub ***must not*** appoint that fiduciary.

**I.2.C.5.g . Examples: Acceptable Mitigating Circumstances**

***Example 1***:  The proposed fiduciary’s credit report shows six delinquent accounts and four accounts that have gone to collection.  The adverse entries on the credit report require the FE to contact the proposed fiduciary.  The proposed fiduciary provides clarification that the Federal Government is their employer and recently there was a government shutdown that lasted several pay periods, and the fiduciary was unable to work.  Due to lack of income, the proposed fiduciary could not pay bills.

***Example 2***:  The proposed fiduciary’s credit report shows their DTI is 80 percent of the household income requiring the FE to contact the proposed fiduciary since this is over the 75 percent maximum.  The FE learns the proposed fiduciary suffered a major life illness and medical bills have increased the monthly bills dramatically.  However, without taking the medical expenses into consideration, the credit report shows the DTI would have been 50 percent.

**I.2.C.5.h . Examples: Justifications for Non-Appointment of a Proposed Fiduciary**

***Example 1***:  The proposed fiduciary’s credit report shows six delinquent accounts and four that have gone to collection.  The adverse entries on the credit report require the FE to contact the proposed fiduciary.  The proposed fiduciary provides clarification that the Federal Government is their employer and recently there was a government shutdown that lasted several pay periods, and the fiduciary was unable to work.  Due to lack of income, the proposed fiduciary did not pay bills.  However, during the analysis of the credit report, the FE identifies the bills were not paid, and several were in collection status prior to the shutdown.

The FE learns the proposed fiduciary had a major life illness and medical bills have added to the monthly bills.  However, the credit report shows that prior to the illness the DTI was 77 percent.

**I.2.C.5.i . Obtaining a CBI During an IA**

[38 U.S.C. 5507](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5507) requires that VA request information on the criminal background of any person before appointing that person as fiduciary.

FEs obtain the CBI through CLEAR prior to conducting the interview.  Obtaining the CBI at the earliest practical opportunity prevents further investigation of a person that will not be appointed based on the results of the inquiry.  If the available identifying information is insufficient to locate the CBI for the proposed fiduciary, obtain the required information by telephone and document on a [*VA Form 27-0820*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-27-0820-ARE.pdf).

***Exceptions***:  CBIs are *not* required for

* an entity, such as a trust department in a bank that provides trust services, or
* a beneficiary appointed as SDP.

The default and unedited CBI report as established by Pension and Fiduciary (P&F) Service must be uploaded to the eFolder regardless of whether the subject of the report is appointed as fiduciary.

Hubs must run the CBI within one year of the fiduciary appointment date.

***References***:  For more information on

* obtaining and reviewing CBIs when EIA criteria have been met, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.6.c](#6c) and [d](#6d), and
* utilizing the CBI when face-to-face contact will not occur with the fiduciary during the IA, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1.g](#1g).

**I.2.C.5.k . Review of CBI During an IA**

The CLEAR report will return a calculated risk result of either green, yellow, or red.  Use the table below to determine the appropriate actions to take based on the calculated risk result.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **If the CLEAR calculated risk result is ...** | **Then the proposed fiduciary is considered** **...** |
| green | low risk.  No further investigation of the CBI is required for the satisfactory CBI.  The FE must document the green result in the field examination. |
| yellow | moderate risk.  The FE must   * document the negative entries found on the CBI * investigate all entries within the CBI to    + ensure no items represent a bar to being a fiduciary per [38 CFR 13.130](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=2b2f9c659302bdb9695e64790fd0e9af&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1130&rgn=div8), and   + determine whether the totality of the entries should disqualify the fiduciary from serving, and * annotate in the field examination the situational details and the satisfactory CBI if situational details indicate the negative entries on the CBI are justifiable.   ***Note***:  If the situational details cannot be justified, the FE must not appoint that person as fiduciary. |
| red | high risk.  Do not investigate the CBI further and do not appoint the fiduciary unless one of the specific exceptions outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.l](#5l) applies. |

When investigating a yellow calculated risk result, if the individual was convicted of any felony offense (minimum period of imprisonment is one year or more) regardless if any imprisonment occurred, the FE must make further inquiry to assure the conviction is not a bar to service.

If the person was convicted of a felony offense, that person can be certified as fiduciary only if it is determined that the person is appropriate based on the circumstances.  All the following conditions must be met to appoint the person as fiduciary:

* the conviction occurred more than 10 years ago
* the conviction did not involve fraud, theft, bribery, embezzlement, identity theft, money laundering, forgery, abuse/neglect of another person, or any other financial crime
* the individual is the preference of the beneficiary
* no other person or entity is willing or qualified to serve, and
* the Fiduciary Hub Manager (FHM) or designee determines the appointment of the individual poses no risk to the beneficiary and is in the beneficiary’s best interest.

***Important***:  As justification for the appointment, the FE must evaluate and document the information obtained about a prospective fiduciary’s criminal background history based upon the facts and circumstances of the particular case.

***References***:  For more information on

* bars to serving as a fiduciary, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.b](#5b)
* examples of acceptable CBI mitigating circumstances, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.l](#5l), and
* example of justifications for non-appointment of desired fiduciary based on CBI, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.n](#5n).

**I.2.C.5.l . Justification or Mitigation of CBI**

An FE is able to justify a yellow result based on [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.k](#5k).  Some examples of when this would be appropriate are as follows:

***Example 1***:  The calculated risk result for the proposed fiduciary is yellow with a score of 150.  A review of the specific risk indicators shows a single possession of a controlled substance misdemeanor charge from multiple years prior.

***Result 1***:  Since the single charge is not a bar to service per [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.b](#5b) and does not indicate the fiduciary is currently unfit to perform fiduciary responsibilities, the CBI is satisfactory.

***Example 2***:  The calculated risk result for the proposed fiduciary is yellow with a score of 300.  A review of the specific risk indicators shows six separate charges all from a single instance three years prior.  None of the offenses were bars to service per [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.b](#5b), and several were administrative in nature.  After the single instance, no further crimes are noted on the CBI.

***Result 2***:  Since the charges are not a bar to service per [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.b](#5b), and the totality of charges do not indicate the fiduciary is currently unfit to perform fiduciary responsibilities, the CBI is satisfactory.

Separate from justifying a yellow risk score result, hubs are permitted to mitigate a red risk score based on very specific criteria.  The table below provides scenarios outlining the allowable parameters along with the rationale for the mitigation.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Allowable Scenario** | **Rationale for Mitigation** |
| The proposed fiduciary is the beneficiary’s spouse, and the CBI includes a bankruptcy resulting in a red risk score.  The bankruptcy is verified as joint with the beneficiary on the CBI and removal of the bankruptcy would result in a yellow risk score or better. | Since the bankruptcy is verified as joint with the spouse beneficiary (showing the beneficiary as a co-petitioner), and removal of the bankruptcy results in a yellow risk score or better, the CBI is satisfactory if the FE is able to justify the yellow result based on [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.k](#5k). |
| The calculated risk score for the proposed fiduciary is red due to a non-dismissed misdemeanor charge.  It is a single misdemeanor charge with no additional charges and removing the score associated with the misdemeanor results in a green risk score. | Since the charge is not a bar to service per [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.b](#5b), and removal of the score associated with the charge would result in a green risk score, the CBI is satisfactory. |
| The calculated risk score for the proposed fiduciary is red due to non-dismissed misdemeanor charge(s) that occurred more than 10 years prior to the proposed date of appointment.  The charge is not automatically excluded from the risk score as it pertains to harm to another or a financial crime and removing the score associated with the misdemeanor would result in a yellow risk score or better. | Since the charge is not a bar to service per [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.b](#5b), and removal of the score would result in a yellow score or higher, the CBI is satisfactory if the FE is able to justify the yellow result based on [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.k](#5k). |

***Notes***:

* In such cases where the criteria are met to mitigate a red risk score, the *Criminal Background Inquiry High-Risk Score Mitigation Request* template must be used as shown in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.m](#5m).
* If application of the mitigation results in a yellow score, then justification of the yellow result based on [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.k](#5k) must be provided within the template.
* The approved request must be uploaded to the eFolder with the field examination.

**I.2.C.5.n . Example: Justifications for Non-Appointment of Desired Fiduciary Based on CBI**

***Example***:  The calculated risk result for the proposed fiduciary is yellow with a score of 300.  A review of the specific risk indicators shows a single charge of extortion with a threat to injure a person.  When discussing the instance with the proposed fiduciary, it is discovered the beneficiary was the individual extorted.

***Result***:  Even though the single charge is not a bar to service per [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.b](#5b), the beneficiary being the victim in the crime represents an unsafe situation, and requires the FE to not appoint the proposed fiduciary.

***Note***:  The determination of whether the crimes listed on a yellow calculated risk result justify non-appointment is reliant upon the judgement of the FE after careful review of the facts and circumstances of each unique situation.

**I.2.C.5.o . Cases Not Requiring Mitigation**

When obvious errors in the CBI report exist, mitigation is not required.  The criteria for these cases are listed as follows:

* The fiduciary has been charged with an offense or offenses meeting the red risk score and the charges remain on the report but are recorded as dismissed.  The charges can be excluded from the score if
  + the charges are clearly documented as dismissed
  + there are no other pending charges or convictions barring the fiduciary from service, and
  + removal of the dismissed charges would result in a yellow risk score or better.
* Any case where the negative information is clearly for an individual other than the proposed fiduciary, and removal of the erroneous entry would result in a yellow risk score or better.

***Important***:

* Hub personnel must validate the discrepancy and document the finding in the eFolder by adding the following note in VBMS when uploading the CBI:  *The red risk score is invalid due to (insert the identified discrepancy)*.
* If removal of dismissed or erroneous entries as described above results in a yellow risk score, the FE must follow the requirements for justification of the yellow risk score, as outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.k](#5k) and [l](#5l).  If the score is still red after removal of the dismissed or erroneous entries, the hub must not appoint the fiduciary, unless the red risk score can be mitigated, as outlined in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.l](#5l).

---

**I.2.C.5.p . Preventing Misuse of a Minor’s VA Funds**

The FE must identify any situation that could lead to the misuse or misappropriation of the minor’s benefits and take action to protect the interest of the minor.

***Example***:  If other adults residing with the proposed fiduciary and minor have no means of support, the FE must take action to prevent the minor’s VA funds from being used to support those adults, by educating the fiduciary the funds are for the minor and not for anyone else.

**I.2.C.6.a . Overview of EIAs**

In accordance with [38 CFR 13.100(g)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=b853b0d69c153de162f93351e4efcf84&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1100&rgn=div8), the FHM will not conduct a face-to-face interview or credit check of the proposed fiduciary when

  + natural, adopted, or step parent and the beneficiary is under the age of majority, or
  + spouse as defined in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.4.a](#4a), or
* the annual amount of VA benefits the proposed fiduciary would manage for the beneficiary does not exceed the amount specified in [38 U.S.C. 5507(c)(2)(D)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5507), as adjusted by VA pursuant to [38 U.S.C. 5312](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5312).

For these situations, the EIA protocol will be used unless otherwise excluded in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.6.b](#6b).

In appointing a fiduciary, the FHM will make every effort to appoint the person, agency, organization, or institution that will best serve the interest of the beneficiary.  Follow the steps in the table below to expedite the appointment.

**I.2.C.6.b . When an EIA Is Not Appropriate**

An EIA is not appropriate when

* there is evidence indicating

  + the beneficiary and/or fiduciary require a face-to-face interview
  + the need for a credit report
  + a strained relationship between the beneficiary and proposed fiduciary, or
  + the proposed fiduciary is unable or unsuitable, or
* the proposed fiduciary

  + cannot be contacted via telephone, or
  + is being evasive or uncooperative.

***Important***:  If the situation meets the criteria for an EIA per [FPM, Part I, 2.C.6.a](#6a), but is excluded from the EIA per this block, the FE ***must*** document the specific reason for requiring the standard IA process.  Include this information with the interview logistics documented per [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1.a](#1a).

When an EIA is not appropriate per the bulleted list above, the hub must not appoint the fiduciary under the EIA process.  The fiduciary must be appointed under the IA or SIA process in accordance with [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1](#1)-[5](#5).

**I.2.C.6.c . Preparing to Contact the Proposed Fiduciary**

Prior to contacting the beneficiary or proposed fiduciary, the FE must review the information in VA systems in accordance with [FPM, Part I, 2.B.1](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000153948/).

When the case qualifies for an EIA, hub personnel must obtain the CBI of the proposed fiduciary and review the CBI in accordance with [FPM, Part I, 2.C.5.i](#5i)-[o](#5o).

The CBI review must be completed prior to contacting the proposed fiduciary to ensure that the person qualifies for fiduciary service.

---

**I.2.C.6.d . Contacting the Proposed Fiduciary**

Face-to-face contact with the proposed fiduciary is not required for EIA in accordance with [38 CFR 13.100(g)](https://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=b853b0d69c153de162f93351e4efcf84&mc=true&node=se38.1.13_1100&rgn=div8).  The FE must interview the proposed fiduciary by telephone or secure and approved videoconferencing methods.

FEs will utilize the CBI to conduct identification protocol, verifying the proposed fiduciary’s

* first, middle, and last name
* last four digits of their SSN, and
* date of birth.

***Important***:  If the proposed fiduciary’s identity cannot be verified utilizing the CBI, refer to the guidance in this section for appointing a fiduciary through face-to-face contact.

As part of the contact with the fiduciary, the FE must document the following fiduciary information in the field examination:

* first, middle, and last name
* SSN
* date of birth
* relationship to the beneficiary
* telephone number
* email, if available
* physical address, and
* mailing address.

The FE must document that the interview was conducted via telephone or videoconferencing in the field examination.  The FE must also evaluate and document all other fiduciary information as required in [FPM, Part I, 2.C](#top) unless specifically listed as an exception for EIAs.

***References***:  For more information on

* fiduciary appointments, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.4](#4), and
* documenting the interview logistics, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1.a](#1a).

**I.2.C.6.e . Documenting the Beneficiary’s Information**

The FE must confirm and document the beneficiary’s information in accordance with [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1](#1), to include the following:

* full name
* date of birth
* SSN
* telephone number
* mailing address, and
* physical address.

This information may have been collected and verified at the time of scheduling; however, if the FE identifies changes in this information during the field examination, the FE must update the beneficiary record with the correct information.

Unless specifically stated as an exception for EIAs, the FEs must also evaluate and document all beneficiary information as required in [FPM, Part I, 2.C.1](#1)-[4](#4).

***Reference***:  For more information on documenting and evaluating the beneficiary’s

* well-being, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.2](#2)

* financial information, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.3](#3), and
* fiduciary preference, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.4.b](#4b).

**I.2.C.6.f . Handling Situations Where Contact With an Adult or Minor Beneficiary 12 Years or Older Is Not Possible**

The proposed fiduciary may advise the FE that the beneficiary is unable or unwilling to speak with VA during the IA.

***Important***:  The FE should not inform the proposed fiduciary that they may refuse to allow the beneficiary to speak with the FE, unless the question is raised by the proposed fiduciary.

The proposed fiduciary’s unwillingness to allow the FE to speak with the beneficiary alone is not evidence that the fiduciary is attempting to delay or refuse to cooperate in the fiduciary appointment process.

FEs must determine if there is good cause for the proposed fiduciary’s reason for requesting that the FE not speak with the beneficiary.

Good cause for not speaking with the beneficiary may include, but is not limited to the

* beneficiary’s inability to communicate verbally with the FE due to disease or disability
* minor's unavailability due to conflicting schedules relating to school or other extracurricular activities such as camps, sports or events, travel at the time of the field examination
* minor declining to speak to the FE, or
* proposed fiduciary's request that the FE not speak with the minor due to the emotional concerns relating to the loss of a parent or other challenges relating to the way in which the child became entitled to benefits.

Accept the fiduciary’s statement as good cause, unless contradictory evidence exists.

If good cause is not shown, the FE must inform the fiduciary that VA may not be able to appoint them as the fiduciary if they are unwilling to make the beneficiary available.

Document the reasons for not speaking with the beneficiary specifically in the field examination and include a brief description of the conversation.  Justification must include good cause, if appropriate.

If the FE determines speaking with the beneficiary will not occur due to the proposed fiduciary’s unwillingness and good cause is *not* shown, the FE must schedule a face-to-face interview and refer to the guidance in this section for appointing a fiduciary.

***Note***:  The FE shall make no more than one attempt to speak with the beneficiary during the EIA field examination.

**I.2.C.6.g . Explaining Fiduciary Responsibilities**

During the telephone contact, the FE must explain the fiduciary responsibilities outlined on the [*VA Form 21P-4703*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf).  The FE must obtain verbal verification that the proposed fiduciary understands and agrees to the fiduciary responsibilities.

Prior to submitting the field examination to the hub for closure, the FE must obtain a signed [*VA Form 21-4703*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf) to verify that the proposed fiduciary agrees to uphold fiduciary responsibilities.  The [*VA Form 21P-4703*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf) can be obtained via mail or uploaded to the eFolder using an approved VA claims submission service website such as [Direct Upload](https://eauth.va.gov/accessva/).  If the [*VA Form 21P-4703*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf) is not received within 14 days, refer to the guidance in this section for appointing a fiduciary through face-to-face contact.

***Reference***:  For more information on listing multiple beneficiaries on *[VA Form 21P-4703](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/bl/20/cio/20s5/forms/VBA-21P-4703-ARE.pdf)*, see [FPM, Part I, 2.C.4.e](#4e).
