Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 02-96, May 13, 1996, Entitlement to Compensation of Veteran Who Violates Parole
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Whether, under 38 U.S.C. § 5313, a veteran who was paroled after being incarcerated for conviction of a felony is entitled to full compensation for a service-connected disability for the period during which he violated the conditions of parole?

**Impact on VBA**

The provisions of 38 U.S.C. 5313 which governs the payment of compensation and DIC to persons incarcerated for conviction of a felony, do not apply to a veteran who is on parole following conviction of a felony and who is in violation of one or more of the conditions of parole, unless the veteran has been reincarcerated.

The veteran was entitled to compensation for a service connected condition.  He was incarcerated in 1983 for conviction of a felony.  His benefits were reduced as required by 38 U.S.C 5313(a)(1)(B).  He was released on parole in 1986.  The terms of his parole required him to remain within the state until January 20, 1989.   In November 1986, the benefits were restored.  In February 1987 he left his last known residence in the state without permission of the federal probation officials.  He was recaptured in February 1995 and returned to prison until November 1995.  The regional office requested an opinion regarding the veteran's entitlement to compensation during the period he evaded parole.

In order for the provisions of 38 U.S.C. 5313 to be met, (1) the beneficiary must be incarcerated in a penal institution in excess of 60 days, and (2) the incarceration must be for the conviction of a felony.  The General Counsel concluded that if a veteran, who is entitled to compensation, has been convicted of a felony and is on parole, but in violation of one or more of the conditions of parole, VA benefits must be paid at the full rate because one of the required elements of the statute has not been met, i.e., incarceration.

**Summary on facts and findings**

The General Counsel noted that in the absence of a clearly expressed legislative intent to the contrary, unambiguous statutory language must be regarded as conclusive.  In the case at hand, the law requires that both elements of 38 U.S.C. 5313 must be present to warrant a reduction in benefits.
