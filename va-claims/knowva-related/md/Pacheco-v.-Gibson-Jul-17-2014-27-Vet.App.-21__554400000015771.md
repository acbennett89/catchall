Decision Assessment Document
 Pacheco v. Gibson, Jul 17, 2014, 27 Vet.App. 21
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that [38 C.F.R. § 3.157(b)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=729d98db3fddbd73f48cb3e6d594a0c5&node=se38.1.3_1157&rgn=div8) is ambiguous and therefore deference is warranted to the Secretary’s interpretation that a previous allowance of non-service-connected pension cannot result in an earlier effective date under [§ 3.157(b)(1)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=729d98db3fddbd73f48cb3e6d594a0c5&node=se38.1.3_1157&rgn=div8) for a claim to reopen a previously denied claim for service-connection.  Rather, the Court explained, under the Secretary’s interpretation, a previous allowance of non-service-connected pension can result in an earlier effective date for an increased pension claim or a claim to reopen a pension claim previously denied because the disability was not permanently and totally disabling.

38 C.F.R. § 3.157(b) provides:  “Once a formal claim for pension or compensation has been allowed or a formal claim for compensation disallowed for the reason the service-connected disability is not compensable in degree, receipt of one of the following will be accepted as an informal claim for increased benefits or an informal claim to reopen.  38 C.F.R. § 3.157(b)(1) directs:  “The date of an outpatient or hospital examination or date of admission to a VA or uniformed services hospital will be accepted as the date of receipt of a claim,”  and that “the previsions of this paragraph apply only when such reports relate to examination or treatment of a disability for which service-connection has previously been established or when a claim specifying the benefit sought is received within one year from the date of such examination, treatment or hospital admission.”

Note: The Court had previous issued a panel opinion on January 16, 2014, holding that “as long as a pension claim previously had been allowed as required in § 3.157(b), a VA examination report will constitute the requisite informal claim for increase or reopening under § 3.157(b)(1), irrespective of whether any disability identified in the original pension claim relates to the same condition as the more recent examination report.”  Pacheco v. Shinseki, 26 Vet.App. 413 (2014).  Thus, the Court found that the veteran’s previously-allowed claim for pension rendered his 2001 examination report an informal claim under § 3.157(b)(1) entitling him to an earlier effective date for his claim to reopen a previously denied claim for service connection.  Id. At 416-417.  The Secretary filed a motion seeking en banc review on the interpretation of § 3.157(b) because it involved an issue of exceptional importance.  The Court granted the motion for en banc review.  The previous decision was withdrawn and the present decision was issued in its stead.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who served in the Army from January 1942 to November 1945, reported that he took cover in a foxhole during combat in the Pacific Theater, injuring his right leg and hip when other soldiers jumped in and landed on him.  He filed a claim for service connection for his injuries in February 1974.  The regional office (RO), in July 1974, awarded pension benefits, but denied service connection for the injuries.  He was informed that his service medical records appeared to have been destroyed in the 1973 National Personnel Records Center (NPRC) fire.

In October 1977, the veteran again filed for service connection for his right leg and hip disabilities.  In February 1978, VA located service department morning reports disclosing that the veteran had been hospitalized and “taken sick” several times during 1943.  The RO, in March 1978, denied service connection on the merits because the morning reports did not specify a diagnosis for his in-service hospitalization or sickness.  The RO also terminated his pension benefits due to excessive income.  The veteran’s subsequent attempts to reopen his compensation claims for leg and hip injuries were denied in 1982, 1988, 1989, and 1995.  The NPRC, in 1998, informed the veteran that it had again attempted to locate his service records but was unable to locate any alternative records sources containing remarks pertaining to diagnosis or treatment.

In May 2001, the veteran received VA medical treatment and a physician assistant indicated that the veteran had severe degenerative joint disease that was probably “post[-]traumatic.”  Less than a year later, in January 2002, the veteran sought to reopen his claim.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) reopened the claim and remanded for a medical opinion on the relationship between his current disabilities and service.  A VA medical examiner opined that the veteran’s current disabilities were related to service and the RO, in December 2004, granted service connection for his right leg and hip disabilities with 90 and 10 percent evaluations.  An effective date of January 23, 2002, the date the RO received the request to reopen, was ultimately assigned.  In October 2008, the veteran submitted additional service records.

The veteran sought an earlier effective date in his appeal to the Board.  Following a Court remand, the Board, in September 2011, denied entitlement to an effective date prior to January 23, 2002, for his service-connected disabilities.  The Board determined that the May 2001 VA treatment record did not meet the criteria for a claim under [§ 3.157(b)(1)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=729d98db3fddbd73f48cb3e6d594a0c5&node=se38.1.3_1157&rgn=div8) because a formal claim for pension or compensation had not been allowed (or denied because the disability was noncompensable) and that the veteran was not service connected for the disabilities at issue at the time of the May 2001 VA examination.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board should have awarded him an earlier effective date of May 11, 2001, the date of the VA examination record, under [§ 3.157(b)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=729d98db3fddbd73f48cb3e6d594a0c5&node=se38.1.3_1157&rgn=div8) and (b)(1) because he was previously awarded pension benefits and filed a claim to reopen – meeting the requirements of the first sentence of subsection (b) – and that he met the requirements of the last sentence of subsection (b)(1) because he filed his claim to reopen within one year of the May 2001 VA examination record.  The Secretary argued that, under the regulation, a previous pension allowance may form the predicate for an earlier effective date for increased pension benefits, but not a reopened claim for service-connection.

The Court held that the plain language of § 3.157(b) and (b)(1) is ambiguous and that the Secretary’s interpretation would be afforded deference so if not plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation, and there is no reason to suspect that it does not reflect the Secretary's fair and considered judgment on the matter.

The Court reviewed the history of § 3.157(b) and its predecessor, [38 C.F.R.](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=729d98db3fddbd73f48cb3e6d594a0c5&node=se38.1.3_1261&rgn=div8) [§ 3.261(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=729d98db3fddbd73f48cb3e6d594a0c5&node=se38.1.3_1261&rgn=div8), and noted that § 3.216(a), prior to 1951, provided for earlier effective dates for claims for increase based upon the submission of certain medical reports.  In 1951, a second sentence was added to allow earlier effective dates for claims to reopen as well, where the claim had been “disallowed for the reason that his service-connected condition was not 10 percent disabling in degree.”

The Court concluded that the revision reflected the Secretary’s desire to allow earlier effective dates for those who service-connected condition worsened, but not those who had yet to establish service connection.  The language also reflected the Secretary’s practice at the time that claims for benefits for disabilities that were service-connected yet noncompensably disabling were considered “disallowed.”  The Court noted that the two separate sentences of § 3.261(a) were combined with the promulgation of [§ 3.157(b)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=729d98db3fddbd73f48cb3e6d594a0c5&node=se38.1.3_1157&rgn=div8) in 1961; however, there was no indication that the logic of the evolution of the language was abandoned.  The Court further observed that the Secretary recently acknowledged in the *Federal Register* that § 3.157(b) has never applied to permit the reopening a claim that was denied because the claimed disability was not service connected.  See VA Compensation and Pension Regulation Rewrite Project, 78 Fed. Reg. 71, 042, 71, 069 (Nov. 27, 2013).

The Court held that deference is warranted to the Secretary’s interpretation of § 3.157(b) and that the Board’s determination that § 3.157(b) was not applicable to the effective date of the veteran’s award of disability compensation was not a violation of law or otherwise clearly erroneous.  Accordingly, the Court found that the veteran failed to meet the criteria of § 3.157(b) for an earlier effective date for an award based on a claim to reopen, as the veteran did not have a claim disallowed for the reason that the disability was not compensable in degree.

The veteran also argued that, under [38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=729d98db3fddbd73f48cb3e6d594a0c5&node=se38.1.3_1156&rgn=div8), he is entitled to an effective date of February 28, 1974, the date VA received his original claim for disability benefits.  He contended that when VA denied his claim in 1974, it did not have access to the Army records it obtained in 1978 or 2008 showing that the had been  hospitalized and sick during 1943.  He maintained that because those records later contributed to his grant of benefits, § 3.156(c) mandates an effective date going back to his original claim.

The Court found that, despite the receipt of potentially new service department records during the pendency of the veteran’s appeal of the assigned effective date of benefits, the Board failed to address § 3.156(c) or whether the service department records received in 2008 were new and material.

Accordingly, the Court remanded the case for the Board to decide the matters under § 3.156(c) in the first instance.
