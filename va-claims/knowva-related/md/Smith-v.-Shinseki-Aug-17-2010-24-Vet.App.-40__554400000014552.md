Decision Assessment Document
 Smith v. Shinseki, Aug 17, 2010, 24 Vet.App. 40
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the statutory presumptions of service connection ([38 U.S.C. § 1112](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1112), later modified by [38 U.S.C. § 1137](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1137)) and aggravation ([38 U.S.C. § 1153](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1153)) do not apply to a veteran who has prior active duty service but whose claim for benefits is based on a later period of active duty for training.

The Court did hold that such a veteran may be entitled to the statutory presumption of a sound condition ([38 U.S.C. § 1111](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1111)); however, the statute requires that there be an examination of the veteran contemporaneous with entry into the period of service on which the claim is based.  In the absence of an examination, the presumption of soundness is inapplicable.

**Impact on VBA:**

References to “active” service in M21-MR provisions concerning the presumption of soundness will be removed where necessary to be consistent with the Court’s decision.  For example, [M21-1MR IV.ii.2.B.3.e](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014553/) refers to “active” service.  The use of “active” service there is inconsistent with the Court’s decision, and the term is not contained in 38 U.S.C. § 1111.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran joined the Army National Guard in December 1981.  Her service with the Army extended more than 180 days, from February through July 1982, which qualified her for status as a veteran for purposes of VA benefits.  She continued service in the National Guard until 2003 and had multiple confirmed periods of active duty for training, including June and July 1999.  The veteran was not examined contemporaneously with her June and July 1999 period of active duty for training.  A private physician saw the veteran in September 1999 for headaches that she reported had begun five months previously.  A large left frontal temporal meningioma was diagnosed.  In October 1999, the veteran underwent surgery to remove the brain tumor.

In October 2001, the veteran filed a claim for service connection for the residuals of the brain tumor, which was denied in March 2003.  She testified before the Board of Veteran’s Appeals (Board) in July 2004 that she had not suffered any head injuries and that her headaches began in approximately 1988.  Following a Court remand, a VA medical examiner reviewed the veteran’s claims file in November 2007 and opined that the headaches noted in June and July 1999 were caused by the meningioma.  The examiner also attributed the veteran’s complaints of headaches, dating back to 1987, to her tumor.

The Board, in denying the appeal in May 2008, concluded that the veteran’s headaches pre-dated her 1999 period of active duty for training and that there was no competent medical evidence supporting an etiological relationship between the veteran’s brain tumor and active service.  The Board also determined that the presumptive periods under [38 C.F.R. § 3.307](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=3fdbeb8453049e144df08e5e7c10f252&node=se38.1.3_1307&rgn=div8) and [3.309](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=3fdbeb8453049e144df08e5e7c10f252&node=se38.1.3_1309&rgn=div8) did not apply to periods of active duty for training.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board erred in not affording her the presumption of service connection for chronic conditions found in § 3.307 and 3.309 for brain tumors, because she is a veteran for purposes of § 3.307 as result of 1982 period of active duty.  She also asserted that she was entitled to the presumption of soundness and aggravation.

The Court concluded that section 1111, the presumption of soundness, applies to “veterans” and contains no qualifying language to exclude a veteran whose claim is based upon a period of active duty for training.  However, section [1111](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1111) requires that a veteran must have been examined upon entry into service in order for the presumption of soundness to apply.  In the absence of such an examination, there is no basis to determine that the claimant was in sound condition upon entry into that period of service on which the claim is based.  Because there had been no examination contemporaneous with the veteran’s entry into her June and July 1999 periods of active duty for training, the Court held that the presumption of a sound condition did not apply to the veteran’s case.

The Court held that the presumption of service connection, under sections [1112](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1112) and [1137](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1137), can never apply to a claim based on a period of active duty for training.  The Court explained that, by definition, the presumption of service connection applies where there is no evidence that a condition began in or was aggravated during the relevant period of service.  By contrast, for a claimant whose claim is based on a period of active duty for training to establish benefits, [38 U.S.C. § 101(24)(B)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/101) requires that there must be some evidence that the condition was “incurred or aggravated” during the relevant period of service.

The Court also held that the presumption of aggravation under section 1153 does not apply to claims based upon a period of active duty for training.  The Court pointed out that the statutory definition of the phrase “active military, naval, or air service,” in 38 U.S.C. § 101(24), differs for a person serving on active duty and one serving on active duty for training.  The phrase is defined as “active duty” in [38 U.S.C. § 101(24)(A)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/101); however, 38 U.S.C. § 101(24)(B) defines the phrase as “any period of active duty for training during which the individual concerned was disabled or died from a disease or injury incurred in or aggravated in the line of duty.”

Accordingly, a veteran whose claim is based upon a period of active duty for training must show that his or her disability was aggravated (or incurred) during the period of active duty for training – resulting in disability during service – before he or she is considered to have “active military, naval, or air service.”  The Court explained that because the presumption of aggravation under section [1153](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1153) applies only to periods of “active, military, naval, or air service,” a veteran of active duty for training service would have to prove in-service aggravation before he or she could be entitled to the presumption of aggravation.  Therefore, section 1153 would never apply to such a veteran in a way that could matter because it could apply only after aggravation during service has already been established by direct evidence.  Thus, the presumption of aggravation under section 1153 was inapplicable to the veteran’s claim because it was based on a period of active duty for training.

The Court affirmed the Board’s May 2008 decision.
