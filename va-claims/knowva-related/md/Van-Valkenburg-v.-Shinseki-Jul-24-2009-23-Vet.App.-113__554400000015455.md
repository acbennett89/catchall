Decision Assessment Document
 Van Valkenburg v. Shinseki, Jul 24, 2009, 23 Vet.App. 113
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court reversed the finding of the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) that the appellant had not filed a “jointly-prescribed form” for DIC benefits within one year of her husband’s death and was not entitled to an effective date of December 1, 1984, the first day of the month that the veteran died, for the award of DIC benefits.

The Court held that based upon the totality of the record, which showed that the appellant had been in receipt of Social Security Administration (SSA) survivor’s benefits since the death of her husband in 1984, and the Secretary’s representation that any type of form filed with SSA requesting survivor’s benefits by the appellant would suffice to warrant an earlier effective date, that the Board’s findings were clearly erroneous.

**Impact on VBA:**

We are presently reviewing [38 C.F.R. § 3.153](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=18e40dda775ad344043baad55198ecba&node=se38.1.3_1153&rgn=div8) to determine if any revisions are required in view of the Court’s decision.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

Upon the veteran’s death in December 1984, the surviving spouse sought certain VA benefits and completed numerous VA forms to include applications for a U.S. flag and burial benefits, releases of her husband’s funds and personal effects, and a request for release of all VA medical records.

On December 18, 1984, VA forwarded the surviving spouse an application for DIC benefits, and informed her that it should be completed and returned within one year.  VA also stated that the appellant could expedite the processing of her claim by furnishing a certified copy of the public or church record of marriage.

The record disclosed that an application for DIC was not received by the RO until March 7, 1995.  DIC benefits were subsequently awarded effective March 7, 1995.  However, the record contains a copy of the appellant’s “Certificate of Marriage Registration” – certified on November 30, 1984 – and other documents received in later 1984 and early 1985.  Also, a November 2000 letter from the regional office to the Department of Defense certified that the appellant was receiving and had been receiving DIC benefits since December 3, 1984.

The Board, in denying the appellant’s claim for an earlier effective date for DIC benefits, found that the preponderance of the evidence supported a finding that the appellant did not file a claim for DIC directly with the Secretary prior to March 1995.

The Court, in discussing the unclear picture of what actions were undertaken by the appellant in initiating a request for and having been awarded SSA survivor’s benefits, noted documents of record indicating that SSA had informed VA that the appellant was in receipt of spouse’s benefits prior to her husband’s death and those benefits would have been automatically converted to survivor’s benefits upon her husband’s death.  The SSA statements indicated that no paperwork was involved; however, one SSA representative had informed VA that a phone call to SSA would have been required from the surviving spouse to complete the conversion.

The Court noted that the Board failed to discuss the November 1984 certified copy of the marriage certificate and the possible import of the November 2000 VA letter to DoD certifying that the appellant had been in receipt of DIC benefits since her husband’s death.  The Court also found inadequate the Board’s reasons for giving less weight to the appellant’s statements that she filed for DIC shortly after her husband’s death because she initially indicated in her 1995 application that she never applied for VA benefits before and there was no DIC application in the record pre-dating the March 1995 application.

The Court found that the Board’s failure to provide an adequate statement of its reasons or bases errors frustrated judicial review.  The Court further found; however, that the need to remand with regard to whether a claim for DIC had been directly filed with the Secretary was mooted with its reversal of the Board’s finding that the preponderance of the evidence supported a finding that an application for survivor’s benefits had not been filed with SSA.

The Court, in addressing the issue of a “jointly prescribed form,” noted that Congress enacted the Servicemen’s and Veterans’ Survivor Benefits Act (SVSBA) in 1956 with the intent of eliminating the administrative maze that applicants for Federal surviving-spouse benefits had to work through to receive full benefits, and facilitating a streamlined process by which surviving spouses could file an application for benefits at either SSA or VA that would automatically constitute an application for benefits at the other agency.

The Court described the implementing regulations and policies regarding a “jointly prescribed form” as required by law as lacking clarity.  In a supplemental filing, the Secretary advised the Court that SSA had migrated almost completely away from the use of paper forms and now asks applicants to apply for benefits telephonically or in person.

The Court indicated that a clear picture of whatever might have constituted a “jointly prescribed form” in the past is now largely ineffective in fulfilling the intent of Congress in passing the SVSBA.  In this context, the Court accepted the Secretary’s representation that any claim, sufficient to reflect an intent to apply for survivor’s benefits, that is filed with SSA will suffice to establish the effective date for DIC.  In reversing the Board’s decision, the Court held that it was indisputable that survivor’s benefits were initiated and awarded either by an application from the appellant or by SSA through automatic conversion.

Based on the totality of the record and the Secretary’s representation that any type of form filed with SSA by the appellant requesting survivor’s benefits would suffice to warrant an earlier effective date, the Court found clearly erroneous the Board’s finding that the appellant was not entitled to an earlier effective date of December 1, 1984, for the award of DIC benefits.

In a footnote, the Court indicated that in light of its decision that the Board clearly erred in finding that the preponderance of the evidence was against an application for survivor’s benefits having been filed after the veteran’s death, it need not decide whether notification of death to SSA that resulted in an award of survivor’s benefits also constituted an “application” for survivor’s benefits for DIC effective date purposes.
