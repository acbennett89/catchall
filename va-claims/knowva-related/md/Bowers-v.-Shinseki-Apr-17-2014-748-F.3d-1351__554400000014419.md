Decision Assessment Document
 Bowers v. Shinseki, Apr 17, 2014, 748 F.3d 1351
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that the Veterans Court correctly interpreted the presumption of service connection for amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) under [38 C.F.R. § 3.318](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=0339e81060694322cab84ae54398e449&node=se38.1.3_1318&rgn=div8) as only applying to individuals having attained veteran status by performing “active military, naval, or air service.”  Individuals who only served in the National Guard would not be entitled to the § 3.318 presumption, as they had not performed “active military, naval, or air service.”

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

Mr. Bowers served in the Army National Guard from March 1972 to March 1978, with a continuous period of active duty for training for more than 90 days from August 1972 to February 1973.  He was diagnosed with ALS in July 2009 and filed a claim for service connection for the disease in September 2009.  The regional office denied the claim two months later.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) denied presumptive service connection for ALS in September 2010.  Mr. Bowers died in December 2010 and the Veterans Court granted his surviving spouse’s motion to substitute.

The Veterans Court, affirming the Board’s decision, held that [§ 3.318](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=0339e81060694322cab84ae54398e449&node=se38.1.3_1318&rgn=div8) requires that an individual attain veteran status as defined as having served in the “active military, naval, or air service” under [38 U.S.C. § 101(24)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/101) with 90 days of continuous active service before the individual is entitled to the presumption of service connection for ALS.  The Veterans Court held that an individual who served on active duty for training is excluded from this presumption – regardless of the length of service – unless it can be shown that “the individual concerned was disabled or died from a disease or injury incurred or aggravated” during that period of active duty for training.

The appellant argued before the Federal Circuit that the Veterans Court erroneously interpreted § 3.318 because the phrase “veteran status” is not used in the regulation; therefore, the court’s imposition of such a requirement is error.  She asserted that any active service of more than 90 days is sufficient to establish entitlement to the presumption under § 3.318 regardless of whether the claimant meets the statutory definition of a “veteran.”

The Federal Circuit acknowledged that the regulation does not refer to “veterans status,” but noted that § 3.318 does provide that the presumption of service connection for ALS applies after discharge or release from active military, naval, or air service, which is specifically defined under section 101(24) to include only individuals who served on active duty or on active duty for training, but only when the individual was disabled or died from a disease or injury incurred or aggravated in the line of duty; neither applied to Mr. Bowers.

The Federal Circuit concluded that the Veterans Court’s requirement of “veteran status” simply acknowledged that the regulation requires active military, naval, or air service.  The Federal Circuit also found that the plain language of § 3.318 mirrors statutory definitions, such as [38 U.S.C. § 1110](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1110) and [1131](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1131), by requiring “active military, naval, or air service” of 90 days or more for the presumption to apply.

The Federal Circuit also rejected the appellant’s assertions that the Secretary’s comments in the Federal Register established the Secretary’s intent to provide the presumption under [§ 3.318](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=0339e81060694322cab84ae54398e449&node=se38.1.3_1318&rgn=div8) to individuals that did not satisfy the statutory requirements of veteran status.  The Federal Circuit found that the Secretary’s comments, like the § 3.318 text, echo the statutory requirements of veteran status.

The Federal Circuit affirmed the Veterans Court decision.
