Decision Assessment Document
 Southall-Norman v. McDonald, Dec 15, 2016, 28 Vet.App. 346 (2016)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that 38 C.F.R. 4.59 is not limited to the evaluation of musculoskeletal disabilities under diagnostic codes predicated upon range of motion (ROM) measurements.

The Court held that section 4.59 is applicable to the evaluation of musculoskeletal disabilities involving actually painful, unstable, or malaligned joints or periarticular regions, regardless of whether the diagnostic code under which the disability is evaluated is predicated upon ROM measurements.

**Impact on VBA:**

Manual M21-1 revisions will be required under III.iv.4.A.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who served from February 2000 to October 2007, filed a claim for a bilateral foot disability and hemorrhoids while in service.  During a July 2007 VA examination, she reported persistent hemorrhoids with fecal leakage; however, a pad was not needed.  She also reported pain in both feet.  The examiner found tenderness, but no pain on motion was exhibited.  Diagnoses of persistent hemorrhoids with rectal pain and bilateral hallux valgus with pain on prolonged standing and wearing high heels were rendered.

The regional office (RO), in March 2008, granted service connection for bilateral hallux valgus and external hemorrhoids with noncompensable evaluations.  The veteran appealed the zero percent evaluations and submitted statements in November 2008 that the hemorrhoids left horrible stains in her undergarments and were constantly oozing pus or bleeding.

In August 2011, the veteran complained of a rectal pain, swelling, and bleeding during a VA examination.  The examiner found no sphincter control impairment or fecal leakage.  The veteran reported bilateral foot pain and cramping when standing and walking during a VA examination conducted the following month.  The examiner found bilateral forefoot pain on manipulation, but no functional loss due to pain.  Bilateral hallux valgus and pes planus were diagnosed.

The veteran testified before the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) in October 2013 that she had constant bilateral foot pain.  She also stated that her hemorrhoid condition had worsened over the past five or six years earlier, which resulted in fecal leakage and required her to daily wear pads since then to prevent staining.

The Board, in February 2014, granted a 10 percent evaluation for the hemorrhoids and remanded the bilateral foot disability for another VA examination.  However, the Court granted a joint motion for remand (JMR) for the hemorrhoids issue and remanded it to the Board.  The JMR noted that the veteran’s reports of fecal leakage at the 2007 VA examination and 2013 Board hearing and directed the Board to address the credibility and probative value of those reports in light of the diagnostic code for impairment of sphincter control, DC 7332, not explicitly requiring that leakage be confirmed by objective findings.

A June 2014 VA examination confirmed bilateral foot pain with weight-bearing.  The examiner diagnosed bilateral pes planus and associated it with the bilateral hallux valgus.  The RO, in September 2014, added bilateral pes planus to the service-connected bilateral foot disability and assigned a 50 percent evaluation under DC 5276 from the date of the June 2, 2014, VA examination.

The Board, in January 2015, denied entitlement to a compensable evaluation for the bilateral foot disability prior to June 2, 2014.  The Board considered the veteran’s complaints of pain, but did not mention § 4.59.  Regarding the hemorrhoids, the Board denied a separate evaluation for sphincter impairment because her reports of fecal leakage were inconsistent throughout the claim period and contradicted by the medical evidence of record.  The Board also noted that the veteran did not always differentiate between fecal leakage and other types of leakage and found that, to the extent that her complaints related solely to fecal leakage, they did not demonstrate constant slight or occasional moderate fecal leakage required for a compensable evaluation under DC 7332.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board provided inadequate reasons or bases for denying an initial compensable evaluation for the bilateral foot disability prior to June 2, 2014, because it failed to discuss § 4.59 in view of the record containing evidence of foot pain on weight bearing, walking, standing, and manipulation, .  The Secretary disputed, asserting that the Board was not required to address § 4.59 because it only applies to evaluating a joint disability under a diagnostic code predicated upon ROM measurements, as the diagnostic codes, DCs 5276 and 5280, do not mention ROM.  The Secretary further argued that, to the extent that § 4.59 is ambiguous on this point, the Court should defer to the Secretary’s interpretation that § 4.59 is inapplicable to diagnostic codes that do not consider ROM as set forth in the VA Adjudication Procedures Manual (M21-1).

The Court found the Board erred in not applying § 4.59 to the bilateral foot disability, as it held that plain language of the regulation is not limited to the evaluation of musculoskeletal disabilities under diagnostic codes predicated upon ROM measurements.

The Court rejected the Secretary’s contention that a minimum compensable evaluation for a musculoskeletal disability is conditioned upon ROM measurements in the diagnostic code, but it is based upon evidence of any actually painful, unstable, or malaligned joint or periarticular region.  The Court concluded that § 4.59 is potentially applicable to the evaluation of musculoskeletal disabilities involving joint or periarticular pathology that are painful, whether or not evaluated under a diagnostic code predicated on ROM measurements.

The Court also rejected the Secretary’s argument that deference be given to its interpretation in the M21-1 that § 4.59 was inapplicable to diagnostic codes not involving ROM measurements, as the Secretary had taken contrary positions in an oral argument and pleadings before the Court.  The Court determined that, in view of such inconsistencies,  the Secretary’s interpretation of § 4.59 does not reflect his considered view on the matter.

The Court, concluding that the Board was required to apply § 4.59 to the bilateral foot disability, as – contrary to the Secretary’s assertion – the record contained competent evidence of painful motion, remanded the issue for the Board to address whether the evidence triggered application of § 4.59.

Regarding the sphincter control issue, the veteran maintained that the Board made a favorable finding that she had sphincter impairment, which cannot be disturbed on appeal.  She also argued that the actual issue on appeal is whether she had fecal leakage warranting a separate compensable evaluation and that the Board improperly found that her lay statements were not credible on the matter.

The Court, finding the veteran’s reports of when she began to use absorbent materials because of fecal leakage to be credible, held that the Board’s reasons or bases for impugning her credibility to be inadequate.  The Court also found that the Board erred by improperly faulting the veteran for not differentiating between the types of leakage that she experienced, as the Board failed to cite favorable evidence of daily fecal leakage and leakage following bowel movements.  The Court noted that although the August 2011 VA examination found no impairment of sphincter control, the record was replete with other evidence suggesting such impairment, which the Board improperly discounted.

The Court remanded the impairment of sphincter control claim because it was unable to conclude that the reasons or bases errors in assessing the veteran’s credibility were harmless.
