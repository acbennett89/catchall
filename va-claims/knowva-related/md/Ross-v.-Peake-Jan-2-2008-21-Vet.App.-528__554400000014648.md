Decision Assessment Document
 Ross v. Peake, Jan 2, 2008, 21 Vet.App. 528
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court rejected the argument that the effective date assigned for a secondary service-connected condition must be the same as the effective date for the underlying condition under [38 C.F.R. § 3.310](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=dad41d7212349a89eb2305e16db6758f&node=se38.1.3_1310&rgn=div8).

The Court held that a claim for secondary service connection is not a claim for increased compensation; thus, the provisions of [38 U.S.C. § 5110(b)(2)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5110) and [38 C.F.R. § 3.400(o)(2)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=dad41d7212349a89eb2305e16db6758f&node=se38.1.3_1400&rgn=div8) are not for application in determining the effective date of claim.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who was already service connected for a heart condition, submitted a statement from a vocational specialist in December 1999 that the veteran’s heart and anxiety condition prevented him from working, which the regional office (RO) accepted as a claim for secondary service connection for depression and for a total evaluation due to individual unemployablity (TDIU).  The RO eventually granted service connection for depression with anxiety, secondary to the service-connected heart condition, with a 70 percent evaluation and also assigned a TDIU evaluation.  An effective date of December 7, 1999, was assigned for both evaluations.  The veteran, disagreeing with the effective date, argued that he was entitled to the same effective date for service connection for depression as for the heart condition.  The Board denied the appeal in June 2005

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board erred by not assigning an earlier effective date based on the provisions of [38 C.F.R. § 3.310(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=fb8f2c69d2d56ff0d43cef818b2c9b95&node=se38.1.3_1310&rgn=div8) and that the effective date for the award of secondary service connection should be the same as the effective date of the underlying condition.  He also contended that the a claim for secondary service connection is a claim for increased compensation and the Board erred by failing to apply the effective date rules for increased compensation under [38 U.S.C. § 5110(b)(2)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5110) and [38 C.F.R. § 3.400(o)(2)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=fb8f2c69d2d56ff0d43cef818b2c9b95&node=se38.1.3_1400&rgn=div8).

The Court, holding that § 3.310(a) requires VA to apply the same rule to the assignment of an effective date for secondarily service-connected conditions as it does for direct service-connected conditions, rejected the veteran’s arguments. Note: The Court rejected this same argument in Ellington v. Nicholson, U.S. Vet.App. No. 04-0403 (July 25, 2007) and Roper v. Nicholson, 20 Vet.App. 173 (2006).

The Court also held that a secondary service connection claim is not a claim for increased compensation and explained that a claim for increased compensation is one where a veteran asserts that an already service-connected condition has worsened and not where a veteran asserts that he or she has incurred *additional* disabilities as a result of the service-connected condition.  Thus, an award of “increased compensation” within the meaning of section 5110(b)(2) does not encompass an award of secondary service connection because secondary service connection requires the incurrence of *additional* disability.

Furthermore, the exception to the general rule for effective dates contained under section 5110(b)(2) and § 3.400(o)(2) for increased compensation claims that allows for an effective date of up to one year prior to date of claim if it is factually ascertainable that increase in disability had occurred during the year prior to the date of claim is not applicable to a secondary service connection claim because it is not a claim for increased compensation.  The date when application for that additional disability was made or when that additional disability was acquired or became manifest, if later than the date of the application, would control the effective date for a secondary service-connection award under section 5110(a).

The Court affirmed the Board’s decision.
