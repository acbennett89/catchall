Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 08-01, Feb 26, 2001, Meaning of “Injury” for Purposes of Active Service – 38 U.S.C. 101(24)
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Whether a former member of the Naval Reserve who reports having been sexually assaulted on two occasions during inactive duty training and who alleges suffering
 from resulting post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) may be considered to have been disabled by an injury in determining whether the member had active service for purposes of 38 U.S.C. § 101(24)?

**Impact on VBA**

Under 38 U.S.C. § 101(2) and (24), inactive duty training may provide a basis for veteran status for purposes of benefits administered by VA only if the individual incurred disability or death from an injury incurred or aggravated in line of duty.  An individual who suffers from post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) as a result of a sexual assault that occurred during inactive duty training may be considered disabled by an “injury” for purposes of section 101(2) and (24).

**Summary on facts and findings**

The claimant, who served in the Naval Reserve, had inactive duty training only.  The claimant reported having been raped two times during periods of inactive duty training.  The rape incidents had not been substantiated.  The claimant sought disability compensation for PTSD claimed to have resulted from sexual assault.

The General Counsel noted that PTSD can result from a traumatic event that occurred in service even though the disorder is not manifested until much later.

In VAOPGCPREC 26-97 the General Counsel stated “[w]ith the recognition of PTSD came the understanding that a veteran’s response to trauma may be delayed.  Thus, the service member may not show signs of a psychiatric disorder during service, although the disorder was the result of service experiences.”  In other words, a claimant may be injured during service but the resulting disability may not be apparent until some time after service.  The General Counsel concluded that, for purposes of section 101(24), PTSD resulting from sexual assault which occurred during inactive duty training may be considered a disability caused by an injury.

The General Counsel expressed no opinion on the numerous factual issues presented in the claim which gave rise to the opinion request.  The General Counsel noted that pursuant to 38 CFR § 3.304(f), if a veteran is diagnosed with PTSD, before the disability can be service connected, it must be determined that the current symptoms are related to an in-service stressor established by appropriate evidence.

M21-1, Part VI, Chapter 11, par. 11.38 should be updated to reflect that PTSD resulting from sexual assault which occurred during inactive duty training may be considered a disability caused by an injury.
