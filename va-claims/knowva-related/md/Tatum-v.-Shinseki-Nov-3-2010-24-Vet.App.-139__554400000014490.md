Decision Assessment Document
 Tatum v. Shinseki, Nov 3, 2010, 24 Vet.App. 139
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that notice of a proposed reduction in evaluation, as required by [38 C.F.R. § 3.105(e)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=835653f3036d142d0c6b61ca07979f41&node=se38.1.3_1105&rgn=div8), does not apply to a staged rating.

Both parties agreed at oral argument that DC 7528 requires a 100 percent disability rating that continues for a minimum of six months from the time that treatment (surgical, X-ray, antineoplastic chemotherapy or other therapeutic procedure) ceases for malignant neoplasms.  The parties also agreed that a VA medical examination must be provided some time after the expiration of six months, but that the 100 percent disability rating potentially can be reduced to a date before the date on which the examination was provided, as long as the examination results supported such a reduction and the reduction did not take effect at a time before the expiration of the six months following cessation of treatment for malignant neoplasms.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.  The Court has held in previous decisions that notice under § 3.105(e) does not apply to a staged rating.  However, the significance of this decision is that § 3.105(e) notice is also inapplicable to disabilities rated under diagnostic codes containing a note that “any change in evaluation … shall be subject to the provisions of § 3.105(e),” *if* the reduction is part of a staged rating assigned in the grant of an original claim for such disability.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who served in the Army from 1958 to 1980, underwent a radical prostatectomy for treatment of prostate cancer on October 29, 2001.  He filed a claim for service connection for prostate cancer on February 28, 2002, and a VA examination was conducted on August 28, 2002.  The examiner concluded that the veteran’s PSA score was zero, there were no nodules or masses in the prostate area, and the residuals of prostatectomy for prostate cancer included erectile dysfunction and hypertension.

The regional office (RO), in September 2002, granted service connection for prostate cancer with a 100 percent evaluation from February 28, 2002, through May 1, 2002, and a zero percent evaluation, from May 1, 2002.  The RO later revised its decision and assigned a 100 percent evaluation from October 3, 2001, through May 1, 2002, and 10 percent thereafter for residuals.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in July 2008, denied an increased rating for the residuals of the prostatectomy for prostate cancer based on the veteran’s urinary frequency.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board erred because the Secretary did not provide [§ 3.105(e)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=835653f3036d142d0c6b61ca07979f41&node=se38.1.3_1105&rgn=div8) notice before “reducing” his 100 percent evaluation under DC 7528 to zero percent in its September 2002 decision.  He claimed prejudice because, if not for such error, the 100 percent evaluation would have continued until VA complied with DC 7528.

Although acknowledging that he was correct that DC 7528 clearly predicates a reduction on compliance with the notice provisions of § 3.105(e), the Court noted that the veteran ignored the plain language of the regulation, which provides that notice is warranted only when there is a reduction in “compensation payments currently being made.”

The Court stated that the record indisputably showed that the veteran was not receiving compensation at the time that the staged rating was assigned.  Although disability compensation would flow from the September 2002 decision, the veteran was not receiving any such compensation prior to the decision; thus, the notice provisions of § 3.105(e) were inapplicable.

Although the Court rejected the veteran’s argument concerning application of § 3.105(e), the Court agreed to the Secretary’s request to remand the case due to the Board’s failure to adequately address the date of cessation of the veteran’s treatment for malignant neoplasms, which determines the minimum six-month period during which the veteran’s 100 percent rating must continue, and whether the veteran suffered a local reoccurrence or metastasis, which would warrant a continued 100 percent rating.

The Court set aside and remanded the Board’s July 2008 decision.
