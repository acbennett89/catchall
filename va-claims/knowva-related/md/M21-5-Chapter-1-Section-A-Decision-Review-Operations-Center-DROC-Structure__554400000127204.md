## 1.  Purpose of the DROCs

---

**Introduction**

This topic contains general information on DROCs, including

* [purpose of the DROCs](#1a), and
* [higher-level reviews (HLRs) at DROCs](#1b).

---

**Change Date**

April 2, 2025

---

**1.A.1.a . Purpose of the DROCs**

In October 2018, the Veterans Benefits Administration (VBA) consolidated Appeals Modernization Act (AMA) higher-level review (HLR) processing into the decision review operations centers (DROCs) to

* centralize processing of all

  + HLRs
  + AMA full grants by the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (BVA), and
  + AMA BVA remands
* achieve a more effective use of limited human resources, and
* improve technical accuracy based on skills specialization and information technology enhancements.

---

**1.A.1.b . HLRs at DROCs**

The Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) implemented the AMA on February 19, 2019, which authorized VA to process HLRs of decisions relating to disability compensation and pension at the DROCs.

***References***:  For more information on

* who may conduct an HLR, see [38 CFR 3.2601(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/section-3.2601), and
* requests to conduct HLRs at specific offices, see [M21-5, Chapter 5, Topic 2, c](void(0)).

## 2.  DROC Location and Jurisdiction

---

**Introduction**

This topic contains information on location and jurisdiction, including

* [location of the DROCs](#2a), and
* [jurisdiction of the DROCs](#2b).

---

**Change Date**

October 3, 2022

---

**1.A.2.a . Location of the DROCs**

The three DROCs are located in

* Seattle, Washington
* St. Petersburg, Florida, and
* Washington, D.C. (remote office).

---

**1.A.2.b . Jurisdiction of the DROCs**

The National Work Queue manages decision review workload distribution through assigned routing rules and established local rules based on available employee capacity, which generally distributes the oldest pending claims to be worked first, except for VBA-designated priority claims.  Those designated priority claims are included in the daily workload distribution to ensure priority processing.

The distribution of the workload is determined by set capacities for each claim life cycle based on the station’s Workforce Information Tool (WIT).

DROC Managers are responsible for ensuring that workload is distributed efficiently and equitably to maximize the DROCs' ability to serve Veterans and their dependents.

As part of efficient distribution, DROC Managers must update and ensure accuracy of their station's WIT on a weekly basis, to include the proper designation of specialized claims processors as noted in [M21-5, Chapter 1, Section A, 3.f](#3f).

***Reference***:  For more information on updating the WIT, see the *[WIT User Guide](https://wit.vba.va.gov/Content/WIT_3.3_User_Guide.pdf)*.

**1.A.3.a . IPCs at DROCs**

The intake processing centers (IPCs) at the DROCs are primarily responsible for

* processing and controlling mail received in the Centralized Mail portal
* managing outgoing paper mail, and
* controlling claims under end products (EPs).

***Reference***:  For more information on IPCs, see [M21-1, Part I, Subpart ii, 1.A.1.b](void(0)).

---

**1.A.3.b . Adjudication Teams at DROCs**

Adjudication team responsibilities include, but are not limited to

* claim development
* rating and non-rating decision activities
* award promulgation
* award authorization, and
* decision notification.

Examples of cases handled by adjudication teams are

* HLRs
* legacy or AMA BVA remands, and
* legacy or AMA BVA grants.

The adjudication team is structured and staffed to process claims for benefits in support of the overall mission of VBA.  Adjudication teams may be cross-functional or position-based.  The team is typically composed of, but not restricted to

* a coach, and
* one or more

  + Veterans Service Representatives (VSRs) that have been trained in claim development and award promulgation
  + Senior VSRs (SVSRs) (GS-12 VSRs and/or certified GS-11 VSRs)
  + Rating VSRs (RVSRs), and
  + Decision Review Officers (DROs).

***Note***:  As a local option, DROCs may add to a team one or more

* Claims Assistants (CAs), and/or
* assistant coaches.

---

**1.A.3.c . QRTs at DROCs**

DROCs *must* establish a quality review team (QRT) to

* perform quality reviews, and/or
* identify and address error trends.

Each QRT is composed of

* a coach, and
* one or more

  + assistant coach(es), and
  + Quality Review Specialists (QRSs).

The QRT is composed of Rating QRSs (RQRSs) and Authorization QRSs (AQRSs) and is responsible for conducting the following:

* Individual Quality Reviews (IQRs) for all

  + CAs
  + VSRs
  + RVSRs
  + DROs, and
  + QRSs
* In-Process Reviews (IPRs)
* Veterans Benefits Management System (VBMS) deferral mitigations
* Office of Administrative Review (OAR) mandated and/or local reviews, as necessary
* error trend identification and resolution
* mentoring and training, as workload permits, and
* VBMS-Rating override review, as outlined in [M21-5, Chapter 3, Section A, 8.b](void(0)).

***Reference***:  For more information on QRTs, see

* [M21-5, Chapter 3](void(0)), and
* [M21-4, Chapter 6](void(0)).

---

**1.A.3.d . Informal Conference Telephone Availability**

DROCs conducting informal conferences are responsible for ensuring telephone access for informal conference scheduling.  The phone must include a functional voicemail accessible by employees to account for time zone differentials.

---

**1.A.3.e . Attorney Fee Coordination**

Agents or attorneys may charge claimants fees for representation after an agency of original jurisdiction has issued notice of an initial decision on a claim.

Each DROC is responsible for adjudicating whether an agent/attorney representative is eligible for direct payment of fees.

***Reference***:  For more information on payment of attorney or agent fees, see [M21-5, Chapter 8](void(0)).

---

**1.A.3.f . Designated Specialized Claims Processors**

Each DROC is responsible for designating specialized groups of trained personnel to ensure high priority and complex processing of claims related to

* military sexual trauma (MST)
* amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)
* traumatic brain injury (TBI)
* herbicide exposure claims (Blue Water Navy (BWN), *Nehmer*, or BWN-*Nehmer*)
* pension claims
* non-rating claims
* Electronic Health Record Modernization (EHRM) impacted claims
* special mission claims, which include

  + Manila - Philippine (excluding Filipino Veterans Equity Compensation)
  + foreign and U.S. Territory
  + Camp Lejeune Contaminated Water, and
  + radiation, and
* mail processing.

Each DROC must designate specialized processors in the WIT according to the following guidance.  For workload requiring WIT input in the SPECIAL TEAM or TEAM fields, please select from the drop-down list available.

**1.A.3.g . Training and Signature Requirements for MST Development and Decisions**

Only a(n) VSR, AQRS, or RVSR who meets the requirements below is authorized to complete development actions that address MST, and only a(n) RVSR, DRO, or RQRS who meets the requirements below is authorized to complete a decision on MST.

The employee must

* be designated by the DROC as an MST claims processor
* be meeting or exceeding performance and quality standards, and
* have completed MST training requirements, as identified in [M21-5, Chapter 2, Topic 4, h](void(0)).

Development actions completed by a VSR require 2 signatures (second-signature review) until the VSR demonstrates an accuracy rate of 90 percent or greater based on a review of 10 consecutive MST cases.  Reviews of development actions completed by an AQRS require 2 signatures until the AQRS demonstrates an accuracy rate of 90 percent or greater based on a review of 10 consecutive MST cases.

For a decision maker, MST grants and denials are evaluated separately for purposes of single-signature authority.  A decision maker may be released to single-signature authority for MST grants upon demonstrating at least 90 percent accuracy on a review of 10 consecutive MST grant decisions, and for MST denials upon demonstrating at least 90 percent accuracy on a review of 10 consecutive MST denial decisions.  MST grants or denials for which the applicable accuracy standard has not been met require second-signature review.

For an RQRS, all MST decisions require second-signature review until the RQRS demonstrates at least 90 percent accuracy on reviews of both 10 consecutive MST grants and 10 consecutive MST denials.

***Notes***:

* OAR requires DROCs to keep a memorandum of record confirming completion of the requirements and designation of single-signature authority for all MST claims processors and QRSs.  See [M21-5, Chapter 1, Section A, 3.j](#3j) for specific requirements the DROC should include in the memorandum.  All memoranda and associated logs must be saved on the [Special Mission Memos and Case Review Logs Repository](https://dvagov.sharepoint.com/sites/vbaoar/PA/SMM), as mentioned in [M21-5, Chapter 1, Section A, 3.j](#3j).
* MST second-signature reviews must be completed by an authorized single-signature designated reviewer.
* All designated reviewers are required to meet the quality standard outlined in their individual performance standards.  Any reviewer whose quality falls below the minimum level in the reviewer's performance standard is prohibited from conducting second-signature reviews until the reviewer's accuracy rate returns to fully successful or better.

---

**1.A.3.h . Training and Signature Requirements for TBI Decisions**

Only a(n) RVSR, DRO, or RQRS who has met the requirements below is authorized to complete a decision on TBI.

The employee must

* be designated by the DROC as a TBI claims processor, and
* have completed TBI training requirements, as identified in [M21-5, Chapter 2, Topic 4, j](void(0)).

Decisions on TBI cases completed by decision makers require 2 signatures until a decision maker demonstrates an accuracy rate of 90 percent or greater based on a review of 10 consecutive TBI cases, including a mix of grants, denials, and evaluations.

In addition, only a VSR who has met the requirements below is authorized to develop a TBI claim.

The VSR must

* be designated by the DROC as a TBI claims processor, and
* have completed the training requirements for TBI determinations, as identified in [M21-5, Chapter 2, Topic 4, j](void(0)).

***Note***:  OAR requires DROCs to keep a memorandum of record confirming completion of the requirements and designation of single-signature authority for a(n) RVSR, DRO, or RQRS.  See [M21-5, Chapter 1, Section A, 3.j](#3j) for specific requirements to include in the memorandum.  All memoranda and associated logs must be saved on the [Special Mission Memos and Case Review Logs Repository](https://dvagov.sharepoint.com/sites/vbaoar/PA/SMM), as mentioned in [M21-5, Chapter 1, Section A, 3.j](#3j).

---

**1.A.3.i . Training and Signature Requirements for ALS Decisions**

Only a(n) RVSR, DRO, or RQRS who has met the requirements below is authorized to complete a decision on a case where the Veteran is either claiming or has been granted service connection for ALS, even if the current issues under decision are not directly related to the service connected ALS.

The employee must

* be designated by the DROC as an ALS claims processor, and
* have completed the training requirements for ALS determinations, as identified in [M21-5, Chapter 2, Topic 4, i](void(0)).

Decisions on ALS cases completed by decision makers require 2 signatures until a decision maker demonstrates an accuracy rate of 90 percent or greater based on a review of 10 consecutive ALS cases, including a mix of grants, denials, and evaluations.

In addition, only a VSR who has met the requirements below may promulgate and/or authorize a case involving an ALS rating.

The VSR must

* be designated by the DROC as an ALS claims processor, and
* have completed the training requirements for ALS determinations, as identified in [M21-5, Chapter 2, Topic 4, i](void(0)).

***Note***:  OAR requires DROCs to keep a memorandum of record confirming completion of the requirements and designation of single-signature authority for a(n) RVSR, DRO, or RQRS.  See [M21-5, Chapter 1, Section A.3.j](#3j) for more information on the memorandum and requirements.  All memoranda and associated logs must be saved on the [Special Mission Memos and Case Review Logs Repository](https://dvagov.sharepoint.com/sites/vbaoar/PA/SMM), as mentioned in [M21-5, Chapter 1, Section A, 3.j](#3j).

---

**1.A.3.j . Second Signature Log and Memorandum for Specialized Processors**

OAR requires DROCs to maintain a log of claims processors on second-signature review for specialized processing to include MST, TBI, and ALS.  DROCs must also maintain a memorandum for each claims processor who has completed training and second-signature requirements.

The log must contain an entry for each specialty case reviewed, and each entry in the log must contain the following information:

* employee name
* employee position
* date action was taken
* benefit claim ID
* date of case review
* reviewer name
* case accuracy percent (100 percent for correct or 0 percent for incorrect)
* corrective action required, if necessary, and
* date corrective action was taken, if applicable.

For any reviewed cases that are incorrect, the log must also contain columns to indicate additional date(s) reviewed following corrective action, reviewer name(s), and any further corrective action needed.

All special processing cases must be second-signed and reviewed for accuracy by authorized single-signature designated reviewers until the individual processing that case has achieved at least 90 percent accuracy over no less than 10 reviews.  DROCs must provide each claims processor who is designated to have single-signature authority for special processing, to include MST, TBI, and ALS, with a formal memorandum confirming completion of training and second-signature requirements.  Refer to the [*Delegation of Single-Signature Authority Memorandum*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/OAR/docs/Delegation_of_Single_Signature_Authority_Memorandum.docx) template.

The DROCs must add all signed memoranda and corresponding logs on the [Special Mission Memos and Case Review Logs Repository](https://dvagov.sharepoint.com/sites/vbaoar/PA/SMM) maintained by OAR.

## 4.  DROC Roles and Responsibilities

---

**Introduction**

* [CAs](#4a)

* [RVSRs](#4c), and
* [DROs](#4d).

---

**Change Date**

April 14, 2026

---

**1.A.4.a . Duties of CAs**

CAs are assigned to the DROC IPC.  This group of employees has varying degrees of responsibility within the IPC primarily revolving around claims control.

Responsibilities include but are not limited to

* controlling and processing of mail inventory
* performing EP and claim label assignment
* controlling and updating electronic records, and
* establishment/control of EPs.

***Note***:  DROCs are responsible to maintain VSRs that can perform the duties outlined in this section.

***Reference***:  For more information on mail intake procedures, see [M21-1, Part II, Subpart i, 1.A.1.a](void(0)).

---

**1.A.4.b . Duties of VSRs (to include SVSRs, GS-12 VSRs, and/or certified GS-11 VSRs)**

VSR responsibilities include but are not limited to

* triaging of electronic files received from BVA, to include remands, grants, and partial grants
* performing initial review and all required actions to move a claim to the next claim life cycle
* reviewing new remands and ensuring that development and/or action is initiated within 15 days of receipt of the file (based on existing BVA timeframes)
* processing mail and establishing claims
* completing any directed development for appeals in the remand stage and other assigned claims
* updating all appropriate systems, to include the Veterans Appeals Control and Locator System (VACOLS), VBMS, Caseflow, etc.
* ensuring VBA electronic systems accurately reflect

  + suspense dates and reasons
  + contentions associated with a claim
  + development undertaken in connection with a claim
  + evidence received in connection with a claim, and
  + claim status and claim label
* contacting claimants who have requested a call related to a claim
* following systems compliance policies/procedures
* generating awards
* authorizing awards
* notifying beneficiaries of proposed adverse actions
* deciding issues that do not require a rating decision
* issuing decisions on non-rating-related HLRs, and
* providing claimants with decision review request notifications.

***References***:  For more information on

* claim development, see

  + [M21-1, Part III, Subpart i](void(0)), and
  + [M21-1, Part IV, Subpart i, 1](void(0))
* claim development in VBMS, see the

  + [*VBMS Job Instruction Sheet - Initial Claim Development*](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/VBMS/Resources_Job_Instruction_Sheets.asp), and
  + *[VBMS Job Instruction Sheet - Supplemental Claim Development](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/VBMS/Resources_Job_Instruction_Sheets.asp)*
* who may request a medical examination or opinion, see

  + [M21-1, Part IV, Subpart i, 1.A](void(0)), and
  + [M21-1, Part IV, Subpart i, 1.B](void(0))
* entering award data in VBMS, see the *VBMS User Guide*
* deciding issues that do not require a rating decision, see [M21-1, Part X, Subpart v, 1.C.1](void(0))
* general authorization and notification issues to include issuing notice of a proposed adverse action, see

  + [M21-1, Part VI, Subpart i, 1.A](void(0)), and
  + [M21-1, Part VI, Subpart i, 1.B](void(0))
* issuing decision notification, see [M21-1, Part VI, Subpart i, 1.B](void(0)), and
* use of the VBMS Package Manager to create packages for centralized printing under the Centralized Benefits Communications Management (CBCM) Program, see the Talent Management System course titled *Centralized Benefits Communications Management (CBCM) Phase One - Centralized Printing* (VA 4415957).

---

**1.A.4.c . Duties of RVSRs**

RVSR responsibilities include but are not limited to

* issuing timely, high-quality decisions on

  + AMA full grants by BVA, and
  + AMA BVA remands
* completing rating actions for all favorable BVA decisions
* requesting any complex medical opinions in accordance with [M21-1, Part IV, Subpart i, 2.A.7.b](void(0)), and
* following systems compliance policies/procedures.

***References***:  For more information on

* analyzing medical evidence to determine entitlement to VA benefits and preparing rating decisions, see

  + [M21-1, Part IV, Subpart ii, 2](void(0)), and
  + [M21-1, Part V, Subpart i, 1.A](void(0))
* reviewing the Compensation and Pension Record Interchange and Joint Legacy Viewer for recent and relevant treatment records, see

  + [M21-1, Part X, Subpart iii, 1.A.1.b](void(0)), and
  + [M21-1, Part III, Subpart ii, 2.B.1.i](void(0)), and
* preparing rating decisions in VBMS, see the *VBMS Rating User Guide*.

---

**1.A.4.d . Duties of DROs**

DRO responsibilities include but are not limited to

* issuing timely, high-quality decisions on

  + grants by BVA, and
  + HLRs
* identifying difference of opinion or duty to assist errors and returning impacted HLR issues for corrective action
* conducting informal conferences and updating controls
* following systems compliance policies/procedures
* participating in mentoring and training, and
* performing appeals processes, to include

  + holding formal hearings, and
  + performing *de novo* review of appeals.

***Reference***:  For more information on the duties of DROs and the DRO review process, see [M21-5, Chapter 7, Section C](void(0)).
