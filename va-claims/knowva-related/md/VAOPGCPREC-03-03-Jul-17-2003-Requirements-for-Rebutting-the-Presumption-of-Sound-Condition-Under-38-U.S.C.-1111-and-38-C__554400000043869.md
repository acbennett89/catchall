Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 03-03, Jul 17, 2003, Requirements for Rebutting the Presumption of Sound Condition Under 38 U.S.C. 1111 and 38 CFR 3.304
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Does 38 C.F.R. § 3.304(b), which provides that the presumption of sound condition may be rebutted by clear and unmistakable evidence that an injury or disease existed prior to service, conflict with 38 U.S.C. § 1111, which provides that the presumption of sound condition may be rebutted by clear and unmistakable evidence that an injury or disease existed prior to service “and was not aggravated by such service”?

Does 38 C.F.R. § 3.306(b), which provides that the presumption of aggravation under 38 U.S.C. § 1153 does not apply when a preexisting disability did not increase in severity during service, conflict with 38 U.S.C. § 1111?

**Impact on VBA**

38 CFR § 3.304(b) is invalid and should not be followed.  General Counsel (GC) held that the presumption of soundness provisions of 38 CFR § 3.304(b) are inconsistent with the implementing statute, 38 USC § 1111, insofar as section 3.304(b) states that the presumption of sound condition may be rebutted solely by clear and unmistakable evidence that a disease or injury existed prior to service.  To rebut the presumption of sound condition under 38 USC § 1111, VA must show by clear and unmistakable evidence both (1) that the disease or injury existed prior to service and (2) that the disease or injury was not aggravated by service.  The claimant is not required to show that the disease or injury increased in severity during service before VA’s duty under the second prong of this rebuttal standard attaches.  (Note: Subsections (1), (2), and (3) of 3.304(b) are still valid as these paragraphs do not conflict with the statute.)

GC also held that the provisions of 38 CFR § 3.306(b), providing that aggravation may not be conceded unless the preexisting condition increased in severity during service, are not inconsistent with the presumption of sound condition as defined in 38 USC § 1111.  Section 3.306(b) properly implements 38 USC § 1153, which provides that a preexisting injury or disease will be presumed to have been aggravated in service in cases where there was an increase in disability during service.  GC stated the requirement of an increase in disability in 38 CFR § 3.306(b) applies only to determinations concerning the presumption of aggravation under 38 USC § 1153, and does not apply to determinations concerning the presumption of sound condition under 38 USC § 1111.

**Summary on facts and findings**

This GC opinion resolves issues recently litigated before the U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims and Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit.  See Cotant v. Principi, 17 Vet. App. 116 (2003).  The statute provides the presumption of soundness, which applies to conditions not noted at entry into service, is rebutted only where clear and unmistakable evidence shows that a condition existed prior to service and that it was not aggravated by service.  Under § 3.304(b) as currently written, if a condition was not noted at entry but is shown by clear and unmistakable evidence to have existed prior thereto, the burden then shifts to the claimant to show that the condition had increased in severity during service.  VA could then deny the claim if the preponderance of the evidence showed no increase in severity during service.  Since the regulation imposes a requirement not authorized by 38 USC § 1111, it is invalid as written.  The practical effect of invalidating the regulation and following the statute is that the burden of proof never shifts to the claimant.  Under 38 USC § 1111, VA can only deny the claim if clear and unmistakable evidence supports a finding that the condition did not increase in severity during service, or that any increase is due to the natural progress of the condition.

Finding that 38 CFR § 3.306(b) applies only to determinations relating to the presumption of aggravation, GC held that § 3.306(b) does not conflict with the presumption of sound condition defined in 38 USC § 1111.  For example, if service connection is granted because VA was unable to rebut the presumption of sound condition, there is no need to consider whether the veteran is independently entitled to the presumption of aggravation under 38 CFR § 3.306(b).

The “presumption of sound condition” does not attach to a condition noted at the time of entry into service.  Although VAOPGCPREC 3 2003 does not specifically address that issue, the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims has consistently held that the “presumption of sound condition” under 38 USC § 1111 “only attaches where there has been an induction examination in which the later-complained of disability was not detected.”  Crowe v. Brown, 7 Vet. App. 238, 245 (1994); Bagby v. Derwinski, 1 Vet. App. 225, 227 (1991).  In other words, if a condition was noted at entry into service, e.g., a defect/diagnosis listed in an enlistment/induction report of medical examination, the “presumption of sound condition” never arises as to that condition.  See Verdon v. Brown, 8 Vet. App. 529, 535 (1996).

Conditions noted at the time of entry into service would be properly considered under the “presumption of aggravation” provisions found at 38 USC § 1153, as implemented by 38 CFR § 3.306(b).  This is consistent with the GC opinion, which stated that the presumptions of soundness and aggravation are independent factual presumptions requiring different burdens of proof, which generally do not apply to the same claims.

38 CFR § 3.304(b) and M21 1, Part VI, Chapter 7, para. 7.05a. should be revised pursuant to this GC opinion.
