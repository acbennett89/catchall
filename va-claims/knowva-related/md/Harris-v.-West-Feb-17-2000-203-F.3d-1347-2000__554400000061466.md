Decision Assessment Document
 Harris v. West, , Feb 17, 2000, 203 F.3d 1347 (2000)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that there is no absolute rule in the statute, regulation, or the case law requiring contemporaneous clinical evidence or recorded history evidence before the presumption of soundness can be rebutted.  A physician’s opinion based on statements made by the veteran about the preservice history of his condition may be sufficient to rebut the presumption of soundness notwithstanding the lack of contemporaneous clinical evidence or recorded history.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran appealed a decision of the United States Court of Veterans Appeals affirming a decision of the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA or Board) which denied entitlement to service connection for a hearing loss.  The veteran served in combat in Korea and stated that he was exposed to noise from artillery fire and that incoming artillery rounds had landed close to him, knocking him down from the concussion on several occasions.  Service medical records, including separation examination, showed normal hearing.  A bilateral high frequency hearing loss was first diagnosed in 1979.  His initial claim was denied in 1988, and, after several denials, he appealed to the BVA.  During a hearing, a cousin testified that the veteran's hearing was fine before he went to Korea, but it was necessary to yell at him after he returned.  The veteran also submitted lay statements indicating he suffered a noticeable hearing loss as a result of his service in Korea.  The veteran argued that under the provisions of section 1154(b), the lay statements he submitted were "satisfactory" evidence and should be accepted as sufficient proof of service connection unless rebutted by clear and convincing evidence.  The BVA denied the appeal, finding that the preponderance of the evidence was against the claim.  The Court affirmed the BVA decision and denied reconsideration on the basis that the BVA had found the lay evidence was not credible in view of the separation examination showing normal hearing, and, therefore, was an unsatisfactory predicate for application of section 1154(b).   On appeal to the Federal Circuit, the veteran argued that the BVA misinterpreted section 1154(b) and that the evaluation of his claim under a preponderance of the evidence standard was contrary to section 1154(b).
