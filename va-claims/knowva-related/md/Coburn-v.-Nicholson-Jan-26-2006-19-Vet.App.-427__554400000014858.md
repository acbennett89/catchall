Decision Assessment Document
 Coburn v. Nicholson, Jan 26, 2006, 19 Vet.App. 427
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that an appellant’s waiver of a remand under *Stegall v. West*, 11 Vet.App. 268 (1998), precludes remand by the Court for an RO’s failure to comply with the orders of a Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) remand.

Concerning the Board’s rejection of an ostensibly favorable medical report, the Court held that reliance upon a veteran’s statements renders a medical report incredible only if the Board has rejected the statements.  A medical report/opinion cannot be rejected solely because it was based upon the veteran’s history without providing reasons and bases as to the credibility of the statements.  Also, the Court, while cautioning the Board that it cannot use its own medical judgment in rejecting medical opinions, noted that nexus evidence may be returned for clarification or a new report when it is rejected due to being confusing or incomplete.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting Manual revision.  This case may serve as an outstanding training tool for RVSRs in evaluating medical nexus evidence and determining when to return an examination for clarification.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran served from January 1954 to January 1956.  All service records were presumed destroyed by the 1973 fire.  A claim for service connection for bilateral hip and leg injuries was filed in February 1994.  In March 2001, the Board, finding that the case contained no medical opinion statement, remanded for an orthopedic examination.

An examination was conducted in October 2002.  The examiner, noting that the claims file and submitted medical history were reviewed, opined that it was “at least as likely as not” that the hip and leg problems were related to an in-service telephone pole incident.  The conclusion was based upon the veteran’s denial of any other history of trauma.

The Board rejected the medical opinion as incompetent because it was based upon the veteran’s self-reported medical history and the examiner, although stating that the claims file was reviewed, appearing to ignore numerous left knee injuries after service.

The Secretary argued for remand under *Stegall* on the grounds that the VA orthopedic examination failed to include review of the claims file, which was mandated by the 2001 remand.  The veteran, arguing for reversal, responded that he was satisfied with the examination and was not seeking a *Stegall* remand, which confers rights upon the veteran, not the Secretary.

The Court, agreeing that *Stegall* confers rights only upon the veteran, declined to order a *Stegall* remand in view of the veteran’s satisfaction with the VA examination.  However, the Court found the Board’s reasons and bases to be inadequate, which are outside of the purview of *Stegall*.

The Court, in rejecting the Board’s rationale in dismissing the medical opinion that the examiner had not reviewed the claims file, found that the examiner specifically stated that the claims file was reviewed and also noted pertinent past medical history and findings.  The Court held that the Board should have discussed whether the examiner’s statement that the veteran denied any other injuries constituted a denial of any post-service injuries or post-service injuries not in the claims file.  The Board had a duty to discuss those aspects of the report that clearly showed a conclusion different than that of the examiner.  It was noted that the duty to render adequate reasons and bases is heightened when service records are unavailable.

The Board was found to have erred in not rendering a finding concerning the credibility of the veteran’s statements to the examiner.  Furthermore, the rejection of the medical report (the only one of record) left the record without medical nexus information; thus, the Board also erred in not explaining why it could proceed to render a decision when no medical nexus evidence was present as in 2001.  An adequate statement of reasons or bases must be rendered if a decision is made without medical nexus information. Remand was ordered.
