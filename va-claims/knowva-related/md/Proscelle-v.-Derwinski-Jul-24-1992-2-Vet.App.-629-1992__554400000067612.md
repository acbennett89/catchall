Decision Assessment Document
 Proscelle v. Derwinski, Jul 24, 1992, 2 Vet.App. 629 (1992)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that because the record before the Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) contained no evidence of the then-current level of the veteran’s service-connected disability, fulfillment of the VA assistive duty in this case includes the conduct of a thorough and contemporaneous medical examination, one which takes into account the records of prior medical treatment, so that the evaluation of the claimed disability is a fully informed one.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, I.1.C.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran suffered a fracture of the right maxilla in service in June 1956.  Service connection for residuals of that fracture evaluated as 0% disabling was established.  In January 1987, the Board upheld the denial of a claim for an increased evaluation.  At that time, the Board also noted that the veteran had filed a claim for service connection for a nervous disorder, but did not decide the claim.  In 1989, the veteran submitted another claim for an increase in the disability evaluation.  VA outpatient records reflected treatment in 1987 for schizophrenia, which was reported to have had its onset 20 years earlier.  On his VAF 1-9, he stated he believed his disability had increased in severity and that he rated a higher disability.  He also stated that he was unemployed and requested that VA consider unemployability as a basis for his claim.  At a personal hearing, he testified that the SC maxillary fracture had caused a nervous condition which had caused him problems in retaining employment.  The BVA upheld the denial of an increased evaluation based upon the outpatient treatment records.

After review of the record, the Court concluded that the BVA erred in failing to assist the veteran in developing the facts pertinent to his claim for an increased rating for the SC condition, and in failing to adjudicate the veteran's claims for SC for a psychiatric condition as secondary to the maxillary condition and for a total disability rating based on unemployability.  The Court held that the claim for an increased rating on appeal is not reopened claim subject to Manio because the veteran claimed that his SC disability had undergone an increase in severity since that prior claim.  The current claim is thus a new claim.  It is not subject to 38 U.S.C. 7104(b) prohibiting reopening of previously disallowed claims except upon new and material evidence.  The veteran only needs to submit a well grounded claim.  Since the Board had no record of the current level of the disability, its duty was to assist the veteran by conducting a thorough and contemporaneous medical examination.  The Court also pointed out that the hearing officer was required to suggest to the veteran that he submit, or seek to have VA obtain, medical evidence of the then-current level of the SC disability.  He/she was also required to suggest to the veteran the submission of evidence that would be advantageous for his claim that his current psychiatric disability was causally related to his SC disability.
