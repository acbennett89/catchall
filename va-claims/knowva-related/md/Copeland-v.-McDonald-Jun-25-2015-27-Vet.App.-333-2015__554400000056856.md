Decision Assessment Document
 Copeland v. McDonald, Jun 25, 2015, 27 Vet.App. 333 (2015)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that 38 C.F.R. 4.71a, Diagnostic Code (DC) 5284, does not apply to the eight other foot conditions specifically listed in section 4.71a.  Therefore, the listed conditions must be rated under the specific diagnostic codes and cannot be rated by analogy under DC 5284.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Manual was revised at III.iv.4.A.3 to comply with the Court’s decision.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran underwent a QTC examination in October 2010 that resulted in a diagnosis of bilateral pes planus, “not due to injury or trauma,” as well as hallux valgus “as a result of a progression of the previous diagnosis.”  On the basis of the examination, the regional office (RO), in December 2013, increased the 30 percent disability evaluation to 50 percent, effective October 30, 2013.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in February 2014, awarded a 50 percent evaluation for the bilateral pes planus from April 8, 2008.  The Board considered whether increased ratings were warranted under other diagnostic codes to include DC 5278 (claw foot), DC 5280 (hallux valgus), and DC 5284 (foot injuries, other).  The Board determined that DC 5280 was potentially applicable in light of the hallux valgus diagnosis; however, a separate disability evaluation was not warranted as the hallux valgus was only “slight to moderate without surgical resection.”  The Board also determined that DC 5284 was potentially applicable, but a higher or separate evaluation was unwarranted under the diagnostic code.

The Veteran argued before the Court that the Board erred in failing to grant separate 30 percent ratings under DC 5284.  He contended that the Board’s selection of DC 5276 over DC 5284 was erroneous as his service-connected foot disabilities were not all contemplated under a single diagnostic code.  Finally, he argued that rating his pes planus and hallux valgus under 5284 is not rating by analogy, as both codes are potentially appropriate diagnostic codes, and that DC 5284 is applicable to all foot conditions as a “catch-all.”

The Court, in addressing whether the veteran’s diagnosed pes planus and hallux valgus may be rated under diagnostic codes other than the specific diagnostic codes for these conditions, relied upon its case law, and reiterated its previous holdings that when a condition is specifically listed in the Rating Schedule, it may not be rated by analogy.

The Court stated that the veteran having been diagnosed with two separate conditions did not change the analysis, as VA must apply the diagnostic codes that specifically pertain to the listed conditions and determine the appropriate disability ratings.   Since both pes planus and hallux valgus were listed in the Rating Schedule, the Court concluded that the Board properly considered whether increased evaluations were warranted under both DC 5276 and DC 5280.

The Court rejected the veteran’s arguments that rating his condition under DC 5284 would not be an analogous rating, holding that DC 5284 is one of nine diagnostic codes that pertain to musculoskeletal disabilities of the foot and application of it to the other eight listed foot conditions would essentially render those diagnostic codes redundant.

The Court, in affirming the Board’s decision, held that, as a matter of  law, DC 5284 does not apply to the eight foot conditions specifically listed in § 4.71a, and rating the veteran’s listed conditions under that diagnostic code would constitute an impermissible rating by analogy.
