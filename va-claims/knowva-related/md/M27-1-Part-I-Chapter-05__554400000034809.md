## 5.01 General Guidance for Processing Correspondence [Back to top](#toc) Change Date: April 03, 2026

1. ### The Importance of Correspondence

   VA routinely communicates with customers in writing. Correspondence from VA to all customers must be clear, concise, and aid effective communication and decision making. The reader must be able to clearly understand the message in a single reading, and the correspondence must be free of errors in formatting, organization, style, substance, and grammar.

   Prior to sending out any correspondence to a customer, the employee must complete a thorough review for quality and correctness. Employees must verify the current mailing address and/or email address of the Veteran/beneficiary/contact for accuracy. Contact information must be updated in the VA system according to M27-1, I.3.10. If a system update cannot be completed, correspondence must include the correct contact information and system notes must clearly indicate the reason the update was not completed.
2. ### Systems Used to Generate Correspondence

   The following systems are used to generate VBA correspondence letters to customers (the list may not be all inclusive):

   * Customer Relationship Management/Unified Desktop-Optimized (CRM/UD-O)
   * Veterans Benefits Management System (VBMS)
   * Personal Computer-Generated Letters (PCGL)
3. ### General Rules for Effective Correspondence

   All correspondence generated by VA must:

   * provide complete, accurate, and understandable information
   * consider the reader's point of view
   * be professional, sincere, and empathetic
   * have proper grammar, tone, and sentence structure, and
   * be free of VA slang.
4. ### Formatting of Written Correspondence

   All official written correspondence must contain the following:

   * VA seal and generic heading
   * date the correspondence is generated
   * reply/reference section
   * employee ID with station number
   * correct salutation with the correct name of the inquirer
     + Public Contact Representatives (PCRs) must not alter the salutation of CRM/UD-O generated correspondence.
     + For non-CRM/UD-O generated correspondence, PCRs must use “Dear” as the salutation.
   * generic “RO Director” closing, to include VA contact information
   * use of 12-point Times New Roman or Arial font type and 1-inch margins
   * appropriate formatting for visually impaired inquirer, if applicable
   * courtesy copied to designated representative, if applicable, and
   * enclosures listed, if applicable.
5. ### Formatting of Electronic Correspondence

   All official electronic correspondence must contain the following:

   * date of response
   * employee ID with station number
   * correct salutation with the correct name of the inquirer
     + PCRs must not alter the salutation of CRM/UD-O generated correspondence.
     + For non-CRM/UD-O generated correspondence, PCRs must use “Dear” as the salutation.
   * generic “RO Director” closing, to include VA contact information
   * appropriately formatted for visually impaired inquirer, if applicable
   * courtesy copied to designated representative, if applicable, and
   * enclosures listed, if applicable.

   Exception: Ask VA (AVA) responders are not subject to the above requirements and must utilize existing formatting within the AVA program.
6. ### Preparing Customer-Requested Letters for Visually Impaired

   For correspondence purposes, a visually impaired

   * Veteran is any Veteran
     + with visual impairment (SC or non-SC) for which VA has assigned a disability rating of at least 70 percent, or
     + who has expressly requested that VA accommodate a visual impairment (whether VA has evaluated the disability or not) with modified correspondence and communication methods, and
   * survivor is any survivor claimant
     + with visual impairment that corresponds to a need for aid and attendance (A&A), or
     + who has expressly requested that VA accommodate a visual impairment (whether VA has evaluated the disability or not) with modified correspondence and communication methods.

   *Note*: Claims processors are responsible for ensuring that visually impaired claimants' claims folders and corporate records are appropriately flashed for ease of recognition.

   *Reference*: For more information on requirements and recognition of visually impaired claimants see [M21-1, X.i.1.8](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000177939/M21-1-Part-X-Subpart-i-Chapter-1-Priority-Processing-Programs-and-Special-Requirements).

   When preparing requested correspondence for a customer with a visual impairment, the following guidelines must be incorporated:

   * use either Times New Roman or Arial font
   * font size must be largely 18-point
   * the width of a column within any table, chart, or box must be adjusted to fit the larger font

   *Note*: After adjusting, if the table, chart, or box cannot accommodate the larger font, then utilize the largest font for those characters within it that will fit neatly.

   * lines must be 1.5 spaces apart to the extent possible
   * the header and footer may generally be left as generated unless it can be easily altered as indicated above, and
   * the letter's default one-inch margin, block-paragraph style, and left justification must not usually be altered.

   Do not increase the size of the address section of the letter, as the window in standard envelopes may not be able to accommodate the increased size.

   *Note*: These print parameters are based on the article, *Tips for Making Print More Readable*, published by the American Foundation for the Blind.
7. ### System Notes

   System notes are required when correspondence is generated and must:

   * document actions taken (i.e. type of correspondence or form(s) prepared, etc.),
   * explain any discrepancy between correspondence and the system of record (i.e. correspondence was sent to a different mailing/email address of record, etc.), and
   * be clear and contain enough information to be understood by an employee other than its author (i.e. include edits made to a letter, etc.). PCRs are encouraged to include the method that the correspondence was sent (i.e. letter sent by fax, forms sent by email, letter and forms provided in-person, etc.
8. ### Sending Correspondence and Forms

   When requested by a first party, correspondence may be mailed, emailed, or faxed to any location identified by the first party requestor after ID protocol requirements have been met.

   When requested by a third party (or the first party if basic ID Protocol has not been completed), correspondence containing a Veteran or beneficiary's personally identifiable information (PII) may be sent only to the mailing address or email address of record. Correspondence redacted of PII may be sent to any mailing address, email address, or fax number identified by the third-party requestor. PCRs must also redact a Fiduciary's PII if an incompetent Veteran/beneficiary requests a benefit letter be sent to a third party.

   *Reference*: For additional information about ID Protocol see [M27-1, I.3.06](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001060/content/554400000034095/M27-1-Part-I-Chapter-03)

   PCRs must offer to send documents (including blank forms) and correspondence by email, downloading from the internet (when applicable), fax, and by physical mail. PCRs must provide instructions on locating forms/documents, and return information (mailing address, fax, or online submission information) or advise the requestor that the return information is on the correspondence. PCRs must offer to provide steps to obtain and utilize VA.gov account features (e.g. letter downloading) when those options are desirable by the requestor.

   *Note*: While PCRs must offer all methods to send correspondence, they must promote sending correspondence by the most expeditious method possible (i.e. email, downloading from the internet, or fax). PCRs must only send correspondence by mail if the other options are unavailable or undesirable by the requestor, and must prioritize using Package Manager when there is a VBMS eFolder.

   * Email
     + PCRs must send an encrypted email with the secured email message template from a “No Reply” mailbox when sending correspondence with PII. PCRs must advise the recipient how to view the encrypted email.
       - Follow guidance in [Encrypted Email of eFolder Documents & PCR-Generated Letters Job Aid](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001001/content/554400000155990/Encrypted-Email-of-eFolder-Documents-PCR-Generated-Letters-Job-Aid)
     + PCRs may utilize the unencrypted “Send Forms” service request functionality in CRM/UD-O when sending only blank forms, fact sheets, or other internet references (i.e. links to information on VA.gov).
   * Internet/Download
     + Requestors that have a VA.gov account can access and download DD-214s, decision notification letters, and benefit letters.
     + Various claim forms may be completed and submitted online.
     + VA forms and fact sheets are available for download from VA.gov.
   * Fax
     + PCRs may fax to the first party requestor or to a third party after confirmation of first party permission.
     + Correspondence that does not contain PII may be faxed to any requestor.
     + PCRs must utilize Right Fax or ECFax.
   * Physical Mail
     + If the individual to which the request pertains has a VBMS record, PCRs must utilize Package Manager to send correspondence to centralized printing.
       - If PCRs determine during the interaction that physical mail is no longer required, PCRs must delete any unsent packages, recipients, and/or distribution information created by the PCR from Package Manager. PCRs must update system notes as applicable to reflect delivery method of documents.
       - If correspondence is accidentally sent to an individual from the wrong Veteran's e-folder, PCRs must:
         * [submit a suppression request](https://forms.office.com/Pages/ResponsePage.aspx?id=Ixtf6a-r7kWCHberJRqzv_sYk1EACSNNjVt0P92vVFRUOU4yTUswSUNPQlJZWExQMjlPRzRDV0ZZVC4u) before 9:00 p.m. ET the day the errant letter was sent:
           + Veteran's name and file number
           + UUID Number, and
           + Which letters are needing to be suppressed.
         * delete the letter after the letter has been confirmed to be suppressed.
     + If the individual to which the request pertains does not have a VBMS record, PCRs must enter a request in the [Referral SharePoint site](https://dvagov.sharepoint.com/sites/VBABASqualitytraining/bastrain/data/SiteAssets/NDR/coordRef/coordinatorReferral.aspx) for the physical mail packet to be generated and sent by local RO personnel. PCRs must advise the mail will be generated and sent within two (2) business days.
       - Public Contact Team (PCT) personnel must review the submission within two (2) business days and either generate the mail or return to the National Contact Center (NCC) for correction.
       - NCC designated management officials (DMOs) must review returned requests and take appropriate follow up action within two (2) business days.
     + PCRs must not create or use test/dummy VBMS records to send physical mail packets through Package Manager.
     + PCRs must follow instructions in the Centralized Printing Job Aid for creating, sending, and deleting packages that are no longer required.

   *Note*: Do *not* release internal procedural information such as Knowledge Management (KM) System articles or guidance issued via email; however, the following information may be sent:

   * summary pamphlets
   * VA benefits information, and
   * general entitlement information.

## 5.02 Signature Authority for Correspondence [Back to top](#toc) Change Date: September 30, 2023

1. ### Correspondence Prepared by NCC, FCC, and PCT

   Correspondence prepared by the National Call Centers (NCCs), AVA responders, Fiduciary Contact Center (FCC), and RO PCT will be released under the authority granted to the NCC Manager (NCCM), FCC Manager (FCCM), and the Veterans Service Center Manager (VSCM) or, in the absence of the NCCM, FCCM, or VSCM, the person designated as acting manager.

   Correspondence prepared and released by the NCC, FCC, AVA responders, and PCT will be prepared with the standard RO header and signature block.
2. ### Special Signature Provisions

   If there are compelling reasons, such as a special correspondence response or an escalated issue, that the NCCM, FCCM, or VSCM feels requires the signature of the NCCM, FCCM, or VSCM, the director may authorize NCC, FCC, or PCT correspondence to be signed with the name and title of the NCCM, FCCM, or VSCM instead of the standard RO signature block.

## 5.03 Handling Incoming Special Correspondence [Back to top](#toc) Change Date: April 03, 2026

1. ### Definitions for Special Correspondence

   *Special Correspondence* is mail, to include email to an RO's Congressional email box, which requires expedited processing, control, and response due to the subject matter and/or the position of the correspondent.

   Examples of special correspondence include:

   * congressional mail and email
   * White House mail, to include referrals from VBA's Office of Client Relations
   * mail from national headquarters of service organizations
   * private attorney correspondence
   * Office of the Inspector General correspondence, and
   * Office of General Counsel correspondence.

   *Simple Special Correspondence* can be easily answered with information that is readily available in the system of records and must have a final response provided within 10 business days from receipt of the request.

   *Complex Special Correspondence* requires extensive information or research, can't be easily answered with information that is readily available in the system of records, and must have a final response provided within 30 business days from the receipt of the request.

   *Notes*:

   * The first business day following receipt of the correspondence item is day one of the business day requirement for final response.
   * If special correspondence is assigned with a designated due date that is less than the standard timeframe (i.e. 10 days or 30 days), the requested correspondence item must be completed by the assigned due date.
2. ### Processing Special Correspondence

   The mail processing activity will identify mail that must be immediately routed to the appropriate team for expedited processing.

   All employees handling special correspondence will maintain appropriate controls to ensure prompt reply or acknowledgement.

   Inquiries pertaining to priority claims must be processed expeditiously. If the claim does not have the appropriate Corporate Flash representing one of VA's priorities, the receiving RO is responsible for inputting the flash(es) to ensure that the claim is distributed by National Work Queue (NWQ). Priority processing criteria are outlined in [M21-1, X.i.1](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000177939/M21-1-Part-X-Subpart-i-Chapter-1-Priority-Processing-Programs-and-Special-Requirements).

   The receiving station of jurisdiction must respond to the inquiry, regardless of the station currently working the claim. All systems (VBMS, CRM/UD-O, etc.) must be reviewed to respond to the inquiry and provide the status of the claim.

   ROs must not request claim jurisdiction from the NWQ or request claims processing actions from specialized processing sites when providing a response, unless claims processing actions are required to adequately resolve the inquiry or expedited/priority processing is required. ROs must collaborate with special mission sites (i.e. BEST sites, etc.), as needed, for a response; ROs must not delay response by denying jurisdiction of the inquiry.

   ROs must use the [VBA Standard Operating Procedures - Congressional Inquiry for Status of Claim or Decision Review](void(0)) to provide standard language and claim specific language paragraphs when responding to special correspondence inquiries. When responding to a claim status inquiry, the status of any development (pending and completed) must be provided (i.e., Records from doctors, Federal custodians, etc.) as well as the claim lifecycle. ROs have discretion to provide additional information where warranted.

   When employees required to use CRM/UD-O are unable to use CRM/UD-O to process or respond to the special correspondence (e.g. response is via email received to mailbox), they must still use CRM/UD-O to log the interaction and/or document notes.

   Employees must follow guidance in [M21-1 II.i.2.B.1.a.](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174859/M21-1-Part-II-Subpart-i-Chapter-2-Section-B-Recording-the-Date-of-Receipt-and-Handling-Signatures-and-Outdated-Forms) to date stamp documents with the date of receipt when they are received with special correspondence.

   Employees must categorize the CRM/UD-O interaction according to M27-1, I.3.04.b. and document special correspondence generated in system notes according to M27-1 I.5.01.g.

## 5.04 Handling Various Types of Incoming Routine Correspondence [Back to top](#toc) Change Date: April 03, 2026

1. ### Definitions for Routine Correspondence

   *Routine correspondence* is correspondence from a member of the public not identified as evidence or a claim.

   A routine correspondence final response must be provided to the customer within 10 business days.

   *Note*: The first business day following receipt of the correspondence item is day one of the business day requirement for final response.
2. ### Processing Routine Correspondence

   Employees completing the action in CRM/UD-O must not establish or clear an EP 400 for routine correspondence.

   Exceptions:

   * Employees unable to utilize CRM/UD-O to process or respond to the correspondence (i.e. housing authority request forms) must establish an EP 400 for routine correspondence. Employees must utilize the “one-time PCLR” function to establish and clear the 400 EP with a single command only when a customer's correspondence is received and responded to at the same time. PCRs must still utilize CRM/UD-O to log the interaction and document notes.
     + A full establishment of the EP 400 will occur if the response is not able to be provided to the customer either during the interaction or in a timely response to a National Contact Center referral, and/or is brokered from a centralized mail hub site.

   Employees must follow guidance in [M21-1 II.i.2.B.1.a.](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174859/M21-1-Part-II-Subpart-i-Chapter-2-Section-B-Recording-the-Date-of-Receipt-and-Handling-Signatures-and-Outdated-Forms) to date stamp documents with the date of receipt when they are received with routine correspondence.

   Employees must categorize CRM/UD-O according to M27-1, I.3.04.b. and document correspondence generated in system notes according to M27-1 I.5.01.g.

   *Notes*:

   * Routine correspondence requests that are received and processed by a centralized mail hub site will be established as an EP 400 with the Brokered - Internal special issue and then brokered to the Regional Office (RO) of jurisdiction that is listed in the Award Station field of the Veteran's Profile in VBMS.
     + If an EP 400 is assigned to an RO that cannot complete the request based in information available in the VA record, employees must broker it to the RO of jurisdiction based on the [VBA Jurisdiction Lookup Tool](http://vbacoweba5.vba.va.gov/bl/21/SOJ/default.asp).
   * ROs must not request claim jurisdiction from the National Work Queue (NWQ) or request claims processing actions from specialized processing sites unless claims processing actions are required to adequately resolve the inquiry.
   * Do not establish an EP 400 for correspondence when there is a request for general information or information not associated with a claim number. See M27-1, I.5.04.e.

   Use the table below to properly manage routine correspondence:

## 5.05 Types of Customer-Requested Outgoing Routine Correspondence [Back to top](#toc) Change Date: October 2, 2026

1. ### Benefit Verification Letters

   Customers routinely request benefit verification or general “amounts” letters. These are letters verifying the monthly dollar amount a Veteran or beneficiary receives.

   A request for proof of VA disability benefits may also include the type of disability and percentage, which are both protected under the Privacy Act.

   Benefit verification letters generally verify the gross amount, not the net amount received. Gross amount refers to the amount the Veteran or beneficiary is entitled to each month before deductions. Net amount refers to the amount that is paid out by VA after deductions.

   Sometimes, a beneficiary will not receive their full entitlement amount. Some common examples of this are:

   * a debt repayment
   * an insurance premium taken out, or
   * an apportionment is paid out for a dependent.

   *Note*: Deductions are still considered income. When a customer is asking for a benefit verification letter, use the gross entitlement amount, or both the gross and net amounts.

   If a customer requests a benefit verification letter for the total yearly amount received, use the following language:

   “Our records indicate that for calendar year XXXX (i.e., appropriate year), the total amount of benefits issued was $XX, XXX.XX.”

   When calculating annual income for a calendar year, use 12 months at one rate to calculate the total annual benefit, unless there has been a payment rate change other than a cost-of-living increase (COLA). In those cases, you will need to calculate the number of months paid at each rate, considering the check received on December 31st as the payment amount received in January.

   Unauthorized third parties may request written verification of the gross benefit amount. In this instance, redact all Veteran/beneficiary PII except his/her name.

   When generating a benefit letter, PCRs must verify the mailing address of record and update VA systems as necessary.
2. ### Amount Letters to Estranged Spouses (For Social Security Purposes)

   On occasion, VA receives inquiries from estranged spouses requesting a “breakdown” of benefits letter for Social Security purposes.

   Typically, this occurs when VA is paying benefits for an estranged spouse on a pension award and the Social Security Administration needs to know what portion of the Veteran's benefit is attributable to the spouse.

   If the estranged spouse is not receiving benefits under an apportionment award, do not provide this information.

   If the requester indicates he/she needs this information for Social Security Administration purposes, advise the requester to contact the Social Security Administration and have that agency request the needed information directly.

   Explain to the requester that our inability to provide this information is due to privacy regulations.
3. ### Service Verification Letters

   Sometimes used in lieu of a DD Form 214, *Certificate of Release or Discharge from Active Duty*, or other discharge document, service verification letters outline the specifics of a Veteran's military service and certify the information to be accurate.

   For customers asking for a copy of a DD Form 214, always ask if a service verification letter will suffice.

   To generate a service verification letter, the following information must be verified in VA systems:

   * all the service information including Entry on Duty (EOD) and Release from Active Duty (RAD) dates, branch of service, character of discharge, and separation reason,
   * character of service must show "HON" or "UHC,"

   *Note*: The service verification letter states that the Veteran is “honorably” discharged. For VA and Office of Personnel Management (OPM) purposes, discharges of Honorable (HON) and Under Honorable Conditions (UHC) count as being “honorably” discharged.

   * branch of service code is not "ARNG" or "ANG,"
   * separation reason is “Satisfactory” or "SAT," and
   * if the Veteran's RAD is prior to June 1, 1968, there is a “Yes” or "Y" in the Verified field of VBMS Veteran > Military Service, or
   * if the Veteran's RAD is June 1, 1968, or later there is a “Yes” or “Y” in either the Verified field of VBMS Veteran > Military Service or System-verified Service field of VBMS Veteran Summary (right hand pane) > Military Service.
4. ### Summary of Benefits Letters

   This letter should be utilized to provide verification for eligibility determination for local, state, and other benefits.

   To generate a summary of benefits letter, the following information must be verified in VA

   systems:

   * all the service information including Entry on Duty (EOD) and Release from Active Duty (RAD) dates, branch of service, character of discharge, and separation reason,
   * character of service must show "HON" or "UHC,"

   *Note*: The service verification letter states that the Veteran is “honorably” discharged. For VA and Office of Personnel Management (OPM) purposes, discharges of Honorable (HON) and Under Honorable Conditions (UHC) count as being “honorably” discharged.

   * branch of service code is not "ARNG" or "ANG,"
   * separation reason is “Satisfactory” or "SAT," and
   * service-connected disability status,

   **Note:** If Veteran is in receipt of Individual Unemployability (IU) benefits, rating must reflect service-connected rating percentage. PCR must not edit the letter to say 100%. PCRs must ensure the appropriate selection is indicated in the Summary of Benefits Letter and Future Exam Fields under Entitled to a Higher Level of Disability due to IU.

   If the customer indicates that the Summary of Benefits Letter does not contain the necessary information to verify eligibility for a local, state, or other benefit, and if the letter cannot be modified to include the required information, follow steps in M27-1 I.5.05.h.

   *Note*: This letter replaces VA Form 20-5455, Statement from Benefit Payment Records, which is no longer available.
5. ### Civil Service Preference Letters

   By law, Veterans who are disabled or who served on active duty in the U.S. armed forces during certain specified time periods or in military campaigns are entitled to preference over others when hiring from competitive lists of eligible candidates, and in retention during a reduction in force (RIF).

   The following civil service preference letters are available for use:

   * non-service connected (NSC) pension
   * 30 percent disability or greater
   * at least 10 percent, but less than 30 percent disability
   * less than 10 percent disability
   * surviving spouse
   * spouse of 100 percent disabled Veteran
   * parent of deceased Veteran, and
   * parent of 100 percent disabled Veteran.

   If the Veteran/relative is applying for a Federal job, Form SF-15, *Application for 10-Point Preference (To Be Used by Veterans and Their Relatives)*, must be attached to the Civil Service preference letter.

   *Notes*:

   * Because provisions of the Privacy Act cover the release of this information, the Veteran must provide consent before a preference letter may be sent to a third person. However, the letter may be released directly to the Veteran.
   * For more information on Civil Service Preference Letters and eligibility criteria, see M21-1 XIII.ii.2.A.
6. ### Disability Percent Letters or Civil Service Preference Letters Requested by Veterans Returned to Active Duty

   For Veterans requesting a disability percent letter or Civil Service preference letter (CSPL) and the Veteran's record reflects a terminated status because he/she returned to active duty, do not send any letter stating the Veteran is evaluated at X% or eligible to receive compensation at a particular dollar amount.

   According to [OPM guidance](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/veterans-services/vet-guide-for-hr-professionals/), to receive a preference, the Veteran must have been discharged or released from active duty in the Armed Forces under honorable or general conditions. Therefore, if the Veteran is still on active duty, no CSPL can be provided.

   Advise a Servicemember who is on active duty and wants this type of letter that VA can provide the letter, if appropriate, after he/she has been released from active duty.
7. ### Commissary Letters

   Unlimited exchange and commissary store privileges are available to:

   * honorably discharged Veterans with a service-connected disability rated at 100 percent, to include a rating of Individual Unemployability (IU)
   * unremarried surviving spouses and dependents of 100 percent service-connected Veterans
   * retired members of the armed forces
   * recipients of the Medal of Honor, and their dependents and orphans,
   * Purple Heart recipients
   * former Prisoners of War
   * all Veterans with service-connected disabilities ratings of 0 to 90%, and
   * individuals approved and designated as the primary family caregivers of eligible Veterans under the Department of Veterans Affairs Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers.

   The following commissary letters are available for use for 100%-rated Veterans and their surviving spouse:

   * no future exam
   * future exam, or
   * surviving spouse.

   Include DD Form 1172, *Application for Uniformed Services Identification Card DEERS Enrollment*, as an attachment with this letter.

   *Notes*:

   * VA provides certification of total disability.
   * National Guard members, Reservists, and their dependents may also be eligible if they meet the above criteria.
   * Veterans eligible solely under the *Purple Heart and Disabled Veterans Equal Access Act of 2018* who are eligible to obtain a Veteran Health Identification Card must obtain a VHIC from the VA to facilitate Department of Defense and Coast Guard installation and privilege access. In this case, PCRs must refer Veterans in need of a VHIC to the Health Eligibility Center (HEC) at 877-222-8387 and caregivers with questions to their local Caregivers Support Coordinator (CSC).
8. ### Referral for State-specific Letter or Pension Aid & Attendance/Housebound Breakdown Letter

   If a customer is requesting a state-specific benefit letter or a Pension Aid & Attendance/Housebound breakdown letter, and no NCC Public Contact Representative (PCR)-generated letter will suffice (i.e. Summary of Benefit Letter), NCC PCRs must:

   * create a [Contact Referral SharePoint](https://dvagov.sharepoint.com/sites/VBABASqualitytraining/bastrain/data/SiteAssets/NDR/coordRef/coordinatorReferral.aspx) site entry to request the letter, and
   * enter a note in the system documenting the action taken, type of letter requested, and address/fax number/email address where the letter must be sent.

   Public Contact Teams must:

   * complete the request within two (2) business days and enter the disposition of the request in the Referral SharePoint site
   * add a system note documenting the actions taken, and
   * reject requests sent to the wrong regional office by selecting This Request Routed to Incorrect SOJ.

   National Call Center (NCC) Designated Management Officials (DMOs) must:

   * review rejected requests sent via email to NCC mailbox (DMOs may need to review call recordings or contact the requestor, as needed, to determine the appropriate request/regional office)
   * confirm via button in SharePoint review was complete and appropriate actions taken, and
   * submit a new referral request, as appropriate.

   VA Form 27-0820 is a series of seven forms. The purpose of the VA Form 27-0820 (series) is to:

   * record discussions involving potentially controversial matters between VA employees and claimants, representatives of claimants, or other persons
   * record evidentiary information or facts received by a VA employee that may supplement the record
   * request action be taken in response to information provided by the customer, or
   * document information provided to the customer or actions taken by the VA employee.

   *Note**s*:

   * Do not use VA Form 27-0820 to document information that must be submitted in writing by the customer.

## 5.08 Requests for Copies of Correspondence in the eFolder [Back to top](#toc) Change Date: April 03, 2026

1. ### What Can Be Released from the eFolder

   NCC and PCT PCRs can resend copies of correspondence VA previously sent to the Veteran/beneficiary when it is available in the eFolder. Copies may be sent via mail, fax, or encrypted email to a first-party requestor without a written request from the Veteran/beneficiary.

## 5.09 PCT Handling of Claim Related Evidence and Forms [Back to top](#toc) Change Date: April 03, 2026

1. ### Receiving Claim Related Evidence and Forms

   PCT PCRs must:

   * Review claim related evidence and forms (including original documents) in accordance with [M21-1 II.i.2.A](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174858/M21-1-Part-II-Subpart-i-Chapter-2-Section-A-Process-Overview-for-Screening-Mail).
   * Accept claim related evidence and forms and record the date of receipt according to [M21-1 II.i.2.B](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174859/M21-1-Part-II-Subpart-i-Chapter-2-Section-B-Recording-the-Date-of-Receipt-and-Handling-Signatures-and-Outdated-Forms).
     + Ensure that signatures are acceptable according to M21-1 II.i.2.B.2.
     + Ensure that alternate signatures are acceptable according to M21-1 II.i.2.B.3.

   PCT PCRs must use CRM/UD-O to log the interaction and complete updates (i.e. name, date of birth, contact information, dependents, submit an intent to file (ITF), etc.), as necessary.

   If a Veteran or claimant provides a completed physical form during a PCT interview that can be processed through CRM/UD-O (i.e. 21-0538, 21-686c, 21-0966, etc.) the action must be completed through CRM/UD-O (i.e. generate and route the VA Form 27-0820, update dependent status, submit the ITF, etc.). The forms or evidence provided must still be date stamped.

   PCT PCRs are not required to take actions in CRM/UD-O if a Veteran or claimant is visiting PCT to drop off claim related evidence or forms and refuses the PCTs offer to complete the action in CRM/UD-O. In this case, the evidence or forms must still be date stamped.
