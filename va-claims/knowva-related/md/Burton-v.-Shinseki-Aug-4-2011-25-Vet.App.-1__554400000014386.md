Decision Assessment Document
 Burton v. Shinseki, Aug 4, 2011, 25 Vet.App. 1
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that VA’s interpretation of [38 C.F.R. § 4.59](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=ff7eda9fa76eedfec4024b09d4db6818&node=se38.1.4_159&rgn=div8) not being limited to arthritis claims is not inconsistent with the regulation or otherwise plainly erroneous.

The Court also held that separate evaluations may not be assigned for loss of flexion and abduction of the shoulder.  Because these limitations arise from one disability, separate evaluations would violate [38 C.F.R. § 4.14](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=ff7eda9fa76eedfec4024b09d4db6818&node=se38.1.4_114&rgn=div8).

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The regional office (RO), in January 2003, granted service connection for a left shoulder disability and assigned a noncompensable evaluation from August 28, 2002, on the basis of a November 2002 VA examination showing full range of motion with no pain.  A left shoulder surgery on May 23, 2003, entitled the veteran to a temporary total evaluation through September 30, 2003.  The noncompensable evaluation was reinstated October 1, 2003.  The RO later awarded a 10 percent evaluation, effective March 9, 2004, on the basis of a March 2004 VA examination showing pain on motion and some functional loss with repetition.

Following a Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) remand, the RO, in October 2008, reexamined the evidence and maintained the zero percent evaluation, effective August 28, 2002; increased the rating to 10 percent, effective December 2, 2002; maintained a 100 percent rating, effective May 23, 2003; and assigned a 10 percent evaluation effective October 1, 2003.  The RO also increased the rating to 20 percent, effective March 9, 2004; and awarded a 10 percent rating, effective April 21, 2005.  The Board decision affirmed these ratings.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board failed to adequately address [38 C.F.R. § 4.40](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=ff7eda9fa76eedfec4024b09d4db6818&node=se38.1.4_140&rgn=div8), [4.59](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=ff7eda9fa76eedfec4024b09d4db6818&node=se38.1.4_159&rgn=div8), and painful motion generally, or otherwise assign a minimum compensable rating for his painful joints.  He also argued that the Board erred by not assigning two separate 20 percent ratings for his left-shoulder injury, based on his limitation on abduction and flexion.

The Court, in examining § 4.59, found that, although arthritis is explicitly mentioned in the first and third sentences, the majority of the regulation provides guidance for noting, evaluating, and rating joint pain, and that such guidance is devoid of any requirement that the pain be related to arthritis.  The Court did not find that the introductory phrase regarding arthritis to be insignificant or superfluous but rather than it defines the subject of the next three sentences and does not limit the entire regulation.  The Court also observed that the title – “Painful motion” – reflects the subject matter of the regulation in general and implies no limitation to arthritis claims.

In applying its holding to the evidence of record, the Court concluded that the Board’s actions were correct, except for the period from August 28 to December 1, 2002.  The Court found that the Board referenced § 4.40 and pain on motion throughout its decision.  Furthermore, although the Board did not explicitly address § 4.59, it reasonably addressed joint pain and assigned a compensable rating with regards to the time periods other than August 28 to December 1, 2002.

However, the Court did agree with the veteran that the Board failed to address § 4.40, 4.59, and painful motion generally with regard to the August 28 to December 1, 2002 period.  Although the Board noted § 4.40 and found that the veteran exhibited no “pain on moment of his left shoulder,” it failed to address a September 2002 VA medical examiner’s assessment that the veteran was “able to hold his arm behind his head while explaining pain” and the diagnosis of shoulder pain, which reasonably raises the applicability of § 4.59.

The Court observed that the Board noted that the November 2002 VA examiner's report found full range of motion and listed the veteran’s subjective complaints of pain on motion, but the report recorded range of motion without addressing whether he had any actual pain on motion.  The Court found that the Board’s failure to discuss adequately these potentially favorable or incomplete reports, in conjunction with its failure to apply [§ 4.59](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=ff7eda9fa76eedfec4024b09d4db6818&node=se38.1.4_159&rgn=div8), frustrates judicial review of the Board’s finding of no pain on movement.

The Court held that because the Board provided an inadequate statement of reasons or bases and the application of § 4.59 may alter the Board’s determination that a compensable rating is unwarranted for the August 28 to December 1, 2002 period, it set aside the part of the Board decision that denied an initial rating in excess of zero percent from August 28 to December 1, 2002, and remanded the case for further adjudication.

With regard to two separate disability ratings for the abduction and flexion limitations of the veteran’s left shoulder, the Court found that the record on appeal does not reflect that the veteran raised this argument to the Board.  Furthermore, the Court found that, although the veteran suffers from both these limitations, [§ 4.14](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=ff7eda9fa76eedfec4024b09d4db6818&node=se38.1.4_114&rgn=div8) does not permit separate ratings under such circumstances.  Accordingly, the Court found that it was not error for the Board not to address the issue of separate disability ratings.
