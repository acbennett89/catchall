Decision Assessment Document
Suttman v. Brown, May 18, 1993, 5 Vet.App. 127 (1993)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that when, on the basis of the evidence of record, two or more provisions of VA's rating schedule are potentially applicable to the evaluation of a particular disability, VA must provide reasons or bases for its decision to rate that disability under one such provision rather than another potentially applicable provision.

The Court also held that this requirement is heightened where the disability has been rated as an unlisted condition by analogy to another condition.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.5.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran was a Japanese POW from 4/9/42 to 9/8/45.  Service connection was awarded for ulcers on left leg from shrapnel burns, evaluated at 10%.  Benefits were terminated when he returned to active duty.  In 9/65 the left leg condition was evaluated as 0% disabling.  In 1975, the veteran requested reinstatement of the 10% evaluation and also asserted that his heart condition was a result of the POW incarceration.  In March 1976, the claim was denied.  In 9/85, claims for service connection for beriberi and beriberi heart disease were denied as not shown on the most recent examination.  In 12/88, service connection was established for peripheral neuropathy, bilateral, 10% for each extremity.  In 4/89, the veteran submitted an NOD concerning the evaluation of his SC condition.  BVA upheld the denial.  On his appeal to the Court, the veteran alleged that the BVA committed error by failing to award service connection for beriberi and beriberi heart disease, to provide adequate assistance in developing evidence pertinent to that claim and to award equitable relief under 38 USC 503.  He also asserted that the failure to provide such assistance and award such benefits had violated his constitutional right to equal protection of the law.

Where a review of all documents and oral testimony reasonably reveals that the claimant is seeking a particular benefit, BVA is required to adjudicate the issue of the claimant's entitlement to such a benefit, or, if appropriate, to remand the issue to the RO for development and adjudication of the issue; however, BVA may not simply ignore an issue so raised.  In view of the section 1112 presumption of service connection for beriberi in former POWs and the VA's own medical evidence establishing the presence of peripheral neuropathy as a "probable residual from beriberi", the Board was required to adjudicate the issue of the veteran's entitlement to service connection for beriberi.  The Board was also required to consider and discuss the evidence that he had suffered a cardiac condition as the result of beriberi, and to explain the significance of that evidence in demonstrating entitlement to SC for beriberi.

The Court pointed out that DC 6314 is a specific code for beriberi and that it provides that a rating may be assigned based on residuals including peripheral neuropathy.  Based on the diagnosis of peripheral neuropathy as a probable residual from beriberi, DC 6314 clearly was potentially applicable to the evaluation of the veteran's disability and BVA was required to consider and discuss whether the service-connected disability should have been rated under that provision.
