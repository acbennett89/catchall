**Decision Assessment Document**

***Harris v. McDonough*, 33 Vet.App. 269 (February 16, 2021)**

**U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)**

**What this case is about:**

A former service member filed a writ of mandamus (writ) seeking to compel the Regional Office of Jurisdiction (ROJ) to address his notice of disagreement (NOD), including whether VA’s character of discharge (COD) determination was valid.

**Impact on VBA:**

The M21-1 Adjudication Procedures Manual will be updated at X.iv..1.A.1 and X.v.1.C. to reference this case.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The former service member served on active duty from October 1989 to April 1993. He was discharged under other than honorable conditions (OTH) due to a pattern of misconduct. In April 2010, a VA regional office issued an administrative decision finding that his character of discharge (COD) was dishonorable for VA benefits based on willful and persistent misconduct, shown by several periods of absence without leave (AWOL), without evidence of insanity. The former service member did not appeal that decision.

In June 2015, the former service member filed a claim for posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD). VA administratively denied the claim because the April 2010 COD determination was a bar to benefits and had become final. In July 2015 the former service member submitted evidence showing his PTSD was the result of in service events, and a medical opinion from a private psychologist linking his PTSD to his military service.

In January 2018, the former service member filed a claim for a mental health condition and again asserted PTSD stressors. The following month, the Regional Office of Jurisdiction (ROJ) notified him that his mental health claim could not be processed because the April 2010 COD determination was final and a bar to VA benefits.

In October 2018, the former service member submitted a Notice of Disagreement (NOD) stating that he disagreed with the "COD determination" and that he "should be eligible for benefits." Later in October 2018, the RO notified him that it had received his NOD and would issue a Statement of the Case in due course. Nonetheless, in January 2020, ROJ notified the former service member that his October 2018 NOD was invalid because the one-year time limit to appeal the April 2010 COD determination had expired and that determination was final. The January 2020 notice explained that if he disagreed with the decision not to accept his NOD, he had the option to file a supplemental claim, request higher-level review, or appeal to the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA).

In February 2020, the former service member's counsel contacted VA and explained that the former service member had tried to reopen the April 2010 COD determination, but VA had refused to process the claim and had cancelled a pending appeal on that issue. Counsel argued VA was required to accept the current appeal, regardless of whether VA believed new and material evidence had been submitted, as the former service member had the right to challenge ROJ’s decision.

In April 2020 counsel for the former service member filed for a writ of mandamus with the Court to compel VA to re-adjudicate its previous determination that the former service member's COD was a bar to VA benefits, based on new and material evidence. He asserted the former service member's January 2018 claim necessarily included a request to reopen the April 2010 COD determination.

The Court first found that although the former service member could seek an upgraded discharge from the service department, only VA adjudicators could determine a character of discharge when presented with a benefits claim based on a former service member's other than honorable service. As neither VA nor the Court had jurisdiction over the service department, relief through the service department was not an adequate alternate remedy. Second, the Court noted that the former service member had a statutory right to a decision on benefits subject to one review on appeal to the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA) under 38 U.S.C. § 7104. Finally, because the former service member had the right to a decision, which might result in higher-level court review, the Court found that VA’s refusal to issue such an appealable decision frustrated judicial review.

Thus, the Court found the former service member was entitled to a writ compelling VA to issue a decision on whether he submitted new and material evidence showing the COD determination barred him from benefits. The Court ordered VA to issue an appealable “decision adjudicating [the former service member's] January 2018 claim, including a response to his assertion that the claim includes a request to reopen the April 2010 COD decision, and, if appropriate, adjudicating whether reopening of the April 2010 decision is warranted.”
