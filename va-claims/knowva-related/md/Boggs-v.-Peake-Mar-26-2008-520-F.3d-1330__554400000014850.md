Decision Assessment Document
 Boggs v. Peake, Mar 26, 2008, 520 F.3d 1330
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that claims based upon distinctly diagnosed diseases or injuries cannot be considered the same for purposes of [38 U.S.C. § 7104(b)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/7104) merely because those diseases or injuries involved overlapping symptomatology.  The proper reading of 38 U.S.C. § 7104(b) is that claims based upon distinctly diagnosed disease or injuries must be considered separate and distinct claims.

The “factual basis” of a claim for purposes of 38 U.S.C. § 7104(b) is the veteran’s disease or injury, rather than the symptoms of the veteran’s disease or injury.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran served on active duty from December 1950 to March 1954.  In 1955, he filed a claim for service connection for a “left ear condition.”  A VA examination diagnosed chronic ear infection of the left ear, which had originated in 1941, with conductive hearing loss.  The regional office (RO) denied the claim.  No appellate action was undertaken.

In October 2002, the veteran filed for service connection for hearing loss of the left ear.  He submitted a private physician’s report to support his claim, which contained a diagnosis of sensorineural hearing loss.  (Note: Conductive hearing loss often results from ear infections or obstructions in the middle or outer ear, whereas sensorineural hearing loss, which involves injury to the inner ear, typically originates from acoustic trauma or reported exposure to loud noise.)  The RO, after initially treating the claim as an attempt to reopen his 1955 claim and notifying him that new and material evidence would be required, ultimately treated it as a new claim in its decision denying service connection.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board); however, treated it as a claim to reopen the 1955 claim on the basis that both claims involved hearing loss of the left ear.  The Board, finding that new and material evidence had not been presented, denied the claim.

The Veterans Court, in upholding the Board denial, held that hearing loss is the same injury whether it involves injury to the inner ear (sensorineural hearing loss) or to the middle or outer ear (conductive hearing loss) as it involves the same symptoms.

The Federal Circuit held that the Veterans Court erred as a matter of law in finding that claims based upon distinctly diagnosed diseases or injuries can be considered the same under section 7104(b) because those diseases or injuries involve overlapping symptomatology.

The Federal Circuit referenced the legislative history of [section 7104(b)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/7104), which indicated that the “factual basis” of a claim for service connection is the underlying disease or injury, rather than the symptoms of that disease or injury.  If a veteran’s claim does not have the same factual basis as a prior claim, then the veteran is not seeking to reopen his prior claim but is opening a new claim.

The Federal Circuit, in supporting its decision, reasoned that it was consistent with its precedent in [*Ephraim v. Brown*, 82 F. 3d 399 (Fed. Cir. 1996)](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/082/82.F3d.399.94-7035.html), distinguishing claims based upon distinct medical diagnoses is more accurate and reliable than distinguishing claims according to subjective symptomatology.  The consideration of symptoms is only appropriate in determining compensation to which a veteran is entitled.

The Federal Circuit stated that a later-filed claim could be prejudiced by a prior claim merely because the two claims involved different diseases or injuries with overlapping symptoms if the symptomatology standard were used.  Pursuant to section 7104(b), the two claims must be considered independently because they rest on distinct factual bases.

The Federal Circuit reversed the Veterans Court decision and remanded for a determination of whether the 2002 claim is based upon a different diagnosed disease or injury than his 1955 claim.  If so, the 2002 claim is a new claim that must be reviewed on the merits.
