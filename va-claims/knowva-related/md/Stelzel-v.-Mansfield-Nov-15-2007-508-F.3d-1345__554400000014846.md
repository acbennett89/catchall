Decision Assessment Document
 Stelzel v. Mansfield, Nov 15, 2007, 508 F.3d 1345
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that 38 U.S.C. § 3012(b)(6) (1962) (presently [38 U.S.C. § 5112(b)(6)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5112)) does not require VA to provide a veteran with sixty days notice before making a disability rating decision if the veteran’s overall compensation was not reduced, even though a *disability evaluation* was reduced. Note: The Federal Circuit explained that even though it interpreted the applicable statute extant in 1965, its decision would not change under the current version of the statute as section 5112(b)(6), although having been amended since 1965, retained its substance, as the changes were minor.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The VA informed the veteran in October 1965 that it had granted service connection for a new disability, ulcers, but simultaneously had reduced the evaluation for his service-connected chronic brain syndrome from 50 to 30 percent.  Although the 10 percent evaluation for the ulcers would have increased his overall combined evaluation to 80 percent, the reduction in the evaluation for chronic brain syndrome resulted in overall rating being continued at 70 percent.

The veteran, in 2004, claimed that VA had committed clear and unmistakable error (CUE) in the 1965 decision.  Although conceding that the merits of the reduction were correct, the veteran argued that the applicable statute and regulations required six days notice before the decrease in the rating for the chronic brain syndrome could be effectuated.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals and Veterans Court determined that no CUE had been committed.

The Federal Circuit concluded that the applicable statute clearly and unambiguously imposed a sixty-day notice requirement only in the event of a reduction or discontinuance of a monthly payment.  The Federal Circuit also found that the implementing regulations were found to be consistent with the statute (as with the statute, it was noted that the current version of the applicable regulation is not materially different from its 1965 forerunner).  Thus, no error was found in the previous determinations that CUE had not been committed.

The Federal Circuit affirmed the Veterans Court’s decision.
