Decision Assessment Document
 Willsey v. Peake, Aug 11, 2008, 535 F.3d 1368
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that the precedent opinions of *Russell v. Principi*, 3 Vet.App. 310 (1992) and *Cook v. Principi*, 318 F.3d 1334 (Fed. Cir. 2002) provide the rule of law for determining clear and unmistakable error (CUE).

*Russell* provided the following three-pronged test for establishing CUE in order to revise a final VA decision:  (1) Either the correct facts, as they were known at the time, were not before the adjudicator or the statutory or regulatory provisions extant at the time were incorrectly applied; (2) the error must be “undebatable” and the sort “which, had it not been made, would have manifestly changed the outcome at the time it was made,” and (3) a determination that were was CUE must be based on the record and the law that existed at the time of the prior adjudication in question.

Note: The Federal Circuit adopted the *Russell* test in substance in *Cook*.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, whose service included duty in Vietnam, filed a claim for service connection for post traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) in April 1983.  VA medical records showed treatment for PTSD in July and August 1982.  A VA examination undertaken in June 1983 failed to diagnose PTSD; however, the examiner noted that he had not seen the earlier records concerning PTSD treatment and recommended that VA obtain those records.

The regional office (RO) denied the claim in September 1983.  The decision did not resolve if those records were obtained.  No appellate action was taken.  The veteran’s attempt to reopen the claim in 1996 was denied and not appealed.  In October 1998, he again filed to reopen.  The RO granted service connection for PTSD in June 1999 with a 30 percent evaluation; however, it also determined that the 1983 decision did not contain CUE.  A 100 percent evaluation was eventually granted effective the date of the October 1998 claim.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in December 2003, denied the request for revision of the 1983 decision and an earlier effective date, as it found no CUE in the 1983 or 1996 decisions.  The Court of Veterans’ Appeals (Veterans Court) upheld the Board’s decision.  The veteran argued before the Federal Circuit that the Federal Circuit had jurisdiction to consider his contention that the Veterans Court did not apply the *Russell* test for determining CUE.

Note: The Federal Circuit’s jurisdiction to review a Veterans Court decision is limited.  The Federal Circuit can review a Veterans Court decision with respect to the validity of a decision on a rule of law or to the validity or interpretation of any statute or regulation relied on by the Veterans Court in making that decision.

The Federal Circuit, noting that a rule of law may include case law of the sort enunciated in *Russell*, rejected VA’s position that the rule of law concerning CUE is established by [38 U.S.C. § 5109A(a)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5109A), rather than *Russell*.  Although section 5109A(a) does provide that a RO decision is subject to revision based upon CUE, *Russell*, rather than the statute, sets forth the test for when CUE is present.

Upon determining that it had jurisdiction, the Federal Circuit determined that the 1983 RO decision did not contain CUE.  The Federal Circuit found no evidence that the correct facts, as they were known at the time, were not before the adjudicator or that the decision was so unreasonable as to contain undebatable error.  The Federal Circuit also found that the existing record and law at the time of the 1983 decision did not compel a finding of CUE.

The Federal Court affirmed the Veteran’s Court decision.
