Decision Assessment Document
Colon v. Brown, Apr 17, 1996, 9 Vet.App. 104 (1996)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that VA failed to comply with 38 C.F.R. 3.205(c) by neither providing the appellant with an opportunity to submit a signed statement that she had no knowledge of an impediment to the common law marriage in the state or territory in which the appellant resided.

38 C.F.R. 3.205(c) provides that where a surviving spouse has submitted proof of marriage in accordance with subsection (a) and also meets the requirement of 38 C.F.R. 3.52, the claimant's signed statement that he or she had no knowledge of an impediment to the marriage to the veteran will be accepted, in the absence of information to the contrary, as proof of that fact.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into guidance contained in the M21-1 Manual under III.iii.5.C.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The appellant and the veteran were married in February 1954.  The appellant was divorced from the veteran in July 1989.  In August 1989, the veteran submitted an application for benefits in which he indicated that he had been married once, was then divorced, and did not live with his former spouse.  The veteran died in November 1990.  In her December 1990 application for death benefits, the appellant stated:  "I divorced my husband on 7/21/89, however he did not abandon the house.  He remained in the house until his death.  I attended him during his sickness period."  In its September 1993 decision, the Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) determined that the "appellant's subsequent cohabitation with the veteran ... did not constitute a valid marriage."  The Board concluded that, even if the appellant and the veteran had cohabited from the time of the divorce until the veteran died, the appellant did not qualify as a "surviving spouse" as defined in 38 U.S.C. 101(3) and 38 CFR 3.50.

The Court noted that the appellant had argued that, notwithstanding the July 1989 divorce decree, she was the spouse of the veteran at the time of his death because she and the veteran had lived together from the time of their marriage in 1954 until his death in 1990.  Section 103(a) of title 38 provides that if evidence establishes that a person, without knowledge of any legal impediment, entered into a marriage with a veteran which, but for a legal impediment, would have been valid, and thereafter cohabited with the veteran for one year or more immediately before the veteran's death, the purported marriage shall be deemed to be a valid marriage.  The Court also noted that  38 CFR  3.205(c) states that the claimant's signed statement that he or she had no knowledge of an impediment to the marriage to the veteran will be accepted, in the absence of information to the contrary, as proof of that fact.  The Court found that the appellant was arguing that, although she has now conceded that common-law marriages are not valid under Puerto Rican law, she was unaware of that fact when she cohabited with the veteran after the divorce decree was issued and thus an otherwise invalid common-law marriage should be deemed valid.  The Court held that the VA has not provided the appellant with an opportunity to submit a signed statement pursuant to section 3.205(c), or in the event of such a statement, provided an adequate statement of reasons or bases on the question whether she had the requisite knowledge of the legal impediment.
