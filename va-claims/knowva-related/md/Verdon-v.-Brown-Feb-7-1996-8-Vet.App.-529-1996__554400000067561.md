Decision Assessment Document
 Verdon v. Brown, Feb 7, 1996, 8 Vet.App. 529 (1996)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court rejected the veteran's argument that any residuals of in-service medical or surgical treatment should be service-connected and held that where a preexisting disability has been medically or surgically treated during service and the treatment has ameliorated the disability so that it is no more disabling than it was at entry into service, the presumption of aggravation in 38 CFR 3.306 does not attach.  The Court relied on 38 CFR 3.306(b)(1) which states that "the usual effects of medical and surgical treatment in service, having the effect of ameliorating disease or other conditions incurred before enlistment, including postoperative scars, absent or poorly functioning parts or organs, will not be considered service connected unless the disease or injury is otherwise aggravated by service."

I**mpact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, I.1.C.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran's 1985 induction exam and a follow-up orthopedic exam noted angulation of  the veteran's great toes (hallux valgus) and bunions.   In April of 1987 he underwent arthroscopy for a right knee injury.  In October 1987 he had a bunionectomy.   In February of 1988 the veteran was discharged as "unfit because of physical disability."  His right knee condition was listed as the condition causing unfitness and the foot condition was listed as a condition that was "not separately unfitting and did not contribute to the unfitting condition."

 In April 1988 the veteran filed a claim for service connection for the knee and foot conditions.  The RO granted 20% for the knee injury and denied service connection for the foot condition on the grounds that the foot condition preexisted service.  The RO stated that the 1987 bunionectomy was considered to have been remedial surgery to correct the preexisting defect.  In September 1989 the veteran filed a claim for increase in the knee condition and for service connection for the foot condition.  The RO denied and he appealed.  In May of 1993 the veteran wrote a letter to his VFW representative stating that he agreed with the 20% evaluation for the knee "at this time" but that he wanted to appeal the denial of service connection for the foot condition.  The RO got a copy of this letter.

When the case got to the Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) it did not address the issue of the knee condition citing the letter from the veteran to his VFW representative in which he stated that he agreed with the 20% rating.  The Board upheld the denial of service connection for the foot on the grounds that the in-service bunionectomy was remedial of a pre-existing condition and that the condition had improved by the time of the veteran's discharge.  The veteran appealed.  He argued that he had never formally abandoned his claim for increase in the knee condition and that the residuals of his in-service bunionectomy should be service-connected.
