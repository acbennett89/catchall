Decision Assessment Document
 Darby v. Brown, May 9, 1997, 10 Vet.App. 243 (1997)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that because the veteran is not entitled to the presumption of 38 U.S.C. 1116, and 38 C.F.R. 3.307 and 3.309, in order for the appellant to have a well grounded claim for DIC, there must be competent medical evidence in the record showing an etiological relationship between the veteran’s cause of death and his period of military service.

Although lung cancer is a disability for which a presumption of service connection attaches to veterans who were exposed to Agent Orange in Vietnam, the presumption is rebuttable under 38 C.F.R. 3.307(d).  In this case, the medical evidence established that lung cancer was not a primary cancer but was metastatic from a primary stomach cancer.  The presumption of service connection established by 38 C.F.R. 3.309(e) was thus rebutted.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran served in Vietnam and as diagnosed with stomach cancer, which eventually metastasized to his lungs.  He died in February 1987 with carcinomatosis of the heart, lungs, liver, and abdomen due to or as a consequence of primary adenocarcinoma of the stomach.  The Board of Veterans' Appeals denied the widow's DIC claim, finding that there was sufficient affirmative evidence indicating intercurrent disease in the veteran's death certificate as to show that the veteran's lung cancer was note related to Agent Orange exposure.
