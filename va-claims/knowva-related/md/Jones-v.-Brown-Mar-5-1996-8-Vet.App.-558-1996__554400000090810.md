Decision Assessment Document
Jones v. Brown, Mar 5, 1996, 8 Vet.App. 558 (1996)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court, noting that a determination of eligible claimant status for pension as the surviving spouse does not conclusively establish entitlement to the accrued benefits at issue in this case, held that a claim for accrued benefits can be well grounded even when there is no claim pending on the date of the veteran's death.  Any other conclusion, according to the Court, would impose a restriction on the statutory provision that accrued benefits may be based on evidence in the file at the date of the veteran's death.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding will be incorporated into guidance contained in Manual M21-1, IV.ii.2.A.4.b

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

In July 1986, the veteran filed an application for compensation or pension stating that he was married to the appellant and that this was his only marriage.  The RO did not determine his eligibility for additional dependency benefits because the claims file contained conflicting information regarding whether the veteran was, or had been, married to two other women.  In February 1987, the RO sent a letter to the veteran requesting evidence of the termination of those prior marriages.  In response, the veteran stated that, while he had previously lived with two other women in "common law" relationships, he had separated from each woman for many years.  In April 1987, the RO sent the veteran another letter requesting certain evidence regarding those relationships.  The veteran failed to respond within one year and died on August 12, 1988, without having provided the requested information.

The appellant filed a claim for benefits and was awarded pension as the surviving spouse of the veteran.  Subsequently, the appellant questioned the RO's refusal to consider her the veteran's legal spouse for purposes of accrued benefits.  The RO denied accrued benefits and, after allowing the pension claim, developed for evidence to "deem valid" the marriage to the veteran.  The appellant promptly submitted the evidence and filed an NOD with the decision to deny accrued benefits.  During a hearing, the appellant testified that the veteran had provided a copy of their marriage certificate to VA.  The Board of Veterans' Appeals denied the claim, concluding that it was not well grounded.
