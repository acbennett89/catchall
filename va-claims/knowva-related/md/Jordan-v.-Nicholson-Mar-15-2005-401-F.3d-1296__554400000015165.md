Decision Assessment Document
 Jordan v. Nicholson, Mar 15, 2005, 401 F.3d 1296
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that if VA correctly applied a regulation in a prior final decision, the fact that the regulation was later found to be invalid does not establish that the prior final decision contained clear and unmistakable error (CUE) warranting retroactive correction and retroactive payment.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change but this is an important CUE case.

****Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:****

In [VAOPGCPREC 3-2003](http://www.va.gov/ogc/docs/2003/PREC3-2003.DOC) the General Counsel held that a long-standing VA regulation concerning the presumption of sound condition upon entry into service ([38 C.F.R. § 3.304(b)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=e0f05789050dd55e710d7f8a448316da&node=se38.1.3_1304&rgn=div8)) was inconsistent with the governing statute and was therefore invalid.  Based on that analysis, the appellant asserted that the regulation was void ab initio and that VA’s 1983 decision denying his claim based in part on section 3.304(b) therefore was the product of CUE. The Federal Circuit rejected that argument for two reasons.  First, the court noted that [38 C.F.R. § 20.1403(e)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=661691e12d474771409ffb379eb3257b&node=se38.2.20_11403&rgn=div8) reasonably provides that the otherwise correct application of a statute or regulation cannot be CUE, even if there is a subsequent change in the interpretation of the statute or regulation.  Second, the court stated that the appellant’s “void ab initio” argument did not give adequate weight to the finality of judgments and that the Supreme Court has repeatedly denied attempts to reopen final decisions even in the face of new judicial decisions finding a statute unconstitutional.
