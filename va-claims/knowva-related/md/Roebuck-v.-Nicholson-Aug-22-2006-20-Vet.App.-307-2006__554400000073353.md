Decision Assessment Document
Roebuck v. Nicholson, Aug 22, 2006, 20 Vet.App. 307(2006)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that when a claimant raises more than one theory in support of a claim during the time while that claim is still pending before VA, if the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) bifurcates those theories or arguments and addresses them in separate decisions, the time for appeal is not ripe until the Board issues a final decision denying all theories.  The 120-day requirement for filing an appeal will not begin to run until the Board has denied all theories in support of a claim that it has identified for consideration.

In view of caselaw and applicable regulations, the Court determined that a claim is an application for entitlement to a VA benefit based on a current disability.  Consistent with their own caselaw, the Court recognized that although there may be multiple theories or means of establishing entitlement to a benefit for a disability, if the theories all pertain to the same benefit for the same disability, they constitute the same claim.

The Court also held that the Board’s statement of reasons and bases were inadequate as it failed to consider 38 C.F.R. 3.156(b) and the new and material evidence submitted in connection with the June 1997 claim for entitlement to service connection for a lung disorder due to nicotine dependence.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated as guidance contained in the Manual M21-1, III.ii.2.B.1.m.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons**:

The veteran served honorably in the Navy from 1969 to 1973.  A claim for service connection for a lung disorder secondary to tobacco use and nicotine dependence was filed in June 1997.  In February 1998, a claim was submitted for service connection for a lung disorder secondary to asbestos exposure.  The regional office (RO) denied the claim for service connection for a lung disorder due to nicotine dependence on June 9, 1998, finding that the only evidence submitted was the veteran’s own statement, which failed to establish a well-grounded claim.  Later in the same month, June 29th, the veteran provided a letter from his mother in support of his assertion that the tobacco addiction began in service.

In a July 1998 statement, the veteran attributed symptoms to asbestos exposure and that he was appealing the denial of service connection for a lung disorder due to nicotine dependence.  The RO denied the claim for a lung condition due to asbestos exposure in April 1999.  Service connection for a lung disorder due to nicotine dependence was again denied in January 2000.  The RO indicated in March 2000 that it had construed his June 1998 letter and supporting lay statement as new and material evidence, but had not received a Notice of Disagreement (NOD) regarding the June 1998 decision.  A NOD concerning the January 2000 denial was filed in April 2000.  The Board remanded the asbestos claim in January 2001 for additional development.

The Board, in December 2002, determined that new and material evidence had been submitted to reopen a claim for service connection for a lung disorder due to nicotine dependence, but denied the case on its merits.  The Board also determined that the June 29, 1998, communication, which included a statement from the veteran’s mother, did not constitute a NOD, but should be construed as a claim to reopen based on new and material evidence.  However, the date of claim to reopen controls determination of the date of claim.  Thus, the veteran’s claim was denied as 38 U.S.C. 1103(a) precluded award of compensation benefits based on a tobacco-related disability for claims filed after June 9, 1998.

The Board also indicated that it was undertaking additional development on the “issue” of entitlement to service connection for a lung disorder secondary to asbestos exposure.  This claim was denied in October 2003.  The veteran filed a Notice of Appeal listing the October 2003 decision as the decision he was appealing.

The veteran argued before the Court that it has jurisdiction to consider both nicotine dependence and asbestos exposure issues as they concern the same claim.  He also averred that the June 29, 1998, letter was a valid NOD.  Furthermore, even assuming that it was not a valid NOD, his mother’s statement constituted new and material evidence submitted prior to the one-year period to appeal the RO decision and should have been considered as a submission filed in connection with the original claim under 3.156(b) and not as a claim to reopen.

The Court concluded that the theories of nicotine dependence, decided in December 2002, and asbestos exposure, decided in October 2003, are two theories that pertain to the same benefit (service connection) for the same disability (lung disorder); therefore, they constitute the same claim.

Accordingly, the case was not “ripe for judicial review” in December 2002 because the Board had not yet completed its decision-making process.  Because it would have contravened principles of judicial economy to ask a claim to appeal a nonfinal Board decision, the Court rejected the Secretary’s various arguments that the veteran’s appeal be dismissed.

The Court also found that the Board failed to consider the provisions of 38 C.F.R. 3.156(b) as the evidence (lay statement of veteran’s mother) was filed less than a month following the June 9, 1998, decision that denied service connection on the nicotine dependence theory.  As the veteran had one year to appeal the decision, any evidence submitted during this time frame, in concert with 3.156(b), must “be considered as having been filed in connection with the claim that was pending before the appeal period.”  Therefore, the Board should have considered this evidence as having been filed in connection with the June 1997 claim for service connection for a lung disorder based on the nicotine dependence theory.

The Board’s decisions were vacated and remanded for readjudication consistent with the Court’s opinion.
