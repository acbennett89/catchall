Decision Assessment Document
 Sharp, Haverkamp, and Rogers v. United States, Aug 26, 2009, 580 F.3d 1234
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that the United States Court of Federal Claims correctly determined that [38 U.S.C. § 1311(e)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1311) partially repealed [10 U.S.C. § 1450(c)(1)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/1450).  The partial repeal allows qualifying surviving spouses who remarry after the age of 57 to receive VA dependency and indemnity compensation (DIC) and DoD Survivor Benefit Plan (SBP) payments,[[1]](#f1) without offset of either benefit.

Note: SBP is an insurance-style program allowing eligible servicemembers and military retirees to elect to have premiums deducted from their pay in order to provide their spouses with additional benefits after their deaths.  The SBP offset provision under 10 U.S.C. § 1450(c)(1) directs that SBP payments be reduced by the amount the recipient receives in DIC benefits.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The appellees (collectively referred to in the singular as “Sharp”) are surviving spouses of deceased veterans, whom were also military retirees.  The surviving spouses all remarried after age 57.  Sharp qualified for both DIC and SBP benefits.

Prior to 2003, surviving spouses who remarried were no longer eligible for DIC benefits.  Congress passed the Veterans Benefits Act of 2003, which restored DIC benefits to surviving spouses who remarried after age 57.  The revision to 38 U.S.C. § 1311(e) provided that, “notwithstanding any other provision of law,” those remarried spouses who are simultaneously eligible for other benefits on the basis of their status as a surviving spouse do not suffer a reduction in their benefits under other laws due to the DIC payments.

The Federal Circuit observed that the SBP and the post-2003 DIC benefit schemes have contradicting provisions regulating offsets for those in receipt of both benefits, as the DIC scheme appears to prohibit a reduction in benefits; however, the DoD continued to enforce the SBP offset and reduced Sharp’s payments by the amount she received in DIC benefits.

Sharp filed suit in the Court of Federal Claims in July 2007, asserting that the government improperly reduced her SBP payments by the amount of her DIC payments.  The court granted summary judgment in her favor, holding that [section 1311(e)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1311) modifies or partially repeals [10 U.S.C. § 1450(c)(1)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/1450) to the extent that SBP payments are not to be reduced by the amount of DIC payments to those surviving spouses who receive DIC by virtue of their having remarried after age 57.

The Federal Circuit agreed with Sharp and the trial court, finding that the plain language of the statute and legislative history demonstrated congressional intent that benefits not be offset for DIC recipients who remarry past the age of 57 and are also entitled to SBP payments.

The Federal Circuit affirmed the judgment of the Court of Federal Claims.
