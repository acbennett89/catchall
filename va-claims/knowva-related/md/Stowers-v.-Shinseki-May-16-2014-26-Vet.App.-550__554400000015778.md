Decision Assessment Document
 Stowers v. Shinseki, May 16, 2014, 26 Vet.App. 550
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that, based upon the unique and specific facts presented in this case, where VA grants service connection based, in part, on a medical nexus opinion that relies upon service medical records (SMRs) that were not associated with the claims file at the time VA first decided the claim, and have not been associated with the claims file when VA assigns an effective date for the award of benefits, VA must consider its duty to attempt to obtain such records under [38 U.S.C. § 5103A(c)(1)(A)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5103A) and the potential applicability of [38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=3165a9d922f4a0154708cc186233f43c&node=se38.1.3_1156&rgn=div8) in determining whether revision of the assigned effective date is warranted.

Note: Section 5103A(c)(1)(A) provides that in claims for disability compensation, the Secretary shall attempt to obtain a veteran’s service medical records if relevant to the claim.  Also, [38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=3165a9d922f4a0154708cc186233f43c&node=se38.1.3_1156&rgn=div8) provides an exception to the general rule that the effective date of an award based on a request to reopen is the date VA receives the request.  If at any time after a claim is denied, VA receives or associates with the claims file service department records that existed but had not been associated with the claims file at the time VA first decided the claim, VA will reconsider the claim.  See 38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)(1).  If VA makes an award based in whole or in part on the newly associated service records, the effective date will be the date entitlement arose or the date VA received the previously decided claim, whichever is later.  See 38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)(3).

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who served from March 1979 to May 1992, filed a claim for service connection for a back injury in December 1992.  SMRs initially obtained by VA contained no references to a back injury.  The April 1992 separation examination evidenced no spine disability; however, the accompanying report of medical history disclosed recurrent back pain caused by lifting heavy loads.  A January 1993 VA examination yielded a diagnosis of intermittent back strain.  The veteran complained of occasional muscle spasm and “muscle knots” in the lower back; however, clinical findings and X-rays were normal.  The examiner noted the veteran’s report of a 1979 in-service back injury caused by lifting.

The regional office (RO), while observing the SMRs to be incomplete, denied the claim in March 1993, as it found no in-service complaints or treatment for back problems and the VA examination yielded normal findings regarding the back.  The veteran requested reconsideration, submitting SMRs that showed a September 1979 lifting injury to the low back and recurrences of back pain in September 1981.  The RO considered the evidence and continued the denial in May 1993.  The veteran did not contest the denial and it became final.

The veteran filed to reopen his claim for service connection for a low back disability in February 2008.  In April 2008, he submitted a letter from a private physician that disclosed a diagnosis of degenerative arthritic change of the lumbosacral spine.  The physician opined that the low back disability was due to in-service injuries and stated that he had reviewed “military health records” that showed a 1979 back sprain suffered while loading ammunition into helicopters, a back injury incurred from 1983 to 1984 caused by loading missile onto fighter jets, and a 1986 re-injury of the back incurred while loading missiles onto jets.

The veteran informed the RO that a “second part” of his SMRs was kept by Langley Air Force Base and he was unable to make copies of the records, but he had authorized and requested VA to obtain the records.  A VA contract examination in October 2008 confirmed the private physician’s findings.

The RO, in December 2008, granted service connection for degenerative arthritis of the lumbar spine with a 20 percent evaluation effective February 19, 2008, the date the VA received his request to reopen his claim.  The veteran appealed the effective date of the grant.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in September 2012, denied entitlement to an earlier effective date of service connection for the low back disability, finding that there had been no identification of further available evidence not already of record that would be relevant to the current claim on appeal, nor were there any service department records associated with the claims file since the May 1993 decision that would implicate [§ 3.156(c)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=3165a9d922f4a0154708cc186233f43c&node=se38.1.3_1156&rgn=div8).

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board clearly erred in finding that VA satisfied its duty to assist, as VA failed to obtain his complete SMRs.  He contended that there is a reasonable possibility that obtaining complete SMRs could show that the 1983/1984 and 1986 in-service injures referenced by the private physician’s letter, and that associating these newly obtained SMRs with the claims file, along with the private physician’s opinion could form the basis for an earlier effective date of entitlement to benefits under § 3.156(c).

The Court found that the Board, in determining that VA had satisfied its duty to assist in determining proper effective date benefits, erred in not addressing the SMRs that the private physician relied on in forming his opinion that were not previously of record or explaining why evidence of the existence of the SMRs was not “identification of further available evidence not already of record.”  The Court concluded that the Board failed to explain why these SMRs not previously of record would not be relevant to the issue of entitlement to an earlier effective date.  The Court explained that this is error because the Secretary has explained that under § 3.156(c), SMRs newly associated with the claims file and forming the basis for a positive medical nexus opinion can establish entitlement to an effective date as early as the date of the original claim for service connection.  Note: In the proposed rule for revision of § 3.156(c), the Secretary explained that the new rule would apply where VA denied service connection because of no evidence of an in-service injury and, years later, VA receives service department records that show an in-service injury and obtains a medical opinion that links that injury to the current disability, which results in a grant of service connection.  Although the doctor’s opinion was not a document that met the definition of proposed 3.156(c)(1), the service department record showing incurrence, which provided the basis for the medical opinion, is such a document.  See 70 FR, 35388-35389 (June 20, 2005).

The Court held that the Board, in failing to address this issue, provided an inadequate statement of reasons or bases for its determination that VA satisfied its duty to assist.  The Court set aside the Board decision and remanded for the Board to consider whether VA satisfied its duty to assist.  If the Board determines that the SMRs are potentially relevant to the correct effective date for benefits for service connection or degenerative arthritis of the lumbar spine, the Court directed that it must remand the case for the RO to attempt to obtain those records.
