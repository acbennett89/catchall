Decision Assessment Document
 Mitchell v. Shinseki, Aug 23, 2011, 25 Vet.App. 32
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that pain alone does not constitute a functional loss under VA regulations that evaluate disability based upon range-of-motion (ROM) loss.  Thus, the Court rejected the veteran’s argument that painful motion is to be considered the same as limited motion in the context of Diagnostic Codes (DC) 5260 and 5261.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.  However, examination worksheets and disability benefits questionnaires (DBQs) will be revised in accordance with the Court’s holding that if pain is associated with movement, the examiner must give an opinion on whether pain could significantly limit functional ability during flare-ups or when the joint is used repeatedly over a period of time and that such opinion, if feasible, be expressed in terms of the degree of additional ROM loss due to pain on use or during flare-ups.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, after submitting a claim for disability compensation residuals of a left knee reconstruction, underwent a VA examination in January 2006.  During the examination, she reported intense pain with standing, walking, and certain movements that affected her employment as a security guard and other daily activities.  The examiner documented zero degrees of left knee extension, passive flexion to 100 degrees without pain, and active flexion to 115 degrees with pain.  The regional office (RO), in April 2006, assigned a 10 percent evaluation for the left knee disability on the basis of “painful and limited motion of a major joint.”  The veteran appealed the evaluation.

Another VA examination undertaken in October 2006 exhibited 90 degrees of flexion and five degrees of extension of the knee.  The examiner documented that all motions appeared to be painful, and noted separately that ROM was not additionally limited following repetitive use.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in February 2009, found that the ROM of the left knee did not warrant a compensable evaluation under DC 5260 or DC 5261 and that 10 percent was the appropriate rating for painful motion under DC 5003.  The Board also found that even considering functional impairment “due to such factors as pain on motion, weakened movement, excess fatigability, diminished endurance, or incoordination,” an evaluation greater than 10 percent was not warranted.

The veteran argued before the Court that she should be awarded the maximum evaluations for loss of ROM under DCs 5260 – 30 percent – and 5261 – 50 percent – because she experiences pain throughout the entire ROM of her left knee even though there is no actual limitation of motion.  The veteran specifically asserted that painful motion should be considered limited motion, based upon statements of the Court in *Lichtenfels v. Derwinski*, 1 Vet.App. 484 (1991) and *Hicks v. Brown*, 8 Vet.App. 417 (1995).

The Court rejected the veteran’s arguments that its holdings in *Lichtenfels* and *Hicks* (which is based upon *Lichtenfels*) require that painful motion be deemed limited motion.  The Court in *Lichtenfels* clearly stated that the equivalence of painful motion with limited motion was only based upon the specific language and structure of DC 5003 regarding arthritic pain.  Note: In *Lichtenfels*, the Court stated:  “Read together, DC 5003 and [§ 4.59](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=ff7eda9fa76eedfec4024b09d4db6818&node=se38.1.4_159&rgn=div8) thus state that painful motion of a major joint or groups caused by degenerative arthritis, where the arthritis is established by x-ray, is deemed to be limited motion and entitled to a minimum 10 percent rating, per joint, combined under DC 5003, even though there is no actual limitation of motion.

The Court in this case stated that *Lichtenfels* and *Hicks* establish the specific principle that painful motion is equivalent to limited motion only in the context of evaluations under DC 5003, as opposed to the broader principle that painful motion is always equivalent to limited motion.

The Court concluded that nothing in its caselaw supports the veteran’s contention that she should be given the maximum disability ratings under DCs 5260 and 5261 simply because she experiences pain throughout the range of motion of her left knee.

The Court held that pain itself does not constitute functional loss under VA regulations applicable to the musculoskeletal system.  Pain in a particular joint may only result in functional loss if it limits ability to “perform the normal working movements of the body with normal excursion, strength, speed, coordination, or endurance.”  See [38 C.F.R. § 4.40](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=ff7eda9fa76eedfec4024b09d4db6818&node=se38.1.4_140&rgn=div8).

The Court, although rejecting the veteran’s arguments as to painful motion being the equivalent of limited motion, found that the Board’s implicit findings that the October 2006 medical opinion was adequate for rating purposes and that the duty to assist was satisfied were clearly erroneous.

The Court indicated that in the context of examinations evaluating functional loss under DCs based upon limitation of motion and when pain is associated with movement, *DeLuca v. Brown*, 8 Vet.App. 202 (1995), requires examinations to comply with the requirements of [§ 4.40](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=ff7eda9fa76eedfec4024b09d4db6818&node=se38.1.4_140&rgn=div8).  In such examinations, the examiner must express an opinion on whether pain could significantly limit functional ability during flare-ups or upon repeated use over a period of time.  Such determinations should, if feasible, be portrayed in terms of the degree of additional ROM loss due to pain on use or during flare-ups.  The Court found that the examiner, in October 2006, did not provide this information, or otherwise explain why such detail feasibly could not be determined, which rendered the report inadequate for rating purposes.

Although the examiner noted no additional limitation after repetitive use, no initial finding as to the degree of ROM loss due to pain on use was given, as required by *DeLuca*.  The Court also found the opinion to be inadequate because the examiner did not discuss whether any functional loss was attributable to pain during flare-ups, despite the veteran’s complaints of flare-ups.  The Court concluded that because the examiner failed to address any ROM loss specifically due to pain and any functional loss during flare-ups, the examination lacked sufficient detail necessary for a disability rating and should have been returned for the required detail to be provided.

The Court vacated the Board’s decision and remanded for further adjudication consistent with its decision.
