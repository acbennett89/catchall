Decision Assessment Document
 Criswell v. Nicholson, Dec 4, 2006, 20 Vet.App. 501
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court, while acknowledging that the law requires VA to “give a sympathetic reading” to a veteran’s filings by determining all potential claims raised by the evidence and applying all relevant laws and regulations, held that where no intent can be found to apply for VA benefits, a claim for entitlement to such benefits has not been reasonably raised.

Reaffirming well-settled case law, the Court stated that the mere existence of medical records generally cannot be construed as an informal claim as there must be some intent by the claimant to apply for a benefit.

The Court recognized two exceptions to this general rule:  (1) When an underlying claim has been awarded and the medical records demonstrate that the veteran’s disability has increased, or (2) when an underlying claim has been denied and the medical records evidence new and material evidence to reopen the claim.[[1]](#f1)

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision of Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran rendered honorable service from March 1944 to August 1945.  Upon separation from service he immediately filed an application for compensation due to trenchfoot.  A temporary rating of 50 percent for bilateral trenchfoot was granted.  A February 1947 VA examination yielded findings of cold-weather residuals of the upper extremities.  The following month the regional office (RO) terminated the temporary rating and assigned a 30 percent evaluation for bilateral trenchfoot.

The veteran filed a claim for service connection for a hand disability due to frostbite and increased evaluation for the residuals of frozen feet in August 1997.  Service connection for cold-weather residuals of the hands was granted by the RO in July 1999 with an effective date of August 1997.  The veteran appealed to the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (BVA) for an earlier effective date of service connection for the upper extremity disabilities.  BVA, concluding that a claim for cold weather injury of the hands had not been filed until August 1997, denied the veteran’s claim.

The veteran argued before the Court that he had reasonably raised a claim for cold-weather residuals of both hands in 1947, and the claim remained pending and unadjudicated until July 1999.  The Secretary responded that the 1947 medical records do not reflect an intent to seek VA benefits for an injury to the veteran’s hands.

The Court observed that nothing in the 1945 application for compensation benefits or in the 1947 medical records, although containing complaints relating to the hands, indicates an intent to apply for anything other than disability compensation for his feet.  Absent such intent there can be no informal claim.

The BVA decision was affirmed as the Court determined that BVA was not clearly erroneous in finding that a claim for cold-weather injury of the hands had not been filed prior to August 21, 1997.
