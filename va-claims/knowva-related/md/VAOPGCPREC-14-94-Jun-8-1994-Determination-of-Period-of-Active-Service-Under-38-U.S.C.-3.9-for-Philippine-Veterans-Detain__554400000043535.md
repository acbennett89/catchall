Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 14-94, Jun 8, 1994, Determination of Period of Active Service Under 38 U.S.C. 3.9 for Philippine Veterans Detained or Interned by the Enemy
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Phillipine Service under 38 C.F.R. § 3.9(b).

**Impact on VBA**

The phrase "immediately following a period of active duty" in section 3.9(b) refers to events which closely follow and directly relate to a period of active duty.

**Summary on facts and findings**

The question presented involved recognition of service as a prisoner of war (POW) based on claimed detention by the enemy following release from active duty.

General Counsel noted that section 3.9(b) preceded section 3.203, and is an exception to the general rule that service must be verified by the military.  Because there was no definition of the phrase "immediately following a period of active duty," General Counsel reviewed this matter and found that a close temporal relationship is included provided that events are directly related to the preceding period of active service.  Due to logistical factors or flight, members of the Phillipine Commonwealth Army or Regular Phillipine Scouts may not have been captured for a day or two following capitulation.  In such cases, the detention may be so closely related to the period of active duty as to warrant inclusion under section 3.9(b).

General Counsel also observed that service is terminated by any of the events described in section 3.9(a)(1)-(5), and determined that a service department finding under the Missing Persons Act does not preclude a decision that active service terminated based on missing-in-action status under section 3.9(a)(4).

The opinion refines criteria for finding that Phillipine service continued following release from active duty based on POW status.  Since few claims are potentially affected, no supplementary guidance is necessary.

No regulatory or procedural directives are required to implement this General Counsel opinion.
