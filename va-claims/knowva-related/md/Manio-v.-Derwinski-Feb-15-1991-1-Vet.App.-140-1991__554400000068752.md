Decision Assessment Document
 Manio v. Derwinski, Feb 15, 1991, 1 Vet.App. 140 (1991)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that with claims to reopen previously and finally disallowed claims, the BVA must conduct a two-part analysis.  First, it must determine whether the evidence presented or secured since the prior final disallowance of the claim is new and material.  If BVA determines that the evidence is new and material, it must reopen the claim and evaluate the merits of the veteran’s claim in light of all the evidence, both new and old.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated in the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.2.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The appellant claimed service connection for pyorrhea and resulting tooth loss.  The denial of serice connection was originally upheld by BVA decision dated 1-27-88.  The denial was again confirmed by BVA decision dated 11-14-88.  The veteran submitted a private physicians statement as new evidence.and BVA decision dated 11-20-89 once again uphold the denial.  The court concluded that the BVA found that the evidence offered by the appellant to reopen his claim was new and material.  They stated that when a claimant requests that a claim be reopened after an appellate decision and submits evidence in support thereof, a determination as to whether such evidence is new and material must be made and, if it is, whether it provides a new factual basis for allowing the claim.  An adverse determination as to either question is appealable.  The case was remanded to BVA for consideration of the claim in light of all the evidence and the application of the benefit of the doubt doctrine.
