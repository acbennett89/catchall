Decision Assessment Document
 Horn v. Shinseki, Jun 21, 2012, 25 Vet.App. 231
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that an unexplained “X” in a box on a medical examination board (MEB) report cannot constitute clear and unmistakable evidence of a lack of aggravation; therefore, it is insufficient to rebut the aggravation prong of the presumption of soundness.  Note: Pursuant to the two-pronged test to rebut the presumption of soundness under 38 U.S.C. § 1111, VA must clearly and unmistakably show that the condition pre-existed service and was not aggravated by service.

The Court emphasized that VA may not deny claims involving the presumption of soundness based solely upon MEB reports that contain no supporting analysis.  Note: An unrebutted presumption of soundness will not necessarily lead to service connection; a nexus between a current disability and the in-service aggravation must be shown before service connection can be granted.

The Court also noted that [38 U.S.C. § 1111](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1111) and [1153](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1153) are distinctly different statutes.  If an entrance examination notes a preexisting condition that is alleged to have been aggravated, the veteran may claim service-connected aggravation of that condition under section 1153.  In such cases, the burden falls on the veteran to establish that the condition worsened in service.  If worsening is shown, it is presumed aggravated by service and VA can only rebut this presumption by clear and unmistakable evidence showing that the aggravation was due to the condition’s natural progression.

Conversely, if no disorder is shown on the entrance examination, the presumption of soundness is applied under section 1111 and the burden falls upon VA to rebut the presumption by clear and unmistakable evidence that an injury or disease manifested in service *both* preexisted service *and* was not aggravated by service.  Under section 1111, the veteran has no burden to produce evidence of aggravation.

**Impact on VBA:**

Manual [M21-1MR, IV.ii.2.B.5.c](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014553/) will be revised in accordance with the Court’s holding to clarify the evidentiary standard under section 1153.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran served from October 1, 1970, to November 24, 1970.  His induction examination showed no hip condition and indicated that he was fit for service.  He was treated for complaints of left hip pain during the first three weeks of service and diagnosed with Legg-Calve-Perthes disease, a childhood hip disease involving osteochondrosis of the expanded articular end of the femur.

A MEB report from November 17, 1970, found the veteran medically fit for retention, but diagnosed Legg-Perthes disease and indicated with an “X” that the condition existed prior to service and was not aggravated by service.  He was separated from service a week later.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in November 2009, found that there was clear and unmistakable evidence that the Legg-Perthes disease preexisted service.  The Board concluded that there was no competent evidence of worsening of the veteran’s preexisting hip disorder during service and no hip injury was shown in service.  The Board found it significant that the MEB proceedings included contemporaneous in-service medical opinion evidence that the veteran’s Legg-Perthes disease was not aggravated during service.

The Court, finding that that the record was replete with medical records indicating that the veteran’s Legg-Perthes disease was diagnosed when he was approximately age six, agreed with the Board that the evidence of record constitutes clear and unmistakable evidence that the Legg-Perthes disease preexisted service.  However, the Court found that the Board erred in conflating the requirements of [sections 1111](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1111) and [1153](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1153) and failing to recognize VA’s burden to prove a lack of aggravation under section 1111 for a condition not noted upon entrance into service.

The Court, in finding errors in the Board’s reasoning, observed that there is no requirement that a specific injury or trauma be shown for a preexisting condition to have been aggravated.  VA must prove a lack of aggravation, not lack of injury.

The Court found the Board’s reliance on the absence of evidence in the record of worsening to be flawed because the absence of evidence may not be considered as substantive negative evidence and, in the presumption of soundness context, such reliance constitutes an impermissible burden shift from VA back to the veteran, which is precluded under section 1111.

The Court found that the only affirmative evidence regarding aggravation was a box marked “X” on the MEB form indicating that the condition had not been aggravated by active duty, which was without any analysis or medical explanation accompanying this conclusion.  The MEB report provided no means of determining whether the MEB found that there was no increase in disability during service or that any increase was due to natural progression.  Thus, the Court concluded that such evidence falls short of clear and unmistakable evidence and that no weight could be assigned to an unexplained conclusory opinion in a service-connection context.  VA, in such circumstances, must seek other evidence consistent with the standard of clear and unmistakable evidence to rebut the presumption of aggravation prong under section 1111.  Note: The Court gave examples of such evidence to include VA obtaining a postservice medical opinion.

The Court reversed the Board’s finding that the aggravation prong of the presumption of soundness was rebutted and directed the Board to enter a finding that the veteran’s preexisting Legg-Perthes disease was aggravated in service.  The Court remanded the case for development on the other service-connection issues and directed that any VA medical examiner(s) must assume that the veteran aggravated his pre-existing Legg-Perthe’s disease during service.
