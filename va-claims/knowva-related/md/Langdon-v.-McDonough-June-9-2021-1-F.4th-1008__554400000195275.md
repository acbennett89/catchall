Decision Assessment Document

Langdon v. McDonough, June 9, 2021, 1 F.4th 1008

United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (Federal Circuit)

**What the case is about:**

The Veteran appealed a final decision of the United States Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court) that denied him a higher rating for his service-connected thoracic spine disability. The appeal required the Federal Circuit to determine if the VA’s own regulation requires it to treat the thoracolumbar spine as a single unit when applying the General Rating Formula for Diseases and Injuries of the Spine in 38 CFR §4.71a.The Federal Circuit held that the plain language of the regulation requires VA to treat the thoracolumbar spine as a single unit when applying the General Rating Formula for Diseases and Injuries of the Spine in 38 CFR §4.71a.

**Impact on VBA:**

M21-1 V.iii.1.B.3.1.a. will be updated to reference this case.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The Veteran served on active duty in the Navy from 1980 until 1996. After leaving service, he sought disability compensation for a spine condition effecting his thoracic and lumbar regions. VA determined he had a service-connected thoracic spine injury, a non-service-connected lumbar spine injury, and only fifty-five degrees of forward flexion for his thoracolumbar spine. It also determined that the Veteran’s service-connected thoracic spine injury caused no functional impairment, and that his non-service-connected lumbar spine injury caused his reduced flexion. Because his service-connected injury caused no functional impairment, VA assigned a zero percent disability rating under 38 C.F.R. § 4.71a.

The Veteran appealed VA’s determination to the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), challenging the zero per-cent rating for his thoracic spine injury. He claimed entitlement to a twenty percent rating based on his limitation of thoracolumbar flexion. The Board disagreed but increased the Veteran’s rating to ten percent based on back pain under 38 C.F.R. §§ 4.45(f) and 4.59. The Court affirmed the Board’s decision, and the Veteran appealed to the Federal Circuit.

The appeal required the Federal Circuit to determine if VA is required to treat the thoracolumbar spine as a single unit under the General Rating Formula for Diseases and Injuries of the Spine in 38 CFR §4.71a. The Federal Circuit ultimately held that the plain language of that regulation requires that VA treat the thoracolumbar spine as a unit when applying the General Rating Formula.

The Federal Circuit began its analysis by noting that the General Rating Formula does not distinguish between the thoracic and lumbar spine segments. It noted the General Rating Formula uses the phrase “thoracolumbar spine” not the words thoracic or lumbar and does not separate out the segments of the thoracolumbar spine. Likewise, Notes accompanying the General Rating Formula repeatedly use the word thoracolumbar, but do not refer to the thoracic and lumbar spine segments separately. Additionally, the diagrams in Plate V depict thoracolumbar flexion as the movement of the entire thoracolumbar spine. The Federal Circuit found, therefore, that the plain language of the General Rating Formula treats the thoracic and lumbar spine segments as a single unit.

The Federal Circuit also noted that the General Rating Formula was amended in 2003 to eliminate separate criteria for the thoracic and lumbar spine segments. Prior to that amendment VA was required to consider separate diagnostic characteristics for thoracic and lumbar spine segments, including, for example, evaluating range of motion for each spine segment. However, because that task proved difficult, VA proposed “that the general rating formula provide criteria for the cervical and thoracolumbar spinal segments only,” reasoning that, “[b]ecause the thoracic and lumbar segments ordinarily move as a single unit, it is clinically difficult to separate the range of movement of one from that of the other.” Consequently, VA adopted the current version of § 4.71a to require consideration of the thoracolumbar spine as a whole.

The Federal Circuit found the 2003 amendment shows VA knows how to promulgate regulations that parse out diagnostic criteria by individual segments of the spine and that despite knowing how to separate out spine segments VA consciously chose to treat the segments that comprise the thoracolumbar spine as a unit. The Federal Circuit found that decision powerful evidence demonstrating VA did not intend its current regulations to allow for separate evaluation of the thoracic and lumbar spine segments.

The Federal Circuit rejected an argument that regulations do not allow Veterans to receive compensation for non-service-connected disabilities and that the Veteran in this case might improperly receive compensation for his whole thoracolumbar spine (including his non-service-connected lumbar spine injury) despite having only a service-connected injury to the thoracic spine. The Federal Circuit determined that argument was based on the incorrect premise that the Veteran lacks the injury for which he will receive compensation- a service-connected thoracolumbar spine injury. Since VA made the choice in its regulation to treat the thoracolumbar spine as a unit for rating purposes, the Federal Circuit declined to rewrite the plain regulatory language.

The Federal Circuit therefore held that VA’s regulation requires it to rate the thoracolumbar spine as a single unit when applying the General Rating Formula for Diseases and Injuries of the Spine in 38 CFR §4.71a, found a remand unnecessary, and reversed the judgment of the Veteran’s Court.
