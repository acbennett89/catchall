Decision Assessment Document
Ollis v. Shulkin, May 26, 2017, 857 F. 3d 1338 (Fed. Cir. 2017)
U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that when a claim for compensation under 38 U.S.C. 1151 is predicated upon a theory of referral from VA to a non-VA medical provider, and such claim is considered under section 1151(a)(1)(A), which is based upon a demonstration of negligence or similar instance of fault, the evidence must show that (1) VA medical care actually caused disability or death; and (2) the disability or death was a foreseeable risk of VA negligent conduct.

The Federal Circuit’s decision indicated that fault could be established by VA referring a veteran to a particular non-VA provider who negligently treats the veteran or recommending a procedure that is performed by a non-VA provider.

Section 1151(a)(1)(A), in such circumstances, requires a showing that VA was negligent, or otherwise at fault, in referring to the non-VA provider or for recommending the procedure that was performed by a non-VA provider; and the disability or death was proximately caused by the VA negligence.

The Federal Circuit also held that when a claim for compensation under section 1151 is predicated upon a theory of referral from VA to a non-VA medical provider or facility involving an unforeseeable event under section 1151(a)(1)(B), the statutory provision requires (1) that VA medical care (the referral) proximately cause the treatment or procedure by a non-VA medical provider during which the unforeseeable event occurred; and (2) that the unforeseeable event causes the disability or death.

Under section (a)(1)(B), the two components of the chain of the causation, proximate cause between VA medical care and the non-VA treatment and proximate cause between the unforeseen event occurring during the non-VA treatment and the disability, do not require an element of fault by VA.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Federal Circuit’s holding overturns the Veterans Court 2015 decision.  The holding will be incorporated into the Manual under IV.ii.2.G.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who served from June 1975 to March 1976, was diagnosed with atrial fibrillation in 1997, which was manifested by daily episodes of dizziness, light-headedness, and faintness.  He underwent an ablation procedure at the Nashville VA Medical Center (VAMC) in 1999; however, the episodes continued.

Upon visiting the Nashville VAMC in June 2007, the veteran indicated that he had experienced another episode in January 2007.  He stated that he did not desire to undergo another ablation and expressed interest in the MAZE treatment, which involves a surgical division of the normal conduction pathways between the sinoatrial node and the atrioventricular node by a series of incisions in the left atrium to create a maze of conduction pathways.  The purpose of the MAZE procedure is to allow a normal impulse to activate the atrium while eliminating macroreentrant circuits and is done for the relief of atrial fibrillation.

The MAZE procedure was not available at the VAMC; however, a VA physician had indicated in the medical progress notes that the MAZE procedure was an available option and “would be the current preference.”  The VA physician indicated that it was unavailable at the VAMC, as specialized operators and equipment were required; however, “it could be performed at other local institutions.  Recommendations are provided.”

In July 2007, the veteran began consulting with private physicians concerning the surgical and medical approaches to atrial fibrillation and he subsequently underwent the MAZE procedure at the Methodist Medical Center the following month, which was paid for by the veteran and his private insurance company.  The private treatment notes contain no indication of a VA recommendation or referral.

The veteran, in July 2008, filed for VA benefits for his disabilities related to the August 2007 MAZE procedure, which he stated was performed at the Methodist Medical Center where “the VAMC Nashville, Heart Department, referred me.”  He contended that the VA should be held liable for the treatment he received as a result of VA’s referral or recommendation.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in April 2014, found that a VA physician had recommended the MAZE procedure as one option to treat atrial fibrillation, but the procedure was ultimately performed at a non-VA facility by a non-VA employee.  The Board, in denying the veteran’s appeal, found no evidence that VA required the private provider to act on its behalf,” or that VA supervised or had a contract with the private physician that performed the MAZE procedure.  The Board concluded that the facts of the case fell outside of section 1151.

The Veterans Court, in October 2015, affirmed the Board’s decision, holding that disability compensation may not be paid under 38 U.S.C. § 1151 for additional disability incurred as a result of a medical procedure conducted by a non-VA employee in a non-VA facility even if a VA physician had provided advice or recommendations concerning such procedure to the veteran.  The Veterans Court also found no due process right to notice that referral to a private doctor could affect benefits under section 1151.

The Federal Circuit indicated that the question presented in the case is how to construe the statutory requirements of section 1151 when the disability-causing event occurred during a medical procedure not performed by a VA doctor or in a VA facility.  The Federal Circuit more specifically considered the application of section 1151 to referral situations.

The Federal Circuit found no error in the Veterans Court analysis that if even if the veteran could meet the cause requirement under section 1151(a)(1)(A), the veteran’s argument regarding VA fault for negligent referral under section 1151(a)(1)(A) was “speculative at best” because there was no proximate cause between VA negligence and the injury.  However, the Federal Circuit determined that the question remained whether the veteran’s VA medical doctors were negligent under section 1151(a)(1)(A) by recommending the mini-MAZE procedure to him.  The Federal Circuit remanded for consideration of the question.

The Federal Circuit held that when a claim for disability compensation under 38 U.S.C. § 1151 is predicated upon a theory of referral involving an unforeseeable event under section 1151(a)(1)(B), section 1151(a)(1)(B) requires that VA medical care proximately cause the medical treatment or care (here, the mini-MAZE procedure) during which the unforeseeable event occurred (here, the severance of the phrenic nerve); and section 1151(a)(1)(B) further requires that the unforeseeable event proximately cause the disability (here, shortness of breath and decreased lung function).

The Federal Circuit found that the Veterans Court erred in framing the question as to whether the veteran’s disability was a remote consequence of VA medical treatment, whereas the correct inquiry under section 1151(a)(1)(B) is whether the medical procedure was a remote consequence of VA medical treatment, that is, whether VA medical treatment proximately caused the veteran to undergo the mini-MAZE procedure.  The Veterans Court also erred in focusing on whether VA medical treatment caused the veteran to utilize the private medical providers, rather than whether VA treatment caused him to have the mini-MAZE procedure itself.

The Federal Circuit remanded for the Veterans Court to address the not reasonably foreseeable and proximate cause of the disability or death requirements consistent with its holding.

The Federal Circuit rejected the veteran’s argument that VA’s failure to provide him notice that a referral to a private facility for his mini-MAZE procedure could extinguish his eligibility for benefits under section 1151 constituted a due process violation.  The Federal Circuit held that there was no due process right to notice regarding conditions that might in the future affect an individual veteran’s right to monetary benefits before the veteran incurs an injury or applies for such benefits.
