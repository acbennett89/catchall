Decision Assessment Document
Mittleider v. West, May 8, 1998, 11 Vet.App. 181 (1998)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that when it is not possible to separate the effects of a nonservice-connected (NSC) condition from those of a service-connected condition, reasonable doubt, under 38 C.F.R. 3.102, should be resolved in the veteran’s favor with regard to the question of whether certain signs and symptoms can be attributed to the service-connected condition.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.5.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The regional office, in November 1994, increased the rating for the veteran’s posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) from 30 to 50 percent, effective July 1990.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in August 1996, in concluding that the criteria for a rating in excess of 50 percent had not been met, determined that the veteran’s PTSD was “productive of considerable, but not severe impairment of social and industrial adaptability.”

The Court found that the Board’s statement of reasons or bases to be inadequate by failing to explain why the veteran’s PTSD, which materially contributed to a serious employment handicap, was productive of only considerable, rather than severe, impairment of social and industrial adaptability, which would require assignment of a 70 percent under Diagnostic Code (DC) 9411, 38 C.F.R. 4.132 (1996).

The Court observed that the veteran had been diagnosed with NSC personality disorders and there was no medical evidence of record separating the effects of the service-connected PTSD from the NSC personality disorders.  The Court also noted that a VA physician had stated that the veteran’s PTSD was “muddled” by his personality disorders and drug abuse.  Although the Board noted the VA physician’s statement, it never discussed whether it considered all of the veteran’s various symptoms in assigning a rating for his service-connected PTSD.

The Court vacated and remanded the Board’s decision with instructions that it provide an adequate statement of reasons or bases as to why a 70 percent evaluation should not be assigned and to consider the applicability of 3.102 and VA’s interpretation of the regulation under 61 Federal Register 52698 (October 8, 1996).  Also, in view of the change in the rating criteria for mental disorders in October 1996, the Board was also instructed to apply whichever rating criteria was more favorable in determining whether the veteran was entitled to an increased evaluation.
