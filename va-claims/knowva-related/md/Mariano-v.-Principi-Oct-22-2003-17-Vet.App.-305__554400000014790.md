Decision Assessment Document
 Mariano v. Principi, Oct 22, 2003, 17 Vet.App. 305
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The issue on appeal is an increased disability rating for service-connected residuals of a gunshot wound (GSW) to the veteran’s left shoulder, affecting Muscle Group (MG) I, currently rated as 10 percent disabling.  The Court held, among other things, that VA’s interpretation that [38 CFR 4.71a](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=cd0747128bc9e911b0c6087c1c632930&node=se38.1.4_171a&rgn=div8), Diagnostic Code 5201 (DC 5201) measures abduction only is invalid. (This assessment will cover only this particular holding – for more information please see the Court’s opinion.)

**Impact on VBA:**

The rating criteria under DC 5201 for "Arm, limitation of motion of" can be satisfied with range of motion measurements taken in either the shoulder forward elevation (flexion) plane or the shoulder abduction plane; see Plate 1 in the Rating Schedule.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The CAVC addressed whether flexion not above shoulder level qualifies for a 20% disability rating under DC 5201. The CAVC held the Secretary’s interpretation that DC 5201 measures abduction only was invalid for at least three reasons.  First, Plate I in the rating schedule shows range of motion (ROM) measurements for flexion, abduction, external rotation, and internal rotation. Second, DC 5201 does not explicitly refer to any specifically identified ROM measurement.  Third, the title of DC 5201 is "Arm, limitation of motion of." The title appears generic and does not specify limitation based on abduction.  The two possible interpretations remaining for DC 5201 are limitation of motion (LOM) in all planes and LOM in any plane.  On remand, the CAVC directed the Secretary to clearly and unambiguously answer the questions presented by DC 5201. Mindful that interpretative doubt is to be resolved in favor of the claimant, the CAVC noted it could be very difficult for a claimant to satisfy the requirements of DC 5201 if LOM in every plane is required.

Please note: When considering the disability evaluation to be assigned for arm LOM under DC 5201, decision makers must carefully analyze the credibility and probative value of ROM measurements for both shoulder forward elevation (flexion) and shoulder abduction. Utilizing the DC 5201 rating criteria, decision makers should assign a disability evaluation which supports the highest overall disability evaluation for arm LOM based on shoulder forward elevation (flexion) or shoulder abduction ROM measurements.
