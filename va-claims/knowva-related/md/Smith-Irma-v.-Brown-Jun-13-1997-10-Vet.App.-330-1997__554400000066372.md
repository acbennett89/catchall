Decision Assessment Document
 Smith (Irma) v. Brown, Jun 13, 1997, 10 Vet.App. 330 (1997)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court invalidated 38 C.F.R.  20.1302, which gave the Board of Veterans Appeals (Board) jurisdiction to decide an appeal pending at the time of the claimant's death.  The Court also invalidated a portion of 38 C.F.R. 20.611, which permitted a representative to request continuance of the appellate action after the death of a claimant.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Manual M21-1 was revised to add I.3.A.7.a to incorporate the Court's holding.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran had active service in the U.S. Navy from June 1942 to December 1945. In October 1991, he filed a Notice of Disagreement (NOD) as to an August 1991 regional office (RO) decision granting service connection for PTSD and assigning a 10% disability rating, effective August 7, 1989.  On February 24, 1992, the veteran filed an NOD as to a January 1992 RO decision denying his September 1991 original claim for service connection for a generalized fungus infection.  In January 1992 and April 1993, respectively, the veteran filed a VA Form 9, Substantive Appeal to the Board of Veterans' Appeals, for each claim.  On April 10, 1993, the veteran died.

On April 22, 1993, the surviving spouse filed an application for dependency and indemnity compensation (DIC). A May 1993 certification of appeal from the RO included the surviving spouse's name as the “appellant” on the appeal for service connection for generalized fungus condition and for an increased rating for PTSD.  A January 1994 written submission to the Board from the American Legion, which had been the veteran's representative on these claims, “insist[ed] that the full benefit of reasonable doubt be afforded the veteran's widow by the Board” and noted that it “would be fundamentally unfair for the Board ... to deny this widow's claim for benefits for reasons not set forth in the statement of the case.”

In the May 1995 decision, the Board denied the veteran's two disability-compensation claims, noting that his appeal with respect to the two RO decisions was pending at the time of his death and that, pursuant to 38 C.F.R. 20.1302, “these claims are properly before the Board and the appellate process may be completed without application from the survivors.” The Court also noted that the appellant had filed a claim for “death benefits, claiming service connection for the cause of the veteran's death, in April 1993”; that the “death claim was denied by rating decision of May 1993”; and that “this issue is not before the Board.”
