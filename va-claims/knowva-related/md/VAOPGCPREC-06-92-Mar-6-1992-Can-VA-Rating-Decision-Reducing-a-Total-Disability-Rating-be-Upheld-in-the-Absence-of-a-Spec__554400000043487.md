Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 06-92, Mar 6, 1992, Can VA Rating Decision Reducing a Total Disability Rating be Upheld in the Absence of a Specific Reference in the Decision 38 CFR 3.343(a)
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Under what circumstances, if any, may a Department of Veterans Affairs Regional Office (VARO) rating decision reducing a total disability rating be upheld in the absence of a specific reference in the decision itself to 38 C.F.R. § 3.343(a)?

**Impact on VBA**

1.  A rating decision may be upheld, in the absence of a specific citation of 38 CFR 3.343(a), if all the evidence of record shows that it was considered and applied to the case.  Failure to cite the regulation in a rating does not necessarily mean it was not considered, nor does it render the decision void ab initio.  Reversal is required only when a failure to consider this regulation results in prejudicial error.

a.  Use of regulatory wording is not required, although it may help a reviewer determine whether or not the rule was applied, if it was not specifically cited.

b.  BVA may use a letter notifying the veteran of reduction, or a subsequent Statement of the Case, as evidence that the regulation was actually considered, even though it was not specifically cited in the decision.

c.  A BVA decision may correct a nonprejudicial failure by the agency of original jurisdiction to consider the regulation.

d.  Rating decisions are not required to cite all applicable laws and regulations.  BVA decisions, however, must include all issues and laws material to a case.  Furthermore, where a veteran raises a well-grounded issue, BVA must cite all pertinent laws or regulations.

2.  An opinion by General Counsel, in a memorandum or brief to the Court, sets forth the Department's position in the case in which it was filed.  It has binding effect only to the extent that the Court specifically adopts the argument in the decision.  Concerning arguments used in SWAN (89-75), the General Counsel arguments, adopted by the Court, overlooked the possibility of nonprejudicial error.  Accordingly, the Department is not bound to use the same argument regarding application of section 3.343(a) in future cases.

**Summary on facts and findings**

 In the CARTER case, a 100% rating for psychiatric disorder had been reduced to 70%, without specific reference to 38 CFR 3.343(a) in the rating decision.  During a hearing on appeal before BVA, however, the veteran's representative argued that 38 CFR 3.343(a) should be applied.  The BVA affirmed the reduction, citing the regulation and providing appropriate rationale for its decision.  A claim for increase was later filed, and a 100% evaluation was subsequently established from date of claim, based on new and material evidence.  The effective date was affirmed on appeal by BVA, but the Court subsequently ordered BVA to review the reduction for voidance, in light of SWAN.

The BVA Chairman referred the case to General Counsel for further guidance on the several issues presented, noting that it was not common practice to cite pertinent regulations in rating decisions historically.

Upon reconsideration of the memoranda filed in SWAN, General Counsel noted that Department attorneys had interpreted 38 CFR 3.343(a) more restrictively than written, in that reductions are permitted where improvement occurs while a veteran engages in activities demonstrating the capacity for work, even though the veteran is unemployed and not looking for work.

There is no requirement that ratings specifically cite all the regulations considered.  Moreover, failure to consider a pertinent regulation results only in a voidable decision.  In this regard, actual voidance would depend upon whether or not the oversight resulted in a prejudicial, as distinct from harmless, error.  Based on THOMPSON, a BVA decision is not disturbed where the ultimate outcome of the case was not prejudiced by error.

Despite due-process requirements for citation of pertinent regulations in Statements of the Case, and duty-to-assist considerations, there may be instances where the ultimate outcome was not prejudiced by failure to include 38 CFR 3.343(a) in the Statement of the Case.  There is no notice problem if it is apparent that the veteran is aware of the regulation, and has even argued it, or where the issue is otherwise raised on appeal by the veteran and/or representative.  Moreover, in such a case, the subsequent BVA decision would cure any defect caused by failure of the rating board to consider section 3.343(a) in the rating decision.

Finally, no mass review of prior claims is necessary, since there is a rebuttable presumption favoring the propriety of past uncontested dispositions, and because there is no reason to believe that previously appealed decisions were incorrectly decided.

Paragraph 9.08(a)(3) of M21-1 Part VI has been revised to require citation of 38 C.F.R. § 3.343, whenever a total rating is reduced.
