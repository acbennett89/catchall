Decision Assessment Document
 Vilfranc v. McDonald, Jan 5, 2017, 28 Vet.App. 357 (2017)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that a veteran is entitled to only a single disability rating for limitation of inter-incisal motion under 38 C.F.R. § 4.150, Diagnostic Code (DC) 9950.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran is service-connected for temporomandibular joint (TMJ) dysfunction, which is assigned a 10 percent evaluation under DC 9905.  VA examinations from 2011 disclose the veteran to complain of jaw pain, difficulty chewing, and muscular fatigue when chewing.  Objective evidence of pain was found on examination.  Maximum inter-incisal range of motion was 40 millimeters during a January 2011 examination and 40 to 50 millimeters in May 2011.  Repetitive motion testing did not result in additional loss of motion.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in January 2015, denied the veteran’s appeal for an evaluation in excess of 10 percent for the TMJ dysfunction, finding that the evidence did not approximate the criteria for a 20 percent evaluation.

The veteran argued before the Court that, because DC 9905 contains no indication that only one disability rating is to be assigned, application of 38 C.F.R. § 4.25 requires that she be afforded separate ratings for each TMJ.  She also argued that evidence of pain in each TMJ warrants a separate evaluation under 38 C.F.R. § 4.59.

The Secretary responded that DC 9905 clearly precludes separate ratings and that the TMJs, although paired, operate as a single unit and cannot operate independently.

The Court, rejecting the veteran’s § 4.59 argument, observed that the veteran was already in receipt of compensation for TMJ limitation of motion under DC 9905.  Therefore, she is not in need of the minimum compensable evaluation for painful motion under § 4.59, as the regulation is meant to compensate a veteran whose pain does not cause enough limitation of motion in a joint to reach a compensable level.  The regulation is not for application in this case, as the veteran already has a compensable level of limitation of inter-incisal motion.

Turning to the question of whether separate ratings may assigned under DC 9905, the Court found the regulation to be ambiguous regarding the matter.  However, finding the Secretary’s interpretation that separate ratings are precluded under the regulation to not be plainly erroneous, the Court afforded deference to the Secretary’s interpretation and held that only a single disability evaluation may be assigned on the basis inter-incisal range of motion under DC 9905.

The Court affirmed the Board’s January 2015 decision.
