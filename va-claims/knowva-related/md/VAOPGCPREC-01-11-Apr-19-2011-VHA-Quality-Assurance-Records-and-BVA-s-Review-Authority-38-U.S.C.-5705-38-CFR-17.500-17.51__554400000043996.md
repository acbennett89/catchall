Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 01-11, Apr 19, 2011, VHA Quality-Assurance Records and BVA's Review Authority - 38 U.S.C. 5705; 38 CFR §§ 17.500-17.511
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

38 U.S.C. § 5705(b)(1) and its implementing regulations, 38 C.F.R. §§ 17.500 through 17.511, do not authorize disclosure of quality-assurance (QA) records or documents to an agency of original jurisdiction (AOJ) or the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) for purposes of adjudicating a claim or an appeal to the Secretary of a claim decision.

Pursuant to 38 C.F.R. § 17.508(a), access within VA to confidential and privileged QA records and documents is restricted to employees who need such information to perform their governmental duties and who are authorized access by the VA medical facility director, Veterans Integrated Service Network (VISN) director, the Under Secretary for Health (USH), or their designees.  However, in accordance with 38 C.F.R. § 17.508(c), any QA record, whether confidential and privileged or not, may be provided to the General Counsel or any attorney within the Office of General Counsel (OGC), wherever located.

General Counsel also indicated that 38 U.S.C. § 5103A, a general statute requiring VA to assist claimants in obtaining evidence necessary to substantiate their claims for VA benefits, is applicable to QA records.  However, the statute is in conflict with section 5705, which is specifically intended to foster an atmosphere in which health-care reviewers will candidly critique medical procedures and practices and prohibits disclosure of QA records except as specifically authorized.  General Counsel stated that nothing indicated that Congress, by requiring VA to assist claimants in substantiating their claims, intended to repeal its near-absolute stricture against public disclosure of QA records.

However, in view of its determination that OGC attorneys are authorized to examine QA records to determine whether they are protected by section 5705 and implementing regulations and in order to give effect to both statutes to the extent possible, General Counsel concluded that VA’s duty to assist under section 5103A(a) requires AOJs and the Board to request access to any QA records relevant to a claim, provided that the claimant furnishes sufficient information to locate the records or documents.  If the appropriate VHA officials deny the request on the basis that the QA records may not be disclosed because they are privileged and confidential, the AOJ or Board may appeal the denial to OGC under 38 C.F.R. § 17.506.  If VHA or OGC concludes that such records are not confidential and privileged, they may be considered in deciding the claim.  This limited duty gives as much effect as possible to Congress’ mandate to assist claimants in the substantiation of their claims, without abrogating Congress’ mandate to protect QA records from unauthorized disclosures.

**Impact on VBA**

M21-1MR, IV.ii.1.A will be revised in accordance with the OGC Precedent Opinion.

**Summary on facts and findings**

The Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court), in Hood v. Shinseki, 23 Vet.App. 295 (2009), held that if VA withholds quality assurance records on the basis that such records are privileged documents, VA must comply with the requirements of § 17.501(b), i.e., to make some showing as to whether it described, in advance and in writing, the quality assurance activity for which it seeks to invoke the protections.

The Court also noted that it was unclear as to why the Board would not have access to such records if only to determine whether such records have been properly categorized as privileged.  Subsequent to Hood, the Board requested an opinion from the OGC concerning the procedures VHA uses in designating QA documents, what types of documents qualify as QA documents, the Board’s review authority of such documents, and whether VA’s duty to assist in claims development requires the Board to attempt to access QA records.

OGC stated that for a record or document to be protected from disclosure under section 5705(a), VA must designate the systematic health-care review activities to be carried out by or for VA for purposes of improving the quality of VA medical care or the utilization of VA health-care resources in VA health-care facilities.  Only records or documents resulting from those activities that have been described in advance and in writing by the USH, a VISN director, or VHA medical facility director are protected by section 5705 and its implementing regulations.  OGC stated that disclosure of QA documents to a VA claims adjudicator or the Board, even for the limited purpose of determining whether documents are actually protected by section 5705 or may be released to an involved veteran, is not authorized by statute or regulation.

In addressing the applicability of section 5103A, OGC indicated that although QA records relating to an event that forms the basis for a compensation claim under section 1151 might contain information pertinent to the claim, such records would not necessarily be necessary to substantiate the claim.  A private or VA medical opinion on whether the hospital care, medical or surgical treatment, or examination at issue satisfied the applicable standard of care might be sufficient for those purposes.  Failure to ensure the confidentiality of QA records would be contrary to statutory and regulatory requirements and would seriously undermine the QA process and, ultimately, adversely affect the quality of care provided in VA healthcare facilities.

General Counsel concluded that QA records that are protected under section 5705 and its implementing regulations, and therefore are unavailable for consideration in deciding a benefit claim, cannot be considered “evidence and material of record” within the meaning of 38 U.S.C. § 7104(a).  Thus, withholding protected QA records, even if pertinent to a claim, would not violate section 7104(a).
