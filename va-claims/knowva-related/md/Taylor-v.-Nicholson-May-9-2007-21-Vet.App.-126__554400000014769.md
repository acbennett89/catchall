Decision Assessment Document
 Taylor v. Nicholson, May 9, 2007, 21 Vet.App. 126
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that, for the purpose of accrued benefits, a claim is still considered pending, and not finally adjudicated, if the one year period for filing of a Notice of Disagreement (NOD) has not expired after the date of notice of an award or disallowance.

**Impact on VBA:**

Manual [MR VIII.3.1.a](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000015019/) will be revised to comply with the Federal Circuit’s holding.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran’s September 1992 claim for disability compensation was denied several times prior to an August 2000 regional office (RO) decision that granted service connection for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) with a 100 percent evaluation and effective date of June 2, 1997, which represented the date of receipt of a claim to reopen.

The veteran died on September 26, 2000.  The surviving spouse filed a claim for DIC benefits the following month, which necessarily included a claim for accrued benefits.  The DIC claim was granted; however, the Board continued the RO denial of accrued benefits.

The Board determined that the disability compensation claim for COPD was not pending at the time of death, all payments due the veteran were paid to him, and there were no accrued benefits due.  In concluding that there was no pending claim the Board found that the veteran had not initiated an appeal by filing a NOD before he died.  The Court noted that although this was correct, the Board failed to give import to the fact that the veteran had a full year after the August 2000 RO decision, that assigned a 100 percent rating effective June 2, 1997, to submit an NOD.

The Court noted that at the time of the veteran’s death, almost 11 months remained of the period in which an NOD could have been filed.  The Court deemed this to be significant because a pending claim is defined under [38 C.F.R. § 3.160](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=09cd81ab0fde3bade1c99c39dc90d0d2&node=se38.1.3_1160&rgn=div8) as “an application, formal or informal, which has not been finally adjudicated” and a finally adjudicated claim is a claim that has been allowed or disallowed with such action “having become final by the expiration of 1 year after the date of the notice of an award or disallowance,” or by denial on appellate review, whichever is earlier.

The Court indicated that since the surviving spouse takes the veteran’s claim “as it stands on the date of death,” the appellant filed the accrued benefits claim while her husband’s claim was still pending, i.e., it was not yet a “finally adjudicated claim.”  Because the appellant asserted error in the effective date the RO assigned, the Board erred by not reviewing the claims file to determine if there was error or not.

The Court rejected the Secretary’s argument that there was no error warranting remand because the veteran was not entitled to an earlier effective date as a matter of law because the effective date was the date of his claim to reopen and no earlier date may be assigned.  The Court noted that this assertion is generally true; however, there are some exceptions such as previously missing service records.  Thus, an earlier effective date is not precluded as a matter of law.

The Court also rejected the Secretary’s assertion that no accrued benefits were payable because the veteran had been paid back to 1997 and accrued benefits can only be paid for two years prior to the veteran’s death.  This argument had been rejected by the Federal Circuit in [*Terry v. Principi*, 367 F.3d 1291, 1294-95 (Fed.Cir.2004)](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000015407/), which held that the accrued benefits are not restricted to the two years immediately preceding death and may be paid for any two-period prior to death.  Thus, if an earlier effective date were granted, the appellant would be entitled to payment of accrued benefits not to exceed two years.  Note: The two-year limitation was removed by the Veterans Benefits Act of 2003, but only for deaths occurring on or after December 16, 2003.

The part of the January 2005 Board decision denying accrued benefits for compensation for COPD was set aside, and that matter was remanded by the Court.
