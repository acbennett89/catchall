Decision Assessment Document
 Allen and Key v. Nicholson, Mar 16, 2007, 21 Vet.App. 54
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that service in the National Guard, while serving a state, cannot establish eligibility for VA benefits as such service cannot be characterized as active duty under [38 U.S.C. § 101(21)(A)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/101).

A National Guardsman is only eligible for VA benefits if ordered into Federal service by the President under [10 U.S.C. § 12401](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/12401) or to perform duty under the provisions of [32 U.S.C. § 316](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/32/316), [502](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/32/502), [503](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/32/503), [504](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/32/504), or [505](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/32/505).  Note: These sections respectively apply to the President detailing National Guardsman to train civilians in the use of military firearms, inactive duty for training, inactive duty and active duty for training, participation in field exercises, and providing authority for National Guardsman to attend active duty schools.

The Court also held that when it is determined that there is no legal entitlement for a previous award of VA benefits, VA is not required to apply [38 C.F.R. § 3.105(d)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=835653f3036d142d0c6b61ca07979f41&node=se38.1.3_1105&rgn=div8) and demonstrate that the original decision was clearly and unmistakably erroneous to sever service connection.

**Impact on VBA:**

In cases where the award of service connection was clearly illegal, that is, prohibited by law based on, among other issues, type of service performed, the provisions of [§ 3.105 (a) and (d)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=835653f3036d142d0c6b61ca07979f41&node=se38.1.3_1105&rgn=div8) are not applicable to severance of service connection.  Although the claimant, in such cases, is not afforded the benefit of application of § 3.105(d) before severance action can be initiated, the procedural due process provisions contained in [38 C.F.R. § 3.103](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=835653f3036d142d0c6b61ca07979f41&node=se38.1.3_1103&rgn=div8) must still be afforded to the claimant prior to severing benefits.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The appellants served in the New Mexico Army National Guard (NMANG) and were ordered into active military service of the state to quell a riot at the Santa Fe state penitentiary.  Both sought entitlement to service connection for post traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) based upon their participation in quelling the riot.  Service connection for PTSD and total disability evaluations were granted to both appellants. Note: The Court consolidated the appeals as both underlying Board decisions based upon the same material facts and identical issues, and the same counsel represented both appellants.

Shortly after the regional office’s (RO) grants, the NMANG inspector general advised the RO that several Guardsmen should not have been awarded VA disability compensation in relation to their National Guard duty in quelling the penitentiary riot, as it was state duty.  The RO requested and received an advisory opinion from VA’s Compensation and Pension Service, which opined that active military service of the State of New Mexico by a NMANG to control the Santa Fe prison riot does not meet the definition of active duty under [38 C.F.R. § 3.6(c)(3)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=835653f3036d142d0c6b61ca07979f41&node=se38.1.3_16&rgn=div8).

The RO notified the appellants of its intention to sever service connection.  In effectuating the severance, the RO concluded that clear and unmistakable error (CUE) had been committed in awarding service connection for PTSD.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in its January and April 2004 decisions affirming the RO’s severances, found that the RO satisfied all procedural requirements specified by regulation where severance of service connection is contemplated and agreed that CUE had been committed.  The Board noted that receipt of the NMANG inspector general’s report was a new material fact that was not before the RO at the time of the decisions granting service connection.

The appellants argued before the Court that the record did not contain CUE, VA erred in relying upon § 3.6(c)(3) as the regulatory provision makes no distinction between state and Federal duty for the purposes of veterans benefits, and the Board erred in relying upon the 2001 C&P advisory opinion as it was tantamount to a “post hoc *re-weighing* of the facts and does not constitute clear and unmistakable evidence to support … VA’s burden to sever” service connection.

The Court noted that the reserve components of the armed forces include the National Guard, but only “while in the service of the *United States*.”  Referencing Supreme Court and Federal Circuit case law, the Court indicated that a National Guardsman holds a status as member of the federal military or the state militia, but never both at once and National Guardsmen only serve in the federal military when they are formally called into the military service of the United States.  At all other times, they serve solely as members of the State militia under the command of a state governor. See *[Perpich v. Department of Defense,](http://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/496/334)* [496 U.S. 334 (1990)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/496/334); [*Clark v. United States*, 322 F.3d 1358, 1366 (Fed. Cir. 2003)](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/322/322.F3d.1358.02-5062.html).  Therefore, the Court held that basic eligibility for veterans benefits for a National Guardsman requires having been ordered into Federal service by the President or performing full-time duty under one of the stipulated provisions of 32 United States Code.

The Court agreed with the Board’s finding that the appellants did not have qualifying service for VA benefits because their service during the prison riot did not meet the definition of active duty under § 3.6(c)(3) as state service under a governor’s order into active service for the State of New Mexico does not constitute federal service under [section 101(21)(A)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/101).  Furthermore, the record contained no evidence that the appellants, at the time of the prison riot, were serving under orders pursuant to a stipulated section of Title 32 United States Code to qualify for VA benefits.

Accordingly, there was no authority for VA to continue disability compensation payments for the appellants’ service in the prison riot.

The Court also noted its recent holding in [*Stallworth v. Nicholson*, 20 Vet.App. 482 (2006)](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014885/) that VA is not limited to the law and record that existed at the time of the original decision in severing service connection under [§ 3.105(d)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=835653f3036d142d0c6b61ca07979f41&node=se38.1.3_1105&rgn=div8).

Because the appellant’s service during the prison riot did not constitute active duty under [§ 3.6(c)(3)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=835653f3036d142d0c6b61ca07979f41&node=se38.1.3_16&rgn=div8), VA benefits were not warranted as a matter of law.  Therefore, since the appellants had no legal entitlement to VA service-connected benefits, the provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.105(a) and (d), which limit VA’s ability to sever service connection, did not apply.  The Court indicated that since it was “clearly illegal” to award service connection under these circumstances, the Board did not have to determine whether CUE had been committed in the RO decisions.

The Court rejected appellants’ argument that reliance on the C&P advisory opinion was impermissible because it was “post-hoc re-weighing of the facts,” finding that the advisory opinion was not a re-weighing of the facts previously known, but was a clarification of what the law always was, as applied to the facts as they existed, that NMANG service pursuant to state order to quell a state prison riot was not “active duty” for VA benefits purposes.  The advisory opinion also demonstrated that the correct facts were not properly before the adjudicator as the later obtained NMANG records determined that the appellants did not have qualifying Federal service at the time the RO awarded service connection.  The Court stated that this was not a new fact, but reflective of the true state of the facts at the time of the underlying RO decision.  The Court noted that there was no authority for the RO to award the appellants benefits in the absence of verified service in the U.S. Armed Forces. See Cahall v. Brown, 7 Vet.App. 232, 237 (1994) (“VA is prohibited from finding, on any basis other than a service department document, which VA believes to be authentic and accurate, or service department verification, that a particular individual served in the U.S. Armed Forces”).

The Court affirmed the Board decisions.
