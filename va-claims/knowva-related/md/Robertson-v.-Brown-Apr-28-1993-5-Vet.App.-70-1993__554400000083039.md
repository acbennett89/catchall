Decision Assessment Document
Robertson v. Brown, Apr 28, 1993, 5 Vet.App. 70 (1993)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court, in rejecting the veteran's argument that, regardless of actual disability, he was entitled to a "moderately severe" rating simply because his wound included debridement, held that the criteria under 38 C.F.R. 4.56(c) were only factors to be considered in evaluating muscle injuries and that no specific fact established entitlement to any particular rating.  The adjudicator must consider all the factors set forth in the regulations and make determinations based on the facts of the particular case.

The Court also held that 38 C.F.R. 4.56(d) constituted a "totality-of-the-circumstances test and no single factor is per se controlling in evaluating muscle group injuries.

(VA revised 38 C.F.R. 4.56 in June 1997 subsequent to the Robertson opinion.  However, the Court later concluded in Tropf v. Nicholson, 20 Vet.App. 317 (2006) that its holding in Robertson was still valid, as it noted that VA had not intended substantive changes in amending the regulation.)

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.4.A.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran sustained a gunshot wound in Korea fracturing the spinous process of L-3 and affecting muscle group XX.  He also sustained mild damage to the latissimus dorsi.  A rating decision in 1954 assigned a 20% evaluation for GSW, back, involving Muscle Group XX.  A claim for increase was filed in December 1988.  A Hearing Officer decision in August 1990 denied an increase and determined that no clear and unmistakable error was committed in the 1954 rating decision.  A March 1991 Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) decision denied an increased evaluation, but granted a separate 20% evaluation for residuals of gunshot wound of the left shoulder girdle.  A request for reconsideration of the decision was granted.  The Board held that the recorded medical history showed a relatively mild function impairment of back.  Consequently, the Board denied a retroactive rating in excess of 20% based on clear and unmistakable error in the March 1954 decision.

The veteran argued before the Court that the relevant regulations require an award of a moderately severe disability rating, as opposed to a moderate rating, merely because his wound included debridement.  However, the Court noted that under 38 CFR 4.56, the mere fact of debridement does not establish entitlement to more than a "moderate" disability evaluation.  All the factors set forth in the regulations concerning muscle injuries must be considered.  Therefore, evidence of debridement alone, with no ascertainable residuals, only minimal loss of mobility, and no history of unemployability, is not sufficient to demonstrate a clear and unmistakable error in the 1954 RO decision.
