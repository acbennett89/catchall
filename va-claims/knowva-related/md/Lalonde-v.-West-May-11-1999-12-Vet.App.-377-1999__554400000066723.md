Decision Assessment Document
 Lalonde v. West, May 11, 1999, 12 Vet.App. 377 (1999)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the effective date of an award of service connection is not based on the date of the earliest medical evidence demonstrating a causal connection, but on the date that the application upon which service connection was eventually awarded was filed with VA.  The Court concluded that because the veteran had not been granted service connection for his anxiety disorder, the mere receipt of medical records cannot be construed as an informal claim.  See 38 CFR  3.157; Brannon v. West, 12 Vet. App. 32, 35 (1998).  Also, the Court pointed out that, without a diagnosis or opinion from a physician regarding a causal connection, it is not reasonable to expect VA to treat a communication regarding an alleged secondary condition as an informal claim.

**Impact on VBA:**

The guidance contained in the Court's holding has been incorporated into Manual M21-1, III.iv.5.C.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

In May 1951, the veteran was granted service connection for a chronic duodenal ulcer.  In October 1951, he was admitted to a VA hospital for complaints of epigastric pains and a burning sensation in his stomach.  The medical examiner noted that “there [was] moderate nervousness and tension associated with this stomach condition.”  In April 1952, the veteran underwent a subtotal gastrectomy.  On July 2, 1964, the VA regional office received an application for compensation and pension benefits.  The veteran described the nature of the disability as follows:  “Post gastrectomy symptoms since about 1952.  At the present time my symptoms are becoming worse; feelings of weakness, nervousness.  I worry quite a bit and my mental condition is becoming worse.”  He also noted on the form that “service-connection has already been established.”  A VA psychiatric evaluation in October 1964 diagnosed the veteran with moderate depressive reaction.  No etiology for the condition was given by the examiner.  The regional office increased the veteran’s evaluation for status post gastrectomy due to a duodenal ulcer and established a 30% non service connected evaluation for depressive reaction.  Eligibility for non-service connected pension benefits was denied.

Medical records up until 1983 documented continued treatment for and complaints of a nervous condition.  However, no examiner since 1951 had expressed an opinion regarding the etiology of the veteran’s nervous condition.

In March 1983, the veteran submitted, in support of his claim for an increased rating for his service connected ulcer disability, a statement from Earl H. Morrogh M.D.  Dr. Morrogh opined that the veteran “has a nervous problem following this gastric difficulty and takes tranquelizing [sic] type medicine at all times.”  The veteran complained in a December 1984 VA medical examination that his mental condition was becoming worse.  The examiner, however, did not review his nervous system.  In February 1985, the veteran wrote to his Congressman.  On a copy of a supplemental statement of the case which he had sent to his Congressman, the veteran had written: “I have been on nerve medicine for over [twenty-five] years.  This nervous condition is very common in patient[s] as me, who rec[eive]d [an] 80% sub[]total on their stomach[s].  This condition has never been considered in my case.”  The regional office received a copy of this statement in March 1985.  In November 1988, the veteran filed a claim for an increased evaluation of his service-connected condition.  The claim was denied.

On March 30, 1993, the regional office received a claim for an increased rating from the veteran. In February 1994, the regional office received a 1967 Social Security Administration hearing examiner’s report that indicated that a medical examiner had opined that the veteran’s nervous condition was probably aggravated by his stomach problems.  In March 1994, the regional office received a letter from Catherine McDonald, M.D., stating that the veteran’s anxiety and depression were “related” to his gastric problems.  In an April 1994 VA examination report the examiner provided the following diagnosis:  “Major depression, moderate (which seems to be related to his chronic problems with anxiety and depression secondary to his partial gastrectomy in 1953 when he had 80% of his stomach removed).”  The VA regional office granted service connection for anxiety and depression rated as 30% disabling, effective from March 30, 1993.  Following the veteran’s claim (and subsequent appeal) for an earlier effective date, the Board of Veteran’s Appeals (BVA) found that the veteran had not filed a claim, formal or informal, prior to March 30, 1993, for compensation benefits for his nervous disorder.

The veteran argued that he filed a formal or informal application for secondary service connection of his mental disorder in 1964, and that therefore, the effective date for his grant of secondary service connection should relate back to that date.  The Court stated that pursuant to that application, the regional office afforded the veteran a psychiatric examination and determined that his anxiety disorder was a non-service connected condition.  The veteran did not appeal that determination and it became final.  Therefore, the Court concluded, the date of the 1964 application cannot service as the effective date of the recent award of secondary service connection for anxiety disorder based on a later application.

The veteran contended that a review of the record “clearly shows that Mr. Lalonde has consistently complained, at least since 1964, of a nervous condition, which resulted form his gastrointestinal problems.”  The Court pointed out that the veteran’s brief did not point to any communication, besides the 1964 application, that could be construed as a formal or informal claim for benefits prior to 1993.  The BVA found that the statement received by VA in 1985, which the veteran had sent to his Congressman, did not relate the veteran’s nervous disorder to his stomach disorder and did not identify the benefit sought by the veteran.  The BVA also noted that the veteran had filed a specific request for consideration of secondary service connection for bowel obstruction, and therefore did understand how to file a claim for secondary service  connection.

The Court stated that the effective date of an award of service connection is not based on the date of the earliest medical evidence demonstrating a causal connection, but on the date that the application upon which service connection was eventually awarded was filed with VA.  The Court also stated that furthermore, because the veteran had not been granted service connection for his anxiety disorder, the mere receipt of medical records cannot be construed as an informal claim.  See 38 CFR § 3.157; Brannon v. West, 12 Vet. App. 32, 35 (1998).  Finally, the Court pointed out that, without a diagnosis or opinion from a physician regarding a causal connection, it is not reasonable to expect VA to treat a communication regarding an alleged secondary condition as an informal claim.
