Decision Assessment Document
 Osborn v. Nicholson, May 18, 2007, 21 Vet.App. 223
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that interest income received from the redemption of U.S. Savings Bonds is not countable income for VA pension purposes.

The Court explained that the interest income is profit realized from the disposition of “personal property”; therefore, is excluded as countable income pursuant to [38 U.S.C. § 1503(a)(6)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1503) and its implementing regulation, [38 C.F.R. § 3.272(e)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=09cd81ab0fde3bade1c99c39dc90d0d2&node=se38.1.3_1272&rgn=div8).  Note: The statute provides that  “profit realized from the disposition of real or personal property other than in the course of business” be excluded as countable income for pension purposes.

**Impact on VBA:**

We will change the Manual to comply with the Court’s decision.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran was awarded VA disability pension benefits in 1989.  In 2000, the regional office (RO) was notified of the veteran’s receipt of $3,605.00 of unearned income in 1997, which consisted of interest on U.S. Savings Bonds (this amount was later revised to $1,802.00 because the bonds were held jointly with his son [see [38 C.F.R. § 3.271(d)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=09cd81ab0fde3bade1c99c39dc90d0d2&node=se38.1.3_1271&rgn=div8)]).  The RO determined that the veteran’s 1997 pension benefits should be revised to reflect the receipt of interest income from the savings bonds.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in July 2005, determined that interest income on the redemption of U.S. Savings Bonds is countable for purposes of pension benefits because bonds are not personal property that can be sold for profit, but an investment that increases in value by accruing interest.  The Board concluded that there was no provision of law for the exclusion of U.S. Savings Bond interest from countable income.

The veteran argued before the Court that the bonds were personal property and the interest should be excluded from income pursuant to § 3.272(e) as profit realized when he disposed of the bonds.

The Court found that the Board erred in determining that savings bonds are not personal property in view of the plain meaning of the term and the absence of any such authority offered for its findings under [section 1503(a)(6)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1503) and [§ 3.272(e)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=09cd81ab0fde3bade1c99c39dc90d0d2&node=se38.1.3_1272&rgn=div8).

The Court determined that the statute and regulation focus on “disposition” of property from which profit is realized and excluded form income for VA pension purposes.  With a brief discussion of the meaning of “disposition” and the Court’s noting that VA had not limited the scope of the statute by regulation, the Court concluded that in view of the plain meaning of the word, the Board erred by limiting the scope of § 3.272(e) to profit realized solely from the “sale” of property.

Furthermore, the Court noted that § 3.272(e) expanded upon the statute by clarifying that interest is considered as profit under its income exclusions.  The Court reasoned that because the regulation expressly excepts “interest on deferred sales” from the exclusion, the inclusion of interest must have been contemplated by the exclusion in the first instance.  The Court surmised that such a conclusion is consistent with the purposes of the statutory exclusion – to avoid jeopardizing a family’s income due to one time payments resulting from the disposition of property otherwise included as part of the veteran’s net estate.

The Board’s July 2005 decision was reversed and the case was remanded for adjudication consistent with the Court’s holding.
