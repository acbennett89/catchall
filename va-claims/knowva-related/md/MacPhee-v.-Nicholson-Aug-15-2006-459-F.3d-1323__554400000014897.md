Decision Assessment Document
 MacPhee v. Nicholson, Aug 15, 2006, 459 F.3d 1323
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that an informal claim under [38 C.F.R. § 3.157(b)(1)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=1065cca60c116354b2c2ff30f09e7c16&node=se38.1.3_1157&rgn=div8) is a claim to increase or reopen a disability determination only for a condition that has been previously found to be service connected.

A medical examination report will only be considered an informal claim for increase in disability benefits if service connection has already been established for the disability.  Medical reports concerning a disability for which service connection has not been granted cannot be used to trigger § 3.157(b)(1) application in compensation cases.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting Manual change or regulatory revision.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

Service connection was granted for post traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) in 1982 with a 30 percent evaluation, which was reduced to 10 percent in 1986.  The veteran was hospitalized at a VAMC in August 1988 due to excessive drinking and anxiety.  PTSD was the primary diagnosis and the treatment records contained a psychologist’s opinion that it appeared unlikely that the veteran could maintain sobriety unless he was able to deal with his PTSD symptoms.  The September 1988 discharge summary showed diagnoses of PTSD and alcohol dependence.  The RO maintained the 10 percent evaluation; however, the veteran sought increase three years later and was granted a 50 percent evaluation that was subsequently increased to 100 percent effective April 20, 1992.

The veteran contends that he is entitled to a decision based upon an unadjudicated informal claim raised by the 1988 medical records for entitlement to service connection for alcohol dependence secondary to service-connected PTSD.  He alleges that the Veterans Court misinterpreted § 3.157(b)(1) when it failed to remand for adjudication of an alleged informal claim raised by the 1988 medical records for increased disability benefits due to alcohol dependence as secondary to service-connected PTSD, and mistakenly applied [38 C.F.R. § 3.155(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=1065cca60c116354b2c2ff30f09e7c16&node=se38.1.3_1155&rgn=div8). (Note: The Veterans Court concluded that the medical reports were not sufficient to state an original claim under § 3.155(a) as medical evidence reflecting treatment for and diagnoses of an alcohol-related problem does not indicate an intent to apply for secondary service connection for alcohol dependence or to identify the benefit sought. *See Brannon v. West*, 12 Vet.App. 32 (1998).)

The Federal Circuit concluded that the veteran’s interpretation contradicted the plain language of [§ 3.157(b)(1)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=1065cca60c116354b2c2ff30f09e7c16&node=se38.1.3_1157&rgn=div8), which requires the condition identified in the medical examination report to have been previously found to be service connected for application of the regulatory provision.

The Federal Circuit also rejected the veteran’s other arguments, which included averring that [38 C.F.R. § 3.310](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=1065cca60c116354b2c2ff30f09e7c16&node=se38.1.3_1310&rgn=div8) together with § 3.157(b)(1) and VA’s obligation to determine all potential claims raised by the evidence can create an informal claim out of a “potential claim” for increased disability due to alcohol dependence.  This line of reasoning was repudiated as there had been no claim or determination that the alcohol dependence is “proximately due or the result of” PTSD.  Accordingly, the alcohol dependence could not be viewed as part of the veteran’s original PTSD claim.

The veteran’s assertion that VA must give a sympathetic reading to a veteran’s claim by determining all potential claims raised by the evidence was similarly rejected by the Federal Circuit as § 3.157(b)(1) mandates that a medical report can only be considered an informal claim when related to a disability for which service connection has been previously established.

The decision of the Veterans Court was affirmed.
