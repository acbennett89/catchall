Decision Assessment Document
 Spicer v. Shinseki, May 30, 2014, 752 F.3d 1367
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that, under Diagnostic Code [DC 5005](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=1551a35655374430d60d3c862f0cd692&node=se38.1.4_171a&rgn=div8 "Electronic Code of Federal Regulations") and [38 C.F.R.§ 4.45(f)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=1551a35655374430d60d3c862f0cd692&node=se38.1.4_145&rgn=div8 "Electronic Code of Federal Regulations"),  a minor joint group is affected and rated on par with a major joint only when two or more minor joints suffer from limitation of motion.  Accordingly, limitation of motion in a “group of minor joints” requires more than one minor joint suffering from limitation of motion to qualify for the 10 percent rating under DC 5003.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who served from February 1984 to February 1987, suffered a fracture of his left little finger in service, requiring fusing of the finger joint.  In 2007, a VA examiner diagnosed degenerative arthritis of the distal interphalangeal (DIP) joint of the left little finger.  The regional office (RO) granted service connection for the left finger disability, but denied a compensable evaluation.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) denied the appeal for increased evaluation, finding that although the left finger disability was manifested by pain and limitation of motion, the criteria for a compensable evaluation under either DC 5227 or 5230 had not been met.

The Veterans Court affirmed the Board’s decision.  The Veterans Court, citing [§ 4.45(f)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=d2f649f927b5bc67ef98a4cdd9ff50f0&node=se38.1.4_145&rgn=div8), found that the DIP joint is not a major joint or minor joint group for the purpose of rating disabilities from arthritis.  The Veterans Court also concluded that DC 5003 was inapplicable to the facts of the case.

The veteran argued before the Federal Circuit that degenerative arthritis in a single DIP joint results in a “group of minor joints affected by limitation of motion” that warrants a 10 percent rating under DC 5003.  He asserted that a minor joint group is affected when one of its minor members is affected, just as the minor joint group is affected when two or more members are affected.

The Federal Circuit rejected the veteran’s arguments, agreeing with the Secretary that the Veterans Court did not err in interpreting DC 5003 to require limitation of motion in more than one minor joint.  The Federal Circuit held that the plain language of DC 5003, read in view of § 4.45(f), makes clear that a “minor joint group is affected” only when two or more joints suffer limitation of motion.  Thus, DC 5003 clearly requires limitation of motion in two or more interphalangeal joints to warrant a 10 percent rating.

The Federal Circuit affirmed the Veterans Court decision.
