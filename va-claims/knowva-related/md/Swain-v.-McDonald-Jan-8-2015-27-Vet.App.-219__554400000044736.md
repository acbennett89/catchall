Decision Assessment Document
 Swain v. McDonald, Jan 8, 2015, 27 Vet.App. 219
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that 38 C.F.R. § 4.85(a)does not control the effective date for the assignment of a disability evaluation for hearing loss.

Note: 38 C.F.R. § 4.85(a) provides:  “An examination for hearing  impairment for VA purposes must be conducted by a state-licensed audiologist and must include a controlled speech discrimination test (Maryland CNC) and a puretone audiometry report.

**Impact on VBA:**

Manual M21-1, III.iv.4.B.3.k has been revised to implement the Court’s decision.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, in October 2007, filed for an increased evaluation for his service-connected bilateral hearing loss, which had been rated noncompensable since 1955.  A February 2008 VA audiometric examination, utilizing the Maryland CNC test, did not reveal hearing loss to a compensable level.  The regional office (RO), in March 2008, continued the noncompensable evaluation.

The veteran appealed the decision and submitted two private audiometric examination reports from November 2009 and December 2010 that contained diagnoses of “normal precipitously sloping to profound” hearing loss and mild to profound hearing loss in the right ear and mild to severe hearing loss of the left ear.  Neither examination indicated the test used to obtain the results and the veteran’s hearing threshold levels, in decibels, were presented in a graphical format.

Following a Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) remand, a VA audiometric examination using the Maryland CNC test was conducted in June 2013, which revealed a “normal precipitously sloping to profound” bilateral hearing loss.  The examiner opined that the results were consistent with the veteran’s private audiometric tests results from 2009 and 2010.  The RO granted a 10 percent disability rating for bilateral hearing loss, effective June 2013, the date of the VA audiometric examination.  The veteran appealed the effective date and the Board remanded the case to determine the type of hearing tests used in the 2009 and 2010 private audiometric examinations, and to have a VA examiner interpret the numeric results of these examinations.

The 2009 examiner responded that she had used the NU-6 Word List, not the Maryland CNC test.  The 2010 examiner did not respond.  The 2013 VA examiner identified the numeric results obtained in the 2009 and 2010 examinations and opined that they were consistent with the results of the 2013 VA examination.

The Board, in December 2013, noting that disability ratings for hearing loss are required by regulation to be based on the results of a Maryland CNC test, affirmed the RO’s assignment of June 12, 2013, as the effective date for the 10 percent evaluation for hearing loss, as this was the date of the only favorable examination to have used the Maryland CNC test.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board incorrectly interpreted the requirement for using the Maryland CNC test contained in § 4.85 as a requirement for establishing the effective date of an increased disability rating as the date of the test.  The veteran did not argue that the 2009 and 2010 examinations alone were sufficient to support his application for increased benefits, but maintained that the 2013 examiner’s opinion that the 2009 and 2010 examinations were consistent with the 2013 examination was sufficient to support a finding that the effective date of his compensable disability rating should be the date of the 2009 audiometric examination.

The Court, in holding that § 4.85 does not control the effective date of the appellant’s claim, found that neither the plain language of the regulation nor prior case law supported an interpretation that the effective date of a claim for benefits for hearing loss is controlled by § 4.85(a).  The Court concluded that there was no basis for restricting the effective date for hearing loss rating to no earlier than the date of the Maryland CNC test.

The Court stated that unless specifically noted in statute or regulation, 38 U.S.C. §§ 5110 and 38 C.F.R. § 3.400 explicitly govern the effective date for disability compensation claims, including increased rating claims, and the provisions generally tie an effective date to the earliest date a disability or an increase in disability is ascertainable or to the date the claims was received, whichever is later.  The Court noted that specific to an increased rating claim, section 5110(b)(3) provides that  “the effective date of an award of increased compensation shall be the earliest date as of which it is ascertainable that an increase in disability had occurred, if application is received within one year from such date.”

The Court, finding that the ascertained fact is that the veteran had hearing loss as of November 10, 2009, consistent with the June 2013 Maryland CNC test results, and inasmuch as that is the effective date sought by the veteran, reversed the Board’s decision and remanded the case with instructions that the Board assign an effective date of November 10, 2009, for the 10 percent disability rating.
