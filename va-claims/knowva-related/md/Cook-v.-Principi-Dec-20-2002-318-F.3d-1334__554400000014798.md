Decision Assessment Document
 Cook v. Principi, Dec 20, 2002, 318 F.3d 1334
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

In this en banc decision, the Federal Circuit overruled *Hayre v. West* to the extent that it held that the existence of “grave procedural error renders a decision of VA non-final”. In addition, the Court held that a failure of VA to assist the veteran to the extent required by applicable law and regulations cannot constitute CUE under [38 USC 5109A](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5109A).

**Impact on VBA:**

The impact of this decision will be significant and should reduce the likelihood of remands based upon “grave procedural error”.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

In this case, the veteran claimed that he was entitled to an earlier effective date for an award of service connection.  He argued that failure to give him a proper medical examination (at the time of his earlier, denied claim) rendered the RO’s denial of service connection non-final.

In [*Hayre v. West*, 188 F.3d 1327 (Fed. Cir. 1999)](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/188/188.F3d.1327.98-7046.html), the Federal Circuit judicially created a 3rd exception to the finality rule of [38 USC 7105(c)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/7105). By statute, if a veteran does not appeal an RO decision, that decision is final with 2 exceptions: (1) new and material evidence is presented or secured (*see* [38 USC 5108](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5108)); and (2) where clear and unmistakable error is found (*see* [38 USC 5109A(a)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5109A). In *Hayre*, the Court held “where there is a breach of the duty to assist in which the VA failed to obtain pertinent SMRs specifically requested by the claimant and failed to provide the claimant notice explaining the deficiency, the claim does not become final for purposes of appeal”.  In reaching this holding, the Federal Circuit stated that in cases of “grave procedural error” the Veterans Court has consistently held that RO or Board decisions are not final for purposes of direct appeal.  This became a judicially created exception to the rule of finality.  In *Cook*, the Federal Circuit overruled *Hayre* to the extent that it created an additional exception to the rule of finality applicable to VA decisions by reason of “grave procedural error”.

In addition to the holding that there can be no judicially crafted exception to the rule of finality, the Court also held that a breach of the duty to assist the veteran to the extent required by applicable law and regulations cannot constitute CUE under 38 USC 5109A.  In so holding, the Court expressly concluded that a breach of the duty to assist the veteran does not vitiate the finality of an RO decision.
