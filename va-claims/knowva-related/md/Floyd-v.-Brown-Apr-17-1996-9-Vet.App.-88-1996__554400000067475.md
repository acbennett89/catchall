Decision Assessment Document
 Floyd v. Brown, Apr 17, 1996, 9 Vet.App. 88 (1996)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court noted that, in its decision, the Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) evaluated the left mandible condition under two diagnostic codes, 9904 and 9905.  The Board found that the veteran's condition did not warrant an evaluation greater than 10% on a schedular basis.  However, the evidence indicates that pain appears to have affected the veteran's masticatory function, which is a consideration under DC 9904.  Despite the history of pain and loss of masticatory function, the VA examination did not discuss whether that pain had indeed resulted in ratable functional loss.  Therefore, the claim for an increased evaluation for the TMJ pain was remanded for the conduct of a thorough and contemporaneous examination specifically taking into consideration and describing the effect, if any, of pain on functional and masticatory loss.  As the Court pointed out in Schafrath v. Derwinski, 1 Vet. App. 589, the VA is directed, under 38 CFR 4.40 and 4.45, to consider pain in assessing functional loss.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran appealed a May 1992 Board decision that denied an increased schedular rating for service-connected residuals of a fracture of the left mandible with temporomandibular joint pain (TMJ), granted an additional 10% evaluation for the TMJ condition on an extraschedular basis, and did not reopen service connection claims for bilateral hearing loss and bilateral tinnitus secondary to the service-connected left mandible condition.
