Decision Assessment Document
Jensen v. Shulkin, Sep 12, 2017, 29 Vet.App. 66 (2017)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the terms “loss, loss of use” in 38 U.S.C. 2101(a)(2)(B)(i) for the purpose of determining entitlement to specially adapted housing (SAH) is not defined by 38 C.F.R. 3.350(a)(2)(i), which defines loss of use for special monthly compensation (SMC) purposes.

The Court held that loss of use under section 2101(a)(2)(B) exists if a veteran has suffered a deprivation in his ability to use his lower extremity so severe that he is precluded from perambulating without one of the required assistive devices (braces, crutches, canes, or a wheelchair).  Locomotion is precluded even if a veteran is capable of occasionally moving without assistance.

However, the Court also held that, to establish entitlement to SAH benefits, the loss of use due to a permanent and total disability that involves both lower extremities must be so severe that it precludes locomotion without the regular and constant use of assistive devices.

The Court, in addressing the Secretary’s concern that adopting such an interpretation would create automatic SAH eligibility every time an individual is prescribed an assistive device in conjunction with a lower extremity disability, held that that SAH entitlement requires that a veteran must (1) have a permanent and total disability (2) due to a disorder that (3) involves both lower extremities and (4) causes a loss of use so severe that it precludes locomotion without the regular and constant use of assistive devices.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court’s holding will be incorporated into guidance contained in Manual M21-1, IX.i.3.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran’s service-connected disabilities included a right foot fracture, degenerative disc disease of the lumbar spine, and bilateral lower extremity radiculopathy.  Separate 10 percent evaluations are in effect for the neurological deficits of the right and left lower extremities.  He reported that he started using a cane in 2004 and a nurse practitioner wrote in 2008 that the veteran was severely limited in his ability to walk.  A total disability evaluation due to individual unemployability has been in effect since October 2005.

The veteran’s physicians recommended that he attend aqua therapy sessions to alleviate the symptoms of his disabilities, which the veteran found beneficial and believed that it slowed the progression of his disabilities.  He filed an application for a one-time SAH grant of $50,000 to defray the cost of installing a heated therapy pool in his home.  The regional office denied the claim in 2008.

In November 2010, a VA examiner indicated “gnawing” low back pain that intensified during the day.  The veteran used a cane and brace to walk, had an antalgic gait, and was unable to walk more than a few yards.  Following a Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) remand, the veteran underwent another examination in September 2012.  The examiner reported that the veteran had mild constant pain, moderate intermittent pain, moderate paresthesias and/or dysesthesias, and mild numbness of his lower extremities.  The examiner noted that the veteran used a cane, but no limp or favoring of a leg was shown.

The Board, in October 2015, concluded that locomotion was precluded without the aid of braces, crutches, cane, or wheelchair.  However, the Board denied entitlement to SAH benefits because it determined that the service-connected disabilities did not result in loss of use of the lower extremities.

The Court, in reviewing the statutory and regulatory history and other interpretative factors, concluded that the definition of loss of use under section 2101(a)(2)(B)(i) is not the same definition for loss of use under 3.350(a)(2)(i).  The Court stated that the most apparent reason that the SMC regulation does not define the terms for SAH in section 2101(a)(2)(B)(i) is that the two provisions apply to different abnormalities, as section 2101(a)(2)(B)(i) sets the standard for awarding SAH to a veteran suffering from loss of use of the lower extremities, while 3.350(a)(2)(i) defines loss of use of a foot.

The Court also defined “loss of use” for SAH purposes under section 2101(a)(2)(B) as existing when a veteran has suffered a deprivation in his ability to use his lower extremity is so severe that he is precluded from perambulating without one of the required assistive devices.  The Court indicated that the definition includes locomotion that is precluded even if a veteran is capable on occasion of moving about unaided.

The Court concluded that the veteran had been deprived of his lower extremities to such a degree that locomotion is precluded.  The Court also noted that the Board had explicitly or implicitly conceded that the veteran met all other SAH requirements.  Accordingly, the Court reversed the Board’s decision and directed that the veteran be provided with the SAH benefits.
