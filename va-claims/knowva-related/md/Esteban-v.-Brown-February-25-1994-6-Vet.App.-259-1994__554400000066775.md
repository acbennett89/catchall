Decision Assessment Document
 Esteban v. Brown, February 25, 1994, 6 Vet.App. 259 (1994)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that even if the diagnoses at issue are of the same disability, different symptoms of impairment associated with a disability may be rated separately as long as this does not constitute twice rating the same manifestation.  The critical element is that none of the symptomatology for any one of these three conditions is duplicative of or overlapping with the symptomatology of the other two conditions.

**Impact on VBA:**

The guidance contained in the Court's holding has been incorporated into the Manual M21-1, III.iv.5.C.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran asserted that he is entitled to separate ratings for three facial problems:  disfiguring scars; painful scars; and facial muscle damage resulting in mastication problems.  The Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) conceded that the veteran may be rated as 10 percent disabling under any of the three diagnostic codes associated with these conditions, but refused to grant appellant separate ratings under DC 7804 (painful scars) or under DC 5325 (facial injury; 10 percent rating “if interfering to any extent with mastication”), apart from the assigned 10 percent evaluation under DC 7800.  Thus, as none of the three DCs in question provide that a veteran may not be rated separately for the described conditions, pursuant to 38 C.F.R. 4.25, appellant’s conditions are to be rated separately unless they constitute the “same disability” or the “same manifestation” under 38 C.F.R. 4.14.

The Board concluded that the veteran was  only entitled to a 10 percent rating for moderately disfiguring scars under DC 7800.  However, the Court observed that the condition embodied under DC 7800 is entirely cosmetic in nature.  Such rating does not contain any component of pain or muscle damage.  The critical element is that none of the symptomatology for any one of these three conditions is duplicative of or overlapping with the symptomatology of the other two conditions.  the Court found the veteran's symptomatology to be distinct and separate: disfigurement; painful scars; and facial muscle damage resulting in problems with mastication.  Thus, as a matter of law, the Court concluded that the veteran was entitled to combine his 10 percent rating for disfigurement under DC 7800 with an additional 10 percent rating for tender and painful scars under DC 7804 and a third 10 percent rating for facial muscle injury interfering with mastication under DC 5235.

The Court has essentially held that even if the diagnoses at issue are of the same disability, different symptoms of impairment associated with a disability may be rated separately as long as this does not constitute twice rating the same manifestation.
