Decision Assessment Document
Williams v. Wilkie, Aug 7, 2018, 30 Vet.App. 134 (2018)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that “deformity” under 38 C.F.R. 4.115b, Diagnostic Code (DC) 7522, means a distortion of the penis, either internal or external.

**Impact on VBA:**

Manual M21-1, III.iv.4.I will be revised to comply with the Court’s holding.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who served in Vietnam, was awarded service connection for prostate cancer in December 2006.  In June 2007, the veteran underwent a radical prostatectomy resulting in severe erectile dysfunction (ED).  The following month, he was awarded special monthly compensation (SMC) for the loss of use of a creative organ.  In March 2008, service connection for ED was awarded with a noncompensable rating.  The veteran appealed the evaluation.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) denied a compensable evaluation in July 2012; however, the Court remanded the case in January 2015 for a medical examination, as the Secretary conceded that VA had not provided an examination that addressed whether the veteran had an internal deformity.

The veteran underwent a VA examination in December 2015.  The examiner concluded that there was no evidence of penile deformity and there was no specific tests for “internal deformity” of the penis that would show evidence of nerve damage.  The examiner noted that nerve damage generally does not cause deformities, but loss of function, and ED is generally not associated with any deformity.  The RO continued the denial of the compensable evaluation in a decision issued the same month.  The veteran responded that the December 2015 examination was inadequate.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in a June 2016 decision, denied entitlement to a compensable evaluation for ED, as it concluded that there was no penile deformity.  The Board referenced the M21-1 Adjudication Procedures Manual, which indicated that “a compensable rating under [DC] 7522 is not warranted in the absence of deformity, and instructs that such deformity be ‘evident.’”  The Board also found that awarding a compensable evaluation for ED, regardless of the severity, under DC 7522 would constitute impermissible pyramiding because the veteran was already in receipt of SMC benefits for the loss of use of creative organ.

The veteran argued before the Court that he suffers from internal and external penile deformities; thus, the Board clearly erred in denying a 20 percent evaluation under DC 7522.  He also contended that the Board failed to discuss the definition of “deformity” or “evident” found in the Manual M21-1, and instead relied on an inadequate December 2015 VA examination to support its determination.

At the outset, the Court agreed with both parties that the Board erred by concluding that awarding compensation under DC 7522 in addition to SMC for loss of use of a creative organ would constitute impermissible pyramiding.  VA’s regulations clearly provide that a veteran is not barred from receiving SMC under 38 U.S.C. 1114(k) in addition to schedular compensation for a disability.

The Court, noting that VA has not expressly defined the term “deformity” under DC 7522, indicated that it would assign the ordinary meaning to the world.  The Court, referencing Dorland’s Medical Dictionary, which defines deformity as “distortion of any part or general disfigurement of the body,” held that deformity under DC 7522 means a distortion of the penis, either internal or external.

The Court found that the Board failed to address whether severed nerves caused by a radical prostatectomy meet the definition of penile deformity.  Accordingly, the Court remanded the case for the Board to apply the ordinary meaning of deformity, and determine whether the veteran is entitled to a compensable rating under DC 7522.
