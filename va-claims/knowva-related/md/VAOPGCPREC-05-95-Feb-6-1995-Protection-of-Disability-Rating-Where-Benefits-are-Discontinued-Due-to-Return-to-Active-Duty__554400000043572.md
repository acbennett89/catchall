Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 05-95, Feb 6, 1995, Protection of Disability Rating Where Benefits are Discontinued Due to Return to Active Duty
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Do the provisions of 38 U.S.C. § 110 and 38 C.F.R. § 3.951, as interpreted by the Court of Veterans Appeals (CVA) in Salgado v. Brown, 4 Vet. App. 316 (1993), protect a disability rating established over twenty years ago, where compensation was discontinued upon the veteran's reentry into active service shortly after the rating was established and was not reinstated upon the veteran’s discharge from service?

**Impact on VBA**

Under 38 U.S.C. § 110, a disability which has been continuously rated at or above a particular evaluation for twenty or more years for compensation purposes cannot thereafter be rated at less than that evaluation, in the absence of fraud.  The protection provided by this statute, however, is dependent upon the disability being "continuously rated" at or above the level in question.  Where compensation is discontinued following reentry into active service in accordance with the statutory prohibition on payment of compensation for a period in which an individual received active-service pay, the continuity of the rating is interrupted for purposes of the rating protection provisions of 38 U.S.C. § 110 and the disability cannot be considered to have been continuously rated during the period in which compensation is discontinued.

**Summary on facts and findings**

The veteran was awarded a 30% evaluation for service-connected malaria in March 1946.  In August 1946, he reentered active service and the award was discontinued.  He was discharged from service in August 1949.  He attempted to reopen his claim in December 1969, and was advised to submit evidence of recent activity of malaria.  He did not pursue the claim.  He again requested reinstatement of his compensation in September 1975 and was again advised to submit evidence that malaria had recurred.  He again did not pursue the claim.  In O.G.C. Prec. 31-90, the General Counsel held that the provisions of 38 U.S.C. § 110 "protects against reduction only those ratings for compensation purposes which have been the foundation for the payment of awards for a period of at least 20 years."  In decision of the United States Court of Veterans Affairs (the Court) in Salgado v. Brown, 4 Vet. App. 316 (1993), the Court rejected that conclusion holding that section 110 confers protection regardless of whether a monetary award had actually been paid.  Implicit in the holding was the conclusion that section 110 protects the status of a disability evaluation, not the payment of monetary benefits based on that status.  The Salgado case involved a disability evaluation which had been in effect for more than 20 years, but compensation was not paid because the veteran failed to waive military retired pay.  In deciding this issue, the General Counsel noted that Salgado relied on the Court's interpretation of the phrase "for compensation purposes" in section 110, but did not address the meaning of the phrase "continuously rated."  General Counsel further held that the prohibition in 38 U.S.C. § 5304(c) against payment of compensation for any period in which a person receives active service pay is suggestive of an interruption in the veteran's rating, "in that there can be no rating in effect for compensation purposes in the absence of underlying eligibility for compensation.  The situation in this case may be distinguished from the situation in Salgado in that the veteran in Salgado could have elected to receive compensation benefits at any time in lieu of military retired pay.  Additionally, the stop award action taken in this case interrupted the continuity of the veteran's rating since discontinuance of the award was based on a determination of ineligibility for compensation.  General Counsel also noted that a review of VA regulations leads to the conclusion that reentry on active duty results in termination of the veteran's award and accompanying disability rating.  This case was also noted to be factually distinguishable from Salgado in that Mr. Salgado's rating had been confirmed and continued no fewer that eight times over a period of some twenty three years, whereas the veteran in this case declined the VA's invitations to be examined or to otherwise verify that symptoms of malaria persisted.  "To conclude that such a rating must be protected would be to suggest that veterans could, by their failure to comply with legitimate requests for necessary information, maintain their ratings at unjustifiably high levels."

Amend M21-1, Part VI, par. 9.02e to delete the reference to protection under section 110 and 38 C.F.R. § 3.951(b) accruing to cases in which the veteran returns to active duty.  Announce during the next Judicial Review Conference Call that Addendum I to the May 6, 1993, conference call should be changed to reflect this decision.
