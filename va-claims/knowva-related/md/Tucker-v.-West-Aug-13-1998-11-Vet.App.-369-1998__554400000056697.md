Decision Assessment Document
 Tucker v. West, Aug 13, 1998, 11 Vet.App. 369 (1998)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the relevant inquiry in determining entitlement to special monthly compensation (SMC) based on loss of use of a foot under 38 C.F.R. 3.350(a)(2) and 4.63 is not whether amputation is warranted, but whether the effective function remaining was other than that which would be equally well served by an amputation with the use of a suitable prosthetic appliance.

The Court held that the Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) applied the incorrect standard and erred in concluding that because the veteran's situation did "not warrant amputation" that he was not entitled to SMC.

The Court also held that, pursuant to 38 C.F.R. 4.40, the Board was also required to consider the impact of pain in making its determination regarding loss of use of the lower extremities and articulate how pain was factored into that decision.

**Impact on VBA:**

Historical Manual M21-1, Part VI, Paragraphs 8.04 and 8.07 were revised in accordance with the Court's holding.  This guidance is presently found under Manual M21-1, IV.ii.2.H.1.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran served in the U.S. Marine Corps from June 1967 to July 1969.  In 1968 he stepped on an explosive device while on patrol in Vietnam, receiving multiple shrapnel wounds to all extremities and the right eye.  In January 1970, the regional office (RO) determined that he did not qualify for SMC based on permanent loss of use of one or both legs.  His claim for SMC was reopened in May 1980.  A July 1980 VA medical examination noted that he “has pain in both feet” and “cannot balance without crutches.”  The VA physician made the observation that the calluses of his hands made using the crutches difficult.  The claimant was bearing weight mainly with his crutches since the right foot was too painful to touch the ground and the left foot had limited motion and was also painful.  The burden of his weight was carried with his upper extremities and shoulders.  An October 1980 RO rating decision awarded the appellant 10% disability for the development of calluses secondary to his service-connected leg disability.  No decision was made on the reopened SMC claim.

On October 24, 1990, a claim for SMC based on a long history of loss of use of both legs was received.  After considering the results of a November 1990 VA examination, the RO determined that the evidence “does not show the existence of no effective function remaining in the veteran’s bilateral lower extremities” and concluded that entitlement to SMC was not established.  The appellant filed a Notice of Disagreement (NOD).  Following a hearing in July 1991, he was given a VA disability examination specifically to determine whether he had lost the use of both legs.  In October 1991, the hearing officer found that the appellant qualified for SMC effective October 24, 1990.

In January 1992, the appellant requested an earlier effective date for SMC.  The RO accepted this as an NOD and issued a Statement of the Case (SOC).  The appellant filed an appeal to the Board, and a personal hearing was conducted in January 1993.  In 1994, the Board remanded the matter to the RO to determine “whether all the prior rating decisions not addressing the issue of entitlement to [SMC] on account of loss of use of both lower extremities were erroneous.”  The RO’s April 1995 decision denied an earlier effective date for SMC.

In the decision currently under appeal, the Board denied the appellant entitlement to SMC earlier than October 24, 1990, on the basis that “it had not been shown that there was no effective function remaining in either of the veteran’s lower extremities.”  The Board’s decision then dismissed the findings of the July 1980 examination, stating, "An October 1980 rating decision failed to address the issue of entitlement to special monthly compensation on the basis of the loss of use of the lower extremities."
