Decision Assessment Document
 Mauerhan v. Principi, Oct 30, 2002, 16 Vet.App. 436
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court rejected the veteran’s argument that the DSM-IV criteria should be the exclusive basis for rating service-connected post traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and held that [38 C.F.R. 4.130](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=fd77f92fa4e5e08257433ce4a356941d&node=se38.1.4_1130&rgn=div8) “is a reasonable and permissible construction of [38 U.S.C. 1155](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1155)”.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact upon VBA.  Good training tool.

**Summary of the facts and **Court’s reasons**:**

The veteran, who was evaluated as 30% disabled under diagnostic code 9411 (found in section 4.130) argued that the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA) erred in relying on the 30% and 50% rating criteria listed under 38 C.F.R. 4.130, as requirements rather than examples, because those criteria apply to all mental disorders.  Instead, he claimed that the BVA should have considered the criteria specific to PTSD set forth in the DSM-IV.  He contended that many of the symptoms listed in section 4.130 are not found in the DSM-IV as being associated with PTSD and, as a result, do not apply to PTSD claims.

The veteran’s assertion that the symptoms listed in DSM-IV should, in effect, replace the criteria listed in DC 9440 as the basis for rating PTSD claims is not supported by a plain reading of the regulation.  The Court noted that although section 4.130 makes clear than an understanding of the DSM-IV is essential for properly applying the rating formula, nowhere does it state that consideration of the DSM-IV in any way relieves VA from fully considering the specific rating criteria outlined in 38 C.F.R. 4.130 when rating the severity of a claimant’s disorder.

The Secretary acted within his authority to adopt and apply a schedule of ratings when he chose to create one general rating formula for mental disorders in [38 C.F.R. 4.130](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=fd77f92fa4e5e08257433ce4a356941d&node=se38.1.4_1130&rgn=div8). The Court pointed out that the use of the term “such as” (in section 4.130) demonstrates that the symptoms after that phrase are not intended to constitute an exhaustive list, but rather are to serve as examples of the type and degree of the symptoms, or their effects, that would justify a particular rating.  Accordingly, the evidence considered in determining the level of impairment under 38 C.F.R. 4.130 is not restricted to the symptoms provided in the diagnostic code.  Instead, the rating specialist is to consider all symptoms of a claimant’s condition that affect the level of occupational and social impairment, including, if applicable, those identified in the DSM-IV.
