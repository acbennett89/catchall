Decision Assessment Document
Ricafort v. Nicholson, May 7, 2007, 21 Vet.App. 198
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that [38 C.F.R. § 3.669(d)(1)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=09cd81ab0fde3bade1c99c39dc90d0d2&node=se38.1.3_1669&rgn=div8) prohibits assignment of an effective date for restoration of benefits earlier than the date of the pardon.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran had recognized guerrilla service in the Army of the Philippines from 1943 to 1945.  In January 1986, he submitted a claim for additional benefits for a child he claimed as a son and dependent.  At the time the veteran was entitled to a 20 percent disability compensation rating.  The regional office (RO) undertook a field examination to determine if the veteran was the father of the claimed dependent.  The RO discovered that the veteran was actually the child’s uncle, the child was not a dependent, and that the veteran was not the father of three other children for which he was receiving compensation.

The RO concluded there was sufficient evidence for forfeiture consideration.  In July 1987, Compensation and Pension Service issued a decision that the veteran had forfeited his benefits based on a finding that the veteran knowingly furnished fraudulent evidence with the intention of deceiving and defrauding VA by his attempt to obtain benefits to which he had no legal entitlement.  The veteran filed a timely Notice of Disagreement; however, the RO did not issue the Statement of the Case until March 1999.  A Substantive Appeal was not timely filed.

In February 2001, the veteran submitted a statement to the RO indicating that he had been granted a full Presidential pardon for his attempt to defraud VA and requesting reinstatement of his compensation retroactive to July 1987.  The RO resumed the veteran’s monthly compensation effective January 20, 2001, the date of the Presidential pardon, with a payment date of February 1, 2001.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) upheld the RO decision in April 2004.

The veteran argued before the Court that he is entitled to an effective date of July 20, 1987, as the effective date for restoration of his benefits because the Presidential pardon was “full and unconditional.”

The Court concluded that [§ 3.669(d)(1)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=09cd81ab0fde3bade1c99c39dc90d0d2&node=se38.1.3_1669&rgn=div8) specifically and unambiguously prohibits the assignment of an effective date earlier than the pardon.

The Board decision was affirmed.
