Decision Assessment Document
 Singleton v. Shinseki, Mar 25, 2010, 23 Vet.App. 376
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the procedural protections regarding reduction of stabilized evaluations under [38 C.F.R. § 3.344](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=8534e547f6bda2793816ea2a3f17956b&node=se38.1.3_1344&rgn=div8) only apply to prospective rating reductions and are inapplicable to retroactively assigned staged disability ratings.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in December 2007, assigned the following staged ratings for the veteran’s service-connected mental disorder:  50 percent from April 11, 1980, to December 9, 1980; 100 percent from December 10, 1980, to October 31, 1991; 70 percent from November 1, 1991, to December 28, 2000; and 100 percent from December 29, 2000.

The veteran argued before the Court that because he was assigned a 100 percent staged rating for more than five years (December 1980 to October 1991), the disability rating was stabilized under [§ 3.344(c)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=8534e547f6bda2793816ea2a3f17956b&node=se38.1.3_1344&rgn=div8), and could not be reduced without VA first providing him a full and complete medical examination that supported such a reduction.

The Secretary, contending that the regulation was inapplicable to staged ratings, asserted that the purpose behind § 3.344 was to protect a veteran, who was reliant on his disability compensation, from an arbitrary reduction of his disability rating.  Where benefits are reduced retrospectively in the staged ratings context, there is no danger that a veteran will be deprived of income that he or she is accustomed to using to meet day-to-day expenses.

The Court, finding the Secretary’s interpretation of § 3.344 as not “plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation,” granted deference to the Secretary’s position that § 3.344 was inapplicable to the staged disability-ratings context.

The Court affirmed the Board’s December 2007 decision.
