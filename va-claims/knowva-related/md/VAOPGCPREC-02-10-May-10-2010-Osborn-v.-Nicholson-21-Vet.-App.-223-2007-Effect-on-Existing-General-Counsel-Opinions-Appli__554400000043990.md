Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 02-10, May 10, 2010, Osborn v. Nicholson, 21 Vet. App. 223 (2007) - Effect on Existing General Counsel Opinions, Application to Other Benefit Programs
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

General Counsel held that VA, in applying the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court) holding in Osborn v. Nicholson, 21 Vet.App. 223 (2007) when computing income for improved pension purposes under 38 U.S.C. § 1503(a)(6), must exclude the interest received from the surrender of a life insurance policy from income as profit realized from the disposition of personal property other than in the course of business.

The Court’s holding in Osborn that interest received from the redemption of a Series EE U.S. Savings Bond is excludable from annual income computations under section 1503(a)(6) also applies to parents’ dependency and indemnity compensation (DIC) and section 306 pension, but not to annual income calculations for old-law pension.

Interest paid to a holder of a Series HH U.S. Savings Bond is counted as income for purposes of improved pension, section 306 pension, old-law pension, and parents’ DIC because the interest is paid semiannually without redemption of the bond.

General Counsel also held that it is not possible to provide a single definitive rule as to whether Osborn applies to municipal bonds because debt obligations issued by states, municipalities, or other political entities vary.  However, as a general rule, if a bond requires redemption for the payments of accrued interest, as with a Series EE U.S. Savings Bond, then the statutory exclusion for profit realized from the disposition of real or personal property applies.  If accrued interest is payable on the bond without redemption, then it does not qualify for the exclusion.

**Impact on VBA**

M21-1MR, V.iii.1.I.58.v; V.iii.1.C.14.c; V.iii.1.D.27.a and V.iii.1.I.58.t will be revised in accordance with the General Counsel opinion.

**Summary on facts and findings**

In Osborn, the Court held that accrued interest received from the redemption of a Series EE U.S. Savings Bond was profit realized from the disposition of personal property other than in the course of business with the meaning of section 1503(a)(6) and 38 C.F.R. § 3.272(e) and must be excluded from income for purpose of determining entitlement to VA improved pension.

General Counsel concluded that the Court’s holding did not invalidate or change General Counsel Precedent Opinions 4-89, 23-90, 1-97, 10-97, or 15-97.  However, the Osborn decision conflicted with General Precedent Opinion 1-93, which held that:  (1) proceeds of a life insurance policy that is surrendered for cash should not be considered income for purposes of determining entitlement to improved pension under title 38, United States Code, to the extent that such proceeds consist of return of sums paid as part of the insurance premiums; but (2) interest on the policy holder’s monetary contribution should be considered income.

General Counsel explained that the inapplicability of the Osborn exclusion for calculating income from interest from Series EE U.S. Savings Bonds is not extended to old-law pension because its governing law does not provide an exclusion for “profit realized from the disposition of real or personal property other than in the course of a business.”  The laws pertinent to section 306-pension and parents’ DIC contain an exclusion.

The applicability of the exclusion in section 1503(a)(6) requires a realization of profit from the disposition of property; however, since accrued interest is not paid upon the redemption of a Series HH U.S. Savings Bond, General Counsel concluded that Osborn was not controlling with regard to interest paid on Series HH bonds.

General Counsel noted that municipal bonds generally pay interest semiannually; thus, Osborn would not apply to such bonds for the same reason it was concluded that the holding was inapplicable to Series HH U.S. Savings Bonds.  Such interest would be counted as income for purposes of old-law pension, section 306 pension, improved pension, and parents’ DIC.  However, if the accrued interest were payable only upon redemption of a municipal bond, then Osborn would apply, and the exclusion for profit realized from the disposition of real or personal property would be applicable.
