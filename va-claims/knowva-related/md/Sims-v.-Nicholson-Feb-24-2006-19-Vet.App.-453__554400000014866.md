Decision Assessment Document
 Sims v. Nicholson, Feb 24, 2006, 19 Vet.App. 453
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the VCAA does not apply to a veteran’s request for restoration of competency status.  The Court explained that an application for competency restoration does not constitute a “claim” for benefits under Chapter 51 of Title 38, but, rather, is a petition concerning the manner of distribution of benefits under Chapter 55.

Also, the Court concluded that the current version of [38 C.F.R. § 3.353](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=bab86542e1a00b34e41771028e831f8f&node=se38.1.3_1353&rgn=div8) relegates the role of  the Veterans Service Officer (VSO) in such cases to developing information and referring new information concerning competency to the rating board (VSO is now commonly known as the Veterans Service Center Manager).  Determinations concerning competency are reserved to the rating board.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting Manual revision or regulatory changes.  The Court provided an in-depth discussion of 38 CFR § 3.353 and found no inadequacy with the current regulation.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran was granted service connection for schizophrenia in February 1995 and awarded a 100 percent evaluation.  The RO also determined the veteran to not be competent to receive direct payment of VA benefits.

Requests to reconsider competency status were received in 2000 and 2001.  Competency status was not restored and the RO’s decisions were upheld by the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (BVA) in January 2004.

VA examinations, field examinations, a private psychiatric examination, and mental health clinic records were discussed by the BVA in denying the petition for competency restoration.  BVA only noted one report by a private physician in support of competency; however, the BVA designated this evidence to have less weight than the other evidence, which was unfavorable, as the physician did not review the veteran’s claims file.

The veteran, arguing for remand, claimed BVA failed to ensure VCAA compliance and neglected to discuss the presumption in favor of competency under 38 C.F.R. § 3.353(d).  The veteran also alleged that a VSO was not involved in adjudication as required by *Coleman v. Brown*, 5 Vet.App. 371, 374 (1993).

The Court held that the VCAA is inapplicable because a petition for competency restoration does not qualify as a claim for benefits.  Appellant had already received a benefit, and only the determination of how it would be distributed was still in question.

Rejecting the VSO involvement claim under *Coleman*, the Court noted that VA amended § 3.353 in 1995.  The Court’s opinion referenced VA’s explanatory statement for revising the regulation, which stated that while the VSO was intended to have an integral role in developing evidence concerning competency cases, the regulation’s intent was to give rating boards the sole responsibility for incompetency determinations without VSO participation.  The current regulation clearly illustrates that the role of the VSO is relegated to developing and reporting new information that may be capable of demonstrating competency.  Contrary to the veteran’s assertion, the Court found the VSCM to have developed information concerning the veteran’s claim for competency; however, new information regarding competency was not obtained.

The Court also rejected the veteran’s assertion that the BVA failed to discuss the presumption in favor of competency under [38 C.F.R. § 3.353](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=bab86542e1a00b34e41771028e831f8f&node=se38.1.3_1353&rgn=div8), which provides a resolution in favor of competency where reasonable doubt arises regarding  mental capacity to manage one’s affairs.  The Court held that, when there is no reasonable doubt as to the competency of a claimant, the Board has no obligation to fully discuss the presumption in favor of competency in its decision.  BVA concluded that there was not an approximate balance of positive and negative evidence to trigger the presumption of competency under § 3.353(d); thus, the veteran was adjudged as remaining incompetent for VA purposes.  The Court found BVA to have sufficiently explained its rationale for finding the medical reports and opinions adequate to determine that the preponderance of the evidence demonstrated continued incompetence.  The BVA decision was affirmed.
