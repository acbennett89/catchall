Decision Assessment Document
Langdon v. Wilkie, Feb 5, 2020, No. 32 Vet.App. 291 (2020)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the General Rating Formula for Disease and Injuries of the Spine under 38 C.F.R. 4.71a requires that a disability be service connected before it can be compensated through the rating schedule; therefore, the thoracolumbar spine is rated together when both parts are service connected or the impairment caused by the two segments cannot be separated.

In the present case, the Court held that the functional impairment caused by the veteran’s non-service-connected lumbar spine disability cannot be considered when rating his service-connected thoracic spine disability, as there is medical evidence distinguishing between impairments caused by the thoracic and lumbar spine disabilities.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court’s holding will be incorporated into the Manual M21-1 at III.iv.5.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

In July 2009, the veteran filed for service connection for a spine condition involving the lumbar and thoracic regions.  The regional office (RO), in September 2009, granted service connection for a thoracic spine condition with a noncompensable evaluation.  The veteran appealed the evaluation and the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) remanded the thoracic spine claim for a new examination.  The Board also determined that the RO had not adjudicated the veteran’s claim for service connection for a lumbar spine disability and directed the RO to adjudicate the lumbar spine claim before readjudicating the thoracic spine rating.

In a May 2016 VA examination report, the examiner opined that the lumbar spine disability was less likely than not related to an in-service lumbar strain.  The examiner commented that there was no current functional limitation related to the thoracic condition and that the decreased range of motion was caused by or related to the lumbar spine condition.  In an August 2016 decision, the RO denied service connection for a lumbar spine condition.  No appellate action was taken by the veteran in response to the decision.

The Board, in November 2017, awarded a 10 percent evaluation for the thoracic spine condition on the basis of subjective reports of painful motion pursuant to 38 C.F.R.  4.45 and 4.59.  The Board, in denying a higher evaluation, relied on the May 2016 VA examination report that had found no functional limitation due to his thoracic spine disability and no pain on examination to include after repetitive motion, as well as no other symptoms approximately a rating in excess of 10 percent.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board incorrectly applied Diagnostic Code (DC) 5237 in denying a disability evaluation greater than 10 percent, as the plain language of DC 5237 considers the thoracic and lumbar spine as a single unit and that the Board was required to address them as such, rather than separating the symptoms associated with each.  He alleges that under this interpretation he is entitled to a 20 percent evaluation because the evidence shows forward flexion not greater than 60 degrees.  The veteran maintains that under the 2003 amendment to the general formula for the spine, it is clear that DC 5237 requires that his thoracic and lumbar spine disabilities must be considered together.

The Court rejected the veteran’s argument, holding that it is clear that the rating schedule is meant to compensate only service-connected disabilities.  The Court observed that although DC 5237 calls for the thoracic and lumbar spines generally to be rated as a unit, it does not mandate that they be rated together.  DC 5237 provides for such unitary treatment only when both segments of the spine are service connected or when it is not possible to separate the functional limitations of an injury and assign them to each part of the spine when only one part is service connected.  See Mittleider v. West, 11 Vet.App. 181 (1998).

The Court characterized the veteran’s argument as asking the Court to skip over establishing service connection for his lumbar spine and direct VA to compensate him for symptoms caused by a condition for which he has been denied service connection.  The Court noted that the logically up-stream element of service-connectedness is distinct from the logically downstream element of compensation level.

In addressing the 2003 revisions to the rating schedule, the Court rejected the veteran’s argument that the change shows that the Secretary intended for the thoracic and lumbar spine to constitute one disability for rating purposes, regardless of which is service connected.  The Court agreed with the Secretary that the revision provided that in most instances, the thoracic and lumbar spine will be considered together but leaves open the possibility that they could be separated, as in this case.  The Court found that, in the present case, there is unchallenged medical evidence attributing the veteran’s functional impairment solely to his nonservice-connected lumbar section of the spine.

The Court also noted that the 2003 revision was consistent with its decision in Mittleider in which it held that when it is not possible to separate the effects of the service-connected and nonservice-connected condition, signs and symptoms should be attributed to the service-connected condition.  Thus, in amending the rating schedule, VA put Mittleider into effect by recognizing that it would be clinically difficult to expect a medical professional to separate the limitations flowing from injuries to different portions of the spine.  However, no such difficulties were shown in the present case.  The Court concluded that under the logic of Mittleider and the permissive language of the 2003 amendment, the Board was under no obligation to consider the effects of the veteran’s thoracic and lumbar spine disabilities together, and it was not error for the Board to rely on medical evidence that separated the two disabilities.

The Court affirmed the Board’s November 2017 decision.
