Decision Assessment Document
Philbrook v. Wilkie, May 19, 2020, 32 Vet.App. 342 (2020)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that 38 U.S.C. 5313(c) precludes the award of a total disability evaluation due to individual unemployability (TDIU) to a veteran while confined in a state mental institution following the conviction of a felony.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court’s holding will be incorporated into guidance in the Manual M21-1 at IV.ii.2.F and III.v.8.A.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, upon discharge from service in 2004, was awarded service connection for posttraumatic stress disorder with a 70 percent evaluation.  In October 2009, he was charged with attempted murder, first-degree assault, and unlawful use of a weapon.  In April 2011, an Oregon State court determined that the veteran’s actions constituted the crime of attempted murder, a class A felony.

The state court found the veteran “guilty except for insanity of the crime of attempted murder.”  Under Oregon law, a person “guilty except for insanity if, as a result of the mental disease or defect at the time of engaging in criminal conduct, the person lacks substantial capacity either to appreciate the criminality of the conduct or to conform the conduct to the requirements of law.”  In view of the veteran’s danger to others, the court ordered the veteran for “care, custody, and treatment” for a maximum period not to exceed 20 years and committed him to the custody of the Oregon State Hospital.

While in custody, the veteran filed for a TDIU evaluation in September 2011 and March 2013.  Following denial of the claim at the regional office, the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in June 2018, finding that the veteran had been continuously incarcerated for a felony since October 2009, concluded that section 5313(c), as implemented by 38 C.F.R. § 3.341(b), prohibited TDIU entitlement as a matter of law.

While the appeal was pending at the Court, the Court was notified that the veteran was released from the Oregon State Hospital in April 2018 and a decision review officer later awarded a TDIU evaluation from April 2018.  However, the Court determined it still retained jurisdiction over the issue as the veteran sought TDIU entitlement for a specific date, chiefly the date prior to his release date in April 2018 when he was incarcerated, which did not render the issue moot.

The veteran argued before the Court that section 5313(c) did not apply to him on the basis that he was not confined in a jail or prison, rather his confinement was in a state hospital.

The Court rejected this argument, concluding that the plain language of section 5313(c) coheres with the purpose of the statute, which is to correct the perceived problem of paying benefits to veterans while incarcerated when at the same time monies are being paid to maintain the same individuals in penal institutions.

The Court concluded that there was nothing in the plain language of the statute that would exclude the veteran from its prohibition of a TDIU award while confined to a state hospital.

The Court indicated that both parties appeal to state law was unnecessary, as absent a plain indication to the contrary, when Congress enacts a statute, it is not making the application of the federal act dependent upon state law.  The Court concluded that there was no indication that Congress wanted section 5313’s application to be dependent on state law, as it did in other veterans law contexts, such as the validity of marriage under 38 U.S.C. 103(c).  Furthermore, the Court stated that making the statute’s application dependent on the varying terms or specific practices employed by states in their criminal justice and penal systems would impede the statute’s purpose, to avoid duplicative Government expenditures that would result in a windfall for those convicted of felonies.

The Court held that under the plain terms of the statute, the veteran, while confined at taxpayer expense at a state hospital after being adjudged guilty except for insanity of attempted murder, fell within the TDU prohibition provision of the statute.

The Court affirmed the Board decision.
