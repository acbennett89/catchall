Decision Assessment Document
 Harper v. Brown, Mar 4, 1997, 10 Vet.App. 125 (1997)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the effective date of an increased rating would be the date of claim only if the increase in disability occurred more than one year prior to receipt of the claim.

**Impact on VBA:**

The guidance from the Court's holding is contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.5.C.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran served in the U.S. Army from June 1980 to April 1981.  In June 1984, he was granted service connection for chronic schizophrenia, rated at 30% disabling, effective December 1981.  On July 16, 1990, the veteran submitted a claim to a regional office (RO) for an increased rating for his chronic schizophrenia.  On June 15, 1991, the appellant went to an emergency room "complaining of depression, many problems, homicidal and suicidal thoughts", and was hospitalized until July 10, 1991.  The veteran did not return to his job after the hospitalization.  The RO, in February 1994, granted the veteran a 100% disability rating based on hospitalization, effective June 15, 1991, and a 100% disability rating based on individual unemployability, effective August 1, 1991.  The veteran's March 1994 "Statement in Support of Claim" that indicated, in essence, that he was entitled to an effective date before June 15, 1991, for his 100% rating, resulted in a March 1995 BVA decision denying an earlier effective date.
