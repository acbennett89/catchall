Decision Assessment Document

Andrews v. McDonough, June 22, 2021, 34 Vet.App. 216

United States Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court found that the Board of Veterans Appeals (Board) adopted the M21-1’s substantive recognition of specific risk factors concerning hepatitis C infections and accepted the Veteran’s assertion that he did not have any hepatitis C risk factors outside of service. Based on the circumstances of this particular case and the lack of any reasonable basis to believe that additional development could alter the approximate balance of evidence—the Court reversed the Board’s decision denying service connection and ordered VA to award service connection for the Veteran’s hepatitis C.

**Impact on VBA:**

M21-1, V.iii.6.2.e. will be updated to reference this case.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

In February 2010, the Veteran claimed service connection for hepatitis C as resulting from various risk factors he experienced while in service, including air gun immunization, dental work, and sexually transmitted disease (STD).

A VA medical examination conducted in 2010 included the examiner’s opinion that the cause of the Veteran’s hepatitis C was "unknown" but was not related to his in-service immunizations because "air gun injections are not a risk factor" for the condition. The regional office therefore denied his claim.

The Veteran appealed that denial and the Board remanded the hepatitis C claim in 2017 to obtain a new etiology opinion because the first VA medical examiner failed to consider the Veteran’s treatment for a STD while in service. The Board’s remand cited VA's Adjudication Procedures Manual (M21-1), which identifies "[h]igh risk sexual activity" as "a risk factor for hepatitis C."

In September 2017 the same examiner who provided the 2010 etiology opinion considered the Veteran’s in service STD and opined that the Veteran’s hepatitis C was less likely than not related to service as there was "no new information" showing a link to service.

In the 2018 decision under review, the Board denied service connection for hepatitis C. Referencing the M21-1, the Board noted that the Veteran competently reported experiencing some hepatitis C risk factors in service. However, the Board placed "great probative weight" on the September 2017 opinion and discounted the evidence submitted by the Veteran. Importantly, the Board also noted the Veteran's averment that he had no hepatitis C risk factors outside of service.

Both the Veteran and VA acknowledged that the Board erred in relying on an inadequate medical exam. The dispute in this case centered on the proper remedy given that error. The Veteran asked the Court to reverse the Board's ruling that service connection was not warranted for his hepatitis C. He argued that reversal is the only proper remedy given that the evidence of record meets the "benefit of the doubt" standard spelled out in the law. In contrast, VA argued for remand because the Court cannot apply the benefit of the doubt doctrine in the first instance since doing so would exceed its statutory authority and amount to initial appellate fact finding.

The Court discussed what role the M21-1 plays in decisions of the Board and Courts. The Court also provided analysis concerning the merits of the parties’ arguments regarding whether reversal or remand was warranted in this particular case.

The Court noted the Board’s 2017 remand order incorporated the M21-1 provision relevant to hepatitis C into the development of the claim and the Board applied substantive provisions of the M21-1 regarding risk factors in its 2018 denial decision. The M21-1 provision relied on by the Board (M21-1 III. iv. 4. H.2.e.) recognizes various risk factors for hepatitis C, such as blood transfusions, tattoos or body piercings, puncture with non-sterile needles, high-risk sexual activity and, (possibly) air gun injections. That M21-1 provision also instructs regional office adjudicators to "[r]esolve reasonable doubt . . . in favor of the Veteran when the evidence favoring risk factor(s) in service is equal to the evidence favoring risk factor(s) before or after service."  The Court specified that the Board adopted the M21-1's substantive recognition of specific risk factors for hepatitis C. The Court noted that the Board: (1) must have accepted as credible the Veteran’s assertion that he had no non-service hepatitis C risk factors, and; (2) did not dispute the Veteran’s in-service risk factors for a hepatitis C infection. Instead, the Board merely discounted the likelihood that the Veteran's hepatitis C originated from some of the in-service risk factors based upon an inadequate medical opinion that either failed to understand the theory of service connection at issue or rejected the possibility that certain recognized risk factors could lead to hepatitis C.

The Court stated that no matter how low the Board considered the likelihood that the Veteran’s in-service risk factors caused his hepatitis C, that likelihood had to be weighed against the absence of any non-service risk factors. Citing 38 C.F.R. § 3.102, the Court therefore specified that the Board’s denial of service connection for hepatitis C in this case was clearly erroneous.

The Court noted that additional development should not be undertaken when evidence present is sufficient to grant a claim, and that remand is inappropriate where the purpose is simply to obtain more evidence so that VA can properly deny a claim. The Court noted that regardless of the relative probative value of the evidence supporting the Veteran’s reported in-service risk factors in this case, evidence of those risk factors at least matched non-existent evidence of pre- or post-service risk factors.

Based on the adoption of the M21-1 standards by the Board, the conceded presence of in-service risk factors, the absence of any non-service risk factors, and the lack of any reasonable basis to believe that additional development could alter the approximate balance of evidence in this case—the Court REVERSED the Board's denial of service connection and ordered VA to award service connection for the Veteran’s hepatitis C.
