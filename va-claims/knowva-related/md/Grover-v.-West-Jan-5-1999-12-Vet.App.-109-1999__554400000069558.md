Decision Assessment Document
 Grover v. West, Jan 5, 1999, 12 Vet.App. 109 (1999)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that in order to find clear and unmistakable error (CUE) it must be determined (1) that either the facts known at the time were not before the adjudicator or the law then in effect was incorrectly applied, (2) that an error occurred based on the record and the law that existed at the time the decision was made, and (3) that, had the error not been made, the outcome would have been manifestly different.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.2.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran had active service from October 1975 to April 1976.  Entrance examination was negative relating to the right large toe.  As a result of his entrance examination, the veteran was characterized as “fit for world-wide duty.”  During the same month, the veteran was examined for a right toe injury incurred in basic training.  An x-ray was negative for fracture but he was granted a temporary waiver from duties.  Following further complaints of pain in the same toe, the veteran was evaluated in February 1976, at the dispensary of a different service base, and was noted as having a history of a fractured toe during basic training.  The record did not show a separation examination.

A December 1985 VA physical examination report showed tenderness and a limited range of motion at the metatarsal phalangeal joint of the great toe.  The diagnosis was residuals of an injury to the veteran’s right foot.  An x-ray evaluation of the right foot at that time “showed only slight spurring on the plantar surface of the os calcis, [ . . .]which is the ‘irregular quadrangular bone at the back of the tarsus.’”  Dorland’s at 1195, 245.  The regional office (RO) denied service connection for residuals of a right foot injury in a May 1986 decision, indicating that there was “insufficient evidence to relate any of the veteran’s conditions to his current disability.”  This decision stated that “the condition noted in service appeared to be acute and transitory as seen by the release to world wide duty” following basic training.  This May 1986 rating decision also referenced an orthopedic examination report which belonged to another veteran and had been erroneously included in the veteran’s claim file.  This (mistakenly filed) orthopedic report showed degenerative arthritis of the left knee, bilateral pes planus, and a history of a ruptured Achilles tendon. The rating decision was not appealed and became final.

Following a joint motion for remand to determine, in part, whether there was CUE in the May 1986 rating decision, the veteran underwent a VA joints examination that took note of his history of tripping and hurting his right first toe during basic training.  The examiner diagnosed the veteran with moderate degenerative joint disease of the right first toe and indicated a possible correlation between the veteran’s disability and the injury suffered during service.  As a result, the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (BVA) determined that new and material evidence had been submitted and granted the veteran service connection for his right foot injury.  The BVA did not find CUE, however, in the May 1986 decision, noting that although the December 1985 VA examination showed that the veteran had residuals of an in-service injury it did not identify any particular disability.

In finding that there was no CUE in the RO’s May 1986 decision, the Court stated that all relevant facts that existed at the time of the ratign decision were before the RO and it made its decision based on those facts.  The Court noted that the December 1985 VA examination indicated that there had been a fracture in service and diagnosed the veteran with residuals of a right foot injury.  Since the service medical records do not show a fracture, and in fact an inservice x-ray is negative for a right toe fracture, the 1985 examination report lacks reliability.  The examination report had apparently relied upon the veteran’s history of fracture.  The Court concluded that a fracture is not the kind of disability observable by a lay person.  Rather a competent medical opinion is required in this case.

The Court pointed out that the RO’s erroneous finding that the service medical records showed  that he was deemed fit for word-wide duty after, rather than before, basic training, but concluded that this was not CUE.  For the following reasons, the Court determined that it was harmless error.  Due to the acute and transitory nature of the veteran’s injury and the lack of evidence demonstrating that the veteran’s condition in May 1986 was related to service, the error “did not affect the outcome of the veteran’s claim significantly enough to rise to the level of CUE.”  According to the Court, the inclusion in the veteran’s file of another veteran’s examination report did not result in a CUE.  All applicable evidence was before the adjudicator, “and a decision was made based on that correct information.  There [wa]s no evidence indicating that the RO relied upon the medical information of another veteran when it made its decision.”

Regarding the veteran’s argument that the RO failed to apply the provisions of 38 CFR § 3.303(b) and to consider his claim under the theory of continuity of symptomatology, the Court noted first that the RO determined that the veteran had submitted only medical evidence of an injury in service that was “acute and transitory” and that the evidence did not support a finding of chronic disability.  “The only post service medical evidence of record was a December 1985 VA physical examination, during which the veteran claimed to have been suffering from a ‘fractured’ toe since basic training; however, an X-ray evaluation established no such fracture.”  Thus, the RO correctly determined that due to the nature of the veteran’s in-service injury and his failure to seek a medical opinion regarding his disability for over 10 years, there is insufficient evidence to satisfy the requirements of section 3.303(b).
