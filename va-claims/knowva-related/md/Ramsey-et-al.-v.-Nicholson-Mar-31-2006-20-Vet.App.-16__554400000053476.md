Decision Assessment Document
Ramsey et al. v. Nicholson, Mar 31, 2006, 20 Vet.App. 16
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court recognized that it would be appropriate for VA to impose a stay in the processing of appeals at the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (BVA) in certain limited circumstances.  However, the Court held that the Secretary does not have unilateral authority to stay cases at the BVA or Regional Office pending an appeal to the Federal Circuit of a decision by the Court.  They further held that unilateral imposition of a stay by the Secretary or BVA Chairman as to the effect of any of the Court’s decisions is unlawful.

The Court held that the proper course for the Secretary to implement a stay as to the effect of any Court decision would be to file a motion for stay with the Court.  They also suggested that VA promulgate a regulation that governs circumstances under which a stay may be properly authorized.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.5.C.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

In April 2005, the Secretary issued a stay action on certain tinnitus claims pending the outcome of VA’s appeal to the Federal Circuit of Smith (Ellis) v. Nicholson, 19 Vet. App. 63 (2005) (holding that bilateral tinnitus qualifies for two 10 percent ratings, one for each ear).  Petitioners sought a writ of mandamus for extraordinary relief from the Court to rescind the April 2005 Secretary’s stay action claiming, inter alia, that (1) in issuing the stay, the Secretary was in violation of 38 U.S.C. § 7107 (requiring the Board to consider and decide its appeals in docket number order); and (2) the stay creates an unreasonable delay and causes economic hardship.  The Court denied the writ.

The Court held that section 7107 is not an absolute limitation on the Secretary's and Board Chairman's authority to manage the administrative appeals system, including the imposition of a stay, where appropriate.  Section 7107 must be read so as to afford the Board some flexibility in considering and deciding appeals so that efficiency and fairness in processing appeals is promoted.  Congress did not intend to preempt the authority of the Secretary, Chairman, and Board to manage the Board and its work to produce fair, efficient, timely, effective review of appeals.

Although the Court denied the writ and clearly indicated that a stay may be appropriate in certain circumstances, the Court held that the Secretary's authority to stay cases at the Board does not include the unilateral authority to stay cases at the Board (or RO) pending an appeal to the Federal Circuit of a decision by this Court. The Court indicated that unilaterally implementing a stay of a Court decision pending Federal Circuit Appeal not only affects VA’s internal matters but directly impacts numerous Court decisions, as they are binding and have precedential effect the date of issue.

Where the Secretary desires to stay the effect of any decision from this Court pending appeal, the proper course is to file a motion for stay in the court that has jurisdiction over the particular case. See Standard Havens Prods., Inc. v. Gencor Indus., Inc., 897 F.2d 511, 512 (Fed. Cir. 1990).  Although typically the court of jurisdiction will be the Court of Appeals for Veterans’ Claims, the Court directed the Secretary to file the motion with the Federal Circuit in view of the procedural posture of Smith.  The Court stayed its judgment for 30 days to allow the Secretary the opportunity to file a motion for stay in the Federal Circuit.

Mr. Ramsey’s case, and the issue of the writ, has not yet concluded.  The Secretary did not file a motion for stay in the Federal Circuit, as suggested by the Court.  Shortly after the Court entered its judgment, Mr. Ramsey filed another similar petition.  The petition is again pending before the Court.
