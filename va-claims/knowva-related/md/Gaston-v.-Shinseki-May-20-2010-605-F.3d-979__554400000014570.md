Decision Assessment Document
 Gaston v. Shinseki, May 20, 2010, 605 F.3d 979
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that the Veterans Court correctly construed [38 U.S.C. § 5110(b)(2)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5110) and its implementing regulation as requiring that an increase in a veteran’s service-connected disability *must* have occurred during the one year prior to the date of the veteran’s claim in order to receive an effective date of up to one year prior to the date of the claim.

If the increase in disability occurred more than one year prior to the date of claim, the veteran would not be entitled to an effective date prior to the date of claim under section 5110(b)(2).

The Federal Circuit also held that [38 C.F.R. § 3.340(a)(1)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=8c491e7a585ffa26b0265c43c92cc143&node=se38.1.3_1340&rgn=div8) merely sets forth the general standards for total disability ratings and does not provide an independent basis for the award of a total disability rating based on individual unemployability (TDIU).

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.  However, please note that VA or uniformed services medical records may provide a basis for an effective date earlier than one year prior to the date of claim for increased evaluation for a service-connected disability if these records adequately demonstrate that the disability was of such severity to warrant a higher evaluation at such date.  See [38 C.F.R. § 3.157(b)(1)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=8c491e7a585ffa26b0265c43c92cc143&node=se38.1.3_1157&rgn=div8).  This case does not affect that established rule.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who served from 1970 to 1987, had established service connection for multiple conditions, to include posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD), by 1995.  In March 1999, he filed a formal claim for TDIU and an increased rating for PTSD.  In March 2000, the veteran was granted an increased evaluation for PTSD and his left knee, as well as TDIU.  An effective date of March 25, 1999 – the date of claim – was established for the TDIU grant and increased evaluations.  The veteran appealed the effective date for the TDIU evaluation.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals, finding no evidence of increased disability within the year preceding receipt of the March 1999 claim, denied the appeal.

Before the Veterans Court, the veteran argued that because his Social Security Administration records demonstrated that his condition had worsened in October 1994, it was “factually ascertainable” that his disability had increased before his TDIU claim was filed, and he was therefore entitled to an effective date of one year prior to VA’s receipt of the claim.  The Veterans Court explained that section [5110(b)(2)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5110) only permitted an effective date earlier than the date of the veteran’s claim if his disability had become worse during the year prior to his claim.

The veteran argued before the Federal Circuit that the Veterans Court misconstrued section 5110(b)(2) by limiting it to cases in which the evidence demonstrates that an increase occurred during the one year prior to the claim.  The veteran contended that if there is evidence that an increase occurred during *or before* the one year prior to the date of claim, the effective date for increased compensation will be based upon the “facts found” as required by section 5110(b)(2), but no earlier than the “one-year look-back period.”

The Federal Circuit held that the plain language of section 5110(b)(2) only permits an earlier effective date for increased disability compensation if that disability increased during the one-year period before the filing of the claim.  The Federal Circuit also reviewed the legislative history and concluded that the purpose of the statute was to provide veterans a one-year grace period for filing a claim following an increase in a service-connected disability.

The Federal Circuit held that, consistent with the plain language of the statute and legislative history, the only reasonable construction of section 5110(b)(2) is that a veteran’s claim for increased disability compensation must be filed within one year of an increase in the disability, as shown by the evidence, in order to obtain an effective date earlier than the date of the claim.

The Federal Circuit also rejected all other arguments proffered by the veteran, to include his contention that [§ 3.340(a)(1)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=8c491e7a585ffa26b0265c43c92cc143&node=se38.1.3_1340&rgn=div8) provides a separate basis for a TDIU award.

The Federal Circuit affirmed the Veterans Court decision.
