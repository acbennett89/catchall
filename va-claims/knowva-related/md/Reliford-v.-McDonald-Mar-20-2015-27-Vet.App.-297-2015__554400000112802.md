**What the case is about:**

The Court held that while 38 U.S.C. 5121 and 5121A are closely linked pathways for obtaining accrued benefits, both section 5121 and section 5121A provide separate and distinct procedural paths for pursuing accrued benefits.  The key distinction between the sections is that claims for accrued benefits under section 5121 must be adjudicated “based on evidence in the file at the date of death,” while eligible accrued-benefits-beneficiaries substituted in the deceased claimants’ underlying claims are afforded the ability to further develop the record, including via the Secretary’s duty to assist.

The Court also held that VA’s procedures under Fast Letter 10-30 (Aug. 10, 2010) require VA to inform an eligible accrued benefits claimant of the right to waive substitution under section 5121A and have the claim for accrued benefits to proceed on the basis of the evidence of record that existed at the date of the claimant’s death under section 5121.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court’s holding has been incorporated into the Manual under Part VIII, Chapter 2.  Although Fast Letter 10-30 has been rescinded and incorporated into the Live Manual.  Much of the guidance from Fast Letter 10-30, to include the claimant’s right to waive substitution, has been continued in the Live Manual.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in February 2007, denied the veteran’s claim for service connection for scoliosis, hypertension, and disabilities secondary to scoliosis or hypertension.  The Court, in 2008, remanded the case for further development.  The veteran died in April 2009 while his claim was on remand to the Board.  The following month, the surviving spouse filed a VA Form 21-534, “Application for Dependency and Indemnity Compensation, Death Pension and Accrued Benefits.”  VA’s subsequent notification letter informed the appellant that she “may submit additional evidence in support of the appeal or waive the opportunity to submit additional evidence.”

The Board, in July 2012, certified the substitution of the appellant for her deceased husband and remanded the case for further development.  In August 2012, a medical opinion was obtained indicating that it was not as least as likely as not that the veteran’s scoliosis and hypertension were related to service.  The Board, in September 2013, denied service connection for scoliosis and hypertension and various disabilities secondary to scoliosis or hypertension.

The appellant argued before the Court that she filed a claim for accrued benefits and never sought “substitution.”  Therefore, the Secretary erred by processing her claim as a request for substitution and the Board subsequently erred by relying on evidence the Secretary obtained and added to her husband’s file after his death.  The appellant contended that section 5121A does not alter the requirement in section 5121(a) that the record is closed after the veteran dies for accrued-benefits claims filed pursuant to section 5121.  She further noted that an accrued-benefits claim under section 5121 is a claim separate from a deceased claimant’s underlying claim for benefits.

The Court identified that the question before it is whether a proper interpretation of sections 5121A and 5121(a) requires, or at a minimum allows, the Secretary to unilaterally process an accrued-benefits claim solely as a request for substitution.

The Court noted that the two paths to accrued benefits under sections 5121 and 5121A are closely linked, as only potential accrued-benefits beneficiaries under section 5121 are eligible for substitution under section 5121A.  However, while substitution under section 5121A continues the deceased claimant’s underlying claim, it does not create a new and independent substantive claim for VA benefits, as does an accrued-benefits claim under section 5121.  The Court concluded that both section 5121 and section 5121A provide separate and distinct procedural paths for pursuing accrued benefits with the key distinction being that a claim for accrued benefits under section 5121 must be adjudicated “based on the evidence in the file at date of death,” while eligible accrued-benefits beneficiaries substituted in the deceased claimants’ underlying claims are afforded the ability to further develop the record, to include via the Secretary’s duty to assist.

The Court observed that neither section 5121 nor section 5121A speak directly to the issue of whether the Secretary may treat a claim for accrued benefits as a request for substitution; however, it determined that it need not rule on the matter as the Secretary, in this case, failed to comply with his own established procedures under Fast Letter 10-30, which specifically permitted an accrued-benefits claimant “to waive the opportunity to substitute/submit additional evidence in support of the claim.”

The Court found that although the Secretary notified the appellant that she could waive the right to submit additional evidence, he did not notify her that she could waive substitution, in contravention of the Fast Letter 10-30 guidance.  Based on the particular facts of the case, the Court concluded that it could not hold that the error was harmless because the Board had not yet adjudicated the appellant’s accrued benefits claim against the factual background.

The Court set aside the Board decision and remanded the case for adjudication of the appellant’s claim based on the file as it existed at the time of her husband’s death.
