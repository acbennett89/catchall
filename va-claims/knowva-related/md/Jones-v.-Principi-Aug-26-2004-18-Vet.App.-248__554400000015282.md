Decision Assessment Document
 Jones v. Principi, Aug 26, 2004, 18 Vet.App. 248
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court set aside the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA or Board) denial of a rating in excess of 10% (under diagnostic code (DC) 5301) for a through and through gunshot wound (GSW) injury which involved muscle groups (MGs) I and II.  The CAVC remanded the claim and directed the BVA to consider [38 C.F.R. § 4.56(b)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=4d74e2efbe1996ce3a0c7a7fda093f3b&node=se38.1.4_156&rgn=div8) (which permits separate ratings for each MG) in its reevaluation of the veteran’s muscle disabilities of both MGs I and II.  As for the veteran’s claim that his scars (one entry-wound scar, one exit-wound scar, and a thoracotomy scar) should be evaluated separately under [38 C.F.R. § 4.118](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=4d74e2efbe1996ce3a0c7a7fda093f3b&node=se38.1.4_1118&rgn=div8), DC 7804, the court remanded this claim so that a scar examination could be performed.  The court pointed out that obtaining a rating in accordance with section 4.56(b) does not necessarily preclude a separate scar rating under DC 7804.  The CAVC pointed out, however, that “Note 1” of DC 7804 limits the application of that rating to scars that are “superficial [and] painful on examination” but that are “not associated with underlying soft tissue damage”.  The CAVC also remanded an issue as to whether DC 7804 provides ratings for scars cumulatively or individually for the Secretary to address this issue in the first instance.

**Impact on VBA:**

This case may be used as a training tool in rating gunshot wounds.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

Separate ratings for disabilities of muscle groups I and II: The Court noted that in addition to reorganizing the classifications of muscle disabilities, VA, in its 1997 amendments, replaced the content of subparagraph (b) of § 4.56.  Because the appellant's disabilities involve residuals of a GSW to the right chest with muscle group injury and because the post-1997 § 4.56(b) provides separate ratings "for each group of muscles damaged," a provision that may be more favorable to the appellant, the Court employed the post-1997 version of the Schedule for Rating Disabilities for the purposes of this appeal.  Moreover, the post-1997 version of section 4.56 was in effect at the time of the October 2000 Board decision.

The Court concluded that the Board failed to recognize the veteran’s eligibility for separate ratings under [38 C.F.R. § 4.73](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=4d74e2efbe1996ce3a0c7a7fda093f3b&node=se38.1.4_173&rgn=div8), DC 5301 (MG I) and DC 5302 (MG II) pursuant to the express authority provided in the post-1997 version of § 4.56(b) for rating "as no less than a moderate injury . . . each group of muscles damaged" as a result of "[a] through-and-through injury with muscle damage.".  Specifically, the undisputed evidence of record reflects examiners' observations as to the anterior point of entry of the missile at the  pectoral muscles (MG II), the posterior point of exit at the serratus anterior (MG I) and that the muscle disabilities that the appellant sustained from the GSW involve MGs I and II; thus, he suffered "[a] through-and-through injury with muscle damage,” 38 CFR § 4.56(d).  Hence, he is, as a matter of law, entitled to have "each group of muscles damaged" rated "as no less than a moderate injury" using both DC 5301 and DC 5302.  Accordingly, the Court set aside the Board's denial of an increased rating, in excess of 10%, for residuals of a GSW of the right chest, involving only an injury of MG I, and remanded the matter with directions that the Board consider section 4.56(b) in its reevaluation of the appellant's muscle disabilities of both MGs I and II under diagnostic codes 5301 and 5302.

Entitlement to ratings under DC 7804: Because [section 4.56](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=4d74e2efbe1996ce3a0c7a7fda093f3b&node=se38.1.4_156&rgn=div8) does not contain any provision regarding the painfulness of a scar that is a component of a muscle disability, the symptomatology used to rate a "superficial[ and] painful" scar under DC 7804 is not "duplicative of" or "overlapping with" the muscle-injury evaluation criterion that is contained in 38 CFR 4.56(d) .  Thus, obtaining a rating in accordance with section 4.56(d) does not necessarily preclude a separate scar rating under DC 7804.  However, as noted above, "Note (1)" of DC 7804 limits the application of that rating to scars that are "superficial[ and] painful on examination" but that are "not associated with underlying soft tissue damage”.  Hence, to the extent that the appellant's scars are painful and are "not associated with underlying soft tissue damage”, the appellant may be rated under both section 4.56(d) and DC 7804.

With respect to the appellant's thoracotomy, entry-wound, and exit-wound scars, the Court noted that the record contains evidence as to the painfulness or tenderness of those scars but does not reflect any information as to whether the appellant's scars are "associated with underlying soft tissue damage."  Because it is unclear whether the appellant's scars are associated with underlying soft tissue damage, the Court remanded this matter for VA to provide a special scar examination.

Separate Ratings for Multiple Scars under DC 7804: The Board found that the appellant was not entitled to a separate rating under DC 7804 for each scar.  Although the appellant asked the Court to find that the term "scars" in DC 7804 is ambiguous and to interpret it in his favor so as to provide a separate rating for each scar, the Court stated that it was not prepared to make any such interpretation as to whether DC 7804 provides ratings for scars cumulatively or individually.  These are the Court’s reasons:  First, until such time as there is a determination that the appellant has at least two scars that are not associated with underlying tissue damage, it would be premature for the Court to make any such interpretation.  Second, even assuming that the appellant has multiple scars not associated with underlying tissue damage, there are a variety of interpretative issues presented that the Court believes the Secretary should address in the first instance.  For example, [section 4.118](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=4d74e2efbe1996ce3a0c7a7fda093f3b&node=se38.1.4_1118&rgn=div8), as it relates to scarring, references both "scars" and "scar," and the consequences of such intermittent use of the plural and the singular are not entirely clear.  More specifically, DC 7804, under which the appellant seeks multiple ratings, contains the plural and its "Note (1)" contains the singular.  Moreover, the Court noted the possible disconnect between DC 7804, which may permit unlimited multiple ratings for less significant scarring, and DC 7800, which addresses more significant scarring but provides a maximum rating of 80%.  Similarly, it appears that interpreting "superficial[ and] painful" in DC 7804 to mean that each scar, regardless of size or physical location, could be rated at 10% may not comport with DC 7801, which provides ratings for severe scarring, of which the ceiling is a maximum 40% rating for a "scar[], other than [the] head, face, or neck, that [is] deep or that causes limited motion," for an "area or areas" exceeding one square foot. In this regard, the Court noted that DC 7801 appears to account for the physical size of a scar but not the number of scars.  Hence, the Court remanded this matter for the Secretary to address these issues utilizing the results of the special scar examination as appropriate.
