Decision Assessment Document
 Paulson v. Brown, Mar 21, 1995, 7 Vet.App. 466 (1995)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that an appellant's account of a prior condition is an “inadequate basis” upon which to determine that a condition pre-existed service.

The Court held that an individual who has served only on active duty for training must establish a service-connected disability in order to achieve veteran status and to be entitled to compensation.  The definitional statute, 38 U.S.C. 101(24), makes a clear distinction between those who have served on active duty and those who have served on active duty for training (as well as those who have served on inactive duty for training).  That distinction dictates the conclusion in this case.  Since the appellant served only on active duty for training and has not established any service-connected disability, the Board did not err in concluding that the presumption of soundness was inapplicable to the appellant’s case.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, I.1.C.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The claimant served on active duty for training from March 24, 1962, to September 23, 1962.  In July 1988 he filed a claim for service connection for agoraphobia, panic attacks, and anxiety depression.  He indicated that the conditions were aggravated during his active duty for training.  The denial of service connection was upheld by the Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) in October 1990.  In March 1993, the Court remanded the case for readjudication.  On September 29, 1993, the Board issued a decision denying service connection for a psychiatric disorder because it preexisted service and was not aggravated during service.

The Board denied the appellant the presumption of soundness on the ground that he could not be a veteran since active duty for training qualifies as "active military, naval or air service" only if the individual has previously been found to suffer from a service-connected disability.  The Court concluded that an individual who has served only on active duty for training must establish a service-connected disability in order to achieve veteran status and to be entitled to compensation.  To be entitled to the presumption of soundness doctrine, the individual must be a veteran as defined by 38 U.S.C. 101(2).  Therefore, the Board did not err in concluding that the presumption of soundness was inapplicable to this case.

The Court found that the only evidence that the appellant may have had a preexisting psychiatric disorder is his own statement on a VA Form 1-9 that he was denied enlistment in the U.S. Marine Reserves because he was diagnosed with anxiety.  The appellant's account of a prior condition is an inadequate basis upon which the Board could have concluded that he had a condition that preexisted service.  Further, the Board's reliance on 38 C.F.R. 3.303(c) was in error as the Board may not rely on a regulation as a substitute for the requirement that it rely on independent medical evidence.  The Court found that the Board must support its reliance on section 3.303(c) by a statement of reasons or bases that is not merely conclusory or predicated on an inadequate basis.  In light of its findings on the issue of whether the appellant's condition preexisted service, the BVA should also readjudicate the issue of in-service aggravation.
