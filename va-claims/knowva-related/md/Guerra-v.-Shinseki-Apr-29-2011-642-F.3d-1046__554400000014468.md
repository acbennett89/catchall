Decision Assessment Document
 Guerra v. Shinseki, Apr 29, 2011, 642 F.3d 1046
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that [38 U.S.C. § 1114(s)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1114) requires that a veteran must have at least one disability that is rated totally disabling to qualify for special monthly compensation (SMC) under that statute.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran is in receipt of a combined 100 percent disability schedular evaluation.  His service-connected disabilities include an upper extremity gunshot wound, rated 70 percent disabling, and posttraumatic stress disorder, rated 70 percent disabling.  His other service-connected evaluations include separate 40 percent evaluations for right and left lower extremity injuries and a 30 percent evaluation for neuropathy.

The Veterans Court held that the veteran did not meet the threshold requirement for SMC at the (s) rate because none of his disabilities was independently rated as total.  The Veterans Court adhered to its decision in [*Bradley v. Peake*, 22 Vet.App. 280 (2008)](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014811/), in which it held that a veteran with a schedular rating of total disability must have a single disability rated at 100 percent in order to qualify for benefits under section 1114(s). Note: The Federal Circuit indicated that because the veteran’s disability rating was not based on TDIU, it would not address whether and in what circumstances the benefits of section 1114(s) are available to a veteran with a TDIU rating.

The veteran argued before the Federal Circuit that benefits under section 1114(s) should be available to veterans rated totally disabled, irrespective of whether due to a combined schedular total evaluation or a single disability rated 100 percent disabling.  He specifically contended that VA has interpreted its own regulation to allow certain combined ratings to meet the requirement of a single total disability rating through a Manual M21-1 provision that was in effect from 1965 to 1995.  The veteran further argued that VA’s rescission of the Manual provision in 1995 was a substantive rule requiring compliance with the notice-and-comment rulemaking procedures of the Administrative Procedure Act (APA) before making the change.  He claimed that since VA did not follow the APA procedures, the change to the Manual in 1995 is rendered inoperable and deference must be given to VA’s pre-1995 interpretation of the term “single disability.”

The Federal Circuit concluded that the text of section 1114(s) evidences Congressional intent to limit the payment of SMC at the (s) rate to a veteran who has at least one disability rated as totally disabling.  The Federal Circuit acknowledged that the language of subsection 1114(s) is not entirely free from ambiguity; however, it deferred to the VA’s regulatory interpretation of the statute contained in [38 C.F.R. § 3.350(i)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=470fdaf0a28e29f3dada56ae7fb9b979&node=se38.1.3_1350&rgn=div8).  The Federal Circuit stated that the regulation’s reference to a “single service-connected disability rated as 100 percent” resolves any ambiguity in the statute and makes it clear that SMC under section 1114(s) is only payable if the veteran has a single disability that is rated 100 percent.

In addressing the veteran’s specific arguments, the Federal Circuit observed that a Manual reference had been in effect from 1965 to 1995 directing a reader to rules pertaining to TDIU for a definition of the term “single disability” for purposes of section 1114(s).  The rule, under [38 C.F.R. § 4.16(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=470fdaf0a28e29f3dada56ae7fb9b979&node=se38.1.4_116&rgn=div8) provides that, for purposes of TDIU determinations, “one disability” includes multiple disabilities resulting from a single accident and multiple disabilities affecting a single body system.  The Court pointed out that VA deleted this reference pertaining to TDIU rules from the Manual in 1995.

The Federal Circuit stated that the deleted Manual reference stood in contrast to a more formal and explicit statement of the agency’s position issued by the VA General Counsel in a 1991 precedent opinion.  The opinion interpreted [section 1114(s)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1114) and [§ 3.350(i)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=470fdaf0a28e29f3dada56ae7fb9b979&node=se38.1.3_1350&rgn=div8) to provide that the threshold requirement for entitlement to SMC(s) is a disability rated as total.  See [VA OGC Prec.Op. 66-91](http://www.va.gov/ogc/docs/1991/PREC_66-91.doc) (August 15, 1991).  The Federal Circuit also held that neither the 1995 modification to the Manual nor the General Counsel opinion constituted or contained a substantive rule subject to the notice-and-comment requirements of the APA.

The Federal Circuit held that the Veterans Court correctly interpreted subsection 1114(s) and affirmed its decision.
