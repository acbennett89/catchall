Decision Assessment Document
 Sacks v. West, Aug 7, 1998, 11 Vet.App. 314 (1998)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that medical treatise evidence that is too generalized or inconclusive is insufficient to well ground a claim.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran filed a service connection claim for pemphigus vulgaris.  A current disability was shown and service medical records showed sores or blisters like cold sores during service.  To support claim, submitted a medical journal article, which included a case study that stated that pemphigus vulgaris usually presents with erosion of mucous membranes, especially in the mouth.  Often, painful mouth erosions are the only abnormality for months before skin erosions and/or flaccid blisters develop.

The Court found the medical treatise evidence to be too general and inconclusive to well ground the claim.  The Court stated that acceptance of this evidence as sufficient to establish the plausibility of causality would be predicated on simply the instinctive inference of a lay person, rather than upon an adjudicative determination (made by a lay person) as to the credibility and weight of expert evidence.

The Court further explained that this is not to say that medical article and treatise evidence re irrelevant or unimportant; they can provide important support when combined with an opinion of a medical professional.  Furthermore, the Court indicated that its holding did not extend to a situation where medical article or treatise evidence, standing alone, discusses generic relationships with a degree of certainty such that, under the facts of a specific case, there is at least plausible causality based upon objective facts rather than on an unsubstantiated lay medical opinion.

The Court concludes that the medical journal article submitted by the appellant in this case could not by itself satisfy the nexus element of a well-grounded claim.
