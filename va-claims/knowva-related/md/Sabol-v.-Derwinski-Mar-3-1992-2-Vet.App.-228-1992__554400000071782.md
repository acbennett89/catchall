Decision Assessment Document
Sabol v. Derwinski, Mar 3, 1992, 2 Vet.App. 228 (1992)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that, all things being equal, if the evidence remains unchanged, the clear intent of the 1988 change to the rating schedule for psychiatric disorders was that there be an upward revision to an evaluation for a mental disorder.  The Court held that the percentage affixed to a disability flows from its severity, not the other way around.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding provided that the change to the rating schedule for mental disorders in 1988 constittued a liberalizing law/VA issue and warranted application of 38 C.F.R. 3.114.  The Court's holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.5.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

Appellant claimed he was entitled to a 70% rating for anxiety reaction under new criteria in the rating schedule for mental disorders which became effective on February 3, 1988.  Veteran contended that the BVA impermissibly revised the facts found in a previous BVA decision and awarded him a 50% rating for anxiety reaction based on those revised facts.  On March 21, 1989, BVA rendered a decision upholding a 50% evaluation for the SC anxiety reaction disorder.  In that decision, BVA applied the wording in DC 9400 that had been in effect prior to February 3, 1988.  The BVA gave no reasons why it applied the outdated schedular criteria rather than the changes that were effective on February 3, 1988.  In July 1989, the veteran, in response to the 1989 BVA decision, sought entitlement to a rating in excess of 50% for anxiety, based on the new schedular criteria, which were in effect at the time of the BVA decision.  This issue was considered by the regional office and denied.  Veteran argued that no new evidence was submitted and no new psychiatric examination had been performed since the 1989 BVA decision which had found his condition to be "severe," at least insofar as industrial impairment was concerned.  Veteran contended that he was entitled to a 70% rating based on the facts found by the BVA in 1989 and the revised and liberalized schedular criteria.

The Court stated that the purpose of the 1988 change in rating criteria was to provide consistency in describing social and industrial impairment in each of the categories of mental disorders.  Under the pre-1988 rating schedule wording, a veteran suffering from "severe" industrial impairment, whose ability to establish and maintain effective or favorable relationships with people was "seriously" impaired, was entitled to a 50% disability rating.  Under the new rating schedule effective since February 3, 1988, a veteran with the same symptoms is entitled to a 70% rating.  The Court was critical of VA for using medical evidence on which a "severe" evaluation in these areas had been made and for changing the determination to one of "productive of not more than considerable social and industrial impairment" without any additional medical evidence.   BVA may have inverted the decision process and concluded that the rating percentage should be kept the same because there was no change in the evidence, and then made the wording of its factual findings consistent with that determination.  However, the percentage affixed to a disability flows from its severity, not the other way around.  The case was remanded for reconsideration.  If BVA determines that its finding of considerable impairment is adhered to, then the BVA must justify that decision in face of a record that contains no new evidence subsequent to the 1989 decision.  If the BVA finds a 70% rating, then entitlement to a total rating under 38 CFR 4.16(c) must be considered.
