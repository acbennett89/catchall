Decision Assessment Document
 Reonal v. Brown, Sep 8, 1993, 5 Vet.App. 458 (1993)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that a medical opinion which is based on an inaccurate factual premise has no probative value for reopening.  The opinion is not material if it is based on a veteran’s recitation of events that had already been rejected in a previous decision.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.2.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

Appellant (Philippine Scouts 7/46-3/47) was discharged by reason of a preexisting femur fracture.  Service connection was denied in 1948, and this decision was confirmed based on affidavit evidence.  Pension was also disallowed because there was no qualifying service.  A new application was filed in 1988, and a current medical opinion was submitted at that time which attributed lower extremity disability to service (apparently based on appellant's statement).  Compensation was denied because there was no new and material evidence, and pension was denied for lack of qualifying service.

The Court affirmed the pension denial based on an earlier precedent (Philippine Scout enlistments 10/6/45-6/30/47 are not active service for pension by statute, Laruan v. Principi, 4 Vet. App. 100 (1993)).  Even though credible, the new medical opinion was not material since it relied on wholly inaccurate factual data.
