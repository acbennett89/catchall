**Decision Assessment Document**

***Laska v. McDonough*, 37 Vet. App. 460 (September 6, 2024)**

**U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)**

**What the case is about:**

The claimant, a surviving spouse, appealed a decision of the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) that denied entitlement to special monthly compensation (SMC) for traumatic brain injury (TBI) under 38 U.S.C. § 1114(t) (hereafter SMC(t)). The Court held that VA exceeded its authority under subsection 1114(t) by imposing a regulatory requirement in 38 C.F.R. § 3.352(b)(2) that a Veteran show the need for a higher level of care to qualify for SMC(t). The Court held the regulation is invalid.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court invalidated 38 C.F.R. § 3.352(b)(2) and that regulation will need to be revised. M21-1 will be amended to reference this case.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The Veteran served on active duty in Vietnam and sustained a head injury. He was awarded service connection for encephalopathy with left cerebellar dysfunction and loss of part of the skull with retained metallic bodies. He was later awarded SMC under 38 U.S.C. § 1114(s) due to being permanently housebound and SMC under 38 U.S.C. § 1114(l) based on the need for regular aid and attendance. In May 2017, the Veteran filed a claim for service connection for TBI, along with evidence that he required supervision for chronic medication administration and personal safety and that his spouse assisted with his activities of daily living. In November 2017, VA continued the total evaluation for encephalopathy as a residual of TBI and denied SMC(t). The Veteran timely appealed and submitted a medical opinion that in the absence of regular aid and attendance he would require hospitalization, nursing home care, or some other institutional care. The Board remanded for a VA medical examination and opinion to determine whether a higher level of in-home care was being provided. In a January 2022 decision, the Board found the Veteran required regular aid and attendance but denied entitlement to SMC(t) because the Veteran had not shown the need for both aid and attendance and higher-level care.

On appeal, the Court noted that 38 U.S.C. § 1114(r)(2) provides the highest level of SMC for a Veteran who needs a higher level of care than regular aid and attendance and, in the absence of such care, would require hospitalization, nursing home care, or other residential institutional care. The higher level of care is defined as “personal health-care services, provided on a daily basis in the veteran’s home by a person who is licensed to provide such services or who provides such services under the regular supervision of a licensed health-care professional.” While subsection 1114(t) provides SMC in the same amount as subsection 1114(r)(2), SMC(t) is awarded when a Veteran (1) is in need of regular aid and attendance for service-connected residuals of TBI; (2) is not eligible for compensation under subsection (r)(2); and (3) in the absence of “regular aid and attendance” would require hospitalization, nursing home care, or other residential institutional care.

The Court found the plain language of the first requirement is clear and specific, as it specifies that the necessary level of care needed for SMC(t) is the need for regular aid and attendance and does not mention or reference a higher level of care. The Court also determined that the second requirement indicates who is eligible to receive SMC(t), namely Veterans who do not qualify for SMC(r)(2). Lastly, the Court considered that the third requirement of subsection (t) borrows language from subsection (r)(2) by referencing the need for “hospitalization, nursing home care, or other residential care” in the absence of regular aid and attendance, but it does not reference a higher level of care. Because Congress chose to specify a higher level of care in subsection (r)(2), but not subsection (t), the Court declined to read an additional higher-level requirement into subsection (t) that is not apparent on its face or consistent with the statutory scheme as a whole.

The Court noted that, in drafting subsection (t), Congress might have amended subsection (r)(2) to include service-connected TBI residuals if it intended the requirement of a higher-level of care to apply to TBI. Instead, Congress created a separate provision with different criteria for Veterans with service connected TBIs who are “in need of regular aid and attendance.” The Court found that the inclusion of a “higher level of care” in subsection (r)(2) and a different standard in subsection (t) reinforces the conclusion that SMC(t) does not require higher-level care.

The Court also found that implementation of the plain language of the statute would not lead to an absurd result or be against the overall system of disability compensation and special monthly compensation. The Court considered that the plain language of the statute and the legislative history of subsection 1114(t) shows that Congress intended to compensate the cognitive and psychological manifestations of TBI at the same level as the anatomic loss, blindness, and deafness contemplated by SMC(r)(2).

The Court concluded subsection 1114(t) specifies that the requisite level of care for entitlement to SMC(t) is the need for regular aid and attendance and VA’s implementing regulation, § 3.352(b)(2), requires the higher-level care described in subsection 1114(r)(2). Thus, the Court found the regulation exceeded it authorizing statute and set it aside as invalid.
