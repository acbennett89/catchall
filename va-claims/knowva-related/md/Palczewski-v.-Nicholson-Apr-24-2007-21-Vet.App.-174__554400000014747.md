Decision Assessment Document
 Palczewski v. Nicholson, Apr 24, 2007, 21 Vet.App. 174
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that VA’s interpretation of a hearing disability contained in [38 C.F.R. § 3.385](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=f66547960ee2526733a2ba04ba365faa&node=se38.1.3_1385&rgn=div8) was reasonable, in accordance with [38 U.S.C. § 1110](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1110), and a valid exercise of the Secretary’s authority under [38 U.S.C. § 501](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/501).

The Court, noting that it had not previously addressed the “contemporaneous examination” requirement in the context of a service-connection claim, held that the requirement is applicable to a determination of service connection *only* when that determination is based upon whether the level of hearing impairment qualifies as a disability.

While acknowledging that the submittal of medical or lay evidence showing a change or worsening may require a new examination in such cases, the Court held that the mere passage of time between a VA examination and a Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) decision does not render the examination inadequate and automatically require a new examination.

The Court also held that an evidentiary record showing lay evidence of tinnitus during service and evidence of current symptoms of tinnitus, entitles the veteran to a VA examination under [38 U.S.C. § 5103A(d)(2)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5103A).

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

The contemporaneous examination requirement, which requires that the current severity of a “service-connected disability” be adequately demonstrated still only applies to claims for increased evaluation of a service-connected disability.  However, the Court has created an exception to this requirement where, based upon the unique nature of § 3.385, service connection for hearing loss is the issue.  The requirement does not extend to any other service connection claim.

Thus, if service connection for hearing loss is denied solely based upon the hearing loss not meeting the thresholds of § 3.385, a new examination may be required, with consideration given to the provisions of [38 C.F.R. § 3.327](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=f66547960ee2526733a2ba04ba365faa&node=se38.1.3_1327&rgn=div8), if the veteran submits competent lay or medical evidence indicating a worsening of the hearing loss during the appeal period before transfer of the case to the Board.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran served from February 1995 to February 1999.  Audiometric testing upon entrance showed zero-decibel thresholds in all frequencies except the 6000-hertz range with thresholds of 30 decibels in the right ear and 10 decibels in the left ear.  Separation examination audiometric testing showed 10 decibels at 500 hertz and 20 decibels at 6000 hertz on the right.  Testing of the left ear revealed thresholds of 10 decibels at 500 hertz and 45 decibels at 6000 hertz.  All other recorded thresholds were zero decibels.

The veteran underwent a VA audiometric examination in June 1999.  The following decibel thresholds were shown:

|  |  |  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  |  | Pure | Tone | Thresholds |  |
|  | 500 | 1000 | 2000 | 3000 | 4000 |
| Right | 10 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 10 |
| Left | 0 | 0 | 5 | 0 | 0 |

Speech discrimination was 98 percent in the right ear and 96 percent on the left.  The veteran reported intermittent high-pitched ringing in both ears.  The examiner indicated that the veteran’s hearing was normal except for a mild hearing loss in the left ear at 6000 hertz.

The regional office (RO) denied service connection for hearing loss and tinnitus in July 1999.  The Board upheld the denials in February 2004.

The veteran, before the Court, challenged the validity of [§ 3.385](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=f66547960ee2526733a2ba04ba365faa&node=se38.1.3_1385&rgn=div8), arguing there was no statutory basis for the regulation, it was not in accordance with section 1110, and that the Secretary’s authority to restrict payment of compensation for a disability is restricted to 38 C.F.R., Part 4.  He also argued that VA failed to provide a contemporaneous medical examination.  Regarding the tinnitus claim, the veteran contended that his statements regarding ringing in his ears during service are sufficient to establish a nexus between his current diagnosis of tinnitus and service.

The Court, noting that “disability” is not precisely defined in section 1110, indicated that it was extending substantial deference to VA in holding that its interpretation of a hearing disability under § 3.385 was reasonable.  The Court analyzed VA’s explanation of limiting “hearing disability” to losses below the 6000-hertz frequency based on its conclusions that those losses affect speech recognition, which would affect earning capacity.  The Court, finding that VA’s rationale agreed with the Court’s interpretation of the term “disability” as an “impairment of earning capacity,” concluded that VA had provided a reasonable explanation. See *Allen* *v. Brown,* 7 Vet.App. 439 (1995) where the Court held that disability as used in 38 U.S.C. § 1110 “should be construed to refer to impairment of earning capacity due to disease, injury, or defect, rather than to the disease, injury, or defect itself.”

The Court observed that the veteran had provided no evidence indicating an increase in the severity of his hearing loss in the 50-month time frame between the June 1999 RO and February 2004 Board decisions.  The Court also noted that its case law concerning a “contemporaneous” examination had only been applied in situations where service connection was established and only the issue of current level of disability was before the Board.

The veteran’s argument June 1999 VA examination constituted medical evidence of a disability because it showed high frequency hearing loss was rejected as the Court explained that a disability under § 3.385 was not shown.  The Court further found that the record contained no competent lay or medical evidence of diagnosed disability or persistent or recurrent symptoms of disability between the time of the RO and Board decisions to trigger an additional examination necessary to render a decision on the hearing loss claim.  In the absence of any additional evidence, the Court described the June 1999 VA examination as contemporaneous with the July 1999 RO decision reviewed by the Board.

The Court affirmed the part of the Board decision denying service connection for hearing loss.  The Board’s decision denying service connection for tinnitus was vacated and remanded for additional development to include a VA examination.
