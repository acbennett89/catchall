Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 13-96, Nov 25, 1996, Severance of Entitlement to Benefits Under 38 U.S.C. 1151
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Does the protection of service connection provided by 38 U.S.C. § 1159 apply to disabilities compensated under 38 U.S.C. § 1151?

Is termination of entitlement to benefits under 38 U.S.C. § 1151 subject to the requirements of 38 C.F.R. § 3.105(d)?

**Impact on VBA**

The protection of service connection under 38 U.S.C. § 1159 is not applicable to disabilities compensated under 38 U.S.C. § 1151.

Termination of entitlement to benefits under 38 U.S.C. § 1151 is not subject to the requirements of 38 C.F.R. § 3.105(d), regarding severance of service connection, but is subject to similar requirements under 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.103 and 3.105(a).

**Summary on facts and findings**

Section 1151 provides that disability or death compensation and dependency and indemnity compensation will be awarded, if entitlement is otherwise established, in the same manner as if disability or death were service connected.  Section 1159 of Title 38, United States Code, establishes a limitation on VA's authority to correct clearly and unmistakably erroneous determinations of service connection.  A determination of service connection which has been in force for ten or more years generally may not be severed, even if it was clearly and unmistakably erroneous.  Section 1159 protects only determinations of "service connection."  Section 1159 does not limit VA's authority under 38 C.F.R. § 3.105(a) to correct erroneous VA determinations with respect to any issue of fact or law other than the determination as to whether disability or death resulted from a disability incurred or aggravated in service.  An award of benefits under section 1151 does not involve a determination of service connection for a disability or death, accordingly, the plain language of section 1159 has no application to awards under section 1151.  There is nothing in the legislative history of section 1159 indicating a congressional intent to protect factual determinations under section 1151.  Furthermore, the plain language of section 1151 makes clear that a determination of entitlement under section 1151 does not involve a determination of service connection, but merely provides a basis for awarding benefits as if the disability or death were service connected.  Because section 1159 pertains solely to the specific factual determination of service connection, and not to the manner of awarding compensation of DIC, it was concluded that section 1159 is not applicable to determinations of entitlement to benefits under section 1151.

Concerning the question of whether termination of entitlement to benefits under section 1151 would constitute a severance of service connection subject to the requirements of 38 C.F.R. § 3.105(d), it was determined that, based on the same analysis noted above, the provisions of section 3.105(d) are not applicable to termination of entitlement to benefits under section 1151.  The standards and procedures governing termination of section 1151 entitlement, however, are substantially similar to those in section 3.105(d).  Under section 3.105(a), a prior final decision of entitlement to benefits under section 1151 may be reversed or amended only on the basis of clear and unmistakable error.  The due process provisions of section 3.103 are applicable to terminations of entitlement to benefits under section 1151.  The only significant difference between termination of entitlement under section 3.105(a) and severance of service connection under section 3.105(d) pertains to the effective date of the discontinuance of benefits.  Section 3.105(a) requires that a termination of an award based on clear and unmistakable error be made effective the date of last payment.  Severance of service connection under section 3.105(d) is effective the last day of the month in which a 60-day period from the date of notice to the beneficiary of the final rating action expires.

This opinion does not require a change in current regulations, policies or procedures, however it should be discussed during the next Judicial Review hotline.
