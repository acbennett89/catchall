Decision Assessment Document
 Sharp v. Shinseki, Oct 15, 2009, 23 Vet.App. 267  (2009)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that entitlement to additional compensation for dependents under 38 U.S.C. 1115 is premised on any rating decision establishing compensation under 38 U.S.C. 1114 and a disability rating of not less than 30 percent.

The Court, in interpreting 38 U.S.C. 5110(f), held that the effective date for additional compensation for dependents shall be the same as the effective date of the disability rating granted in the rating decision giving rise to such entitlement, irrespective of any previous grant of section 1115 benefits, if proof of dependents is submitted within one year of notice of the decision.  The Court also held that there can be multiple rating decisions that establish entitlement to additional dependence compensation.

**Impact on VBA:**

Manual changes were required under III.iii.5.L to implement the Court's holding.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) granted service connection for a bilateral hip disorder in August 1995.  The regional office (RO), in implementing the Board’s decision, forwarded the veteran a letter on December 1, 1995, informing him of the need to provide dependents’ information on an attached VA Form 21-686c in order to receive additional compensation for dependents.  The RO letter also indicated that if the information were not received within one year of the date of the letter, additional benefits for dependents would not be paid any earlier than the date the information is received.

The RO received the requested information on December 9, 1996, more than one year after the date of the letter.  The RO notified the veteran that he would begin to receive increased benefits effective January 1, 1997 – the first day of the month following receipt of the evidence because it was not received until more than one year after the RO’s request.  The veteran did not appeal that decision.  The RO, in November 1998, granted the veteran a TDIU evaluation effective December 1, 1988.  The veteran appealed the decision because it did not include additional compensation for dependents retroactive to 1988; however, he died in December 1999, while the appeal was still pending.

The RO, Board, and Court initially denied the appellant’s claim for accrued benefits; however, the Federal Circuit, in 2005, vacated and remanded in part the Court’s decision based on its decision in Terry v. Principi, 367 F.3d. 1291 (Fed. Cir. 2004), which clarified that recover of accrued benefits is not limited to the two-year period immediately preceding death, but could be awarded for any two-year period.  The RO, in November 2006, denied accrued benefits for additional compensation for dependents on the basis that the December 1996 decision granting additional compensation for dependents became final and there is no provision of law providing a second opportunity to establish an earlier effective date.  The Board, in April 2007, agreed with the RO that there was no legal basis to award an earlier effective date after entitlement to additional compensation for dependents had already been established.

The appellant argued before the Court that the TDIU grant, effective December 1, 1998, was a “qualifying disability rating”  entitling the veteran to additional compensation for dependents, effective December 1, 1988.  She contended that section 1115 was clear and unambiguous that a “veteran… shall be entitled to additional compensation for dependents” when certain disability percentages are met and that section 5110(f) does not limit the effective date to a particular rating decision, but applies to any rating decision meeting the percentage requirements of section 1115.

The Court concluded that entitlement to section 1115 compensation does not require a separate claim and that entitlement to additional compensation for dependents under section 1115 is implicitly raised whenever a veteran has a disability rating of at least 30 percent and submits evidence of dependents.

The Court, in interpreting section 5110(f), observed that, because it was ambiguous, and because the Secretary had not offered a persuasive interpretation of the statute, the rule under Brown v. Gardner, 513 U.S. 115, 118 (1994) requires an expansive reading of the statute.  In applying the rule that “interpretive doubt is to be resolved in the veteran’s favor,”  the Court held that section 5110(f) permits the reading whereby any rating decision meeting the statutory criteria of section 1115 can be the basis of an effective date for the award of additional compensation for dependents.

In applying its holding to the case, the Court found that the November 1998 rating decision awarding TDIU, effective December 1, 1988, was a rating decision that established additional compensation, and VA received the requisite information regarding dependents within one year of the 1998 decision.

Accordingly, the Court held that the appellant was entitled to dependency compensation at the TDIU rate from the effective date of her husband’s TDIU rating, December 1, 1988.  However, the accrued benefits claim is limited to two years before the veteran’s death, based on the version of 38 U.S.C. § 5121 in effect in 1999, when the veteran died.

The Court reversed in part the Board’s April 2007 decision and remanded for implementation of its opinion that portion of the decision that found that there was no provision in law that entitled the appellant to an earlier effective date for additional compensation for dependents.
