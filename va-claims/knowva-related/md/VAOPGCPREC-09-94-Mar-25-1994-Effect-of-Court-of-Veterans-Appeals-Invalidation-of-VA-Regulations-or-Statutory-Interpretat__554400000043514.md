Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 09-94, Mar 25, 1994, Effect of Court of Veterans Appeals' Invalidation of VA Regulations or Statutory Interpretations
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Retroactive effect of Court of Veterans Appeals (CVA or Court) decisions.

**Impact on VBA**

Decisions of the CVA invalidating VA regulations or statutory or regulatory interpretations do not have retroactive effect in relation to prior "final" adjudications of claims, but should be given retroactive effect as they relate to claims still open on direct review.

**Summary on facts and findings**

The General Counsel noted that new precedents are retroactive in pending cases based on recent Supreme Court decisions:

When this Court applies a rule of federal law to the parties before it, that rule is the controlling interpretation of federal law and must be given full retroactive effect in all cases still open on direct review and as to all events, regardless of whether such events predate or postdate our announcement of the rule.

Harper v. Virginia Dept. of Taxation, 113 S. CT. 2510 (1993).

The General Counsel indicated that benefits would be retroactive in cases that are still open to direct review based on Harper.  Accordingly, for those cases pending on the date of a Court decision invalidating a regulation or statutory interpretation, the effective date of an award of benefits would be the date of the claim, even though it would pre-date the Court decision.  See 38 C.F.R. § 3.400.  However, the General Counsel also noted that the principles of finality that govern VA decisions suggest that this rule may not apply in finally decided claims because such cases are not still open to direct review.

The General Counsel then observed that, arguably, 38 CFR § 3.105(a) is the only basis for a retroactive award when a regulation or statutory interpretation is invalidated.  Although CVA had not specifically addressed this situation, several cases indicated that CVA had not applied its precedents retroactively in clear and unmistakable error cases.

After considering several historical General Counsel and Solicitor General opinions, the General Counsel concluded that:

The application of section 3.105(a) to grant retroactive benefits when the CVA invalidates a VA regulation would be erroneous because the regulation states on its face that section 3.105(a) does not apply to changes of law or legal interpretation.  Moreover, because section 3.105 and its earlier versions predated judicial review, the drafters could not have contemplated that decisions would have been reversed for clear and unmistakable error as the result of judicial invalidation of VA regulations.

The General Counsel opinion makes clear that new Court precedents which invalidate VA regulations may not be used to find clear and unmistakable error in prior, final VA decisions.  If VA changes a regulation to conform to a CVA holding, the effective date of an award of benefits pursuant to such amendment would be governed by 38 U.S.C. § 5110(g) and 38 C.F.R. § 3.114, for claims filed on or after the date of the CVA holding.

 Based on this opinion, regional offices may now review claims for entitlement to retroactive benefits in cases affected by CVA decisions such as Salgado v. Brown, 4 Vet. App. 316 (1993), and Gregory v. Brown, 5 Vet. App. 108 (1993).

There are three scenarios under this opinion in which effective dates would differ.  The following examples show the scenarios and give the correct effective dates for each.  These examples are based on Gregory v. Brown which invalidated a portion of 38 C.F.R. § 3.53(a) and was decided on May 13, 1993.

In the first situation, a claimant filed a claim on January 12, 1993, and the claim was pending on May 13, 1993.  In this case, any award of benefits would be effective from January 12, 1993.

In the second situation a claimant, who had a final decision denying her benefits in 1985 due to reliance on the now invalidated regulation, files a claim on April 15, 1994, within one year of the Gregory decision.  In this case, any award of benefits would be effective from May 13, 1993.  In this case, any award of benefits would be effective from April 15, 1994.

In the third situation, the claimant files a claim on June 15, 1994, over one year after the Gregory decision.  In this case, any award of benefits would be effective from June 15, 1993.  In this case, any award of benefits would be effective from June 15, 1994.

The General Counsel opinion makes clear that while new or pending claims are subject to review based on new precedents, finally decided claims are no longer open to direct review.

The Regulations Staff should consider whether or not changes may be required to 38 CFR § 3.114 to clarify that implementing procedural and regulatory changes based on a liberalizing CVA decision are ordinarily effective from the date of the Court decision except for claims which were pending on that date.

In addition, the Regulations Staff should consider whether or not section 3.400 should be amended to provide that for pending claims benefits are retroactive to date of claim if awarded pursuant to a later judicial precedent.

M21-1, Part IV, Chapter 38 (or other chapters) should be revised to provide examples of effective dates in claims allowed based on new CVA precedents.

The Judicial Review Staff should advise regional offices of any cases it has logged for review based on Salgado.

Copies of this assessment document will be provided to regional offices to explain effective date considerations based on this precedent opinion.
