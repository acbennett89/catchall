Decision Assessment Document
 Rudd v. Nicholson, Aug 18, 2006, 20 Vet.App. 296
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that VA claimants may not properly file, and VA has no authority to adjudicate, a “freestanding” claim for an earlier effective date in an attempt to overcome the finality of an unappealed regional office (RO) decision.

The Court indicated that claimants in such cases may file a motion to revise based upon clear and unmistakable error (CUE) with respect to the assignment of an effective date in final RO decisions.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Manual M21-1MR will be amended in view of the Court’s holding.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran was awarded service connection for a low back disability and assigned a 40 percent evaluation shortly after separation in 1978.  On November 1, 1994, he filed a claim for service connection for post traumatic stress disorder (PTSD).  The veteran later filed a claim on December 14, 1994, for increased evaluation for his low back disability and for service connection for “bowel syndrome.”

The RO, in March 1995, granted a 60 percent evaluation for the low back disability and service connection for irritable bowel syndrome; the effective date for both awards was December 14, 1994.  The RO denied service connection for PTSD; however, the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) later granted service connection for bipolar disorder.  The RO, in September 1997, implemented the Board decision with an effective date of award of November 1, 1994, for service connection for bipolar disorder.  Neither the March 1995 or September 1997 RO decision was appealed.

In July 1999, the veteran requested earlier effective dates for all his service-connected disabilities.  The Board, in denying the veteran’s claim for earlier effective dates, determined there was no evidence in the record of any earlier communication that could be construed as an informal claim for irritable bowel syndrome or bipolar disorder.  Regarding the low back disability, the Board concluded that there was no evidence of an earlier informal claim or evidence of an ascertainable increase in the service-connected back disability in the year prior to filing the December 1994 claim.

The veteran, before the Court, alleged various duty to notify and assist errors by VA and also contended that there was no authority in a March 2006 single-judge decision for the implicit holding that there is no such thing as a freestanding claim for an earlier effective date.

The Court observed that the effective dates relating to the three disabilities on appeal were assigned by the RO in 1995 and 1997 decisions were not appealed and became final.  The Court indicated that the veteran could only attempt to overcome the finality of these decisions by a request for revision based on CUE or a claim to reopen based upon new and material evidence.  Of these two, because the proper effective date for an award based on a claim to reopen can be no earlier than the date on which that claim was received, only a request for revision premised on CUE could result in earlier effective dates for the service connection and increased rating claims.

Because the veteran did not argue that his request for an earlier effective date should be construed as a motion to revise based on CUE, the Court discerned that the only remaining possibility is some form of freestanding claim for earlier effective dates in the 1995 and 1997 RO decisions, which assigned effective dates that were not appealed and became final.

The Court held that a freestanding effective date claim vitiates the rule of finality and to the extent that the veteran has improperly raised a freestanding claim for an earlier effective date to overcome the finality of the 1995 and 1997 RO decisions, the appeal will be dismissed.  Thus, the Board erred to the extent that it entertained an improper claim without imposing the strictures of finality.  The Court did not address the veteran’s other contentions of error because it did not find a proper claim in the case.

The Court advised the veteran that he was not without recourse, as he remained free to file a motion to revise based upon CUE with respect to the 1995 and 1997 RO decisions.

The Court vacated the 2001 Board decision and dismissed the appeal.
