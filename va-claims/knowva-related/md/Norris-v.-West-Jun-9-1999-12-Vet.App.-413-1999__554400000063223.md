Decision Assessment Document
 Norris v. West, Jun 9, 1999, 12 Vet.App. 413 (1999)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that consistent with case law and the provisions of 38 C.F.R. 3.155(c) and 3.157(b), when VA conducts a medical examination to determine whether the veteran’s condition has improved in order to determine if a service-connected rating should be decreased or continued, if the results indicate an increase in severity in the disability, VA must then evaluate the circumstances as a claim for an increased rating.  Furthermore, when an RO is considering a rating increase claim from a claimant whose schedular rating meets the minimum criteria of 38 C.F.R. 4.16(a) and there is evidence of current service-connected unemployability in the claimant’s claims file or under VA control, evaluation of that rating increase must also include an evaluation of a reasonably raised claim for a TDIU rating.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into guidance contained in Manual M21-1, IV.ii.2.F.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

A May 1979 RO decision increased the veteran’s service connected disability evaluation for anxiety neurosis to 50%.  In 1980, the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA) denied a rating in excess of 50% and denied entitlement to a rating of total disability based on individual unemployability (TDIU).  A November 1985 BVA decision denied a rating in excess of 70%, denied a claim for TDIU and awarded the veteran temporary total disability due to hospitalization.

An April 30, 1987, letter from a regional office notified the veteran that he would be scheduled to report for a physical examination to determine whether his “disability had improved.”  On June 9, 1987, the veteran reported for an examination and was initially evaluated by a VA social worker who concluded:  “This young man does appear to be exceedingly ill and probably so involved in his emotional disturbance he is not able to work at this time.”  A VA psychiatrist also examined the veteran but did not express an opinion regarding the veteran’s employability.  A June 29, 1987, regional office decision continued the 70% disability rating.  This decision did not mention the social worker’s June 9, 1987, opinion about the veteran’s employability.

In November 1988, the veteran voluntarily sought admission to a VAMC for increasing anxiety and was diagnosed as suffering from generalized anxiety disorder.  Upon his release from the VAMC, Dr. George Penn, a VA physician, opined that the veteran was “capable of returning to prehospital activities immediately.”  A January 1989 regional office decision  awarded the veteran temporary total disability for this hospitalization and implicitly continued his 70% rating.  When this rating was reviewed again in February 1989, it remained at 70% effective January 1, 1989.  This decision was not appealed. The veteran was again hospitalized on April 20, 1990.  Following this, the veteran was ultimately awarded, in a BVA decision dated in September 1994, a total rating based upon individual unemployability, effective from April 20, 1990.

In November 1994, the veteran asserted that CUE had been committed in regional office decisions dated May 9, 1986 (this claim was eventually abandoned), June 29, 1987, and February 10, 1989.  The BVA determined that the June 1987 and the February 1989 decisions did not contain CUE.  The BVA stated that “any communication from a claimant indicating an attempt to apply for a benefit administered by VA may be considered an informal claim..”  The BVA concluded that “there was no such communication from the veteran . . . concerning TDIU prior to the rating decision []of June 29, 1987.”  The BVA also concluded that at the time of the June 1987 decision, the veteran had not specifically asserted a claim of entitlement to TDIU and that no such claim had been pending.  The BVA also observed that “it is highly questionable whether a failure to adjudicate a pending claim would ever involve CUE.”

The Court affirmed the BVA decision, but for reasons different than those given by the BVA.  The BVA found that no TDIU claim was reasonably raised to the RO and, therefore, that there was no CUE.  The Court held that a TDIU claim was reasonably raised to the regional office and was not adjudicated.  Thus, the Court found that there was no final regional office decision on the TDIU claim that can be subject to a CUE attack.

The Court pointed out that, under the VA statutory and regulatory scheme, evidence of a veteran’s unemployability arising from an already allowed service-connected disability is indeed evidence of an increase in the severity of that disability.  The Court cited Wood v. Derwinski, explaining that “[w]e start with the premise that [38 CFR] § 3.157(b) was intended to help a veteran avoid a procedural pitfall by directing that the mere submission of additional evidence is sufficient to initiate a claim.”  Wood v. Derwinski, 1 Vet. App. 367, 369, (1991).  The Court stated that in this case we have an adjudicated service-connected compensable psychiatric disorder and a VA examination report that § 3.157(b) mandates that the Secretary accept as an informal claim for a rating increase.  The Court further elaborated that a TDIU rating is not a basis for an award of service connection.  Rather, it is merely an alternate way to obtain a total disability rating without being rated 100% disabled under the Rating Schedule.

The Court held that when VA conducts a medical examination to determine, as in this case, whether the veteran’s condition has improved in order to determine if a service-connected rating should be decreased or continued, if the results indicate an increase in severity in the disability, VA must then evaluate the circumstances as a claim for an increased rating.  Further, the Court held that when an RO is considering a rating increase claim from a claimant whose schedular rating meets the minimum criteria of 38 CFR § 4.16(a) and there is evidence of current service-connected unemployability in the claimant’s claims file or under VA control, evaluation of that rating increase must also include an evaluation of a reasonably raised claim for a TDIU rating.  Where those two criteria are satisfied, a well grounded TDIU claim is included in every rating increase claim and VA would be required to adjudicate that well-grounded TDIU claim.

The Court disagreed with the veteran’s argument that the RO’s failure to adjudicate an informally raised TDIU claim constitutes a final disallowance.  The Court stated that when, as here, VA has failed to comply during the adjudication process with certain procedural requirements mandated by law or regulation, the claim remains pending.  Therefore, there is no final adverse RO decision that can be subject to a CUE attack.  The TDIU claim reasonably raised in 1987 and 1989 thus remains pending at the RO.
