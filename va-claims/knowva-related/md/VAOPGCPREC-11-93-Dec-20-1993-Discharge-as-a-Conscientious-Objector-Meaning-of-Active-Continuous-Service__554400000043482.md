Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 11-93, Dec 20, 1993, Discharge as a Conscientious Objector; Meaning of Active Continuous Service
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

When a claimant has been discharged under honorable conditions as a conscientious objector, does 38 C.F.R. § 3.12(c)(1) bar eligibility for benefits where the evidence does not establish that the claimant refused to perform military duty, wear the uniform, or comply with lawful military orders?  May a period of service be considered "active, continuous service" for purposes of 38 C.F.R. § 3.307(a)(1) where the period of service was interrupted by a thirteen-day period during which the servicemember was classified as being absent without official leave (AWOL)?

**Impact on VBA**

Under the provisions of 38 U.S.C. § 5303(a) and 38 C.F.R. § 3.12(c)(1), a claimant who is discharged under honorable conditions as a conscientious objector is not thereby barred from eligibility for veterans' benefits unless, in addition to being a conscientious objector, the claimant also refused to perform military duty or refused to wear the uniform or otherwise to comply with lawful orders of competent military authorities.

In order to be eligible for service connection on a presumptive basis for certain chronic and tropical diseases, regulations at 38 C.F.R. § 3.307(a)(1) require that a veteran have had at least ninety days continuous, active service.  Not all unauthorized absences result in a break in service for purposes of this requirement.  An absence of thirteen days, after which the absentee voluntarily returned to the absentee's unit, although not creditable for pay or time-in-service purposes, did not constitute a break in service for purposes of the "active, continuous service" requirement imposed by regulations governing presumptive service connection.

**Summary on facts and findings**

The questions presented arose in a reopened claim for service connection for multiple sclerosis.  The claimant entered active service on July 24, 1973, and was discharged as a conscientious objector on November 15, 1973, under honorable conditions.  He was AWOL from August 9 to August 21, 1973.  Thus, the claimant served sixteen days prior to a thirteen-day period of AWOL and eighty-six days following the AWOL period.

Although a discharge under honorable conditions is binding on VA as to character of discharge under the provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.12(a), benefits are not payable regardless of the service department's characterization of the discharge, if the servicemember was discharged or released under certain specified conditions.  Under 38 U.S.C. § 5303(a), those conditions include discharge on the grounds of being a conscientious objector who refused to perform military duty or refused to wear the uniform or otherwise comply with lawful orders of competent military authority.  The records in this case did not indicate that the claimant failed to meet these requirements.  General Counsel noted that not all persons discharged as conscientious objectors have engaged in the conduct specified in section 5303(a).  Department of Defense regulations specify that servicemembers must be counseled regarding the possible effects of being discharged as a conscientious objector.  During pendency of the request for discharge, the servicemember is required by regulations to conform to the normal requirements of military service and to perform satisfactorily such duties to which they are assigned.  Thus, in order to determine whether a separation as a conscientious objector operates to bar benefit eligibility, a factual determination must be made concerning whether the claimant, in addition to claiming conscientious objector status, also refused to perform military duty or refused to wear the uniform, or otherwise comply with lawful military orders.

Concerning the second question presented, General Counsel noted that while 38 U.S.C. § 1112(a) itself does not specify that active service must be continuous, VA's implementing regulations and instructions have long contained such a requirement, and this requirement was upheld by the Court of Veterans Appeals in Lorenzano v. Brown, 4 Vet. App. 446 (1993).  In 49 Op. Sol. 259, 264 (3-20-40), the VA Solicitor held that periods of unauthorized absence are not to be counted in determining length of service for pension purposes.  The Solicitor also stated that "it is conceded an AWOL soldier remains on the active list and that his status is not changed to the point of terminating his period of service."  Thus, while periods of AWOL are generally excluded from length of service calculation, the active status of an AWOL individual is not automatically terminated.  In the case presented, the individual remained assigned to an active unit, returned voluntarily to that unit, and worked through prescribed channels to secure a discharge under honorable conditions.  Such an absence was not found to be indicative of a break in service.  It was noted that Department of Defense policy considers AWOL servicemembers to remain in service with their units until they are administratively dropped from the rolls and classified as deserters after thirty consecutive days of absence.  General Counsel also noted that the provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.12a(a)(1), governing the minimum active duty service requirement, provides that nonduty periods excludable under 38 C.F.R. § 3.15, which include unauthorized absences without pay. are deducted from total time served but are not considered a break in service.  While these provisions do not apply to determinations of presumptive service connection, they demonstrate that VA has generally not considered a period of AWOL to constitute a break in service for purposes of determining eligibility for VA benefits.

As the provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.12a(a)(1) do not exclude periods of nonauthorized absence from the continuous active duty requirement, it would be an anomalous position to include the same periods in determining that a veteran did not meet the "active, continuous service" requirements of § 3.307(a)(1).  Recommend that § 3.307 be revised to reflect the provisions of § 3.12a and this precedent opinion.  Following revision of the regulations, M21-1, Part VI, par. 7.59 should be amended to reflect the changes.  A copy of this assessment document should be furnished regional offices for guidance.
