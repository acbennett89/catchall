Decision Assessment Document
Ward, Neal v. Wilkie, Jun 14, 2019, 31 Vet.App. 233 (2019)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the Secretary’s “permanent worsening” standard for assessing an increase in disability of a non-service-connected (NSC) condition “proximately due to or the result of a service-connected disability” in the secondary service connection context is an impermissible attempt to add requirements that appear in neither the enabling statute, 38 U.S.C. 1110, or the implementing regulation, 38 C.F.R. 3.310, for secondary service connection.

The Court held that providing compensation for only the incremental disability attributed to the service-connected condition does not require that the increase in disability must be permanent.  The Court noted that Allen v. Brown, 7 Vet.App. 439 (1995) ( en banc) interpreted the governing statutes to provide that “any” increase in disability of a NSC condition is compensable and that conditions may wax and wane in severity, but nevertheless be medically ascertainable as an incremental increase in disability, and hence compensable.  Allen also defined “disability” as “the impairment of earning capacity due to disease, injury, or defect.”

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court’s holding will be implemented into guidance contained in the Manual M21-1 under III.iv.6.B and IV.ii.2.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

Veteran Ward is service connected for femur and knee disabilities, to include a total knee replacement, and alleges that his NSC bilateral hip condition resulted, at least, in part, from stress caused by his service-connected disabilities.  Veteran Neal contends that his NSC low back condition resulted from his service-connected left knee replacement.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) remanded both cases for examinations with medical opinions to address whether it was at least as likely as not that a service-connected disability aggravated a NSC disability.  The Board defined “aggravated” in these cases as “permanently increased in severity” beyond the natural progress of the disorder or “permanently made worse” beyond the natural progression of the disability.  The Board denied Veteran Ward’s case in April 2016 and Veteran Neal’s case in March 2017.

The veterans argued before the Court that the “permanent worsening” standard is inconsistent with the governing statute as construed in Allen and with the implementing regulation.  The Secretary, defining “permanence” as “measurability,” argued that any increase in disability must be measurable to compare it with the baseline severity of the NSC condition to establish Allen NSC aggravation under 3.310(b).

The Court, concluding that the Secretary’s reliance on cases construing section 1153 to be misplaced, held that cases construing “aggravation” with respect to 38 U.S.C. 1153 have no relevance in the secondary service-connection context.  In Allen, the Court made it clear that secondary service connection derives, not from section 1153, but sections 1110 and 1131, which do not contain the term “aggravation” in the portions of the statutes from which secondary service connection derives.

The Court explained that the Allen Court clearly construed the statutory phrase “disability resulting from” as encompassing “any additional impairment in earning capacity resulting from an already service-connected condition.”  This concept is incorporated into the implementing regulation, 3.310(b), which provides that “any increase in severity” of a NSC injury or disease will be compensated.

The Court noted that although it is well settled that “aggravation” for purposes of the presumption of aggravation under section 1153 requires a permanent increase in severity, it does not follow that permanent worsening is a requirement for secondary service connection of a NSC injury or disease under sections 1110 and 1131.  The Allen Court decided that compensation was due for any incremental increase in disability – any additional impairment of earning capacity – in NSC disabilities resulting from service-connected conditions, above the degree of disability existing before the increase, regardless of its permanence.

The Court rejected the Secretary’s argument that any incremental increase in disability must be measurable, and to be measurable, the incremental disability must be permanent, which the Secretary based on language in Allen that compensation is “for the degree of disability (but only that degree) over and above the degree of disability existing prior to the aggravation.”

The Court concluded that the “permanent worsening” standard has no application in cases involving an incremental increase in disability of a NSC condition proximately due to or the result of a service-connected disease or injury.  The Court also determined that to the extent that the Secretary employed the word “measurable” in its primary meaning, requiring a numerically quantifiable assessment, that requirement is also not present in the governing statutes or in the implementing regulation.

The Court also addressed Hunt v. Derwinski, 1 Vet.App. 292 (1991), in which it previously indicated that 38 U.S.C. 353, the predecessor statute to section 1153, provided the definition of  aggravation for 3.310, which required permanent worsening of the disability.  The Court held that this pronouncement in Hunt was plainly dicta and to the extent that the Hunt Court may have intended a binding interpretation of 3.310, as it existed at that time, that interpretation was effectively overruled by the en banc Allen decision, which set forth a different interpretation.

The Court remanded both cases for readjudication consistent with its decision.  There was also a motion before the Court to certify a class of “veterans who are or will be subject to the Secretary’s unlawful “permanent worsening” standing for deciding whether a service-connected disability has aggravated a secondary disability.  The Court denied the class certification motion because its decision is precedential, which should have full effect throughout VA.
