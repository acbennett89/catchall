**Part 01 Introduction**

## **Section 1.01 Overview**

1. This chapter provides VBA’s financial procedures to implement VA Departmental financial policies relating to Committees on Waivers and Compromises (COWC) activities at VBA Regional Offices (RO), VBA Regional Processing Offices (RPO), and the Debt Management Center (DMC).
2. This chapter prescribes the required financial procedures to use in maintaining financial control and accountability over waiver and compromise actions.

## **Section 1.02  Benefit Debt Authority**

1. Title [38 of the United States Code (USC), Section 5302](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:38%20section:5302%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section5302)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true), provides the authority for any Veteran payee or beneficiary, indebted to VA as the result of participation in a VA benefits program, to request a waiver of repayment of that debt.  Title 38 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) sections [1.955](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.955) et seq. implement the authority for waiver consideration found in [38 USC 5302](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:38%20section:5302%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section5302)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true "38 U.S.C. 5302") and [5 USC 5584](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:5%20section:5584%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title5-section5584)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true "5") and the compromise authority found [38 USC 3720 (a)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:38%20section:3720%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section3720)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true "38 U.S.C. 3720(a)") and [31 USC 3711](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:31%20section:3711%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title31-section3711)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true "31").   Under  [38 CFR 1.965](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.965), VBA may waive overpayments of home loan. Note: The term “regional office”, as used in 38 CFR 1.955 et seq., includes VA Medical and Regional Office Centers and VA Centers where such are established.
2. Section 252 of the “Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023” (P.L. 117-328) amended 38 U.S.C. § 5302B which establishes prohibitions on the incurrence of debts due to delays in VA processing. Delays in VA processing generally refer to the agency’s failure to timely process information provided by or on behalf of a beneficiary due to operational and administrative constraints wholly outside the beneficiary’s control. In the Secretary of VA *Temporary Timeliness Instruction* memorandum dated December 22, 2023, SECVA further refined and quantified the “applicable timeliness standards” referenced in statute, noting that, absent delays directly attributable to a beneficiary’s actions, VA must issue a decision and associated notice within one year of receiving a claim or other source of information that results in potential debt, and when VA fails to issue timely decision notice within that one-year timeliness period, it will not create or enforce additional debt for overpayments made beyond said period.
3. Benefit debts also include debts that arise out of participation in a VA benefits program (e.g., compensation, education, pension, home loan, etc.) under authority of Title 10 or Title 38 of the United States Code.

1. A waiver should not be granted for drill pay debt.  According to 10 U.S.C. 12316 and 38 U.S.C. 5304(c), the laws prohibit duplication of disability compensation payments when a Veteran receives drill pay for the same period.  Any overpayment of benefits resulting from the dual compensation must be collected and a waiver should not be granted.  (Please note that a financial status report will not be required or considered, if submitted, because a drill pay debt waiver will not be granted regardless.)
2. Also, see Memorandum from the General Counsel (under the Attachments Section below) dated May 29, 2015. Prohibition against duplication of benefits also applies to tort claims.  When a Veteran is injured under circumstances that result in possible entitlement to benefits under 38 U.S.C. 1151, the Veteran may also seek a judgment against the U.S. in a civil action, called a tort claim.  The Veteran’s compensation benefits are subject to offset against the tort claim.  This offset is not a debt and has no waiver right.  (see M21-1, Part IV, Subpart ii, Chapter 3, Section D – Disability Compensation under 38 U.S.C. 1151.

3. Overpayments established under the fugitive felon program are subject to waiver or compromise.

1. Veterans receiving VA benefits who have warrants issued for their arrest may lose their benefits and have to repay received benefits.
2. VA will withhold benefits from Veterans and family members who are categorized as fugitive felons or have felony warrants against them([38 USC 5313B](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:38%20section:5313b%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section5313b)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true); 38 CFR Part 3, [Sections 3.665](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFR24d035b1eee2d6f/section-3.665) and [3.666](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFR24d035b1eee2d6f/section-3.666)).
3. COWC members and other VA employees may not determine that the debtor is not a fugitive felon based on statements or evidence produced by the debtor, a family member, or the debtor’s attorney.  Evidence refuting the fugitive felon status must be provided from an official source including official documents and reports of contact with Bureau of Prison officials, parole officers, and other government officials.
4. For additional guidance on fugitive felon policies and procedures, refer to  in the [M21-1, Part XIV, Chapter 8 - Fugitive Felon Match](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000173306/M21-1-Part-XIV-Chapter-8-Fugitive-Felon-Match?query=fugitive%20felon%20match).

4. [38 CFR 1.962](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.962) states that the death of an indebted payee, either prior to a request for waiver of the indebtedness or during Committee consideration of the waiver request, shall not preclude waiver consideration. There shall be no waiver consideration of an indebtedness that results from the receipt of a benefit payment by a non-payee who has no claim or entitlement to such payment. See Attachments Section below for OGC Guidance on waiver of overpayment after Veteran’s Death dated May 18, 2017.
5. Benefit entitlement terminates upon the death of a Veteran or beneficiary as of the last day of the month preceding death, 38 U.S.C. § 5112(b)(1); 38 C.F.R. § 3.500(g). A benefit payment after the effective date of termination is a payment to which the payee is not entitled by law (see Attachments Section below for OGC Guidance on January\_2025\_Specific Debt Type Question.)  See [VA Financial Policy, Volume XII, Chapter 11](https://department.va.gov/financial-policy-documents/financial-document/chapter-11-waiver-requests-and-processing-cowc/#1105-policies), Paragraph 110501 D. for additional guidance.
   1. The COWC will consider waiver request and reconsideration files by the survivor and representative, if any, of the deceased Veteran’s estate.
   2. Since [38 CFR 20.1302](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-20/subpart-N/section-20.1302) precludes the Board of Veterans Appeal (BVA) from further consideration of an appeal upon the death of a Veteran, the survivor and representative, if any, of the deceased Veteran’s estate will not be provided BVA appeal right.
6. The Veterans Health Administration (VHA) fiscal officer (NOT VBA’s COWCs), including the Consolidated Patient Account Center’s fiscal officer or their designees, will consider all waiver requests for medical or pharmacy co-payment debts and for employee debts arising out of contractual employment.

## **Section 1.03 Non-Benefit Debt Authority**

1. Any debt that does not arise as a result of participation in a VA benefits program is considered a non-benefit debt (e.g., health and life insurance benefits, medical emergency or humanitarian care provided to the public debts, and third-party/station debts).

## **Section 1.04 Employee Debt Authority**

1. [5 USC § 5584](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title5/part3/subpartD/chapter55/subchapter8&edition=prelim) provides the authority for employees to request waivers for erroneous payments of pay and allowances which includes FERS-FRAE and travel, transportation, and relocation expense debts for employees.

1. Delegations of authority and limitations for waiver actions under [5 USC § 5584](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title5/part3/subpartD/chapter55/subchapter8&edition=prelim) are set forth in [38 CFR 1.963a](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.963).
2. The waiver authority applies to employees of an Executive agency. The term "employee" has the meaning given it in 5 U.S.C. 2105. (See 5 U.S.C. 5581, 5584(a), and 5584(g).) While the erroneous payment must be directed to a current employee (i.e., compensation for service as an employee) in order for § 5584 to apply, a former employee may pursue waiver of collection of a covered erroneous payment. Also, an employee's surviving beneficiary or estate that is liable for repayment of an erroneous payment directed to the employee (based on service performed before death) may also seek a waiver. Refer to [OPM Fact Sheet: Waiving Overpayments](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/pay-administration/fact-sheets/waiving-overpayments/).
3. VA COWCs do not have the authority to consider waiver of employee health benefit debts for an employee who was in a leave without pay (LWOP) status and elected to incur a debt to continue to have federal employee health benefit (FEHB) coverage. 
   1. When an employee is in a non-pay status and there are no erroneous payments made to the employee during that time, the FEHB coverage debt may not be considered for waiver under [5 U.S.C. § 5584](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title5/part3/subpartD/chapter55/subchapter8&edition=prelim) in the absence of erroneous payments.
   2. However, if VA under-withholding or not withholding the correct FEHB premiums (e.g., due to HR’s mistake) resulted in the employee receiving erroneous pay, the employee with such a debt is eligible for waiver under [5 U.S.C. § 5584](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title5/part3/subpartD/chapter55/subchapter8&edition=prelim).

4. Waive requests for all overpayment and/or breach of contract or continuing service agreement debts related to the Health Professionals Educational Assistance Program (EAP) of 38 U.S.C. § 7601 *et al*.These debts include, but are not limited to breach of recruitment or relocation incentive service agreement (i.e. Breach of Employee Incentive Scholarship Program (EISP) National Nursing Education Initiative (NNEI), or VA National Education for Employees Program (VANEEP)) is under the jurisdiction of the appropriate VA Staff Office. Effective June 30, 2026, waiver and compromise requests for an employee debt related to a breach of a service obligation (greater than $100) will no longer be adjudicated by HR, instead these requests along with a report of investigation completed by the appropriate VA Staff Office will be transmitted by the FSC to the appropriate COWC (St. Paul or Milwaukee) for adjudication.
5. Waiver requests in relation to the The Sergeant First Class (SFC) Heath Robinson Honoring our Promise to Address Comprehensive Toxics (PACT) Act, Critical Skill Incentive Implementation which is under the jurisdiction of the Under Secretary of the losing Administration.  The losing Staff Office’s Principal Deputy Assistant Secretary or equivalent position is the approving authority for waiver requests for employees within VA Central Office and the Office of Information & Technology.  Effective June 30, 2026, waiver and compromise requests for an employee debt related to a breach of a service obligation in relation to the PACT Act, Critical Skill Incentive Implementation will no longer be adjudicated by HR, instead these requests along with a report of investigation completed by the appropriate VA Staff Office will be transmitted by the FSC to the appropriate COWC (St. Paul or Milwaukee) for adjudication.
6. The Families First Coronavirus Response Act (FFCRA) provided for two leave entitlements that may apply to Federal employees.
   1. Generally, the Federal government must provide its employees with a COVID-19 leave called the Emergency Paid Sick Leave Act (EPSLA).
   2. Certain employees may also be entitled to additional leave under the Emergency Family and Medical Leave Expansion Act (EFMLEA).  Debts incurred as a result of EPSLA or EFMLEA will not be considered for waiver.
7. A report of investigation (ROI) is a document containing employee information and questions as well as information from agency representatives supporting the validity of an employee debt. Reports are investigations are used by waiver and hearing officers to assist in evaluating debt.
8. A waiver is considered when the collection would be against equity and good conscience and not in the best interest of the United States or when the erroneous payment occurred through administrative error.  Even waivers based on administrative error need to have the elements of equity and good conscience applied before making a decision.  An administrative error does not automatically mean a waiver should be approved.
9. An employee's surviving beneficiary or estate that is liable for repayment of an erroneous payment directed to the employee (based on service performed before death) may also seek a waiver.

## **Section 1.05 COWC Authority**

1. COWC were established in St. Paul, Milwaukee, and Buffalo RO to perform the duties and assume the responsibilities delegated by [38 CFR 1.956](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.956) and [1.957](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.957).  The duties, delegations of authority, and all actions required of the COWC are accomplished under the direction of, and authority vested in, the RO Director.
2. COWCs were established in each RO to consider and determine, as limited in [38 CFR 1.955](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.955 "1.955") et seq., settlement, compromise and/or waiver requests concerning debts and overpayments.
3. VBA has implemented a Centralized Committee System to consider waiver and compromise requests; however, the RO Finance Officer retains jurisdiction of compromise debts under $1,000 in accordance with [38 CFR 1.957(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.957).
4. In this capacity, the RO Finance Officer serves as a local COWC.
5. The Centralized Committee System considers waivers and compromises at the jurisdiction locations, depending on the type of debt, indicated below.  [Part 03](#p03) below presents the type of debt and applicable jurisdiction for waiver and compromise requests.

# **Part 02 Roles and Responsibilities**

## **Section 2.01 VA Central Office**

1. The Secretary of Veterans Affairs is a member of the President’s cabinet, serving as the chief advisor to the President on Veterans' affairs.  The Secretary is responsible for VA’s multi-billion dollar budget used to provide a nationwide system of health care services, benefit programs, and national cemeteries for America’s Veterans.  The Secretary sets policies governing the Department’s operations, defines its basic organizational structure, and maintains favorable relations with organizations, groups, and individuals interested in Veterans' affairs.
2. The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (BVA), which reports to the Secretary of Veterans Affairs, reviews determinations on the existence or amount of benefit debts and waiver request denial decisions made by the COWC on benefit debts, as well as waiver determinations made by any Veterans Health Administration (VHA) Fiscal Officer concerning medical or pharmacy co-payment debts.  BVA members include Veterans Law Judges or Attorneys, who are experienced in Veteran laws and case reviews of benefit claims, are the only ones who may issue BVA decisions.  Neither the COWC nor the RO Finance Officer’s compromise decisions can be appealed to BVA.
3. The Assistant Secretary for Management/Chief Financial Officer (CFO), as required by the Chief Financial Officers Act of 1990 and [38 USC 309](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:38%20section:309%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section309)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true), oversees all financial management activities relating to the Department’s programs and operations. Specific responsibilities include the direction, management, and provision of policy guidance and oversight of VA’s financial management personnel, activities, and operations.
4. The Office of Financial Policy (OFP), which reports to the Office of the Deputy Assistant Secretary for Finance/Deputy CFO, is responsible for managing the Cost and Debt Management Service, Management and Financial Reports Service, Accounting, Payroll, and Travel Policy Services, Grants Management Service, and the Systems to Drive Performance Initiatives.  OFP has issued VA Financial Policy for COWC activities.
5. The Debt Management Center (DMC), which reports to the CFO, is responsible for collecting debts resulting from an individual’s participation in VA’s education, pension or disability compensation programs within the DMC’s jurisdiction.  The DMC consults with Veterans and their families in the management and liquidation of their benefit debts.  A Special Committee on Compromises at the DMC has delegated compromise authority on all benefit debts not in excess of $100,000 under its collection jurisdiction.
6. The Financial Services Center (FSC), which reports to the CFO and is located in Austin, TX, performs a full range of financial accounting and reporting services for VA, as a whole.  The FSC establishes a debt record when a debt is established or an overpayment to an employee, or a vendor has been discovered.  For vendor debts, the FSC establishes vendor accounts receivable and reviews the accuracy of debts as expeditiously as possible, if debtors dispute the debts.

## **Section 2.02 VBA Central Office**

1. The Under Secretary for Benefits may assume original jurisdiction of any matter that the COWC has authority to consider, but for which it has not rendered a decision on the issue involved, in accordance with [38 CFR 1.956(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.956).  The Under Secretary for Benefits may also establish an ad hoc board to determine a particular issue, depending upon the circumstances involved in a specific case.
2. The VBA Chief Financial Officer (VBA/CFO) is responsible for VBA’s budget formulation and execution functions, as well as for overall VBA financial management, including accurate financial accounting and reporting, financial policy and systems development, proper certification, approval and audit activities relating to VBA disbursements, and management of VBA’s debt collection process, including any referrals to the COWC.
3. The VBA Finance Center, which reports to the VBA/CFO and is located in Hines IL, is the central operations center for the disbursing of funds for VBA’s benefit systems within the Corporate VETSNET system.  VBA Finance Center’s operations are centered on verifying the recording of cash receipts received at ROs and the DMC, as they relate to the repayment of debts owed by Veterans and the oversight of benefit general ledger accounting.
4. The Finance Services Division, which reports to the VBA/CFO is responsible for referring cases from the COWC/DMC to the Regional Offices (RO) when issues arise that requires action from the RO before waiver consideration can be conducted. Finance Services Division also conducts quarterly quality and timeliness reviews of Committee decisions and provides a quarterly report to the Finance Services Director.

## **Section 2.03 VBA Regional Offices**

1. RO Directors and VA Medical and Regional Office Center Directors appoint a COWC chairperson, members and alternates in accordance with [38 CFR 1.955(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.955).
2. The COWC is a decision-making panel composed of one or more persons ([38 CFR 1.955(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.955)), which will conduct hearings, accept or reject waiver or compromise offers, and consider appeals. The COWC will accept or reject compromise offer on a debt that is not more than $100,000 (exclusive of interest and other late payment charges); reject a compromise offer on any home loan debt, regardless of the amount; or recommend approval of a compromise offer on a debt that exceeds $100,000, exclusive of interest and other late payment charges.
3. The COWC of Jurisdiction is the committee that has the authority to decide waiver or compromise cases for types of debts.  The RO of Jurisdiction is the RO that houses the COWC of Jurisdiction (e.g., the committee that has the authority to hear the case based on type of debt).  ROs of Jurisdiction are listed in [Part 03](#p03) below.
4. The Administrative Control Function (ACF), referred to as the COWC Liaison, describes the station activity that has been assigned administrative control of the COWC process.  There is an ACF at each RO and at the ROs of Jurisdiction (including DMC) that houses the specific COWC of Jurisdiction; for example, the Regional Office at St. Paul, MN.  The RO Finance Officer will normally have the responsibility for the administrative control of each COWC.  However, the RO Director has the authority to reassign the ACF to another station activity, rather than the Finance Officer, whenever the Veteran determines that such reassignment is appropriate.
5. RO Finance Officers, pursuant to [38 CFR 1.957(b)(2) & (3)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.957) as to debts arising within their jurisdictions, are authorized to:
   1. accept compromise offers of 50 percent or more on a total debt not greater than $1,000, exclusive of interest and other late payment charges, regardless of whether there has been a prior denial of waiver; and
   2. reject any offer of compromise on a total debt regardless of dollar amount, exclusive of interest and other late payment charges, regardless of whether there has been a prior denial of waiver.
   3. Compromise offers of less than 50 percent that are not rejected by the Finance Officer will be referred to the COWC of Jurisdiction for resolution.
6. The Regional Processing Office in Buffalo is responsible for Education program debts, and the Restored Entitlement Program for Survivors (REPS) debts.

## **Section 2.04 Other VA Organizations**

1. The District Counsel (DC) and designated staff attorneys are authorized to conduct investigations, examine witnesses, take affidavits, administer oaths and affirmations and certify copies of public or private documents in any matter within the jurisdiction of VA’s General Counsel, delegated or otherwise assigned.  The DC is authorized to, and will, under the guidance of VA’s General Counsel, provide legal services, advice and assistance to VA installations within their assigned region.  In any area of regulatory, assigned or delegated responsibility, the DC may delegate authority to perform, to the extent specified, any legal function under the professional direction of the DC to staff members or other VA attorneys.  The DC may modify, suspend, or rescind any delegated authority.  The DC may provide assistance to the COWC when requested by the chairperson.  The DC, under special delegation from the Department of Justice, can collect certain debts by enforced collection through litigation.  Refer to [38 CFR 14.501](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-14/section-14.501), *“Functions and Responsibilities of District Counsels,”* for additional information related to the DC.

## **Section 2.05 Non-VA Organizations**

1. The Department of Justice (DOJ) has sole authority to collect, terminate, suspend, or compromise any debt referred by VA.  DOJ has the exclusive authority to accept the compromise offer when the claim exceeds $100,000.  DOJ collects certain debts, depending on dollar value, on behalf of VA by enforced collection through litigation and assesses VA a fee prior to forwarding the actual collections.  DOJ also retains sole authority to terminate collection action regarding debts and claims involving fraud, misrepresentation, false claim, or mutual mistake of fact.

# **Part 05 Establishment of Debt and Debt Notification**

## **Section 5.01 Determination of Debts and Permanent Case Folder Documentation**

1. **Financial Policy**
   1. When a debt is determined, such as through discovery of an overpayment, a debt file will be established.  The RO or the DMC Administrative Control Functions for employee debts (see [VA Financial Policy, Volume XII, Chapter 3, Employee Debts](https://department.va.gov/financial-policy-documents/financial-document/chapter-03-employee-debt/) for details.) and benefit debts, respectively, and the FSC for employee and vendor debts, will maintain a debt collection record for each debt.  It is very important to maintain an accurate and fully documented record on all debts.  This record may become a critical matter in those cases where the debtor refuses to pay a debt and the case is referred to the either the DC or the DOJ for litigation or other collection action.
2. **Financial Procedures**
   1. The RO Finance Officer, the DMC Director, Defense Finance and Accounting Service (DFAS) as VA’s payroll provider through the Defense Civilian Pay System (DCPS) and the FSC Director or designee set up a debt record when a debt is established or an overpayment to a Veteran, an employee, or a vendor has been discovered.  For example, an overpayment may be established in a benefit account for various reasons, including changes in the number of credit hours pursued for educational programs, defaults on VA guaranteed home loans, and changes in medical conditions of Veterans.
   2. The DMC ACF for benefit debts, the RO ACF for station debts, or the FSC Director or designee for employee and vendor debts maintain a case file for each debt, including the following:

1. Notices of Indebtedness (NOI) with supporting documentation of the debt;
2. Correspondence sent to the debtor, to include copies or requests for additional information;
3. Correspondence received from the debtor, such as disputes, requests for waiver, compromise, appeals, hearings, suspension of collection action, or termination of collection action;
4. Reports for telephone calls or personal meetings;
5. Signed installment or repayment plans;
6. Detailed determinations resulting from subsequent actions, to include appeal board decisions; and
7. Court meetings.

3. The RO, DMC or FSC obtain financial status reports, credit reports, if applicable, and other supporting documents for inclusion in the debtor’s eFolder (i.e. VBMS or TIMS).  If the debtor has provided a copy of VA Form 5655, Financial Status Report, the copy is added to eFolder by the RO.  If the debtor has not provided this form, the RO or DMC ACF requests the debtor to fill out the VA Form 5655.

3. **Key Internal Controls**
   1. The ACF at the RO or DMC, plus the FSC Director or designee, must file and maintain supporting documentation for each debt to allow the COWC to make an informed waiver or compromise decision and to facilitate future debt collection actions.

## **Section 5.02 Notification of Indebtedness and Due Process Rights**

1. **Financial** **Policy**
   1. VBA will aggressively pursue the collection of all debts while ensuring the debtor is advised of the Veteran’s due process rights regarding the nature and amount of the debt through the issuance of the proper NOI, also known as a demand letter, and is given the required time to repay or respond to the NOI.  DMC does not have jurisdiction over station debts.  Except for defaulted Loan Guaranty home mortgage debts which must be sent by certified mail, the notification is deemed sufficient when sent by ordinary mail, directed to the debtor's last known address, and not returned as undeliverable by postal authorities.
2. **Financial Procedures**
   1. For employee debts, DFAS or the FSC provides the employee debtor the written NOI, which will include the amount of debt, the reason the debt occurred and the time period or date of the debt, the debtor’s rights and the remedies available, including the right to dispute the existence or amount of a debt, request a waiver or a hearing, appeal a decision and request a repayment plan, and the time limits required for a debtor’s response and right to request a waiver.  If there is no response within 30 days, collection by salary offset will begin.  However, if the RO Finance Officer determines that there is no other source of recovery and collection of the debt would be jeopardized by deferral of offset, then an involuntary offset is to commence immediately and notification to the debtor should be made at the time offset begins or as soon thereafter as possible.  Refer to [Section 5.04](#5.04) for information on debt offsets and [Appendix B](#b) for a sample of different NOI letters and the VA Web site where all VA Forms are available.
   2. For benefit debts, the DMC Director, or the RO Finance Officer responsible for collection of the debt, sends the initial NOI to the debtor as soon as practical, by regular postal service, after the debt is established and discovered.  For debts resulting from the default of a guaranteed or direct home loan, the first NOI will be sent by certified mail – return receipt requested.
      1. The DMC or the RO Finance Officer sends subsequent letters at no more than 30-day intervals, when no response is received from the debtor.
      2. Debts $25 or larger and older than 120 days (when the debt is eligible for waiver consideration) and where follow-up with the debtor has not been productive or other collection action has not been taken by the RO Finance Officer will be referred to the Treasury Offset Program.
   3. For vendor debts, the FSC Director or designee establishes vendor accounts receivable, either at the time of mailing the VA‑initiated demand letter (i.e., VA Form FL 4‑521, First Demand Letter Vendor Debts) or upon receipt of the vendor’s credit memorandum.  The demand letter will include the following advice to the vendor:

1. The vendor has the right to dispute the existence and amount of the debt,
2. Interest, administrative costs, and other charges will be assessed if payment is not received in full within 30 days from date of initial notification,
3. Penalty charges will be assessed if the debt is not paid in full or has a current repayment plan within 120 days from the date of initial notification, and
4. If payment is not made within 30 days from notification, the amount owed, plus interest and other late payment charges, will be offset from future payments owed the vendor by VA.
5. Note:  When a vendor debt is associated with a specific contract, the associated contracting officer must be consulted.

4. Beneficiaries who are incarcerated or are fugitive felons are subject to a reduction or termination of VA benefits.  For fugitive felon debts, the RO Finance Office of Jurisdiction files a claim for overpayment of any benefits paid, when law enforcement agencies notify VA that a Veteran has been identified as being either in prison or is a fugitive felon.  During the time a Veteran remains a fugitive, the Veteran may not receive VA benefits, even if the Veteran supports family members.  However, spouses and/or dependents of incarcerated Veterans or fugitive felons may be entitled to an apportionment if they are not also fugitive felons.

1. Note:  If there is a warrant against the Veteran, the Veteran should notify VA immediately to prevent benefit overpayments.  Veterans should also not file new claims for benefits while an outstanding warrant exists.
2. The RO Finance Officer provides the imprisoned Veteran or identified felon with a 60-day due process notification prior to reducing or terminating VA benefits.  Because of this due process requirement, overpayments of benefits usually cannot be avoided.

3. **Key Internal Controls**

1. Debtors must first be notified of their debts in a timely manner so RO, DMC and/or FSC can commence collection activities.
2. Adequate efforts must be made to collect a debt, including demand letters sent every 30 days.
3. All waiver requests made by vendors or third parties that are not eligible for waiver consideration should be provided a letter of ineligibility. Please see attachments for a letter of ineligibility template.

C&P disagreement/dispute requests will be routed through centralized mail to the proper RO SOJ.  Upon completion of the C&P disagreement/dispute review, the VSC emails a Debt Disagreement Decision Notice to DMC’s mailbox at [dmcops.vbaspl@va.gov](mailto:mailto:dmcops.vbaspl@va.gov). # **Part 06 Definitions**

1. **Bad Faith.** This term generally describes unfair or deceptive dealing by one who seeks to gain thereby at another's expense. Thus, a debtor's conduct in connection with a debt arising from participation in a VA benefits/services program exhibits bad faith if such conduct, although not undertaken with actual fraudulent intent, is undertaken with intent to seek an unfair advantage, with knowledge of the likely consequences and results in a loss to the Government.
2. **Benefit Debt.**  Debts of Veterans/employees arising out of participation in a VA benefits program under authority of Title 10 or Title 38 of the United States Code (e.g., compensation, education, pension, and home loan debts).
3. **Centralized Committee System**.  A system in which authority to hear all cases pertaining to a particular type of debt is centralized at one location or set of locations rather than housing a committee at each RO.  This system is used to increase the efficiency of the COWC process and bring uniformity to decisions across VBA.  VBA has centralized the COWC function at four (Buffalo, Milwaukee, and St. Paul) ROs for certain types of debts.
4. **Chairperson.** The COWC chairperson manages the work of the Committee and is responsible for assigning cases to COWC members, preparing quality and timeliness reviews, and training COWC members.
5. **Claims Collection Litigation Report (CCLR).**  VA will complete the CCLR, accompanied by a signed Certificate of Indebtedness, to refer all administratively uncollectible claims to the DOJ for litigation.  This report contains all relevant information on the debtor that VA has in their records and includes previous collection attempts, credit data, and current address.
6. **Close Out**.  Occurs when an agency, after determining that additional future collection efforts on a debt would be futile, reports the amount of a terminated debt to the IRS as potential income to the debtor on IRS Form 1099-C.  For debts not reportable to IRS, close-out never actually occurs.
7. **Compromise**.  Acceptance of less than the full amount of a debt in settlement and full satisfaction of the debt.
8. **COWC Liaison**.  Person designated at each RO whose job it is to communicate with DMC and PMC, coordinate needed actions, process administrative work, and coordinate/arrange hearings (by phone or video-conferencing – COWC member must take part in the hearing).
9. **Cross-Servicing.**  An arrangement between two or more Federal agencies in which a function, such as debt collection, is performed by one agency in support of one or more agencies, and for which reimbursement is required from the agencies receiving the support.  Cross-servicing most often refers to a Federal agency’s referral of a delinquent debt to Treasury for collection action.
10. **Currently Not Collectible**.  Debts terminated but not closed out that VA may still collect in the future.  While this designation may be used for any such debts, the primary use of this designation is for debts VA has terminated and removed from VA’s financial records and have been referred to the Treasury’s Treasury Offset Program and/or for cross-servicing.
11. **Caseflow Intake**. A web-based application designed to support the Appeals Modernization Act (AMA).  Caseflow Intake records the issues on the Veteran’s appeals form and tracks the associated metrics required by statute. For business lines that do not perform their primary tasks in VBMS, Caseflow Intake also records the decision/ disposition of appeals, among other lifecycle functions.  VA business lines use Caseflow Intake to both establish and record the outcome of appeals. (See Attachments Section below for the Caseflow Intake Training Guide.
12. **Debt.** A claim for money made by or owed to the Government, arising out of activities of VA and including overpayments ([38 CFR 1.962](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.962)).
13. **Debtor.**  Any person who owes a debt to VA.  This includes individuals, corporations, partnerships, sole proprietorships, estates, trusts, other Government agencies and other legal entities against which VA has a claim.
14. **Discharge.**  In accordance with [31 CFR 903.5(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-IX/part-903/section-903.5) and [38 CFR 1.944(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFR4168d2fedd001be/section-1.944), a discharge of a debt is the same as a close out of a debt.
15. **Elapsed Time Report**.  The elapsed time report will measure the required time to complete the consideration of any COWC case (original request for waiver, reconsideration request, NOD, reconsideration of a waiver decision or a compromise offer) and will be measured by counting the number of calendar days from the date the case is received by the COWC chairperson after referral from the RO Finance Officer until the date the completed decision is signed by the COWC members. The lapsed time standards will be applied to all cases received within the quarter being reviewed.
16. **Employee Debt.**  Debts owed to VA by an individual who is, or was, employed in a civilian capacity by the Federal Government
17. **Finance Officer.**  The finance officer is the VBA official directly responsible to the VBA facility Director (or equivalent executive) for the performance of finance operations.
18. **Financial Status Report.**  VA Form 5655 used by debtors to list their current income, expenses, assets, and financial condition.
19. **Fiscal Officer.**  The fiscal officer is the VHA official directly responsible to the VHA facility Director (or equivalent executive) for the performance of finance operations.
20. **Fraud and Misrepresentation.** Although these are listed as separate elements in Section [5302](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:38%20section:5302%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section5302)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true)(c), they contain common characteristics and should be considered as a single element. If the Committee on Waivers and Compromises (Committee) feels there was a willful misrepresentation of material fact or the willful failure to disclose a material fact with the criminal intent of obtaining, retaining, or assisting an individual to obtain or retain, eligibility for VA benefits, the case must be referred to District Counsel for a determination.
21. **Fugitive Felon.**  A fugitive felon is anyone facing an arrest warrant for committing a felony or a crime that is punishable by one year in prison.  Anyone convicted of a felony or of violating parole is also considered a fugitive felon.
22. **Litigation.** The act or process of bringing or contesting a legal action in court.
23. **Member.**  The individual who participates on the COWC panel to review waiver requests and compromise offers. The individual has special competence and familiarity with one or more of the debt claim areas (i.e., compensation, pension, education, insurance, loan guaranty, etc.)
24. **Non-Benefit Debt**.  Any debt owed to VA that does not result from participation in any VA benefit program (e.g., employee debt and vendor debts)
25. **Notice of Disagreement (NOD).**  VA Form 10182, Decision Review Request: Board Appeal – Notice of Disagreement (NOD) is a form used by the debtor to directly file an appeal to the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (BVA).  BVA’s review provides three options: Direct Review, Evidence Submission and Hearing Request. Debtor in the legacy appeal process who received a Statement of the Case (SOC) or Supplemental Statement of the Case (SSOC) after February 2019, is eligible to opt-in to the Appeals Modernization process by filing a VA Form 10182 NOD directly to BVA.
26. **Notice of Indebtedness (NOI).**  Written communication with the debtor explaining the details of the debt along with the debtor’s due process rights, including their right to request a hearing and waiver of the debt.  This notice advises the debtor of the timing of repayment and repayment options for paying off the debt, possible course of action to be taken by VA, and the potential effect of added interest and other late payment charges to the debt.
27. **Quarterly Review.**  The purpose of the COWC chairperson’s quarterly review is to evaluate the COWC decision-making function, and to provide a basis for comparison with prior reviews.  Trend data deficiencies in the quality review, as well as elapsed processing time deficiencies, will be examined to see if an acceptable level of performance is being accomplished.  Also, the reviews will be used to support future training sessions.
28. **Refund.**  Action taken by the Finance Officer to return monies to the debtor that were collected and deposited by VBA prior to final COWC and/or BVA decisions on approving a waiver or compromise request.
29. **Regional Office**.  A collection of 56 benefits offices that provide benefits information and process claims.  At least one VARO is located within every state and as well as the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and the Republic of the Philippines. Some ROs also provide out-based services to Veterans being discharged from active service at various military separation centers around the country and some foreign locations.
30. **Report of Investigation.**  The Report of Investigation is a narrative report used in cases of erroneous payment of pay and/or allowances.  It is prepared by the local Finance Office and includes information is needed by the COWC to determine a waiver or compromise decision.
31. **Reporting Member.**  The individual who is designated by the chairperson to be the presiding officer over a panel.
32. **Statutory Authority.**  The authority vested in the Secretary of Veterans Affairs to compromise debts as contained in [38 USC 3720](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:38%20section:3720%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section3720)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true "38 U.S.C. 3720(a)") and [31 USC 3711](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:31%20section:3711%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title31-section3711)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true "31").  This authority has been delegated, under certain limitations, to COWC, established pursuant to [38 CFR 1.955](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRb1b535e6cffa220/section-1.955).  General principles governing the compromise of debts owed to the Government are set forth in [38 CFR 1.930 through 1.936](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-1/subject-group-ECFRca7c6ffdbcdf690).  [38 USC 3720](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:38%20section:3720%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section3720)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true "38 U.S.C. 3720(a)") authorizes the compromise of debts due the United States, regardless of the amount, that result from payment of a claim under the guaranty or insurance of loans, liquidation of direct loans, liquidation of loans acquired under [38 CFR 36.4318](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-36/subpart-B/section-36.4318) and liquidation of vendee accounts.
33. **Suspension of Collection Action**.  Temporary delay of collection actions on a debt owed to VA.
34. **Termination of Collection Action.**  Refers to a decision made to cease active collection action on a debt, in accordance with criteria set out in the Federal Claims Collection Standards, because such action is not economically worthwhile or is otherwise inappropriate.
35. **Termination, Currently Not Collectible.**  A decision by the Government to stop all collection activity on an account, but to hold the account for possible future collection action.  This decision is made when it is determined that further collection activity will be fruitless or not cost effective at the time but may be warranted at some future date.  Terminated debts may be reactivated and collected if they have been written off, but not closed out.  The word “termination” is often used interchangeably with "write off.”  However, termination is a legal procedure, while write off is an accounting procedure generally associated with removing the debt from an agency records.
36. **Treasury Offset Program**.  A centralized offset program, administered by the Treasury’s Financial Management Service, to collect delinquent debts owed to Federal agencies and States in accordance with [26 USC 6402(d)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:26%20section:6402%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title26-section6402)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true) - collection of debts owed to Federal agencies, [31 USC 3720A](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:31%20section:3720a%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title31-section3720a)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true) - reduction of tax refund by amount of the debts and other applicable laws.
37. **Waiver.**  VA’s decision to give up the right to collect a valid debt, including interest and other late payment charges, which falls under the applicable statutes ([38 USC 5302](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:38%20section:5302%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section5302)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true "38 U.S.C. 5302") and [5 USC 5584](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:5%20section:5584%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title5-section5584)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true "5")) and regulations.
38. **Write Off**.  This process involves reducing receivables in accounting records as a result of the determination that they are uncollectible.  All write-offs will be made through allowance accounts for bad debts.  Under no circumstances are debts to be written off directly to expense.  Write-offs should be distinguished from reversals of receivables.  Federal accounts receivables are not written off; they are cancelled.
