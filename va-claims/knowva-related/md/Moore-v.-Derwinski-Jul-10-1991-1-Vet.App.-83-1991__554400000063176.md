Decision Assessment Document
 Moore v. Derwinski, Jul 10, 1991, 1 Vet.App. 83 (1991)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that substantially gainful employment is that which is ordinarily followed by the nondisabled to earn their livelihood with earnings common to the particular occupation in the community where the veteran resides.  Substantially gainful employment suggests a living wage.

The Court held that the ability to work only a few hours a day or only sporadically is not the ability to engage in substantially gainful employment and that the mere theoretical ability to engage in substantial gainful employment is not a sufficient basis to deny benefits.  Also, the Court indicated that the test of whether a person is unable to pursue substantially gainful employment is whether a particular job is realistically within the physical and mental capabilities of the claimant.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holdings were incorporated into guidance contained in Manual M21-1, IV.ii.2.F.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran was service connected for multiple disabilities, to include right hip and low back conditions, with a combined schedular evaluation of 90 percent.  He held a Master's degree in Education and was self-employed "part-time" as a tutor until some time in 1988.  He earned a total of $8,800 over three years and was approximately 63 years old.  The record contained a lay statement from his son indicating that the veteran was unable to do any work requiring putting pressure on his legs or hips and was unable to stand for a significant length of time nor walk far without causing hip pain.  A VA physician provided a statement describing the functional limitations and symptomatology related to the right hip and low back disabilities and indicated that it was apparent that the veteran could not work.  The Board of Veterans' Appeals (BVA), in December 1989, determined that the veteran was not unemployable.

The Court found that there was no plausible basis in this record for the BVA's finding that appellant was able to engage in substantially gainful employment.  The Court stated that BVA's finding appears to be based upon an erroneous view of the law.  The BVA lists as evidence that appellant last worked full-time in 1982 and that as recently as 1988 he "maintained part-time self-employment as a counselor and tutor".  This is the only evidence that the BVA could point to, aside from the veteran's education, to support its finding.  The Court found that BVA apparently regarded the veteran's recent tutoring work as dispositive of his ability to perform substantially gainful employment.  It is not.  The ability to work only a few hours a day or only sporadically is not the ability to engage in substantially gainful employment.  Substantially gainful employment is "that which is ordinarily followed by the nondisabled to earn their livelihood with earnings common to the particular occupation in the community where the veteran resides."  The Court reversed the BVA decision.
