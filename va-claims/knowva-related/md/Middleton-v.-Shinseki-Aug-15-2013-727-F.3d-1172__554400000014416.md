Decision Assessment Document
 Middleton v. Shinseki, Aug 15, 2013, 727 F.3d 1172
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that the Veterans Court did not err in holding that the “requiring insulin” criterion of the 40 percent evaluation contemplated by [38 C.F.R. § 4.119](http://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/4.119), Diagnostic Code (DC) 7913, means that the veteran must actually be administered insulin.

The Federal Circuit also held that the Veterans Court committed no error when it concluded that [38 C.F.R. § 4.7](http://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/4.7) was inapplicable to the veteran’s claim for a rating in excess of 20 percent, as the 40 percent evaluation under DC 7913 could not be awarded because the veteran only satisfied two of the three elements for the higher evaluation.  The Federal Circuit held that since the 40 percent rating does not contemplate alternative considerations, a veteran must demonstrate all of the required elements in order to be entitled to that higher evaluation.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, in July 2002, was granted service connection for diabetes mellitus with a 20 percent evaluation.  He filed for an increased evaluation in 2008.  A December 2009 VA examination revealed that the veteran’s diabetes was treated with three oral hypoglycemic agents and daily injections of the drug Byetta.  Note: Byetta is a synthetic peptide that induces the body to secrete endogenous insulin.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), finding that insulin was not used to regulate the veteran’s diabetes, maintained the 20 percent evaluation for diabetes mellitus.  The Board found that Byetta, while used to treat diabetes, was not insulin.

The Veterans Court, rejecting the veteran’s argument that Byetta was analogous to insulin, held that the plain language of DC 7913 recites insulin and does not include a supposed substitute.  The Veterans Court also rejected his argument that [§ 4.7](http://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/4.7) applied because his diabetes mellitus more closely approximated the criteria for a 40 percent rating.  The Veterans Court, following its own precedent in [*Camacho v. Nicholson*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/817195/camacho-v-nicholson/), 21 Vet.App. 360 (2007), held that § 4.7 did not apply because the veteran could not be rated 40 percent disabling for diabetes when he only satisfied two of the three criteria required for that rating.

The veteran argued before the Federal Circuit that “requiring insulin” refers more generally to a need for insulin, not a specific method for obtaining it.  He averred that interpretations of ratings that rely on specific medications, rather than impairments, become obsolete as new drugs are introduced; thus, any ambiguity should be resolved in favor of referencing symptoms, whether insulin is directly injected or endogenously created after administering a medication such as Byetta.

The Federal Circuit, in holding that the 40 percent evaluation under DC 7913 requires the actual administration of insulin, observed that the diagnostic code does not authorize a 40 percent rating premised on the administration of another medical compound or pharmaceutical agent other than insulin.  The Federal Circuit also stated that their role was not to rewrite asserted obsolete regulatory provisions.

The veteran argued that the Veterans Court’s holding that requiring insulin is necessary for a 40 percent evaluation conflicts with [38 C.F.R. § 4.21](http://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/4.21), which provides that “it is not expected … that all cases will show all the findings specified.”  He also contended that the Veterans Court further erred in holding that [§ 4.7](http://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/4.7) did not apply to his claim, as his diabetes more nearly approximated the criteria for the 40 percent rating under DC 7913 because control of his diabetes required regulation of activities, which is only associated with ratings equal to or exceeding 40 percent.  The veteran analogized his case to [*Tatum v. Shinseki*, 23 Vet.App. 152 (2009)](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014537/), regarding the application of § 4.7.

The Federal Circuit, in interpreting § 4.7, observed that since it applies “where there is a question as to which of two evaluations shall be applied,” there was no question as to which evaluation shall be applied when a veteran does not satisfy all of the required criteria of the higher rating but does satisfy all of the criteria of the lower rating.  Thus, the Federal Circuit found no error in the Veterans Court analysis that § 4.7 was inapplicable because the veteran did not meet the “requiring insulin” criterion of the 40 percent rating, in view of the plain meaning of “being administered insulin.”

The Federal Circuit also found the veteran’s reliance upon *Tatum* to be misplaced, noting that the Veterans Court, in *Tatum*, distinguished the criteria under DC 7903 in that case, from [*Camacho*](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014801/), which involved interpreting the successive rating criteria in DC 7913.  The Federal Circuit agreed with the Secretary that the enumerated elements of DC 7913 required for a 40 percent rating are part of a structured scheme of specific, successive, cumulative criteria for establishing a disability rating: each higher rating includes the same criteria as the lower rating plus distinct new criteria.  The Federal Circuit stated that the use of the conjunctive “and” in the 40 percent rating of DC 7913 necessitates that all three elements must be satisfied to warrant such a rating.  In contrast, the 20 percent evaluation uses the connector “or” to establish alternate ratings.

The Federal Circuit affirmed the Veterans Court decision.
