**Decision Assessment Document**

**Chavis v. McDonough, April 16, 2021, 34 Vet.App. 1 (2021)**

**United States Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court**)

**What the case is about:**

The Court considered whether the requirement of ankylosis in VA’s Rating Schedule for Diseases and Injuries of the Spine, 38 C.F.R. §4.71a, can be met with evidence of the functional equivalent of ankylosis during a flare up and whether the Board had jurisdiction over the radiculopathy disabilities. The Court found that the provisions of §§ 4.40 and 4.45, “the DeLuca factors,” (allowing for a higher rating based on additional functional loss with use or during flare-ups) are for application when evaluating joint disabilities and their manifestations, which may include ankylosis.  As such, the Court determined that the Board erred when it did not consider whether limitation results in the functional equivalence of joint ankylosis consistent with the regulatory language and existing caselaw.  Given the specific facts of this case, the Court also found that radiculopathy disabilities were part of Veteran’s claim seeking a higher evaluation for his underlying lumbar spine disability and that the Board had jurisdiction over them.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court’s holding will be incorporated into guidance contained in the Manual, under M21-1 III.iv.4.A.2.m

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The Veteran served honorably in the U.S. Army from May 1975 to April 1976. He appealed a Board decision that denied entitlement to a disability evaluation higher than 40% for a low back strain with herniated nucleus pulposus at the L4-L5 spinal level and denied entitlement to evaluations higher than 20% for right and left lower extremity radiculopathy associated with the lumbar spine disability.

The Court considered and addressed whether the requirement of ankylosis in VA's General Rating Formula for Diseases and Injuries of the Spine (General Rating Formula), 38 C.F.R. § 4.71a (2020), can be met with evidence of the functional equivalent of ankylosis during a flare, and held that it can.  Because the Board did not consider whether the Veteran's symptoms resulted in the functional equivalent of ankylosis, the Court set aside that portion of the Board decision that denied a higher evaluation for the lumbar spine disability and remanded that matter to the Board for adjudication consistent with this decision.

The Court, citing several medical dictionaries, noted that ankylosis is a medical term meaning "[i]mmobility and consolidation of a joint due to disease, injury, or surgical procedure," stiffening or fixation of a joint as the result of a disease process, with fibrous or bony union across the joint," "stiffening or immobilization of a joint as a result of injury, disease, or surgical intervention."  The Court also noted that in 38 C.F.R. § 4.71a, under the General Rating Formula, an evaluation is based upon "orthopedic findings," including ankylosis, limitation of motion, muscle spasm, guarding, and tenderness. Note (5) of 38 C.F.R. § 4.71a defines favorable and unfavorable ankylosis, with unfavorable ankylosis being defined as a condition in which the entire spine or an entire spinal segment is fixed in flexion or extension and the ankylosis results in one or more associated complications.  In contrast, ankylosis is considered favorable if the fixation is in a neutral position (0 degrees) or does not result in an associated complication under Note (5). Considering the  regulatory history of  38 C.F.R. § 4.71a, and various provisions of the VA Adjudication Procedures Manual (M21-1), the Court  reiterated that VA considers ankylosis to be an objective finding like limitation of motion, muscle spasm, guarding, and tenderness.

The Court then specified that a Veteran may be entitled to a higher evaluation where there is evidence that his or her disability causes additional functional loss under 38 C.F.R. § 4.40 and that  higher evaluation may also be awarded where there is a reduction of a joint's normal excursion of movement in different planes, including changes in the joint's range of movement, strength, fatigability, or coordination under 38 C.F.R. § 4.45. When evaluating joint disabilities and their manifestations, caselaw dictates that adjudicators must consider §§ 4.40 and 4.45 to fully understand the nature of a veteran's disability, and application of the DeLuca factors (DeLuca v. Brown, 8 Vet.App. 202, 1995) may result in a higher evaluation than one based solely on limited motion if a claimant demonstrates functional loss equivalent to that contemplated by the higher evaluation. The Court found that nothing in relevant regulations or caselaw suggests DeLuca factors are not applicable in the context of ankylosis, particularly since ankylosis is, in essence, complete limitation of motion.

The Court found that the Board committed an error of law when it determined that §§ 4.40 and 4.45 were not for application because the Veteran was already receiving a 40% evaluation for his lumbar spine disability, the highest evaluation available based on limitation of motion. Since the Board incorrectly foreclosed application of §§ 4.40 and 4.45 in evaluating the Veteran's lumbar spine disability, the Court concluded that remand of the lumbar spine claim to the Board for readjudication was warranted.

In addition, the Court addressed whether the Board had jurisdiction over the evaluation of radiculopathy disabilities associated with the Veteran’s lumbar spine disability. Although a dissenting Judge would have determined otherwise, the Court’s panel opinion concluded that the Board had jurisdiction over the radiculopathy increased-evaluation issue. Hence, because the parties agreed that the Board's determination on the merits of that issue was not supported by adequate reasons or bases, the Court set aside the portion of the Board decision that denied evaluations higher than 20% for right and left lower extremity radiculopathy and also remanded that matter to the Board for adjudication.

In this case, the Veteran never filed a Notice of Disagreement specifically concerning the evaluation of his right and left lower extremity radiculopathy. Nonetheless, given the nature and progression of the Veteran’s lumbar spine condition and VA's duty to sympathetically construe his broadly worded, pro se filings, the Court concluded that the issues of increased evaluations for bilateral lower extremity radiculopathy were part of his claim seeking a higher evaluation for the underlying lumbar spine disability. Therefore, the Court concluded that the Board properly had jurisdiction over the issues of increased evaluations for bilateral lower extremity radiculopathy, because the Veteran's radiculopathy was part of his claim seeking higher compensation for his lumbar spine disability.

After consideration of the parties' briefs and supplemental memoranda, oral arguments, the record on appeal, and the governing law, the Court set aside those portions of the Board decision that denied entitlement to an evaluation higher than 40% for a lumbar spine disability and denied entitlement to evaluations higher than 20% for bilateral lower extremity radiculopathy and remanded those matters to the Board for readjudication consistent with the Court’s decision.
