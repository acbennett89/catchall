Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 02-94, Feb 2, 1994, Special Monthly Compensation Under 38 U.S.C. 1114(s)
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Does a temporary total rating based on convalescence, under 38 CFR 4.30, satisfy the requirement in 38 U.S.C. 1114(s) of a disability rated as total for entitlement to special monthly compensation?

**Impact on VBA**

The plain and unambiguous language of 38 U.S.C. § 1114(s) does not restrict the nature of total ratings that may serve as a basis of entitlement to the special rate of disability compensation which section 1114(s) authorizes.  A temporary total rating based on convalescence, under 38 CFR 4.30, satisfies the requirement in section 1114(s) of a disability rated as total.

**Summary on facts and findings**

The language of section 1114(s) is plain and unambiguous.  It requires "a service-connected disability rated as total."  It means a service-connected disability assigned a rating such that compensation is payable at the rate authorized in 38 U.S.C. § 1114(j).  There is nothing in the language of section 1114(s) to indicate that Congress meant to exclude service-connected disabilities rated as total under 38 CFR §§ 4.28, 4.29, or 4.30.  Congress did not manifestly restrict the applicability of subsection (s) to total ratings of indefinite duration, and the application of section 1114(s) to temporary total ratings would not violate the clear purpose of the law.  VA may not impose its own restrictions on the applicability of section 1114(s).  The nature of temporary total ratings is not a persuasive reason for excluding them from consideration under section 1114(s).  The opinion also points out that there is nothing in the language of section 1114(s) to indicate that Congress meant to exclude service-connected disabilities rated as total under 38 CFR § 4.16 (individual unemployability).

As the GC opinion indicates, there is nothing in the language of 38 U.S.C. § 1114(s) requiring a disability to be rated as 100% disabling on a permanent basis before entitlement to subsection (s) can be considered.  A total disability rating for a service-connected condition is one of two requirements before benefits under subsection (s) can be established.  A veteran entitled to benefits under 38 CFR §§ 4.28, 4.29 and 4.30 for one disability must also have an additional service-connected disability independently ratable at 60% or more before subsection (s) benefits can be awarded.

Although the GC opinion also indicates that a total rating based on individual unemployability would convey potential entitlement to section (s), such benefits could not be awarded unless there was a service-connected disability independently ratable at 60% that had not been included in the establishment of entitlement under 38 CFR 4.16.

Remove the restrictive language concerning individual unemployability from 38 CFR § 3.350(i) and consider adding language covering potential eligibility when benefits are awarded under 38 U.S.C. 4.16, 4.28, 4.29 and 4.30.  Revise M21-1, Part VI, ¶ 8.06 to include language that allows for the establishment of benefits under 38 U.S.C. 1114(s) when a veteran has been granted a total rating under the provisions of 38 CFR §§ 4.16, 4.28, 4.29 and 4.30.
