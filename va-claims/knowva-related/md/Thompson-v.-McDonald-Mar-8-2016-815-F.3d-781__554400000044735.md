Decision Assessment Document
 Thompson v. McDonald, Mar 8, 2016, 815 F.3d 781
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that 38 C.F.R. § 4.40 does not provide a basis for a separate rating apart from 38 C.F.R. § 4.71a.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran appealed the regional office’s assignment of a 20 percent evaluation for degenerative disc disease of the lumbar spine from June 1, 2008 to March 8, 2011.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in denying the appeal, found that the low back disability did not cause forward flexion of the lumbar spine to be limited to 30 degrees or less, even considering limitation caused by pain, fatigue, and other factors.  The Board found that forward flexion was limited to 65 degrees with pain throughout the range of motion, and, after repetitive motion testing, was additionally reduced to 50 degrees.

The Board explained that, under Mitchell v. Shinseki, 25 Vet.App. 32 (2011), pain, by itself does not constitute a functional loss entitling a veteran to higher rating under VA regulations that evaluate disability based upon range of motion loss in the musculoskeletal system.  The Board concluded that the veteran’s pain failed to cause sufficient functional impairment to limit the flexion of the thoracolumbar spine to 30 degrees or less, as is required for a higher rating under the General Rating Formula for Diseases and Injuries of the Spine.  As viewed by the Board, the discussion of functional loss under § 4.40 did not supersede the requirements for a higher rating specified in § 4.71a.

The veteran argued before the Veterans Court that the Board erred in relying on loss of motion under § 4.71a to determine his rating without additionally taking into account functional loss due to pain as discussed in § 4.40.  The Veterans Court agreed that the range of motion ratings under § 4.71a do no subsume § 4.40, and 38 C.F.R. § 4.14 does not forbid consideration of a higher rating based on a greater limitation of motion due pain on use including flare-ups.  However, in rejecting the argument, the Veterans Court reasoned that because the conceded additional functional loss due to pain did not limit the veteran’s forward flexion to 30 degrees or less, it did not warrant a 40 percent evaluation.  The Veterans Court indicated that the veteran appeared to ignore the express language he quotes from DeLuca v. Brown, 8 Vet.App. 202 (1995):  “consideration of a higher rating based on a greater limitation of motion due to pain.”

The Federal Circuit, in § 4.40 in its context and its place in the overall regulatory scheme, found that it was clear that § 4.40 must be viewed in light of the explicitly listed disability ratings for the musculoskeletal system in § 4.71a.  The Federal Circuit noted that the VA carefully located § 4.40 and similar guidance under the umbrella heading of the musculoskeletal system and clearly included separate sections explicitly listing relevant disability ratings.

The Federal Circuit held that, given the regulatory framework, it is clear that the guidance of § 4.40 is intended to be used in understanding the nature of a veteran’s disability, after which a rating is determined based on the § 4.71a criteria.

The Federal Circuit described § 4.40 as providing a broad canvas when evaluating a disability.  However, whatever the background, an applicant for disability benefits is rated under § 4.71a.

The Federal Circuit, finding no error in law in the Veterans Court judgment, affirmed its decision.
