Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 05-06, Aug 11, 2006, Incarceration in a Privately Owned and Operated Correctional Facility - 38 U.S.C. 5313
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Incarceration in a correctional facility owned and operated by a private corporation pursuant to a contract with a State department of corrections is “incarceration in a State penal institution” under 38 U.S.C. § 5313.

**Impact on VBA**

No impact requiring regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary on facts and findings**

A veteran in receipt of VA disability compensation was convicted of a felony and sentenced to life imprisonment without parole and committed to the Oklahoma State Department of Corrections.  The veteran was later transferred to a correctional facility owned and operated by a private corporation.  The veteran contested the reduction of compensation benefits, arguing that, because the correctional facility is privately owned and operated, he is no longer incarcerated in a “State penal institution” within the meaning of section 5313 and is entitled to receive his full compensation monies without reduction.

General Counsel, noting that the courts have determined that a private entity has no authority to incarcerate except as authorized by a government agency under law, concluded that incarceration in a private facility contracted by the State is tantamount to incarceration within a State penal institution under section 5313(a)(1) as the private corporation’s authority to confine prisoners solely derives from the State’s inherent authority to incarcerate through its criminal justice system.

The legislative history of the bill that established section 5313 was referenced, which disclosed that the primary purpose for the limitation of compensation payments to incarcerated veterans was to prevent duplication of governmental expenditures, i.e., compensation for the veteran while at the same time paying for the veteran’s incarceration.  Furthermore, VA disability compensation, which is intended to replace lost earning capacity, is unnecessary where a veteran, by reason of imprisonment, suffers no economic detriment as a result of a disability.

General Counsel rejected a narrow interpretation of section 5313 allowing full compensation payments for veterans incarcerated in privately owned and operated correctional facilities as it would thwart the clear intent of Congress to reduce such benefit payments, result in double government expenditures for felons, and produce the unreasonable result of some felons receiving full compensation payments simply because of incarceration in a private facility.

General Counsel concluded that the proper interpretation of the statute is that incarceration in a privately owned and operated facility under a contract with a State to confine convicted felons on behalf of the State is incarceration in a “State penal institution” within the scope of section 5313(a)(1).  Accordingly, veterans in receipt of VA compensation who are incarcerated for the conviction of a felony in such private correctional facilities will have their compensation payments reduced in accordance with section 5313.
