Decision Assessment Document
 Wanless v. Shinseki, Sep 10, 2010, 618 F.3d 1333
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that the Veterans Court properly construed [38 U.S.C. § 5313](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5313) to mandate the reduction in disability benefits for veterans incarcerated in penal institutions that are privately owned and operated under contract with a State.

**Impact on VBA:**

[38 C.F.R. § 3.665](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=7da0c49075440f237dd890ff43db053d&node=se38.1.3_1665&rgn=div8) will be amended in accordance with the Federal Circuit’s holding and the technical amendment “Clarification of Correction Facilities Covered by Certain Provisions of Law” under the Veterans Benefits Health Care and Information Technology Act of 2006, which amended section 5313 to direct the reduction of compensation benefits for a veteran incarcerated in a “Federal, State, local, *or other penal institution or correctional facility*.*”*

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who had been receiving VA compensation since 1981, was convicted by an Oklahoma district court in January 1993 of first-degree murder for the death of his wife and sentenced to incarceration in a penitentiary for life without parole. In February 1993, VA informed him that his compensation would be reduced to $85 per month, as required by section [5313(a)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5313), because of his incarceration due to a felony conviction.

In 1998, the veteran was transferred from a state-operated prison to a privately operated prison run by Corrections Corporation of America. In March 1999, he requested reinstatement of his full compensation pay, contending that he was no longer in a facility that fell under the parameters of section 5313.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) requested that the VA General Counsel provide an opinion on the issue of whether incarceration in a correctional facility that is owned and operated by a private corporation pursuant to a State contract qualifies under the benefit reduction provisions of 38 U.S.C. § 5313. General Counsel, in a precedent opinion issued in August 2006, observed that a private corporation’s authority to confine prisoners under a contract with a State agency derives completely from the State’s inherent authority to incarcerate persons though its operation of a criminal justice system. Thus, it concluded that incarceration in a private facility, pursuant to a contract between the corporation and the State department of corrections, is tantamount to incarceration in a State penal institution. See [VA Gen. Coun. Prec. 5-2006 (Aug. 11, 2006)](http://www.va.gov/ogc/docs/2006/PREC52006.doc). The Board subsequently denied the appeal, finding that the veteran is incarcerated in a State penal institution for VA purposes under a felony conviction sentence.

The Veterans Court, in affirming the Board’s decision, concluded that Congress, in enacting section 5313, intended that compensation be reduced for all VA beneficiaries who are incarcerated for longer than 60 days for a felony, regardless of whether the facility in which they were incarcerated was publicly or privately operated. The Veterans Court also found the 2006 General Counsel opinion to be persuasive.

The veteran argued before the Federal Circuit that the Veterans Court improperly construed the prior version of section 5313 to include privately operated prisons contracted by a State. He contended that veterans incarcerated in facilities operated by private contractors are not subject to section 5313’s mandated reduction in disability benefits.

The Federal Circuit observed that section [5313](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5313) focused on the veteran’s incarceration for a felony conviction in any facility and does not distinguish between government-operated and privately-operated prisons and concluded that the statute’s failure to distinguish between the two types of prisons indicates that incarceration in a privately owned and operated prison under contract with a state is tantamount to incarceration in a state penal institution. The Federal Circuit also found no rationale for treating veterans incarcerated for felony convictions in a privately operated, yet State contracted prisons more favorably than veterans incarcerated in State-operated prisons.

The Federal Circuit indicated that the legislative history and the 2006 amendment to section 5313, which was technical and clarifying, rather than substantive, further supported the conclusion that the prior version of section 5313 applied to privately operated penal institutions contracted by the state. The Federal Circuit also determined that the Veterans Court correctly found the VA General Counsel Opinion to be persuasive.

The Federal Circuit affirmed the judgment of the Veterans Court.
