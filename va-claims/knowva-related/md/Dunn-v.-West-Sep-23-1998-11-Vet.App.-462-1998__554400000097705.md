Decision Assessment Document
Dunn v. West,,Sep 23, 1998, 11 Vet.App. 462 (1998)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that remand of the veteran's PTSD claim was required based on VA's failure to obtain and consider Vet Center medical records which might provide new and material evidence sufficient to reopen his claim for service connection.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into guidance contained in the Manual at III.iii.1.C.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran served in the U.S. Army from August 1969 to March 1972, including one year in Vietnam.  Service medical records mention complaints of right inguinal pain.  In March 1980 a hemior-rhaphy, excision of a spermatic cord lipoma and a right vasectomy were performed.  In April 1980, the regional office (RO) denied service connection for a right hernia.  A notice of disagreement (NOD) was received in May 1980, which added a claim for service connection for a nervous disorder attributed to combat experiences in Vietnam.  In August 1980, the veteran withdrew his claim for a nervous condition and asked that the adjudication of the hernia claim proceed.

In September 1980, the veteran's representative requested that he be examined for PTSD.  An October 1980, VA examination found no psychiatric diagnosis and in November 1980, RO denied service connection for PTSD.  A July 198 1, Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA) decision granted SC for the hernia, assigning a zero percent evaluation, but denied service connection for PTSD because there was no diagnosis.

Private medical records showed the veteran was nervous, anxious, or depressed in June and September of 1982, June to August 1983, August 1986, and June to August 1988.  In October 1988, the veteran filed a claim for a nervous condition.  The RO refused to reopen the veteran's claim for a nervous disorder in December 1988, finding there was no evidence of a link between a nervous condition and military service.  Medical records in July 1989, mentioned treatment at the Princeton Veterans Center.  In an unappealed October 1991, rating decision the RO refused to reopen the veteran's claim because of a lack of evidence of a stressor and the fact there was no diagnosis of PTSD.

In June 1992, the RO received records from the Social Security Administration (SSA) and a copy of their disability determination showing entitlement to benefits had been established based on findings the veteran suffered from severe chronic depression, borderline intellectual functioning and chronic alcohol abuse.  In September and October 1992, records were received from the Beckley VAMC mentioning treatment at the Princeton Veterans Center.  In October more SSA records were received indicating that the veteran had received monthly treatment at the Princeton Veterans Center.  In October 1992, the RO denied an increased rating for the veteran's hernia condition, and confirmed the denial of SC for an acquired nervous disorder.  An NOD was received in November 1992.

In November 1992, a letter from the Princeton Veterans Center was received describing the veteran's nervousness and depression, showing alcoholism was in remission, and reporting his experiences in Vietnam.  The supporting diagnostic and treatment records were not submitted.  The RO confirmed the denial of service connection for PTSD in February 1993, after review of the Princeton letter.  The claim was forwarded to the Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board), and in April 1995 the Board remanded the folder for ftuther development and an examination of the veteran's hernia condition.  On examination there was no evidence of a recurrence of the hernia.  In February 1996, the Board denied the veteran's PTSD claim because there was no diagnosis of PTSD and no exposure to a stressor adequate to cause PTSD had been established.

The Board found that the veteran had failed to provide a competent diagnosis of PTSD, and therefore no new and material evidence had been submitted.  The Court noted that this finding by Board may have been justified if all the evidence of record had been reviewed.  However, Board made its decision without obtaining and reviewing the records of the Princeton Veterans Center which were constructively part of the record.  If these records predated the Board decision on appeal, are within the Secretary's control, and could reasonably be expected to be part of the record, then "such documents are, in contemplation of law, before the Secretary and the Board and should be included in the record .... If such material could be determinative of the claim and was not considered by the Board, a remand for readjudication would be in order." Bell v. Derwinski, 2 Vet.App. 613 (1992).  These records may be relevant to the veteran's ability to prove he suffers from service connected PTSD.  Thus, the issue of service connection for PTSD was remanded by the Court.

The Court determined that the Board decision denying an increased rating for a hernia condition is a finding the Court would review under the clearly erroneous standard.  The Court found that the medical evidence presented a plausible basis for Board's denial, therefore there was no basis for the Court to overtum the decision.  Gilbert v. Derwinski, I Vet.App. 49,53 (1990).
