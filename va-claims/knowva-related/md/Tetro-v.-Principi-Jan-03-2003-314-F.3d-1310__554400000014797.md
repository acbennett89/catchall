Decision Assessment Document
 Tetro v. Principi, Jan 03, 2003, 314 F.3d 1310
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The US Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (Court) held that a breach of the duty to assist the veteran does not vitiate the finality of a Board of Veterans’ Appeals (BVA) decision.

**Impact on VBA:**

No significant impact on VBA as this relates to a BVA decision.  However, the Court’s recent decision in [*Cook v. Principi*, No. 00-7171, slip op. at 9 (Fed. Cir. Dec. 20, 2002)](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014798/), expressly concluded that “a breach of the duty to assist the veteran does not vitiate the finality of an RO decision.”

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

An April 2000 Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (CAVC) decision applied the Court’s (*now overruled*) [*Hayre v. West*, 188 F.3d 1327 (Fed. Cir. 1999)](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/188/188.F3d.1327.98-7046.html), ruling, which had judicially created a 3rd exception to the finality rule of [38 USC § 7105(c)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/7105).  By statute if a veteran does not appeal an RO decision, that decision is final with 2 exceptions: (1) new and material evidence is presented or secured (*see* [38 USC § 5108](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5108)); and (2) where clear and unmistakable error is found (*see* [38 USC § 5109A(a)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5109A)).  In *Hayre*, the Court held that failure of the duty assist was a grave error so as to vitiate finality of a RO decision.  The CAVC applied that holding in April 2000, extending it to vitiate a March 1990 BVA decision which the veteran asserted had violated the VA duty to assist.

Later, the CAVC issued *Simmons v. West*, 14 Vet. App. 84 (2000), (stating a “garden variety” breach of VA’s duty to assist does not toll the finality of an underlying decision by the RO).  Applying the rationale of *Simmons*, the CAVC in September 2000 withdrew its April 2000 decision and declined to grant the veteran an earlier effective date under the “grave procedural error” analysis of *Hayre*.  This matter is on appeal from that September 2000 CAVC decision.  Recently the Court overruled *Hayre* (see *Cook v. Principi*, No. 00-7171, slip op. at 9 (Fed. Cir. Dec. 20, 2002)).  In so holding, the Court expressly concluded that “a breach of the duty to assist the veteran does not vitiate the finality of an RO decision.”  In this case the Court states the same principle applies to a BVA decision, and the BVA’s violation of the duty to assist, if any, does not vitiate the finality of a BVA decision.

Having overruled *Hayre*, the Court found that even a proven allegation of “grave procedural error” based on a VA breach of the duty to assist is insufficient for circumventing [38 USC § 7105](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/7105)’s rule of finality, which by statute requires either the submission of new and material evidence, or a finding of clear and unmistakable error.
