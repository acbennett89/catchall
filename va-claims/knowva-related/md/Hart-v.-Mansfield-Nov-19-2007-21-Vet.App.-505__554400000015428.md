Decision Assessment Document
Hart v. Mansfield, Nov 19, 2007, 21 Vet.App. 505
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court, finding no basis for distinguishing between initial ratings and increased-rating claims for applying staged ratings, held that staged ratings are appropriate for an increased-rating claim when the factual findings show distinct time periods where the service-connected disability exhibits symptoms that would warrant different ratings.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.5.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran filed an increased-rating claim for his left knee disability in May 2001, which was rated 10 percent at the time.  He submitted an April 2001 examination report from a private physician that showed some laxity and moderate chondromalacia patella of the knee.  A VA examination from January 2002 showed normal range of motion without pain and no evidence of laxity.  X-rays showed minimal degenerative joint disease.

The private physician reexamined the veteran in April 2002 and reviewed the January 2002 VA examination report.  He reiterated that laxity was present in the knee, as well as generalized degenerative joint disease, and that the veteran had a 10 to 15 percent permanent partial impairment of function of the whole body secondary to the left knee with additional impairment due to the degenerative changes.

In March 2004, the RO received an October 2002 VA orthopedic consultation report that indicated that X-rays suggested osteoarthritis of the left knee.  The veteran was given another VA examination in 2005 that found no effusion or laxity.  There was no pain; however, some tenderness was present with motion.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) upheld the denial of an evaluation in excess of 10 percent in June 2005 on the basis that the most recent evidence did not show moderate recurrent subluxation or lateral instability.

The veteran argued before the Court that VA developed negative evidence to deny his claim, the Board erred by failing to consider staged ratings before denying his claim, and the Board failed to provide an adequate statement of reasons or bases for finding that the opinion of his private physician was not as probative as a more recent VA examiner’s opinion.

The Court found that the regional office (RO) acted reasonably by scheduling another VA examination to ensure a complete picture of the extent of the veteran’s left knee disability in view of the length of time that had passed during the adjudicating of the veteran’s claim and the possibility of entitlement to a separate disability rating under another diagnostic code for arthritis with limitation of motion.  Thus, the Court, in rejecting this specific claim of the veteran, determined that there was no evidence that VA acted impermissibly in scheduling the examination.

Concerning the staged rating argument, the Court indicated that the relevant temporal focus for adjudicating an increased-rating claim is on the evidence concerning the state of the disability from the time period of one year before the claim was filed until VA makes a final decision on the claim.  While older evidence is not necessarily irrelevant, it is generally not needed to determine the effective date of an increased rating.  The Court held that there was no distinction between initial ratings and increased-rating claims for applying staged ratings.

The Court, noting that the veteran’s claim for increased rating was pending before VA for more than four years during which time he received several medical opinions concerning the lateral instability of his left knee (a criterion for evaluation under DC 5257), found that the Board erred in not providing a basis for its determination that there was no evidence of left knee instability during the time period relevant to the May 2001 increased-rating claim as the Board did not find that the 2005 VA examination report rendered the private physician’s April 2002 findings of left knee instability inaccurate or not probative.

The Court vacated and remanded the Board decision for the Board to provide an adequate statement of reasons or bases concerning whether the veteran is entitled to any staged ratings higher than 10 percent, based on the Board's factual findings that he had left knee instability in April 2002, but not later in January 2005.  Since the Board did not find the 2005 VA examination to be more probative than the private physician’s 2001 and 2002 opinions for the entire time period relevant to the increased rating claim, the Board will also have to determine whether the 2005 VA examination revealed that the state of the left knee disability has existed since he filed the appeal, and that any inconsistencies between the medical examinations must be considered and reconciled at that time.
