Decision Assessment Document
 Bingham v. Principi, Aug 27, 2004, 18 Vet.App. 470
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The CAVC affirmed an October 2001 Board of Veterans’ Appeals (BVA) decision that denied an effective date prior to April 25, 1991, for the award of service connection for bilateral hearing loss.  The appellant argued that VA’s failure to address his entitlement to service connection on a presumptive basis at the time his original claim was filed in 1949 meant that this 1949 claim for service connection on a theory of presumptive eligibility remained pending until the February 2000 grant.  In addition, the veteran argued that [38 CFR 3.400(q)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=69984c7a14cb472819df194c9682c8c7&node=se38.1.3_1400&rgn=div8) applied to new and material evidence submitted during the judicial appeal period following a BVA decision.  Last, the veteran contended that a 1990 letter to the BVA constituted a notice of appeal (NOA) to the CAVC.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact.

**Summary of the facts and Court's reasons:**

In 1949, several years following his discharge from active service, the veteran filed a claim for, among other things, loss of hearing.  The BVA in a November 1950 decision, denied service connection for (among other things) an ear condition.  Beginning in 1988, the veteran attempted to reopen his claim.  The BVA denied the veteran’s April 25, 1991, request to reopen his claim in a November 1996 decision which found that new and material evidence had not been submitted.  The CAVC reversed this November 1996 BVA decision and remanded the claim for further development.  Following this, in a February 2000 decision the BVA awarded service connection for hearing loss on a presumptive basis.  Among the evidence cited by the BVA as showing that hearing loss was incurred within the year following service was a medical report received in 1949.  In its decision promulgating the BVA decision, the regional office determined that April 25, 1991, was the earliest possible date that a communication was received which could be construed as a request to reopen the claim for service connection for hearing loss.  The veteran disagreed with the effective date based upon the arguments above.  He claimed that the effective date should be in September 1949, the date he filed his first claim for service connection.

The Court stated that the appellant’s contentions all appear to be based on the notion that a *theory* of entitlement (e.g., direct service connection or presumptive service connection) is equivalent to a *claim* for benefits (e.g., service connection for hearing loss).  According to the Court, the appellant’s cited authorities (which included *Moody, Szemraj* and *Roberson*) do not support the contention that any purported failure by VA to consider a *theory* of entitlement (e.g., presumptive service connection) renders a *claim* for benefits (e.g., service connection for hearing loss) unadjudicated, i.e., the appellant has not demonstrated that a purported incomplete adjudication is the equivalent of unadjudicated.  Specifically, the cited Federal Circuit cases involve circumstances where there were *distinct claims* for two different benefits, whereas this case involves only *one claim* for one benefit, i.e., service connection for hearing loss.  The Court concluded that, even if the 1950 Board erred in not considering the presumptive-service-connection *theory* for hearing loss, there existed no separate presumptive-service-connection *claim* that could have remained pending.  (The Court noted that this claim did not involve clear and unmistakable error (CUE) and pointed out that the appellant was free to attack the decision on the basis of CUE.)

Regarding the veteran’s remaining arguments, the CAVC noted that it appears that section [3.400(q)(1)(i)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=69984c7a14cb472819df194c9682c8c7&node=se38.1.3_1400&rgn=div8) applies to only the one-year period in which an appellant may appeal an RO decision to the BVA—not to the 120-day period in which an appellant may appeal a final BVA decision to the Court.  Last, the CAVC found that a letter sent to the BVA in 1990 did not satisfy the NOA-filing requirements.
