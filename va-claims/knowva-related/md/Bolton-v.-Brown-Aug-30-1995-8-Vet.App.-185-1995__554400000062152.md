Decision Assessment Document
 Bolton v. Brown, Aug 30, 1995, 8 Vet.App. 185 (1995)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

Incarcerated veterans are entitled to the same care and consideration given to their fellow veterans.

When the medical evidence is insufficient to decide an incarcerated veteran’s claim, VA is still obligated to assist the veteran in obtaining evidence or explain its exhaustive but unsuccessful attempts to obtain that evidence.

**Impact on VBA:**

Manual M21-1, III.iv.3.F contains guidance implementing the Court's holding.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

In November 1984, the veteran was incarcerated with a scheduled release date of March 3, 2005.  In July 1985, he received a 10% rating for service-connected PTSD.  In March 1991, he filed a claim for an increased rating.  He provided an affidavit from a correctional officer at the county facility which stated that the veteran exhibited personality traits which would seriously handicap him in getting and holding a job in a free society.  On remand, the Board ordered the regional office (RO) to have the veteran undergo a VA psychiatric examination.  The RO requested the VA Outpatient Clinic to arrange for an examination of the veteran by coordinating with the warden of the correctional facility.  The Chief Medical Officer at the clinic responded that the clinic was unable to find a fee basis psychiatrist who would do an examination at the institution.  The Board decided that the preponderance of the evidence was against the appeal for an increased rating.

In his appeal, the veteran argued that VA violated its duty to assist by not providing a psychiatric examination as instructed by the Board.  In this case, a current examination was not obtained, and the Board chose to rely upon a 1990 examination by a psychiatry resident who did not have the veteran's claims folder to review.  Such an examination neither fulfills VA's duty to assist, nor constitutes an adequate predicate for both the rating determination and the BVA's decision.  Although the RO claimed an inability to get a fee-basis physician to conduct an examination in the correctional facility, the record contains neither information concerning the efforts expended by the RO in that regard nor any explanation as to why a psychiatrist employed by the VA was not directed to perform the examination.  The Court remanded the case for VA to fulfill its statutory duty to assist the veteran in developing the facts of his claim.
