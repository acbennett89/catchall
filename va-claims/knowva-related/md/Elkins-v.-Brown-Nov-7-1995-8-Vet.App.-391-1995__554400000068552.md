Decision Assessment Document
 Elkins v. Brown, Nov 7, 1995, 8 Vet.App. 391 (1995)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that "mental unsoundness" in 38 C.F.R. 3.302, not further defined by any statute or regulation, is a generic term that necessarily includes both service connectable mental disabilities as well as disorders, such as personality disorders, for which service connection may not be granted under the rating schedule, and that "mental unsoundness" has not been established as a service-connectable disorder.

The Court held that service connection under the rubric of "mental unsoundness", presumed to be the result of suicide or a bona fide attempt, may be granted only where there is present a service-connectable disability and all the requirements for service connection are established.  In this case, the record contained no evidence that the veteran (1) was diagnosed with a compensable psychosis within one year after his discharge, or (2) has a mental disability listed in 38 C.F.R. 4.132, which was incurred in or aggravated by service.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.4.H.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran had active service from October 1967 to August 1968.  On three separate occasions during service, he was diagnosed with inadequate personality after he twice attempted suicide and once suffered an episode of hyperventilation.  He was administratively discharged as a consequence of a psychiatrist's diagnosis of personality disorder.  In 1979, he was admitted to a VA hospital for treatment of a self-inflicted gunshot wound to his abdomen.  His claim for service connection for a nervous condition was denied and subsequently upheld by the BVA in October 1980.  In 1983, he was first diagnosed with paranoid schizophrenia and explosive personality.  His attempt to reopen his claim for an acquired psychiatric disorder, including PTSD was denied.  The BVA concluded that the evidence did not establish PTSD and that the veteran had failed to show that his schizophrenia was service connected.  In 1987, he notified VA of treatment at a psychiatric hospital about one year after his discharge from service.  Those hospital records reflected a diagnosis of passive-aggressive personality and excessive periodic drinking.  In April 1988, the BVA affirmed the denial of the attempt to reopen, concluding that the veteran had failed to establish a new factual basis supporting service connection for a psychiatric disability.  In August 1990, the veteran submitted a request for reconsideration based on allegations of clear and unmistakable error (CUE) in the "original rating decision" and all subsequent decisions.  In 1992, the BVA determined that no "new and material evidence" had been submitted to warrant reopening the claim, and that its previous decision in 1988 was not predicated on obvious error.  In his appeal, the veteran contended that the BVA, in denying reopening in 1988 and 1992, committed CUE because it ignored evidence of presumptive service connection, breached its duty to assist and, failed to apply the provisions of 38 C.F.R. 3.302 in the adjudication of his claim.
