Decision Assessment Document
Allin v. Brown, Feb 14, 1994, 6 Vet.App. 207 (1994)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that an outcome-determinative error must be shown to establish clear and unmistakable error (CUE).

The Court also held that failure to follow the VA Physician's Guide cannot constitute a CUE, as the VA Physician's Guide is neither a statute nor regulation.  Strict compliance with the VA Physician's Guide is not a requirement for an adequate examination, as it is only a general document providing guidance to physicians in assessing injuries and disabilities.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.2.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran (5/41-5/46) was a former Prisoner of War (4/42-8/45) and survivor of the Bataan Death March.  Service connection was in effect for multiple POW-related disabilities with entitlement to a total rating based on individual unemployability from April 1972.  The veteran died of respiratory arrest due to squamous cell carcinoma of the larynx with pulmonary metastases in May 1981.  Service connection for cause of death was denied at that time, and no CUE was later found with respect to pre-1972 disability ratings when a claim to reopen was denied in 1988.

A 1971 rating had confirmed a 30% evaluation for service connected neurosis based on a diagnosis of anxiety reaction with unchanged symptoms.  Although the veteran had been hospitalized in 1968 for fibrillation, service connection was denied for a heart condition due to beri-beri following a negative VA examination which showed no residuals of POW-related diseases or injuries.

On appeal to the Court, appellant argued that a total rating would have been in effect for 10 years but for CUE in the 1971 rating which failed to apply POW presumptions on service connection for various disabilities.  In addition, she argued that VA had improperly rejected CUE claims involving failure to apply the VA Physician's Guide for Disability Evaluation Examinations (VA Physicians Guide) in 1969 and 1971.

The Court rejected the argument that VA rating decisions in 1969 and 1971 involved CUE because the medical examinations upon which they were based did not follow procedures set out in the VA Physicians Guide.  Failure to apply this guide cannot constitute CUE since it is neither statutory nor regulatory.  The Court commented that "a formal regulatory or statutory procedure setting out the matters to be examined in every case would be far too restrictive" since relevant professional considerations vary from case to case.
