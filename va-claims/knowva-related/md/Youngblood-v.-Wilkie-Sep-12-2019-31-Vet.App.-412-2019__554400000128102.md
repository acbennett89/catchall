Decision Assessment Document
Youngblood v. Wilkie, Sep 12, 2019, 31 Vet.App. 412 (2019)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the “one disability” clause of 38 C.F.R. 4.16(a) that allows multiple service-connected disabilities to serve as one service-connected disability is solely restricted to 4.16(a) and may not be applied to serve as a “service-connected disability rated as total” for the purposes of establishing eligibility for special monthly compensation (SMC) under 38 U.S.C. 1114(s).

The Court concluded that because the Secretary plainly stated in the regulation that the purpose of the “one disability” phrase was to assist certain veterans in reaching the baseline disability rating requirements to benefit from 4.16(a), no other purpose can be read into the regulation, including retaining the “one disability” designation to establish “a service-connected disability rated as total” for SMC(s) eligibility.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court’s holding will be incorporated into guidance contained in the Manual M21-1 under IV.ii.2.H.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

In July 2002, the VA regional office (RO) awarded entitlement to a total disability evaluation due to individual unemployability (TDIU).  The RO based the TDIU award on three disabilities:  total left knee arthroplasty, status-post surgical procedures of the left knee, and degenerative joint disease of the right knee.  His left and right knee disabilities were rated 60 percent and 20 percent, respectively, with a combined disability rating of 80 percent.

The RO, in November 2003, granted service connection for renal insufficiency and polycystic kidney disease with a 60 percent rating from January 1999, 80 percent rating from March 2004 until September 2012, and a 100 percent rating after September 3, 2012.  The RO awarded SMC at the (s) housebound rate from September 4, 2012, the date of the increased evaluation for the kidney disease.  The veteran challenged the effective date of the SMC(s) award.

The Board, in December 2017, determined that the veteran was not entitled to an SMC(s) award prior to September 4, 2012.

The veteran argued before the Court that he became eligible for section 1114(s) benefits on July 31, 2001, because the TDIU award that became effective on that date also is “a service-connected disability rated as total.”

The Court rejected the veteran’s argument, concluding that the “one disability” clause in 4.16(a) created from multiple service-connected disorders is not sufficient to serve as “a service-connected disability rated as total” for the purposes of establishing eligibility for section 1114(s) benefits.

The Court found that the Secretary wrote the reason for the “one disability” clause in 4.16(a) right into the regulation.  The Court concluded that the phrase “for the above purpose” in the regulation and its referent plainly convey that the Secretary created the “one disability” clause for the singular purpose of providing a mechanism to assist certain veteran in meeting the threshold schedular disability rating requirements for § 4.16(a) adjudications of their TDIU requests.

The Court held that deciding whether to aggregate disability ratings is the one and only purpose of considering disorders to be “one disability” in 4.16(a).  Once eligibility for 4.16(a) adjudication is established, the “one disability” clause serves no further pursue and adjudication continues in the usual manner.  The Court characterized the “one disability” clause as a mechanism for tabulating disability ratings for certain service-connected disorders under 4.16(a), nothing more.

The Court, concluding that there was no error in the Board’s finding that the veteran was ineligible for section 1114(s) benefits prior to 2012, affirmed the Board’s December 2107.
