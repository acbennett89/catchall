Decision Assessment Document
Francisco v. Brown, Sep 27, 1994, 7 Vet.App. 55 (1994)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that where entitlement to compensation has already been established and an increase in the disability rating is at issue, the present level of disability is of primary concern.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.8.C.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran was awarded a 20% service-connected disability evaluation for a gunshot wound to the left shoulder in September 1949.  In November 1986, he underwent a left total shoulder arthroplasty.  Following the assignment of a total (100%) rating for 13 months, the evaluation was reduced to the pre-surgical 20% evaluation.  He appealed to the Board of Veterans Appeals (Board), which affirmed the decision in November 1992.

In consideration of appellant's allegation that the Board erred in not adequately considering the appellant's entire medical history, the Court stated, "Although a rating specialist is directed to review the recorded history of a disability in order to make a more accurate evaluation, see 38 C.F.R. § 4.2 (1993), the regulations do not give past medical reports precedence over current findings."  The Court concluded that an examination in 1992, which only briefly discussed the veteran's past medical history, afforded a plausible basis for the Board's affirmance.
