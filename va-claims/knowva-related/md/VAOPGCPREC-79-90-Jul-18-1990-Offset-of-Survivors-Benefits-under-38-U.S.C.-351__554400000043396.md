Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 79-90, Jul 18, 1990, Offset of Survivors' Benefits under 38 USC 1151 (formerly 351)
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the opinion is about:**

This opinion previously issued of General Counsel Opinion 1-87, dated November 14, 1986 is reissued as a Precedent Opinion pursuant to 38 C.F.R. §§ 2.6(e)(9) and 14.507.  The case involved a surviving spouse and minor child, both of whom qualified for benefits under 38 USC 1151.  The surviving spouse had claimed damages under the Federal Tort Claims Act in various legal capacities - as personal representative of the veteran's estate, individually, and on behalf of the child.  Upon settlement, payment was made to the surviving spouse as personal representative, and a portion was designated for the minor child.

Analysis of the legislative and regulatory history showed that the 38 USC 1151 offset prevents double-recovery of amounts received in a personal capacity.  Under the terms of FTCA (28 USC 1346(b), the law of the place where a negligent or wrongful act or omission governs liability.  Review of state wrongful-death statutes indicated a representative would receive payments in such cases only nominally, on behalf of individual survivors.  On the other hand, a decedent's cause of action is transferred to the estate, under state survival statutes, and any payments received by a representative in this connection would be subject to creditors' claims.  In that case, the surviving spouse would not be the actual (FTCA) claimant, nor necessarily benefit from any judgment or settlement personally.  Accordingly, any FTCA recovery received by a surviving spouse, as personal representative of the estate under a survival statute, could not be deemed a personal recovery by the surviving spouse individually, for purposes of 38 USC 1151 offset.

**Impact on VBA:**

Delete M21-1 Paragraph 29.52(h)(1) upon revision.  The information is inaccurate and unnecessary.

**Summary of the facts and findings:**

The legal status under which a claimant recovers on a claim under the Federal Tort Claims Act (FTCA), based on death, is relevant to determination of the amount to be offset from DIC benefits pursuant to 38 USC 1151, and this status will generally depend upon the nature of damages recovered:

1. Amounts recovered by an individual under a typical wrongful-death statute may be offset against that person's DIC, even if damages were paid to a nominal trustee.  Each survivor receiving damages is liable for DIC  offset only as to the sums actually included, in the judgement or settlement, as compensation for harm suffered by that individual.

2. Damages recovered by a personal representative under a survival statute are not subject to DIC offset under 38 USC 1151, even though that individual is also the surviving spouse.
