Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 07-01, Feb 14, 2001, Effective Date of Reduction of VA Compensation When Veteran Is Receiving Military Retired or Retirement Pay
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Whether a recipient of military retired or retirement pay whom VA has determined is entitled to compensation under title 38, United States Code, and who has submitted a waiver of entitlement to that pay is a payee for purposes of 38 U.S.C. § 3012(b)(6) (1964) (currently 38 U.S.C. § 5112(b)(6)).

**Impact on VBA**

A “payee” for purposes of 38 U.S.C. § 5112(b)(6) (formerly section 3012(b)(6), and which will be referred to in this assessment only as 5112(b)(6)) is a person who is in continuous receipt of VA compensation, dependency and indemnity compensation, or pension.  The effective date of an award of VA compensation to a recipient of military pay or retirement pay who has submitted a waiver of entitlement to that pay is the date upon which the service department reduces such pay.

Section 5112(b)(6) does not apply where there is no reduction in the amount of compensation being paid to a claimant. Therefore, if VA reduces a veteran’s evaluation while the veteran is receiving military retired or retirement pay during the period in which a service department is processing a waiver of such pay, section 5112(b)(6) is not applicable because, at the time of the reduction, the reduction decision does not result in the veteran getting any less retired or retirement pay than the veteran had been receiving before the decision was made.

**Summary on facts and findings**

On December 7, 1967, the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) received from the veteran VA Forms 21-526e, Veteran’s Application for Compensation or Pension at Separation from Service, and 8-651, Election of Compensation or Pension in Lieu of Retirement or Waiver of Retirement Pay to Secure Compensation or Pension from Veteran’s Administration.  In a March 21, 1968, rating decision, the VA regional office awarded a 100 percent rating for the veteran’s service-connected fracture dislocation of the fifth and sixth cervical vertebrae with secondary quadriparesis, status post surgical fusion of the fifth and sixth vertebrae, and special monthly compensation (SMC) for loss of use of both feet.  The effective date of the award was February 12, 1968.  VA notified the veteran by letter dated March 26, 1968, that VA Form 21-651 (formerly 8-651) was being forwarded to the service department so that a waiver of his retirement pay could be processed.  Based upon VA hospital records showing treatment from December 1967 to April 1968, the regional office, in a May 9, 1968 rating decision, determined that the veteran was entitled to SMC based on loss of use of only one foot, effective April 21, 1968, thereby reducing the amount of compensation to which he was entitled.  VA advised the veteran of this decision in a May 15, 1968 letter as well as his right to appeal it.  VA received the completed VA Form 21-651 from the service department on June 17, 1968, which stated that the veteran's military retired pay had been reduced effective June 1, 1968, and shortly thereafter the veteran began to receive VA monthly compensation in the amount of $400 for the period February 12, 1968, through April 20, 1968, and $347 effective April 21, 1968. The veteran filed a notice of disagreement with the May 1968 rating decision, but he withdrew his appeal in a statement received by VA on October 23, 1968.

In September 1994 (in connection with an appeal of a claim for increase of his SMC) the veteran inquired whether 38 CFR 3.105(e) was applicable to the 1968 reduction of his SMC.  The Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA) found there was no clear and unmistakable error (CUE) in the May 9, 1968 rating decision.  The BVA concluded that section 5112(b)(6) and 38 CFR § 3.105(e) are not applicable to the claim because the veteran did not become a “payee” of VA compensation until the service department processed his waiver of retired pay and notified VA.

The General Counsel noted that the term “payee” is not defined in title 38 of the United States Code.  However, in 38 CFR 3.105(e) the term “payee” is interpreted to refer to a person currently receiving compensation payments for whom a reduction in evaluation of a service-connected disability or employability status would result in a reduction or discontinuance of such payments.  Section 3.105(e) has been amended to show clearly that the 60-day grace period for a proposed reduction in disability evaluation is applicable only to running awards.  The procedure is not to be applied where there is no change in the rate of disability compensation being paid because the reduction in evaluation for one disability is offset by assignment of the same or a higher evaluation for another service-connected disability or where the award has been suspended for failure to report for an examination.

The General Counsel reviewed a 1948 opinion of the VA Solicitor which concluded that when a veteran is receiving military retired or retirement pay and has been determined to be entitled to VA compensation, the effective date of the award of VA compensation is the effective date of the reduction in military pay.

The General Counsel also stated that, even if the veteran could be considered a payee effective February 12, 1968, when VA granted service connection, 38 U.S.C. § 3012 (b)(6) would nonetheless not be applicable to his claim  The plain language of 38 USC §5112(b)(6) shows that it is applicable only when there is a reduction in compensation payments to a payee.  The General Counsel pointed out that reduction in evaluation is not synonymous with reduction in compensation, which is required by section 5112(b)(6).  In the instant case, the veteran suffered no reduction in compensation as a result of the VA decision reducing his SMC because at the time the VA decision to reduce his SMC was made, the did not get any less than he had been receiving before the decision was made.

Based upon 38 C.F.R. § 3.105(e) and its history, the legislative history of 38 U.S.C. § 5112(b)(6) (formerly 3012(b)(6)), and opinions of the VA Solicitor and General Counsel, the General Counsel concluded that the effective date of the veteran's award of VA compensation in the this case was June 1, 1968, the effective date of the reduction in military pay.

Although the impact of this opinion is significant, it does not conflict with the procedures in M21-1, Part IV, para 21.10a.(2)(b).  A reference to this opinion should be added to that chapter.
