Decision Assessment Document
 Davis v. Principi, Jan 11, 2002, 276 F.3d 1341
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

If a condition pre-exists service, evidence of temporary flare-ups of the condition during service is not sufficient for a non-combat veteran to establish aggravation of the pre-existing condition.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new effect, but this case is a good training tool relating to aggravation of a pre-existing condition.

**Summary of the facts and Court's reasons:**

Prior to his entrance into active service in 1971, the veteran had a prior history of mental illness and a prolonged hospitalization for schizophrenia.  He was voluntarily discharged in 1974 in response to charges of, among other things, being absent without leave, assaulting a superior officer and failing to obey lawful orders.  In 1990, the veteran filed a claim for service connection for schizophrenia.  Among the evidence weighing against a finding of service connection was a doctor’s opinion that the veteran’s psychiatric condition was not permanently aggravated beyond the normal progression of the disease.  VA found that schizophrenia was not incurred or aggravated by active service. The Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (CAVC) and the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (Federal Circuit) appealed.

The Federal Circuit noted that where a veteran can show an increase in the severity of a preexisting disability, as distinguished from the mere recurrence of manifestations of the pre-service condition, [38 U.S.C. 1153](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1153) establishes that there may be a finding of aggravation.  The statute expressly excludes from aggravation any increase in disability attributable to the natural progress of the preexisting disease. The Court held that evidence of temporary flare-ups symptomatic of an underlying preexisting condition, alone, is not sufficient for a non combat veteran to show increased disability under section 1153 unless the underlying condition is worsened.
