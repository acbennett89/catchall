Decision Assessment Document
 Douglas v. Shinseki, Apr 17, 2009, 23 Vet.App. 19
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the VA Secretary’s authority to develop a claim includes the gathering of all information and evidence – positive *and* negative – sufficient to render an informed decision, provided that the evidence is procured in an impartial, unbiased, and neutral manner.

The Court held that the Secretary’s authority to develop a claim necessarily includes the authority to collect and develop evidence that might rebut the presumption of service connection.

The Court also held that a request for a medical opinion is not impermissibly leading simply because it raises a theory that might lead to the denial of a claim. It is only impermissible if it communicates that the adjudicator wishes to receive a specific answer to a question posed.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran served from 1969 to 1973 to include service in Vietnam. He was diagnosed with Crohn’s disease in 1976 and treated with Prednisone.

In 2003, he filed a claim for service connection for diabetes mellitus and submitted private treatment records in support of his claim. The records included a physician’s notation that the veteran was beginning to experience significant issues with chronic steroid issues with a diabetic tendency being the foremost issue.

The regional office (RO) ordered a medical examination with a request that an opinion be provided as to whether the veteran’s diabetes was due to use of steroids. The engagement memorandum indicated that Vietnam service was verified and evidence had been provided showing an extensive history of steroid use due to medical conditions. Two VA endocrinologists opined that the veteran’s diabetes was more likely than not secondary to his chronic steroid use. The RO denied the claim and its decision was upheld by the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) in April 2007.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Secretary lacked the authority to develop the rebuttal evidence and/or, if he had the authority to develop such evidence, he did so in a matter that impermissibly suggested or requested an answer that would aid only in denying his claim. He also argued that the Secretary developed this claim solely to rebut the presumptive-service-connection claim in violation of the Court’s holding in [*Mariano v.* *Principi*, 17 Vet.App. 305, 312 (2003)](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014790/) that “VA may not order additional development for the sole purpose of obtaining” unfavorable evidence. He claimed that the engagement memorandum impermissibly requested that the VA examiners rebut his favorable medical opinion in violation of *Colayong v. West*, 12 Vet.App. 524 (1999) [see attached].

The Court, in rejecting the veteran’s arguments, held that the Secretary has an affirmative duty to assist the veteran in developing evidence to substantiate a claim. See [38 U.S.C. § 5103A](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5103A). The Court held that the Secretary is also required to procure evidence in an impartial, unbiased, and neutral manner that includes gathering all positive *and* negative information and evidence that is relevant and material to a claim. The Court noted that Congress explicitly provided that the presumption of service connection was rebuttable when there is “affirmative evidence to the contrary.” See [38 U.S.C. § 1113](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1113).

In addressing the *Mariano* argument, the Court noted that while the duty to assist is not a license to continue gathering evidence in the hopes of finding evidence to support a claim, *Mariano* provides the corollary that it is also not a license to continue gathering evidence in the hopes of finding evidence against a claim. The Court held that the Secretary not only has authority to gather evidence that might rebut a presumption, he also has an affirmative duty to gather the evidence necessary to render an informed decision on the claim if that means gathering and developing negative evidence, provided it is done so in an impartial, unbiased, and neutral manner.

The Court also rejected the veteran’s argument that the RO’s engagement memorandum was tainted because it was obtained for the sole purpose of rebutting the presumption of service connection. The Court found that the engagement memorandum contained no such impermissible suggestion or limitation and characterized the memorandum as asking nothing more than an opinion regarding a possible etiology that was reasonably raised by the evidence submitted by the veteran. The Court observed that engagement letters cannot be prohibited from specifically identifying the adjudicator’s concern, as without guidance, there is great risk that the opinion produced will not address the issue of concern for the adjudicator.

The Court affirmed the Board’s April 2007 decision.
