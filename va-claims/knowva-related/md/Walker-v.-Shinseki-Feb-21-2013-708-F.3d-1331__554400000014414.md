Decision Assessment Document
Walker v. Shinseki, Feb 21, 2013, 708 F.3d 1331
U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that the provisions of [38 C.F.R. § 3.303(b)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=21fb05c6027b812ef7e49b950a3e6f54&node=se38.1.3_1303&rgn=div8), including those pertaining to continuity of symptomatology, which may be used to establish service connection between a current disease and symptoms observed during service or a presumptive period, apply only to chronic diseases identified in [38 C.F.R. § 3.309(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=21fb05c6027b812ef7e49b950a3e6f54&node=se38.1.3_1309&rgn=div8).  For other diseases that may be considered chronic, but are not identified in § 3.309(a), the claim must be analyzed through [38 C.F.R. § 3.303(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=21fb05c6027b812ef7e49b950a3e6f54&node=se38.1.3_1303&rgn=div8), which requires a medical nexus between the condition in service and the current disability as part of the standard three-element test for entitlement to disability compensation.  Note: The standard three element test for disability compensation requires:  (1) the existence of a present disability; (2) in-service incurrence or aggravation of a disease or injury; and (3) a causal relationship between the present disability and the disease or injury incurred or aggravated during service*.  See* [*Holton* *v. Shinseki*, 557 3d. 1363, 1366 (Fed. Cir. 2009)](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000015468/).

The Federal Circuit also held that it overruled all Veterans Court decisions that required a continuity of symptomatology analysis under § 3.303(b) for claims based on chronic diseases not enumerated in § 3.309(a).  The Federal Circuit rejected the argument that continuity of symptomatology in [§ 3.303(b)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=21fb05c6027b812ef7e49b950a3e6f54&node=se38.1.3_1303&rgn=div8) has any role other than to afford an alternative route to service connection for specific chronic diseases listed in § 3.309(a).  This holding extends to chronic diseases “shown as such” in service, and to diseases or symptoms “noted” in service but only later diagnosed as one of the diseases enumerated in § 3.309(a).  Service connection for a disease not listed under § 3.309(a) may not be established on the basis of the chronicity provisions of § 3.303(b), but would have to satisfy the typical three-element nexus analysis of § 3.303(a).

The provisions of § 3.303(b) also provide that if a chronic disease is shown in service or within the presumptive period, subsequent manifestations of the same chronic disease at any later date will be service connected unless clearly attributed to intercurrent causes.  The regulation provides that if a condition noted in service or in a presumptive period is not clearly identified as a chronic disease during such periods, a showing of continuity of symptomatology is necessary to establish service connection for the disease under § 3.303(b).  Continuity of symptomatology is an analytical and evidentiary tool to aid VA adjudicators in the evaluation of whether a chronic disease existed in service.

**Impact on VBA:**

Manual Rewrite revisions will be required in order to comply with the Court’s holdings.

VA is only required to consider “continuity of symptomatology” as a distinct theory of service connection in cases involving a chronic disease identified in [38 C.F.R. § 3.309(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=21fb05c6027b812ef7e49b950a3e6f54&node=se38.1.3_1309&rgn=div8).  VA is also only required to consider chronic diseases shown as such in service, as to permit a finding of service connection of manifestations of the same disease at a later date unless clearly attributable to intercurrent causes, to the diseases enumerated in § 3.309(a).

Although the Federal Circuit clarified that “continuity of symptomatology” is not a generally applicable theory of service connection apart from the nexus analysis under [§ 3.303(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=21fb05c6027b812ef7e49b950a3e6f54&node=se38.1.3_1303&rgn=div8), its decision should not be construed to suggest that the evidence of continuity of symptomatology is irrelevant to determining a nexus.  All evidence bearing upon service connection should be evaluated under the three-part nexus test.  For example, the absence of symptoms for a prolonged period following service may tend to show that a disability is not related to service, while the existence of continuous symptomatology following service may be probative evidence to support a finding of nexus.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran served as a four-engine airplane pilot and flight instructor in the Army Air Force from March 1943 to November 1945.  The regional office (RO) denied his claim for service connection for bilateral hearing loss in 2007.  He appealed the denial to the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board).

The veteran’s service records were unavailable due to fire.  The record included sworn statements from the veteran’s son and wife that his hearing loss began in service and continued throughout his life.  A VA audiologist, in September 2009, examined the veteran.  The examiner relied upon information related from the veteran’s family regarding hearing difficulty and history of bilateral hearing loss.  The examiner noted that noise exposure in service was conceded and diagnosed bilateral hearing loss.  However, the examiner opined that the veteran’s hearing loss was “less likely as not” caused by military service as a pilot, as presbycusis (age-related hearing loss) was the likely etiology.  The examiner also noted that the veteran participated in recreational hunting without hearing protection.

The Board, finding that the audiologist’s opinion was entitled to more weight than the lay statements from the veteran’s wife and son, concluded that the veteran failed to satisfy the three-element test under [§ 3.303(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=21fb05c6027b812ef7e49b950a3e6f54&node=se38.1.3_1303&rgn=div8) to establish service connection for bilateral hearing loss.  It is unclear whether the Board analyzed the claim under [§ 3.303(b)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=21fb05c6027b812ef7e49b950a3e6f54&node=se38.1.3_1303&rgn=div8).

The Veterans Court, in affirming the Board’s decision, also applied the three-element test under § 3.303(a) and concluded that the Board adequately weighed the lay evidence against the audiologist opinion and agreed that the veteran’s hearing loss was more likely than not due to aging and recreational noise exposure.  The Veterans Court did not apply the continuity of symptomatology provisions of § 3.303(b) to the evidentiary record.

On appeal to the Federal Circuit, the appellant argued that a remand was required to consider service connection for hearing loss under § 3.303(b) and asserted that the term “chronic disease” in § 3.303(b) should apply to any disease that ordinarily would be medically defined as chronic.

The Secretary, interpreting § 3.303(b) to have only selected the chronic diseases listed under § 3.309(a) to qualify for assessment under the regulation, argued that since bilateral hearing loss is not listed in § 3.309(a), the continuity of symptomatology provisions of § 3.303(b) are inapplicable.

The Federal Circuit agreed with the Secretary’s argument, concluding that although § 3.303(b) does not contain a specific cross reference to § 3.309(a), a harmonious reading of § 3.303(b), [3.307(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=21fb05c6027b812ef7e49b950a3e6f54&node=se38.1.3_1307&rgn=div8), and 3.309(a) supports an implicit cross reference to § 3.309(a) in § 3.303(b).  The Federal Circuit held that § 3.303(b) is constrained by § 3.309(a); therefore, regardless of when a veteran’s chronic disease is first shown or noted, the regulation is only available to establish service connection for the specific diseases listed in § 3.309(a).

The Federal Circuit, again agreeing with the Secretary’s argument, held that diseases that would be considered chronic in the medical sense, but are not listed in § 3.309(a), may still qualify for service connection under § 3.303(a), rather than 3.303(b), and that evidence of continuous symptoms would be relevant evidence that should be considered when evaluating whether or not there is a nexus.

The Federal Circuit rejected the veteran’s argument that continuity of symptomatology in § 3.303(b) is available to establish service connection for any non-chronic disease or injury.  The Federal Circuit concluded that continuity of symptomatology has no role other than to afford an alternative route to service connection for specific chronic diseases listed in § 3.309(a).  The Federal Circuit also held that suggestions or holdings to the contrary in any Veterans Court decisions, and dictum to the contrary in its precedent, are incorrect and of no effect.

The Federal Circuit affirmed the Veterans Court decision, concluding that the veteran sought compensation for a condition not listed as a chronic disease in [§ 3.309(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=21fb05c6027b812ef7e49b950a3e6f54&node=se38.1.3_1309&rgn=div8); thus, his claim cannot be considered under [§ 3.303(b)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=21fb05c6027b812ef7e49b950a3e6f54&node=se38.1.3_1303&rgn=div8).  Therefore, the Veterans Court did not commit error in denying the veteran’s request for remand to the Board to conduct a § 3.303(b) assessment of the facts of record.
