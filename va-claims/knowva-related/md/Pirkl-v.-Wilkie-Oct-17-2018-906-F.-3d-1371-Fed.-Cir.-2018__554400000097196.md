Decision Assessment Document
Pirkl v. Wilkie, Oct 17, 2018, 906 F. 3d 1371 (Fed. Cir. 2018)
U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that VA, in implementing a finding of clear and unmistakable error (CUE), is required to consider the effects of that CUE finding on the legal and factual basis of the subsequent rating decisions.

In this case, the finding of CUE in a 1953 decision that reduced a 100 percent evaluation required application of the regulations governing reduction of 100 percent evaluations to the 1956 and 1966 decisions that further reduced the disability’s evaluation.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court’s holding will be incorporated into guidance contained in the Manual under III.iv.2.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran was awarded a 10 percent evaluation for paranoid schizophrenia following discharge from service in November 1949.  The regional office (RO) granted a 100 percent evaluation, effective September 30, 1952.  The RO reduced the evaluation to 70 percent in September 1953, to 50 percent in December 1956, and to 30 percent in April 1966.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in January 1967, affirmed the RO decision.  In August 1991, the veteran filed a claim for increase for schizophrenia and was ultimately granted a 100 percent evaluation, from November 30, 1988.

In February 2005, the RO concluded that neither the September 1953 or December 1956 decisions contained CUE.  The veteran appealed the denial of CUE in the 1953 decision to the Board, but not the 1956 decision.  The Board, in August 2006, concluded that the September 1953 decision, which reduced the evaluation for schizophrenia from 100 percent to 70 percent, contained CUE.  The RO, in implementing the Board decision, awarded a 100 percent rating for schizophrenia from September 30, 1952, to February 9, 1957, the date on which the December 1956 decision effectuated the 50 percent rating.  The veteran appealed the RO decision, arguing that proper implementation of the Board decision would entitle him to a 100 percent disability from September 1952 to November 1988.

The Board, in September 2008, concluded that its finding of CUE in the September 1953 decision did not affect the finality of any subsequent decisions that reduced the veteran’s disability rating, and that the RO did not err in its implementation of its August 2006 decision.  The Veterans Court, in affirming the Board decision, concluded that the finding of CUE in the September 1953 RO decision did not affect the subsequent reductions, which are independent of the 1953 decision.

The Federal Circuit, in its 2013 decision (Pirkl I), held that while the finding of CUE in the 1953 decision does not necessarily render null and void a subsequent, independent rating decision, the finding of CUE can change the legal and factual background against which a subsequent rating decision is made.  The Federal Circuit found that the Board’s finding of CUE in the 1953 decision changed the factual and legal background against which subsequent reductions were made, and the Board failed to consider the effect of this change in implementing its finding of CUE.  When the Board determined that the September 1953 decision contained CUE, the veteran’s disability rating was reset to 100 percent as of the date of the 1953 decision; thus, the subsequent reduction of the veteran’s disability rating in 1956 effectively became a reduction from a 100 percent total disability, not from a 70 percent rating.

The Federal Circuit upheld the Veterans Court decision that the subsequent reductions in 1956 and 1966 stand as independent decisions not nullified and voided by the Board’s finding of CUE in the 1953 RO decision.  However, as the finding of CUE in the 1953 decision changed the legal context applicable to the later decisions, the Federal Circuit held that the Board failed to consider the effect of the applicability of § 3.170 or its successor regulations in the 1956 and 1966 decisions.  The Federal Circuit vacated and remanded the Veterans Court decision with instructions to remand the case to the Board to consider the effect of § 3.170 or its successor regulations on the December 1956 and April 1966 reductions.

The Board, on remand, dismissed the appeal for relief of the 1953 CUE past the effective date of the 1956 rating reduction.  The Board, relying on Reizenstein v. Shinseki, 583 F.3d 1331 (Fed. Cir. 2009), concluded that the regulation covering VA decisions that reduce 100 percent ratings did not apply to the 1956 and 1966 reductions.  The Board explained that the regulation applies only when VA considers reducing a rating at a time when the claimant was actually receiving benefits under a 100 percent disability rating, not when a claimant was, like the veteran, legally entitled to benefits under a 100 percent rating but not receiving them because of CUE.  Thus, the Board considered it unnecessary to conduct an analysis of whether the “material improvement” standard from those regulations was met at the time of the 1956 and 1966 rating reductions.  The Veterans Court affirmed the Board’s decision.

The Federal Circuit held that, under Pirkl I and governing law, implementing the 2006 CUE decision requires renewed consideration of the 1956 and 1966 decisions to ensure that the veteran is restored to the ratings he would have had over time, upon application of the governing legal standards, in the absence of a now-revised 1953 reduction of his disability rating from 100 to 70 percent.

The Federal Circuit held that the remedy required under 38 U.S.C. 5109A(b) is to give the veteran the ratings he would have had, over time, if he had retained his 100 percent rating in 1953, which requires examination of the 1956 and 1966 decisions and proceedings to determine what they do or not show about when, if ever, the veteran would have lost his 100 percent rating under the legal standards governing reduction of 100 percent ratings – 38 C.F.R. 3.170 (1949) and 38 C.F.R. 3.343 – had they been applied.

The Federal Circuit rejected the government’s argument that, under Reizenstein, the regulations governing reduction of 100 percent evaluations did not apply to the veteran as of 1956.  The Federal Circuit observed that Reizenstein involved a single VA decision that found, in the past period at issue, the veteran’s disability varied in degree, with an intermediate portion of the period involving total disability.  With a retrospective staged rating in Reizenstein, the regulatory language did not supply an unambiguous answer that it clearly covered a single VA decision situation, whereas it does in this case in which a 100 percent rating was assigned in one decision on an open-ended prospective basis and is superseded by a later decision that assigns a lower evaluation.  The Federal Circuit concluded that the regulation was applicable to the veteran’s situation in 1956, and a reduction, to be lawful, had to meet the regulation’s requirements.

The Federal Circuit found that the Board had not yet conducted the required remedial inquiry to determine whether, and by how much, the veteran’s 100 percent rating would have been reduced in 1956 had the regulation for reductions of 100 percent ratings been applied and then, if the 100 percent rating remained after 1956, whether (and by how much) the 100 percent rating would have been reduced in 1966 had the regulation been applied.  The inquiry involves consideration of whether the medical examinations in 1956 and 1966 would have supported the findings needed under the regulations had it been applied.

The Federal Circuit rejected the government’s argument that corrective relief under section 5109A(b) is curtailed because the CUE is propagated into later decisions, simply because rejections of distinct challenges to the later decisions are final.  The Federal Circuit explained that this entitlement is part of the statutory CUE exception to finality; it is not some additional exception to finality.

The Federal Circuit found no bases requiring the veteran to challenge the 1956 and 1966 decisions not only on the grounds asserted, but also on the grounds that they started with incorrect premises (too low a rating) because of the 1953 error separately being challenged, or establishing that the veteran had to try to keep alive the separate CUE challenged to the 1956 and 1966 decisions while the CUE challenge to the 1953 decision was being considered.

The Federal Circuit reversed the Veterans Court’s decision and remanded for that court to remand to the Board for further proceedings consistent with its opinion.
