Decision Assessment Document
 Guillory v. Shinseki, Jan 3, 2012, 669 F.3d 1314
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit, interpreting the statutes and regulations in effect in 1967, held that the Veterans Court did not err in holding that the loss of use of a foot is subsumed within, and not separate and distinct from, the loss of use of a leg.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who received multiple injuries from a gunshot wound in Vietnam, was granted a combined 100 percent disability evaluation for chronic brain syndrome associated with trauma, with triplegia, in a June 1967 regional office (RO) decision.  Special monthly compensation (SMC) at the (l) ½ rate for loss of use of one lower extremity and SMC at the (k) rate for loss of use of a hand were assigned.  The law at this time required a rating of at least “(o)” to be entitled to the aid and attendance (A&A) allowance.  In 1970, based on clear and unmistakable error (CUE), the RO retroactively increased the (l)½ rate to the (m) rate for loss of use of both lower extremities.  The RO, in 1992, granted service connection for a seizure disorder with a 100 percent rating.  The veteran’s SMC rate was ultimately increased to (o) plus (k), entitling him to (A&A) at the (r) rate, which was awarded May 199, the date he was diagnosed with the seizure disorder.

In September 2001, the veteran filed a CUE motion, seeking a higher rate of SMC under subsection (p) plus A&A at the time of the original rating in 1967 based on the failure to rate and compensate him for the loss of use of hips, thighs, and buttocks and error in the assignment of a 70 percent rating for loss of use of the right hand.  He also argued for an earlier effective date for compensation for his seizure disorder, which would in turn entitle him to an earlier effective date for A&A.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in a 2006 decision following a Veterans Court remand, determined that entitlement to A&A was not shown prior to May 1991, as no CUE was found in denying an earlier effective date for the seizure disorder.  The hips, thighs, and buttocks claims were not specifically addressed.

The Veterans Court, in 2008, affirmed the Board’s decision that the March 1992 RO decision did not contain CUE as to the seizure disorder.  The Veterans Court held that it did not have jurisdiction to review the issues raised by the veteran regarding loss of trunk through the knee, including the buttocks, because those were not the subject of the Board decision currently on appeal.

The Federal Circuit, in 2010, dismissed the appeal insofar as it continued to challenge the effective date for the seizure disorder.  However, it held that the Veterans Court dismissal of the veteran’s claims regarding the loss of use of the trunk, knees, and buttocks was erroneous and remanded for consideration on the merits.  On remand, the Veterans Court found that the Board had accounted for the veteran’s complaints regarding the loss of use of his right and left trunk through the knees, including the buttocks, when it evaluated his condition under subsection (m).  The Veterans Court held that the veteran’s arguments amounted to a disagreement with how the facts were weighed by the RO; thus, the Board adequately supported its finding of no CUE in previous RO decisions with its reasons or bases.

The veteran again appealed to the Federal Circuit, arguing that he met the statutory requirements for A&A in 1967; thus, the initial 1967 RO decision was the product of CUE.  The Federal Circuit determined that the issue turned on whether the restriction in 38 U.S.C. § 314 (o) (the version of section 1114 in effect at the time) that “ no condition be considered twice” should be interpreted to allow a veteran to recover separately for loss of use of his legs and the loss of use of his feet.  The Federal Circuit also noted that [38 C.F.R. § 3.350(e)(2)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=3a77cec38b5c011cb54976b1ff82c1c0&node=se38.1.3_1350&rgn=div8) (1967) stated “determinations must be based upon separate and distinct disabilities.”

The Federal Circuit held that “the Veterans Court did not err in holding that the loss of use of a foot is subsumed within, and not separate and distinct form, the loss of use of a leg.”  The Federal Circuit reasoned that a contrary interpretation would allow any veteran who anatomically lost both of his legs under subsection (n) to automatically qualify for a higher rate under subsection (o) because he necessarily would also have anatomically lost both of his feet.  This would render part of subsection (n) meaningless because veterans who anatomically lost both legs would always be covered by subsection (o).  The Court also noted that [38 C.F.R. § 4.26](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=25d37dcf687cb4d66b0f27417ebf23e7&node=se38.1.4_126&rgn=div8) (1967), in discussing the bilateral factor for combining disabilities, stated:  “The use of the term ... ‘legs’ is not intended to distinguish between the … thigh, leg, and foot, but relates to the … lower extremities as a whole.  The Court, in holding that loss of use of a leg subsumes loss of use of the foot, concluded that the distinction in § 4.26 applied in this instance.

The Federal Circuit affirmed the Veterans Court decision.
