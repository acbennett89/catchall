Decision Assessment Document
Felden v. West, Sep 22, 1998, 11 Vet.App. 427 (1998)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that entitlement to a temporary total disability rating for convalescence requires that a report rendered near the time of a hospital discharge or an outpatient release indicate that a surgical procedure had been performed that would require at least one month for the veteran to return to a healthy state and that it would be proper for a later medical opinion, issued close in time to the discharge or release, to explain how long a period of convalescence would be needed.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into guidance contained in Manual M21-1, IV.ii.2.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran served on active duty in the U.S. Army  from October 1968 to October 1970.  While in Vietnam, he suffered a hyperextension injury to the right little finger.  Service-connection (SC) was established for residuals of right little finger injury, rated 10 percent disabling.  In September 1992, the appellant fractured the proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joint of the SC right little finger. On February 4, 1993, an outpatient arthroplasty of the PIP joint of the right little finger was performed and fixation pins were placed in the finger.   On February 5, 1993, the veteran was given a basket splint for additional protection. On February 12, 1993, the physician stated the appellant would not be able to return to work for at least eight weeks, until approximately April 5, 1993.  VA examination in June 1993 noted arthrodesis PIP, right little finger, with flexor contracture of the distal interphalangeal (DIP) joint at 30 degrees.  The examiner noted the multiple surgeries of tendon repair were unsuccessful thus far.  A second surgery on the right little finger was performed in July 1993.  In September 1993, the VA regional office denied the appellant’s claim for Para. 30 benefits. The appellant testified in an April 1994 hearing that the physician had told him not to do anything “because anything I would do would destruct [sic] his surgery.”  The veteran said he was not working at the time of his February 1993 surgery and was receiving workers’ compensation for a foot injury.  The Hearing Officer confirmed the denial of Para. 30 benefits.  In November 1994, the VARO received a letter from a physician explaining that the veteran had been totally disabled since November 1991 due to severe and chronic symptoms relating to his back, ankle, and shoulder, stating the appellant’s condition is permanent with no hope for recovery  In August 1996, the appellant testified before a member of the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA).  On appeal to BVA, the Board determined, on its merits, that the veteran was not entitled to Para. 30 for the periods after the February or July 1993 surgeries, finding that treatment in February and July did not necessitate at least one month of convalescence.

The Court noted that 38 CFR 4.30(a)(1) provides that a total disability rating will be assigned without regard to other provisions of the Rating Schedule for surgery for a service-connected disability necessitating at least one month of convalescence.  The Secretary claimed that convalescence is the amount of time needed at home to recover.  However, the Court noted that the term convalescence does not necessarily entail in-home recovery.  The veteran had two surgeries performed on his right little finger.  Nine days after the first surgery, the doctor wrote a letter stating the veteran would not be able to return to work for eight weeks.  In November 1993, the physician wrote a letter stating the veteran was totally disabled from February to August 1993 because of treatment related to his finger surgeries.  BVA rejected the physician’s opinion that the veteran could not return to work for eight weeks.  With respect to the timing of the physician’s report, the Court held that entitlement to a temporary total disability rating for convalescence requires that a report rendered near the time of a hospital discharge or an outpatient release indicate that a surgical procedure had been performed that would require at least one month for the veteran to return to a healthy state and that it would be proper for a later medical opinion, issued close in time to the discharge or release, to explain how long a period of convalescence would be needed.  The Court further held that BVA’s determination about the probativeness of the physician’s opinion regarding the eight week period was clearly erroneous.  It was impermissible for BVA to form its own conclusion that the veteran’s surgery did not necessitate at least a month of convalescence without independent supporting medical evidence.  The evidence clearly supported an award of Para. 30 benefits for the applicable period following the veteran’s first surgery.  The Court also found the evidence uncontroverted that the veteran was entitled to Para. 30 benefits for a period following the second surgery.  The Court reversed BVA’s decision denying Para. 30 benefits for the periods February 2, 1993, to May 31, 1993, and July 14, through August 31, 1993.  The issue of whether the appellant is entitled to a discretionary extension of the first Para. 30 benefit beyond May 31, 1993, was remanded to BVA for further proceedings consistent with the Court’s opinion.
