Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 03-06, Jun 23, 2006, Multiple Ratings under Former 38 CFR 4.71a, Diagnostic Code 5285 (2003)
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Former 38 C.F.R. § 4.71a, Diagnostic Code (DC) 5285 (2003), authorizes no more than a single 10-percent increase for demonstrable deformity in the spinal segment (cervical, dorsal, or lumbar) that is the subject of the rating.  If the spine fracture residuals cause limited motion to more than one spinal segment for which service connection is in effect, DC 5285 authorizes a 10-percent increase to the rating assigned to each segment of the spine containing at least one demonstrable deformed vertebral body.

**Impact on VBA**

No new impact warranting Manual revision.  Current regulatory authority and Manual provisions adequately provide for adherence to this opinion.

**Summary on facts and findings**

This opinion was requested in connection with the case of a veteran who suffered an in-service injury to the spine and demonstrated limited spine mobility.  There was no spinal cord involvement and the veteran was not required to wear a neck brace; thus, a 100 or 60-percent rating under DC 5285 was precluded and the veteran was rated in accordance with limited motion.  At least two of the veteran’s lumbar vertebrae appeared to be demonstrably deformed.  The opinion request asked whether the rating assigned in accordance with definite limited motion may be increased by 10 percent for each demonstrably deformed vertebral body or is subject to only one 10-percent increase irrespective of the number of deformed vertebral bodies.

Conceding the ambiguity of the regulation and lack of any contemporaneous documents explaining VA’s intent when it promulgated the 10-percent provision regarding vertebral deformity, General Counsel referred to a November 1955 letter from the Deputy Administrator for Veterans Benefits, which stated that, “under Diagnostic Code 5285, not more than one 10 percent will be allowed for each segment of the spine involved.”  Also, a September 1959 letter from the Acting Assistant Deputy Administrator for Compensation and Pension interpreted the rating schedule that “an additional 10% may be allowed for demonstrable deformity or deformities in each of the segments involved but not more than 10% for each segment.”

General Counsel concluded that these letters reflected the view that, if VA assigns separate rating under DC 5290 through 5292 for limitation of motion in more than one of the three spinal segments, the rating for each segment may be increased by 10 percent, and no more, if there is definite deformity of a vertebral body or bodies in that segment.

VBA’s apparent historical interpretation of DC 5285 was viewed by General Counsel as reasonable and consistent with the language of the provision.  Multiple applications of the 10-percent provision to a single spinal segment would create odd and seemingly unintended disparities in ratings under DC 5285 such as a veteran rated on limitation of motion under DCs 5290-92, which authorize no higher than 40 percent based on loss of motion, receiving a higher evaluation than a veteran whose spine fracture residuals meets the schedular criteria established for the 60-percent rating, even if both veterans had an equal number of demonstrably deformed vertebral bodies.  General Counsel also noted that if VA had intended for deformed vertebral bodies to be such a significant component of the disability rating, the DC 5285 schedular criteria likely would have specifically included deformed vertebral bodies as primary rating criterion, rather than merely allowing the condition to be considered in connection with a limitation of motion disability.

Since VA has historically interpreted the 10-percent provision in DC 5285 in a reasonable manner consistent with the language of the provision and with the terms and purpose of the rating schedule as a whole, General Counsel indicated that the interpretation was valid and authoritative and concluded that DC 5285 authorizes no more than one 10-percent increase for demonstrable deformity of a vertebral body or bodies in each spinal segment.

Although not specifically requested in the opinion request, General Counsel also concluded that the 10-percent provisions could be applied more than once if the veteran suffered from limited motion and vertebral deformity in more than one segment of the spine as the rating schedule provides separate ratings for the cervical, lumbar, and dorsal segments of the spine and contemplates multiple ratings for a limitation of motion disability if more than the first or last vertebrae of an adjacent segment is affected.
