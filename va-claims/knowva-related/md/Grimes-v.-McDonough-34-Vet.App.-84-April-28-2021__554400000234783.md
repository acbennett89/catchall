**Decision Assessment Document**

***Grimes v. McDonough,*** **34 Vet.App. 84 (April 28, 2021)**

**U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)**

**What this case is about:** VA must sympathetically construe claims to include any issue reasonably raised.

**Impact on VBA:** The M21-1 Adjudication Procedures Manual will be updated to reference this case.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:** Veteran served on active duty in the U.S. Air Force from May 1966 to May 1970. In July 2011, he filed a claim for service connection for “loss of hearing, earaches, sinus pressure, tinnitus.” He stated he had no hearing protection in service, and his jaw, sinus issues and hearing were “never the same” after an in-service wisdom tooth extraction.

During his VA audiology examination, he reported that he had to turn up the volume on devices and had difficulty hearing on the telephone. His tinnitus interfered with sleep and daily activities. The Regional Office (RO) asked Veteran to clarify his claims in October 2012. Veteran stated he wished to be considered for only hearing loss, tinnitus and sinus pressure and aches. In October2012 the RO granted service connection for hearing loss and tinnitus and denied service connection for sinusitis. The Veteran appealed the sinusitis denial and the non-compensable evaluation assigned for his hearing loss.

In June 2013, the RO reviewed the claim, and determined that based on Veteran’s clarification of claims, he was also claiming headaches, earaches and ear blockage. Those claims were denied in October 2013.

In September 2013, the RO issued a statement of the case (SOC) denying the sinusitis claim and continuing the non-compensable evaluation of hearing loss. Veteran timely appealed to the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA).

In December 2015, Veteran claimed tinnitus, hearing loss and residuals of an ear cyst.  The RO’s January 2016 rating decision confirmed and continued the hearing loss and tinnitus evaluations and denied residuals of the ear cyst. The following month, Veteran submitted a statement stating he had been diagnosed with hyperacusis. After an audiology exam in which Veteran reported functional impairments including ear pain and low tolerance to loud sounds, in June of 2016 the RO confirmed and continued the hearing loss evaluation without addressing hyperacusis.

Veteran appealed, stating that in addition to hearing loss, he had hyperacusis, which was a condition characterized by an increased sensitivity to certain frequency and volume ranges of sound and is related to exposure to loud acoustic trauma.

Veteran appeared before the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA) in October 2016, at which the presiding BVA member stated that Veteran’s issues were evaluation of bilateral hearing loss and service connection for sinus issues. Veteran asserted he also claimed hyperacusis, but BVA noted this had not been addressed by the RO. In December 2017, BVA denied a compensable evaluation for bilateral hearing loss and remanded for further development a claim for a sinus disability. BVA also required the RO to develop and adjudicate Veteran’s hyperacusis claim. Veteran timely appealed to the Court.

The Court noted that separate diagnoses with separate factual bases should generally be treated as separate claims. However, a claim for service connection may be expanded beyond a Veteran's lay description of a condition to include any disability that is reasonably be encompassed by several factors including: the description of the claim; the symptoms Veteran describes; and any information Veteran submits. The VA is not relieved of the duty to adjudicate the claim simply because Veteran may be wrong about the claimed condition’s nature.

The Court concluded that, when read sympathetically, Veteran sufficiently raised the issue of hyperacusis during his initial July 2011 claim for hearing loss and sinusitis. The Court noted Veteran repeatedly claimed and referred to “earaches,” and other indicia of hyperacusis throughout his initial claim, notice of disagreement and substantive appeal. The Court found that because Veteran did not file a claim for only particular diagnoses, but also stated his condition included hearing problems including hyperacusis, the claim had to be sympathetically read to include hyperacusis. Moreover, once Veteran initiated an appeal that included the issue of entitlement to compensation for hyperacusis, the RO could not exclude hyperacusis from the appeal by reclassifying it as a new claim and then denying it.

The Court referenced controlling precedent specifying that when a Veteran seeks benefits for a specific condition and is later diagnosed with a potentially related condition while the claim is being processed, VA may not reflexivelyt treat the different condition as a separate claim. Because Veteran was diagnosed with hyperacusis during the initial processing of his hearing loss and sinus claims, and before adjudication of either, VA was required to sympathetically read the initial claim and subsequent appeal as including hyperacusis as an issue reasonably raised therein. The Court held that a claim for service connection may encompass a related condition that is initially referenced by the claimant but not diagnosed until later in the appeal stream, regardless of whether the claim is initially granted or denied by the RO.
