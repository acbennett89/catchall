**Subject**

Guidance for implementing United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit Court (Court) decision on the Veterans Appeals Improvement and Modernization Act of 2017 (AMA), *Military-Veterans Advocacy (MVA) v. Secretary of Veterans Affairs, et al*.

**Purpose**

This letter provides information and claims processing guidance to regional offices (ROs) and decision review operations centers (DROCs) related to the Court holding that invalidated three regulations — [38 C.F.R. § 3.155](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3#3.155), [38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3#3.2500), and [38 C.F.R. § 14.636(c)(1)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-14#14.636), as they relate to supplemental claims.

Upon issuance of this letter, the October 7, 2021 interim guidance and frequently asked questions (FAQs) issued on November 5, 2021 are rescinded. Guidance in this letter should be applied when reviewing supplemental claims. Regulatory updates are forthcoming.

**Background**

In 2017, Congress enacted AMA under Public Law 115-55 to replace the existing VA appeals system. VA implemented the law through regulation. Following implementation, four separate petitions were filed that collectively raised 13 rulemaking challenges to these regulations.

On July 30, 2021, the Court issued a decision in [*Military-Veterans Advocacy v. Secretary of Veterans Affairs, et al*](https://cafc.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/opinions-orders/19-1600.OPINION.7-30-2021_1811957.pdf) addressing the regulations VA issued to implement AMA. Of the 13 rulemaking challenges, 10 were dismissed due to lack of standing. The remaining three were given merit and found to have standing, resulting in this precedential opinion. The three regulations issued by VA that were invalidated as inconsistent with the text of AMA were the following:

* [38 C.F.R. § 3.155](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3#3.155), which excluded supplemental claims from the intent to file (ITF) framework,
* [38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3#3.2500), which barred the filing of a supplemental claim when adjudication of the same claim is pending before a federal court, and
* [38 C.F.R. § 14.636(c)(1)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-14#14.636), which limited when a claimant’s representative may charge fees for work on supplemental claims.

**Impacted Regulations**

***38 C.F.R. § 3.155 – How to file a claim***

**Ruling****:** The Court invalidated the portion of the introductory language to [38 C.F.R. § 3.155](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3#3.155), which states “[t]he provisions of this section are applicable to all claims governed by part 3, with the exception that paragraph (b) of this section, regarding intent to file a claim, does not apply to supplemental claims.”

This means an ITF may now be applied to all supplemental claims, whether filed within one year of a decision or after the continuous pursuit period has endedunder [38 U.S.C. § 5104C(a) and (b)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section5104C&num=0&edition=prelim).

Claims processors must give consideration as to whether an ITF of record applies to a supplemental claim not finally adjudicated on or after July 30, 2021, even if the ITF was filed prior to that date. This includes claims pending a VA decision, or where any continuous pursuit period or judicial appeal period has not expired.

**Impact:** Claims processors must consider whether an ITF of record applies to a qualifying supplemental claim. A ***qualifying supplemental claim*** is a substantially complete supplemental claim for the same benefit type, when the supplemental claim is filed either:

* within one year of a decision, or

* over one year after the decision and the continuous pursuit period has ended.

***Active ITF***

There is no change to 38 C.F.R. § 3.155(b)(6), which states VA will not recognize more than one concurrent ITF for the same benefit. There can only be one *active* ITF for the same benefit. In Section II, General Benefit Election, *VA Form 21-0996*, *Intent to File a Claim for Compensation and/or Pension, or Survivors Pension and/or DIC*, includes three options: Compensation, Pension, or Survivors Pension and/or Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC).

Claims processors must continue to apply an active ITF to the next applicable claim. The origination source of the ITF does not dictate what claim form must follow. When the ITF tab in VBMS reflects Compensation as the benefit sought, it applies to the next received qualifying claim for that benefit, whether that claim is received on a *VA Form 21-526EZ* or on a *VA Form 20-0995*.

For information on determining active status of an ITF, see [M21-1, II.iii.2.A.1.d.](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174873/M21-1-Part-II-Subpart-iii-Chapter-2-Section-A-Intent-to-File-ITF#1) and Example 1 in [Appendix A](#App A).The procedural guidance for determining the active status of an ITF and guidance that an ITF may be applied to multiple claims for the same benefit received on the same day has not changed.See Example 2 in [Appendix A](#App A) and [M21-1, II.iii.2.A.1.h.](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174873/M21-1-Part-II-Subpart-iii-Chapter-2-Section-A-Intent-to-File-ITF#1) for information about applying an ITF to multiple claims received on the same day.

***VA.gov and System Functionality***

New system functionality in VA.gov allows a claimant to submit a supplemental claim for disability compensation. Under [38 C.F.R. § 3.155(b)(1)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3#3.155), an ITF for a claim may be submitted through an electronic application when the claimant initiates and saves a claim, prior to filing a complete claim. This means VA.gov will auto-create an ITF when a *VA Form 20-0995*, *Decision Review Request: Supplemental Claim,* is initiated and saved, consistent with how VA.gov currently generates an ITF when a *VA Form 21-526EZ*, *Application for Disability Compensation and Related Compensation Benefits,* is initiated.

**Important:** When an active ITF exists for Compensation, VA.gov will record the subsequent ITF as duplicate in VBMS. Claims processors must closely review the Intent to File tab in VBMS when assigning an effective date.

The system workaround guidance regarding ITF and end product (EP) 040s received within a year of the prior decision included in the October 7, 2021 interim guidance is no longer required as VBMS has been updated. When there is an active ITF, the first claim established with the same benefit type will consume the ITF. This changes the status to *“Claim Received”* in a batch update at the end of the day. An EP 040 is one of the eligible end products to consume active ITFs.

***Continuous Pursuit***

An ITF filed within one year of notification of a VA decision may operate to maintain continuous pursuit if the ITF is followed by a complete supplemental claim, even if the supplemental claim is filed after the one-year period following notice of a decision (see Example 3 in [Appendix A](#App A)). However, this ruling ***does not*** extend the evidentiary submission timeframes for issues like dependency and income as outlined in [38 C.F.R. §3.401(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFR429f47d98271c40/section-3.401) and [38 C.F.R. § 3.660](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFR24d035b1eee2d6f/section-3.660).

***Effective Dates***

Effective date rules have not changed and must still be assigned in accordance with 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.155, 3.400 and 3.2500. This means claims processors must now consider whether an ITF of record applies to a supplemental claim when determining an effective date. See Example 5 in [Appendix A](#App A).

***Applicability***

When reviewing a claim under an EP 040, *Supplemental Claim*, the ITF applies even if the contention is a dependency or ancillary issue. As this Court decision is specific to supplemental claims, ITFs still do ***not*** apply to initial dependency claims, an initial claim for an ancillary benefit, requests for higher-level review, appeals to the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (BVA), duplicate claims and requests for review based on clear and unmistakable error. See [M21-1, II.iii.2.A.1.l.](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174873/M21-1-Part-II-Subpart-iii-Chapter-2-Section-A-Intent-to-File-ITF#1) and Example 4 in [Appendix A](#App A).

***Previously Decided Claims***

Misapplication of the law by claims processors may have occurred in decisions completed on or after July 30, 2021. As a result, claims processors may discover decisions completed on or after July 30, 2021 that applied an ITF to a claim that was received ***after*** a valid supplemental claim was received. If the ITF was not applied to the first application for the same benefit received within one year of the ITF, an administrative error may have occurred. If the ITF was improperly applied to a different claim, any effective date assigned in that decision must also be reviewed. Decisions rendered on both claims will need to be reviewed for corrective action under § 3.105(a). Follow the instructions in  [M21-1, X.ii.5.C.](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000177971/M21-1-Part-X-Subpart-ii-Chapter-5-Section-A-Revision-Due-to-Clear-and-Unmistakable-Error-CUE?query=clear%20and%20unmistakable%20error) and [M21-1, VI.iii.2.B.3.e.](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000179497/M21-1-Part-VI-Subpart-iii-Chapter-2-Section-B-Fiscal-Authorization-FISA#3) for information on eliminating an overpayment from correction of an administrative error. See Example 6 in [Appendix A](#App A).

***38 C.F.R. § 3.2500 – Review of decisions***

**Ruling:** The Court invalidated the last sentence of [38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3#3.2500), “While the adjudication of a specific benefit is pending on appeal before a federal court, a claimant may not file for administrative review of the claim under any of options listed in paragraph (a) of this section.” This prohibited the filing of a supplemental claim when adjudication of the same issue was pending before a federal court.

This means, as of July 30, 2021, VA must accept a supplemental claim for an issue that is also pending before the Court. This includes cases before the U.S. District Courts, the U.S. Courts of Appeals for Veterans Claims (CAVC), and the U.S. Supreme Court. BVA does ***not*** fall under this definition of a federal court and is excluded from this ruling.

The ruling applies to any issue or claim not finally adjudicated prior to July 30, 2021. This includes a claim either pending a VA rating decision or where any continuous pursuit period or judicial appeal period has not expired. Assuming there is no other basis for rejection, the supplemental claim must be treated as properly filed on the date of receipt.

**Impact:** To allow timely consideration of new and relevant evidence, claims processors must accept a supplemental claim filed on or after July 30, 2021 even when the same issue has been appealed and is pending before a federal court at the same time. This includes cases before the U.S. District Courts, the U.S. Courts of Appeals for Veterans Claims, and the U.S. Supreme Court.

BVA does not fall under this definition of a federal court. The existing procedural guidance in [M21-1, II.i.2.A.3.a-c.](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174858/M21-1-Part-II-Subpart-i-Chapter-2-Section-A-Process-Overview-for-Screening-Mail#3) still applies when determining if a claimed issue is duplicative of a legacy or BVA appeal.

Claims processors must accept a supplemental claim in the following instances:

* There is evidence submitted with the claim, or is otherwise of record, that the same issue on the supplemental claim is appealed and pending before a federal court, and
* The application for benefits is complete.

Claims processors must not delay the decision on a supplemental claim based upon the issue pending before a federal court. When the federal court renders a decision on the same issue that is also pending under a supplemental claim, implement the federal court decision under existing guidance. The federal court decision then becomes part of the record to consider with the new and relevant evidence submitted with supplemental claim. The new and relevant evidence was not considered under the federal court’s closed evidentiary record, so the supplemental claim will require re-adjudication to consider all evidence of record.

***Continuous Pursuit***

When the same issue is pending before the federal court, the supplemental claim must be treated as filed within the continuous pursuit period. This impacts the application of an earlier effective date to an award. [38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3#3.2500), continuously pursued issues, addresses how a claim or issue may be continuously pursued. One of the ways to maintain continuous pursuit is addressed in § 3.2500(c)(3). It states, “Following notice of a decision on an appeal to the Board of Veterans’ Appeals, the claimant may file a supplemental claim or file a notice of appeal to the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims.” When a claimant files an appeal with CAVC, continuous pursuit is extended for the issues involved until one year following the CAVC decision. The requirement to file a claim to extend continuous pursuit for that issue is not triggered until CAVC issues a decision.

This means an issue is in a status of continuous pursuit while the judicial appeal is pending with a federal court. The issue is in continuous pursuit status by virtue of the judicial appeal when a supplemental claim is filed during that time for the same issue, even when it is beyond one year following the BVA decision. The earliest effective date under continuous pursuit would apply to any award. See Example 7 in [Appendix A](#App A).

***Previously Decided Claims***

If a claims processor discovers a supplemental claim was rejected based upon the finding that the same issue was appealed and pending before a federal court on or after July 30, 2021, corrective action is needed. Claims processors should do the following:

* Establish the appropriate EP in accordance with [M21-4, Appendix B](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000011474/Appendix-B.-End-Product-(EP)-Codes) to control the claim, and
* Follow the guidance regarding notification requirements in [M21-1, II.iii.1.C.2.](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174872/M21-1-Part-II-Subpart-iii-Chapter-1-Section-C-Screening-Applications-for-Substantial-Completeness-and-Notification-Requirements#2) and take the next appropriate action to progress the claim.

***38 C.F.R. § 14.636 - Payment of fees for representation by agents and attorneys in proceedings before Agencies of Original Jurisdiction and before the Board of Veterans’ Appeals***

**Ruling:** The Court invalidated [38 C.F.R. § 14.636(c)(1)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-14#14.636), which treated decisions on various types of claims as an initial decision in the case and made attorneys and agents ineligible for fees based on those decisions. The Court disagreed and explained that supplemental claims filed after the one-year continuous pursuit period are, for fee purposes, part of the same case as earlier proceedings adjudicating the same issue.

**Impact:** The decision has been implemented in M21-5. Agent and Attorney Fee Coordinator (AAFC) duties have not changed. Follow the existing procedures in M21-5, Chapter 8, Section A, and review all claim forms submitted under AMA with a fee agreement. This includes supplemental claims filed more than one year after the prior decision on the same issue.

**Appendix A: Examples for Implementing *Military-Veterans Advocacy (MVA) v. Secretary of Veterans Affairs, et al***

***38 CFR § 3.155 – How to file a claim***

**Example 1**

Facts:

* An intent to file (ITF) is submitted on January 5, 2023; the VA Form 21-0966, *Intent to File a Claim for Compensation and/or Pension, or Survivors Pension and/or DIC* (VA Form 21-0966),indicates an intent to file for compensation benefits.
* On February 11, 2023, the next compensation claim received is on *VA Form 20-0995,* *Decision Review Request: Supplemental Claim* (VA Form 20-0995).
* A new claim submitted on *VA Form 21-526EZ*, *Application for Disability Compensation and Related Compensation Benefits* (VA Form 21-526EZ) is received on February 23, 2023.
* On March 9, 2023, the supplemental claim is addressed in a rating decision.

Analysis: The rating decision completed on March 9, 2023 that addresses the February 11, 2023 supplemental claim should apply the January 5, 2023 ITF. The ITF would not apply to the new claim submitted on February 23, 2023, as the supplemental claim was received first.

**Example 2**

Facts:

* On December 20, 2022, a claimant calls into the National Contact Center and communicates their intent to file a compensation claim.

* The VA employee completes, signs and submits VA Form 21-0966 on the claimant’s behalf.
* On January 2, 2023, two compensation claims are received, a VA Form 21-526EZ and a VA Form 20-0995.

Analysis: The December 20, 2022 ITF may be applied to both of the January 2, 2023 compensation claims since they were received on the same day.

**Example 3**

Facts:

* A new claim for tinnitus is received on February 8, 2022 on VA Form 21-526EZ.

* The Veteran fails to report to the exam, and there is no medical evidence of record showing a diagnosis of the claimed condition.
* Rating decision dated June 4, 2022 denies entitlement to service connection for tinnitus due to no diagnosis.
* On September 22, 2022, an intent to file for compensation on VA Form 21-0966 is received.
* On September 19, 2023, VA Form 20-0995 for tinnitus is received with medical evidence dated January 12, 2022, which shows a diagnosis of tinnitus.

Analysis: The VA Form 20-0995 received September 19, 2023, is the next received compensation claim, so the September 22, 2022 ITF applies. A February 8, 2022 effective date for tinnitus may be established based on continuous pursuit under 38 CFR 3.2500(h)(1) if service connection is otherwise warranted.

**Example 4**

Facts:

* The Veteran receives a notification letter on January 8, 2021 addressing a claim that increased their combined service-connected disability evaluation to 30%.

* On February 3, 2021, the Veteran submits VA Form 21-686c to add their spouse as a dependent but required information is missing. VA takes action to develop for the information but is unsuccessful.
* The dependency claim is denied on August 24, 2021.
* On September 2, 2022, the Veteran submits an ITF for compensation.
* The next received claim for the same benefit identified on the ITF is a supplemental claim that lists dependency as the issue. Date of receipt of claim is November 5, 2022. The supplemental claim also includes the needed dependency information.

Analysis: The September 2, 2022 ITF applies to the supplemental dependency claim and must be considered as a possible effective date, in conjunction with the information in [M21-1, VII.i.1.C.2](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000176615/M21-1,-Part-VII,-Subpart-i,-Chapter-1,-Section-C---Adjusting-Awards-for-Dependents#2), when determining the date for adding the spouse to the award.

**Example 5**

Facts:

* The Veteran submits an ITF for compensation on March 4, 2020.

* A claim for an increase in the right knee condition is received on April 4, 2020.
* On September 1, 2020 a rating decision that confirms and continues the 10% evaluation for the right knee condition is completed. The Veteran is notified of the decision on the same day.
* A new ITF for compensation is received on August 31, 2021.
* It is applied to the next qualifying claim, VA Form 20-0995 received August 15, 2022. The supplemental claim lists disagreement with the assigned evaluation for the right knee condition and includes new and relevant evidence.
* An examination is completed on October 10, 2022, which contains findings that support a 20% evaluation.
* The October 10, 2022 exam is the earliest date objective evidence supports a higher evaluation.

Analysis: An effective date of October 10, 2022 would be warranted based upon date entitlement arose under 38 CFR § 3.2500(h). An effective date for a continuously pursued claim will be fixed in accordance with the date of receipt of the initial claim or the date entitlement arose, whichever is later.

**Example 6**

Facts:

* An ITF for compensation is submitted on May 15, 2021.

* On July 1, 2021, a supplemental claim for service connection for a spine condition is received with new and relevant evidence.
* The spine condition was previously denied over a year ago, in December of 2018.
* On July 20, 2021, a new claim for posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is received.
* Rating decision dated September 1, 2021 applies the ITF to the new claim, granting PTSD with a 30 percent evaluation effective May 15, 2021, date of receipt of the ITF.
* On October 15, 2021, a rating decision grants entitlement to the spine condition with an effective date of July 1, 2021, date of receipt of claim.
* Upon promulgation, the assigned effective date for the spine condition is questioned, so the rating is returned.

Analysis: The claims processor must follow clear and unmistakable error procedures to correct the effective date assigned for PTSD in the September 1, 2021 rating decision, as the ITF was improperly applied. The October 15, 2021 rating decision granting compensation for the spine condition must also be reviewed, as the May 15, 2021 ITF would be absorbed by the July 1, 2021 supplemental claim. As this is an administrative error, follow the procedures in [M21-1, VI.iii.2.B.3.e](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000179497/M21-1-Part-VI-Subpart-iii-Chapter-2-Section-B-Fiscal-Authorization-FISA#3) to ensure no debt is created for the Veteran.

***38 CFR § 3.2500 – Review of decisions***

**Example 7**

Facts:

* A new claim for service connection for migraines is filed on November 2, 2020.

* Service treatment records are silent for migraine complaints, so the January 15, 2021 rating decision denies entitlement to service-connected compensation.
* The Veteran files VA Form 10182, *Decision Review Request: Board Appeal*, on March 17, 2021.
* BVA upholds the denial in their decision issued on April 1, 2022.
* The Veteran appeals this decision to CAVC on May 15, 2022.
* While the issue is still pending before the Court, the Veteran submits a supplemental claim for migraines on October 12, 2022. New and relevant evidence is submitted, as the Veteran located the headache journal kept during their military service.
* The claims processor determines an exam with medical opinion is needed to consider the new evidence.
* The examiner provides a positive medical opinion in the October 29, 2022 compensation examination.
* On November 10, 2022, the claim processor initiates a rating decision to grant service connection for migraines.
* VBMS contains a notice of appeal, confirming this issue was appealed to the federal court on May 15, 2022.

Analysis: An effective date of November 2, 2020, the date of receipt of claim, may be established in the November 10, 2022 rating decision under 38 CFR § 3.2500(h)(1).

***38 CFR § 14.636 - Payment of fees for representation by agents and attorneys in proceedings before Agencies of Original Jurisdiction and before the Board of Veterans’ Appeals***

**Example 8**

Facts:

* The Veteran was denied entitlement to service-connected compensation for prostate cancer in rating decision dated April 2, 2021.

* The notification letter was sent on April 4, 2021.
* On August 3, 2022, the Veteran submits a 20-0995 for prostate cancer with new and relevant evidence and a valid fee agreement for an accredited attorney.
* Rating decision dated November 5, 2022 grants service connection for prostate cancer based upon the new evidence, resulting in an award.

Analysis: The attorney may be eligible for payment of fees.

**Appendix B: What changed with the *Military-Veterans Advocacy (MVA) v. Secretary of Veterans Affairs, et al* decision?**

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Pre MVA Decision**  **(prior to July 30, 2021)** | **Post MVA Decision**  **(starting July 30, 2021)** |
| An ITF ***did not*** apply to a supplemental claim. | An ITF ***may*** apply to a supplemental claim. |
| An ITF ***would not*** be considered when determining the effective date for issue on a supplemental claim. | An ITF ***must*** be considered when determining the effective date for an issue on a supplemental claim. |
| A supplemental claim ***could not*** be accepted if the same issue was pending before a federal court. | A supplemental claim ***must*** be accepted if the same issue is pending before a federal court. |
| An ITF ***may not*** be used to extend the period of non-finality for a decided claim. | An ITF ***may*** be used to extend the supplemental claim review period of a previous decision. |
| After VA initially adjudicates an issue, agents and attorneys were ***ineligible*** for fees prior to a supplemental decision. | After VA initially adjudicates an issue, agents and attorney are ***eligible*** for fees with a supplemental decision. |

What did not change?

* There is no change to the procedural guidance that allows an ITF to apply to multiple claims received on the same day.
* ITFs still do not apply to a request for a higher-level review, appeals to BVA, duplicate claims and requests for review based on clear and unmistakable error.
* There is no change to the effective date rules. Continue to apply 38 CFR §§ 3.155, 3.400 and 3.2500.
* There is no change to the procedural guidance when an administrative error is identified.
* A supplemental claim cannot be accepted if the same issue is duplicative of a legacy appeal or BVA appeal.

**Appendix C: Frequently Asked Questions: *Military-Veterans Advocacy (MVA) v. Secretary of Veterans Affairs, et al***

**Question**:Will *VA Form 21-0966*, *Intent to File a Claim for Compensation and/or Pension, or Survivors Pension and/or DIC (VA Form 21-0966)*, be updated to add a box for a supplemental claim?

**Answer**: No change to *VA Form 21-0966* is needed.

**Question**:Does an ITF apply to higher-level reviews and Board of Veterans’ Appeals (BVA)?

**Answer**: ITFs do ***not*** apply to requests for higher-level reviews or appeals to BVA.

**Question**:What if the ITF serves no clear effective date value with application to the *VA Form 20-0995*, such as when the issue has been continuously pursued? Should the ITF apply to the supplemental claim, or can the ITF remain open until receipt of a new claim or supplemental claim that has not been continuously pursued?

**Answer**: The ITF must be applied when a substantially complete application for benefits for the same type of benefit identified on the ITF is received. Regardless of whether the ITF would afford the most favorable effective date option, once an eligible claim is received after an ITF is pending, the ITF status changes to *“Claim Received*” and is no longer eligible for application to other subsequently filed claims. See [M21-1, II.iii.2.A.1.d](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174873/M21-1,-Part-II,-Subpart-iii,-Chapter-2,-Section-A---Intent-to-File-(ITF)) on determining active status of an ITF.

**Question**:  What end product (EP) should be used to correct an improper effective date assigned in a supplemental claim when the date of receipt of the supplemental claim was applied rather than the date of receipt of the ITF?

**Answer**: An EP 930 should be used in most scenarios; however, refer to the information in [M21-1, X.ii.5](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000177971/M21-1-Part-X-Subpart-ii-Chapter-5-Section-A-Revision-Due-to-Clear-and-Unmistakable-Error-CUE?query=clear%20and%20unmistakable%20error). Follow the guidance for processing EP 930s starting on page 73 of the [Caseflow Intake training guide](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/OAR/docs/Intake_Comp_TrainingGuide_v16.pdf).

Per [M21-4, Appendix B](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000011474/Appendix-B.-End-Product-(EP)-Codes), an EP 930 applies to reviews and issues where no other EP is applicable and where the appropriate EP credit has already been taken to include the following: missed issues or prematurely cleared EPs correction of previous erroneous actions identified during quality review.

**Question**: Will a review of all decisions made since July 30, 2021 on EP 040s be conducted? Will a special ad-hoc review team be created for this effective date review?

**Answer**: There is currently no plan to conduct a review of all EP 040s.

**Question**: May an ITF be used to extend the finality of a rating decision? For example, a rating decision is completed on August 1, 2021. On July 31, 2022, an ITF is received. If the ITF is not absorbed by another claim, would the claimant have until July 31, 2023 to submit a *VA Form 20-0995* to continuously pursue that prior claim?

**Answer**: Yes, an ITF may be used to extend the supplemental claim review period of a rating decision.
