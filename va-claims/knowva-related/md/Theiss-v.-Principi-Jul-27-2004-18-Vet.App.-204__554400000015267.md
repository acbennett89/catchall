Decision Assessment Document
 Theiss v. Principi, Jul 27, 2004, 18 Vet.App. 204
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What this case is about:**

In the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA or Board) decision here on appeal, the Board, relying on [VA General Counsel Precedent Opinion 3-98](http://www.va.gov/ogc/docs/1998/prc03-98.doc) (G.C. Prec. 3-98) and the amendment to [38 C.F.R. § 3.57(a)(1)(iii)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=69984c7a14cb472819df194c9682c8c7&node=se38.1.3_157&rgn=div8), found that the Secretary specifically had not approved home schools as "educational institutions" for purposes of section [38 U.S.C. § 101(4)(A)(iii)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/101), therefore, that the veteran’s son, who is more than 18 years old and was being home-schooled is not a "child" as defined by that section and thus also was not a child for purposes of entitlement to additional pension benefits under [38 U.S.C. § 1521(c)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1521).  The CAVC held that the BVA's statement of reasons or bases for its decision was inadequate, vacated the BVA decision, and remanded the matter.  The Court held that the amendment to 38 C.F.R. 3.57(a)(1)(iii) (which excludes home school programs from the definition of “educational institution”) is invalid.  The Court also concluded that G.C. Prec. 3-98, upon which the amendment to the regulation was based, constituted a substantive rule that was not adopted pursuant to notice and comment procedures of the Administrative Procedures Act (APA) and was thus invalid.

**Impact on VBA:**

There are significant changes as a result of this case.  First, the provision of section 3.57(a)(1)(iii) which excludes home school programs is invalid and should no longer be applied.  Second, G.C. Prec. 3-98 is invalid.  Consideration will be given to updating M21-1, Part IV, par. 14.06 to reference this opinion and to provide interim guidance regarding home school programs.  The Office of General Counsel is filing a motion for reconsideration or, in the alternative, for full court review.  This assessment will be updated if a new opinion is issued.

**Summary of the facts and Court's reasons:**

In this case, the Board of Veterans Appeals, relying on [§ 3.57(a)(1)(iii)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=69984c7a14cb472819df194c9682c8c7&node=se38.1.3_157&rgn=div8) and [G.C. Prec. 3-98](http://www.va.gov/ogc/docs/1998/prc03-98.doc), determined that the veteran’s 18-year-old son was not a "child" on the basis that by attending home school he was not in pursuit of a course of instruction at an "approved educational institution" for purposes of [38 U.S. C. § 101(4)(A)(iii)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/101).  We note that VA regulatory history shows that section 3.57(a)(1)(iii) was promulgated to implement G. C. Prec. 3-98.   [See the text of the Court opinion for a full accounting of the reasons that the Court rejected the analysis in the General Counsel opinion.]

The Court noted that G.C. Prec. 3-98, referring to paragraph 14.06 of part IV of the M21-1, stated that a lack of accreditation of a school was a valid basis for VA to disapprove that school as an "educational institution." The G.C. opinion concluded: "We believe that these provisions in M21-1 represent a substantive rule because they establish criteria for entitlement to compensation." The G.C. opinion concluded further that section 104(a), which gives the Secretary authority to "approve or disapprove such educational institutions," would provide a legal basis for this substantive rule.  The opinion recommended that, in accordance with the Administrative Procedure Act, [5 U.S.C. §§ 552(a)(1)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/552) and [553](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/553), and [38 U.S.C. § 501](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/501), VA should promulgate a regulation that would specify the criteria for an educational institution that would satisfy the requirements of sections 101(4)(A)(iii) and 104(a).  The CAVC stated that the amendment to 3.57(a)(1)(iii), however, did not use accreditation as a basis to approve or disapprove a school and did not set forth explicit criteria for an "educational institution" that would satisfy sections 101 and 104.

Further, VA published the amendment as a nonsubstantive (or interpretive) rule, Since it was considered by VA to be nonsubstantive, it was first published as a final rule (rather than first being published as a proposed rule) and was not subjected to the notice and comment provisions of the APA.  The CAVC held that, because the amendment established criteria for entitlement to benefits, i.e., it excluded home-school programs from the definition of "educational institution,", the March 2000 amendment to 38 C.F.R. § 3.57(a)(1)(iii) was, as a matter of law, a substantive change in the law.

As a result, the CAVC held that the amendment to § 3.57(a)(1)(iii) is invalid for failure to comply with the notice-and-comment rulemaking procedures that 5 U.S.C. § 553 requires of such substantive rule changes. Because the Board relied (as it was required to) in part on the amended regulation (38 C.F.R. § 3.57(a)(1)(iii)), the Court found that the Board decision on review was erroneous.

The Court noted that if the Secretary, on remand, seeks to promulgate an amendment to the regulation, the amendment must comply with the notice-and-comment rulemaking procedures, as well as with the provisions of the Regulatory Flexibility Act, [5 U.S.C. §§ 603](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/603)-[604](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/604). The CAVC noted that an attempt to satisfy this Act with a bare certification, such as was presented in the regulatory amendment in this case, would likely fail to satisfy that Act because such a certification must be accompanied by "a statement providing the factual basis for such certification." [Carpenter v. Sec'y of Veterans Affairs, 343 F.3d 1347, 1356 (Fed. Cir. 2003)](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014956/) ([quoting 5 U.S.C. § 605(b)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/605)).
