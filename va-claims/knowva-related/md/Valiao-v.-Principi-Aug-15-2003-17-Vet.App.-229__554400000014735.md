Decision Assessment Document
 Valiao v. Principi, Aug 15, 2003, 17 Vet.App. 229
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court affirmed the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (BVA) decision that the claimant, as the deceased veteran’s brother, was not entitled to dependency and indemnity compensation (DIC).  In this case, the BVA’s failure to adequately discuss VA’s duty to notify (a duty expanded by the VCAA) was nonprejudicial error.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact on VBA.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The claimant, the deceased veteran’s brother, asserted that he was entitled to DIC benefits because the veteran’s parents did not file a claim for benefits during their lifetimes.  He argued that his parent’s entitlement to benefits passed to him after their deaths.  The BVA denied the claim, concluding that, as a matter of law, the claimant was not eligible to receive DIC benefits (due to his status as the brother of the veteran) based on the plain language of [38 U.S.C. § 101(5)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/101) and (14) and [38 CFR 3.5(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=fcebb54ad27b4e59f5ba646e47dee42c&node=se38.1.3_15&rgn=div8).  The Court noted that the BVA properly concluded that DIC entitlement stemming from the veteran’s service terminated at the death of his parents.

In its decision, the BVA discussed the applicability of the VCAA and concluded that the regional office had met or exceeded the expanded notice requirements of the VCAA.  The Court found that the BVA erred by failing to adequately discuss the amended duty to notify.  Specifically, the BVA failed to discuss the requirement that VA must indicate which portion of any information or evidence necessary to substantiate the claimant’s claim is to be provided by each party.  The BVA also failed to discuss whether any document in the record, including the RO decision and SOC, satisfied that requirement.  Nevertheless, the Court found that the BVA’s error must be considered nonprejudicial because the claimant’s averment that he was the brother of the deceased veteran was uniquely within the claimant’s knowledge, and, as such, under section 101(14) he is ineligible for DIC as a matter of law.  The Court pointed out that where the facts averred by a claimant cannot conceivably result in any disposition of the appeal other than affirmance of the BVA decision, the case should not be remanded for development that could not possibly change the outcome of the decision.  The failure to carry out such required development under those circumstances is nonprejudicial error under [section 7261(b)(2)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/7261).
