Decision Assessment Document
 Tropf v. Nicholson, Aug 24, 2006, 20 Vet.App. 317
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court, in rejecting the veteran’s appeal for a compensable evaluation for shell fragment wounds (SFW) of Muscle Group (MG) III on the sole basis that metallic fragments retained in the muscle constituted a moderate disability under [38 C.F.R. § 4.56(d)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=dcc91c487672624bc054684e962c9182&node=se38.1.4_156&rgn=div8), held that 38 C.F.R. § 4.56(d) is essentially a totality-of-the-circumstances test and no single factor is per se controlling.

Note:  This decision is actually the Court’s reconsidered opinion of a decision issued April 4, 2006.  The Secretary had requested reconsideration of that decision which has now effectively been withdrawn, with this one issued in its place.  The prior decision had held that [38 C.F.R. § 4.27](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=dcc91c487672624bc054684e962c9182&node=se38.1.4_127&rgn=div8) permits the use of hyphenated ratings only for diseases and not for injuries and consequently that VA’s use of hyphenated ratings for that purpose was improper.  The Court, in a footnote in the instant decision, agreed with the Secretary who had argued, among other things, that the holding was not necessary to its disposition of the case, but cautioned that it did not necessarily agree with the Secretary that the criteria found in section 4.27 allowed the use of hyphenated ratings for both injuries and diseases.

**Impact on VBA:**

38 C.F.R. § 4.27 will be slightly revised to clarify that hyphenated ratings are allowed for both diseases *and* injuries.

Adjudicators, when rating muscle injuries, must consider *all* factors set forth in the regulations and make determinations based on the facts specific to the particular case.  No single factor is controlling for rating purposes.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

Service connection for a SFW to the back with retained metallic fragments was granted in 1970.  A 10 percent evaluation under [38 C.F.R. § 4.118](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=dcc91c487672624bc054684e962c9182&node=se38.1.4_1118&rgn=div8), Diagnostic Code (DC) 7804, was assigned.

A claim for increased evaluation and service connection for SFW residuals of the right upper arm was filed in September 2001.  A VA examination revealed a barely visible scar of the right flank that was mildly tender on palpation.  No visible scar or evidence of residual of shrapnel of the right shoulder was found.  There was no muscle loss, tissue loss, or muscle atrophy of the right shoulder.  X-rays showed small metallic fragments in the soft tissues surrounding the right humerus and of the right lower thoracic back area.

In February 2002, the RO granted service connection for a SFW of the right upper arm with a zero percent evaluation under [38 C.F.R. § 4.73](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=dcc91c487672624bc054684e962c9182&node=se38.1.4_173&rgn=div8), DC 5303.  The 10 percent evaluation for the SFW residuals of the back was continued and the decision indicated that it was assigned under DC 7804-5320.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (BVA) upheld the RO decision in July 2003.

The veteran argued on appeal that BVA erred in failing to assign a separate evaluation of at least 10 percent for a moderate muscle injury to his back under DC 5320, in addition to the 10 percent evaluation for a painful scar under DC 7804.  He also asserted that the SFW of the right arm warrants a 20 percent evaluation on the grounds that the presence of retained metallic fragments are consistent with a moderate muscle injury, rather than the zero percent assigned for a slight injury.

The Court noted that the clear purpose of a hyphenated rating is to add information to help describe the origins of a single disability when the disability is not one listed under the explicit DC for the given condition.  The Court found that the BVA’s interpretation and application of the hyphenated rating was in error in this case because it violated precedent decisions by the Court that require two different disabling conditions (in this case a scar and a muscle injury) to be rated separately.  This issue was remanded for BVA to determine whether a separate disability rating is warranted under DC 5320 for the MG XX injury.

The Court next addressed the veteran's claim that he is entitled to a higher rating under [38 C.F.R. § 4.56](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=dcc91c487672624bc054684e962c9182&node=se38.1.4_156&rgn=div8) as a matter of law because 38 C.F.R. § 4.56(d) bars rating a muscle injury as "slight" if there are retained metallic fragments.  The Court discussed its holding of *Robertson v. Brown*, 5 Vet.App. 70 (1993), in which it rejected the veteran's argument that, regardless of actual disability, he was entitled to a "moderately severe" disability rating simply because his wound included debridement.  The Court stated in *Robertson* that the criteria in 38 C.F.R. § 4.56(c) were only factors to be considered and that no specific fact established entitlement to any particular rating.

VA has revised § 4.56 since *Robertson* by, among other things adding new paragraph (c) which states that, for VA rating purposes, the cardinal signs and symptoms of muscle disability are loss of power, weakness, etc.  The Court, noting that VA commentary indicated substantive change of the regulation was not intended by its amendment of § 4.56, concluded that its holding in *Roberson* – “the adjudicator must consider all the factors set forth in the regulations and make determinations based on the facts of the particular case” – is still valid.

The Court found that new § 4.56(c) had the effect of memorializing the practice of giving the commonly detectable manifestations of muscle disability priority in evaluating all the factors to be considered.  The Court reaffirmed its holding in *Robertson* that, under § 4.56(d), the totality-of-the-circumstances test and no single factor is per se controlling.  Thus, *Robertson* conflicts with the veteran’s argument that a compensable rating was in order based solely on the presence of retained metallic fragments in the muscle.

The Court concluded that BVA’s decision to assign a slight (noncompensable) rating for the SFW arm injury based upon the uncontested finding that the veteran is essentially asymptomatic was not a misapplication of the regulation as a matter of law.
