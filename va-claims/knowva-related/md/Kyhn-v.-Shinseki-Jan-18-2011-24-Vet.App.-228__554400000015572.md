Decision Assessment Document
 Kyhn v. Shinseki, Jan 18, 2011, 24 Vet.App. 228
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that a veteran’s allegation of non-receipt of a notice of scheduled examination and failure to report for a scheduled VA examination, without demonstration of good cause, does not negate the presumption that VA discharged its duty to schedule an examination and notify the veteran of the scheduled examination in compliance with the orders of a remand from the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board).

The Court held that there is no independent requirement for a hard copy of the notice to report for a VA examination to be contained in the claims file for the presumption of regularity to apply.  The Court observed that because the regular practices of VA do not include maintaining a hard copy of the veteran’s notice of the scheduled examination, the absence of any such copy from the claims file cannot be used as evidence to demonstrate that the notice was not mailed.

The Court held that there is no regulatory or internal procedures requirement that VA complete an “Appeal Certification Worksheet” ([VA Form 8](http://www.va.gov/vaforms/va/pdf/VA8.pdf)) a second time when a claim is remanded.  The Court held that the absence of a second VA Form 8 in the claims file cannot constitute clear evidence to rebut the presumption of regularity concerning notice of a scheduled examination.

The Court also held that failure to demonstrate good cause for failing to report for examination scheduled in conjunction with a reopened claim warrants denial of the reopened claim in accordance with [38 C.F.R. § 3.655(b)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/3.655), rather than adjudicating the claim on the merits.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

Note: The Court affirmed the Board’s decision on January 15, 2010; however, the Court granted the veteran’s petition for reconsideration subsequent to the decision and ordered the parties to submit supplemental memoranda.  The Court subsequently withdrew its January 2010 decision and issued this decision in its place.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

In January 2004, the veteran filed a claim to reopen his tinnitus claim, which was previously denied in November 1999.  He submitted a private medical opinion with his claim that linked his tinnitus to in-service noise exposure.  The Board, in January 2006, determined that the veteran had submitted new and material evidence to reopen the claim and remanded the case for the regional office (RO) to afford the veteran a VA audiology examination to determine the etiology and severity of any present tinnitus.

The veteran failed to report for a VA audiology examination scheduled in March 2006.  The RO issued a Supplemental Statement of the Case later in the month denying his claim based on failure to report for the examination.  The veteran responded to a March 2006 request for information by stating that he had no more information or evidence to submit and requested VA to decide his claim as soon as possible.

The Board, in May 2007, denied the veteran’s appeal, finding no probative evidence that his tinnitus was incurred in or causally related to service or aggravated by any service-connected disability.  The Board determined that compliance with the duty to assist under [38 U.S.C. § 5103A](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5103A) had been met because a VA audiology examination was scheduled and notification was mailed to the veteran at his correct address of record.  The Board also noted that, in accordance with 38 C.F.R. § 3.365, failure to report for a scheduled examination warranted the claim to be rated on the evidence of record.

The veteran argued before the Court, inter alia, that the Board failed to comply with the January 2006 remand instructions by failing to provide him with an examination in violation of *Stegall v. West,* 11 Vet.App. 268 and that VA failed to provide him notice of his scheduled examination.

The Court, in August 2009, ordered the Secretary to provide information concerning the regular process by which VA notifies veterans of scheduled VA examinations.  The Secretary informed the Court that the RO electronically communicates a request to the VAMC in whose primary service area the veteran resides, the request is entered into the Automated Medical Information Exchange (AMIE) system, and the VAMC electronically generates a letter to the veteran informing him of the examination, including its time and location.  A hard copy is not retained by the RO or VAMC.  However, the Secretary, in this case, was able to produce a regenerated copy (dated Aug. 28, 2009) of the notification letter sent to the veteran from electronic records.

The Court held that VA had substantially complied with the 2006 remand orders by scheduling the veteran for an examination in March 2006 at an appropriate VA facility and the veteran’s allegations that he had not received the notice of the examination were insufficient to rebut the presumption of regularity that government officials properly have discharged their official duties.

The Court found that the RO had followed the regular process for scheduling and notifying the veteran of an examination and that the examination cancellation notice stated that the notification of the examination was mailed to the veteran at his address; thus, it may be presumed that the VAMC electronically generated and mailed the veteran notice of the scheduled examination at his address of record.

The Court rejected the veteran’s argument that absence of a [VA Form 8](http://www.va.gov/vaforms/va/pdf/VA8.pdf) in the file following the Board’s January 2006 remand rebutted the presumption of regularity.  The Court found that the record contained a completed VA Form 8 from April 2005, prior to the Board’s January 2006 remand, and there is nothing in the regulations requiring VA to prepare a second VA Form 8 for remanded claims that are returned to the Board.

The Court found that the Board erred in adjudicating the claim on the merits, rather than under [38 C.F.R. § 3.665](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=d92531834bdf12f9f1b4f32faa2729a2&node=se38.1.3_1665&rgn=div8), as § 3.665(b) requires that a claim be denied if a veteran, without good cause, fails to report for an examination scheduled in conjunction with a reopened claim for a benefit that was previously disallowed.  However, the Court determined the error was not prejudicial because the veteran was properly denied service connection for tinnitus.

The Court affirmed the Board’s May 2007 decision.
