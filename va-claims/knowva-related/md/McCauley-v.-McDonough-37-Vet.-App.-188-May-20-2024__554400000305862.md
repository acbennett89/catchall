**Decision Assessment Document**

***McCauley v. McDonough*, 37 Vet. App. 188 (May 20, 2024)**

**U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)**

**What the case is about:**The Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) found that severance of service connection was proper for Veteran's type II diabetes, coronary artery disease (CAD) post coronary artery bypass graft, coronary artery bypass graft scars, a non-linear superficial anterior trunk scar, a left lower extremity scar, and a right lower extremity scar. The Veteran appealed. The Court held that the severance standards of 38 C.F.R. § 3.105(d) require VA to prove that service connection cannot be granted or maintained under any theory of entitlement raised by the claimant or reasonably raised by the record in order for severance of service connection to be proper.

**Impact on VBA:**The M21-1 Adjudication Procedures Manual will be updated to reference this decision.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**In August 2016, the Veteran filed a claim for heart disease based on exposure to contaminated water at Camp Lejeune or herbicide exposure near Vietnam. A VA regional office (RO) granted service connection for diabetes and CAD on the basis of presumption due to Agent Orange exposure, despite a November 2016 finding that the claim of herbicide exposure was not verified. The RO also granted service connection for scars secondary to CAD. The rating decision did not address the Veteran’s exposure to contaminated water because it was “a moot point” in light of service connection due to Agent Orange exposure.

In March 2017, the RO proposed to sever service connection for the Veteran’s disabilities due to its error in presuming his exposure to herbicides. In February 2018, the RO finalized the severance of service connection for the Veteran’s claimed disabilities, effective May 1, 2018. The Veteran filed a notice of disagreement.

In September 2019, a deferred rating decision acknowledged that VA had not addressed whether the Veteran’s disabilities were caused by contaminated water at Camp Lejeune. The RO transferred the Veteran’s appeal to the RO in Louisville, Kentucky. In a June 2022 Statement of the Case (SOC), VA continued to deny service connection for the severed disabilities and continued to deny service connection for numbness and tingling of all extremities.[[1]](#_ftn1) The SOC addressed service connection based on contaminated water at Camp Lejeune by informing the Veteran that he failed to respond to a VA request for scientific or medial evidence showing the claimed conditions were medically associated with the exposure to the contaminants. The Veteran appealed to the Board.

The Board found it clear and unmistakable that the Veteran did not serve in the Republic of Vietnam or its territorial sea, and it found that service connection for the claimed disabilities could not be granted on any other basis. The Board concluded that severance of service connection for diabetes mellitus, CAD, and scars was proper, and it denied the claim for numbness and tingling of the extremities. The Board’s decision did not mention exposure to contaminated water at Camp Lejeune. The Veteran appealed to the Court.

At the Court, the Veteran argued that the Board was required to address whether his diabetes, CAD, and scars were caused by exposure to contaminated water at Camp Lejeune, and because the Board did not do so, the severance was void ab initio. VA conceded the Board erred in not addressing exposure to contaminated water at Camp Lejeune, but argued the error did not void the severance.

The Court noted that 38 C.F.R. § 3.105(d) allows severance of service connection only when the evidence establishes that it is clearly and unmistakably erroneous, with the burden of proof on the Government. If service connection is severed despite VA not having met its burden, the severance is a void ab initio and the proper remedy is reversal of the Board decision with instructions to reinstate service connection for the severed disabilities, retroactive to the date of severance.

The Court discussed the difference between the Secretary’s burden when severing service connection versus a claimant’s burden when proving clear and unmistakable error (CUE) in a final decision. A claimant’s CUE challenge is retrospective because it considers whether VA committed a clear and unmistakable error when it denied benefits. This determination is based on the record and the law that existed at the time of the decision. On the other hand, severance is not based on whether the original decision was clearly erroneous, but instead is based on whether the current evidence establishes that service connection is clearly erroneous.

Thus, VA must prove that it is currently clearly and unmistakably erroneous for the claimant to be service connected. If VA only shows that the theory on which service connection was originally granted was erroneous, VA has not necessarily proved that maintaining service connection is clearly erroneous. Accordingly, VA must show that service connection cannot be granted or maintained under any theory of service connection. The Court reasoned that if an unresolved theory could maintain service connection, then VA cannot show that service connection is clearly and unmistakably erroneous.

The Court clarified that VA is not required to invent and reject every conceivable alternative theory for service connection for severance to be proper. Instead, VA must address any theories raised by the claimant or the record. If VA fails to address an alternate theory of service connection raised by the claimant or the record, then VA has not met the standards of § 3.105(d) and the Court must find the severance void ab initio.

In the instant case, the Board was required to address whether the Veteran could maintain service connection based on the raised and unresolved theory of exposure to contaminated water at Camp Lejeune. The Board found severance was proper without mentioning contaminated water at Camp Lejeune and with no consideration of whether the Veteran could maintain service connection under this theory. As a result, the Board did not ensure that service connection is clearly erroneous as required by § 3.105(d).

The Court found the severance was void ab initio and the proper remedy was to reverse the Board’s finding that severance was proper and remand the matter to the Board with instructions to reinstate service connection for diabetes, CAD, and scars retroactive to the date of severance. The Court remanded the Veteran’s claim for numbness and tingling of the extremities because the claim was intertwined with his diabetes claim.
