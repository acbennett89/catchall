Decision Assessment Document
 Camacho v. Nicholson, Jul 6, 2007, 21 Vet.App. 360
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court, deferring to VA’s interpretation of [38 C.F.R. § 4.119](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=7ab6d45757aae630797f2c6885b095f9&node=se38.1.4_1119&rgn=div8), Diagnostic Code (DC) 7913, held that in order for a veteran to be entitled to a 40 percent evaluation for diabetes mellitus, the evidence must show that it is *medically necessary* for the veteran to avoid strenuous occupational and recreational activities.

The Court also held that in light of the conjunctive “and” in the criteria for a 40 percent rating under DC 7913, *all* criteria must be met to establish a 40 percent evaluation.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran filed a claim for service connection for diabetes mellitus in December 2000.  A VA examination report from October 2001 indicated that the veteran took insulin twice daily, was on a diabetic diet, and exercised nearly every day.  The diabetes was characterized as not well controlled.  The regional office (RO), in December 2001, granted service connection for diabetes mellitus with a 20 percent evaluation.

The veteran submitted a letter from his employer, the North Chicago VAMC, which stated that the veteran is a motor vehicle operator, whose duties include transporting patients.  He was deemed disqualified from driving patients due to insulin-dependent diabetes.  The letter was signed by the employee health clinic manager who identified herself as a nurse practitioner with a master of science in nursing (MSN).

VAMC progress notes from 2004 included the following addendum from a physician:  “Recently, [a] question has been raised about the safety.  I would defer it to [m]edical center administration guidelines.”  The physician further indicated that the veteran would be safe to work within the VAMC without the responsibility to act as a van driver.  Other progress notes indicated that he exercised six to seven days per week.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in February 2005, denied the appeal for an evaluation in excess of 20 percent.  The Board found that only two of the three criteria for a 40 percent evaluation were met, as the record did not show restriction of activities.

The veteran argued before the Court that his disqualification by his employer from driving is a “regulation of activities” within the context of DC 7913, which warrants a 40 percent evaluation.

The Court, applying the principles of affording substantial deference to an agency’s interpretation of its own regulations, concluded that VA’s interpretation of DC 7913 in defining “regulation of activities” to mean “avoidance of strenuous occupational and recreational activities” was not plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation.  The Court also held that the regulatory history for DC 7913 supports the view it must be shown that regulation of activities is medically necessary.

The Court concluded that there was a plausible basis for the Board’s determination that no medical evidence showed the veteran’s activities were restricted.  The Court noted that the letter that represented the employer’s decision to disqualify the veteran from driving patients was signed by a nurse practitioner; however, it was not based on an individualized assessment of the veteran’s condition.  The Board found that the nurse made those statements in her capacity as the veteran’s supervisor rather than in the capacity of a medical professional diagnosing the veteran.  Thus, the Court concluded that the letter did not constitute competent medical evidence that the veteran's diabetes actually made him unsafe to drive.

The Court rejected veteran’s argument that pursuant to [38 C.F.R. § 4.21](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=7ab6d45757aae630797f2c6885b095f9&node=se38.1.4_121&rgn=div8) he was not required to meet all criteria for a 40 percent evaluation under DC 7913.  The Court indicated that cases in which it had indicated that § 4.21 applied are those in which the diagnostic criteria was not clearly joined in the conjunctive as in DC 7913.

The Court affirmed the Board’s decision.
