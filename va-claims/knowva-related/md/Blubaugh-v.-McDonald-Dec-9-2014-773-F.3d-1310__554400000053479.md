Decision Assessment Document
 Blubaugh v. McDonald, Dec 9, 2014, 773 F.3d 1310
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that VA must only consider the earlier effective date provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)(3) and (4) if it grants benefits resulting from reconsideration of the merits under 38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)(1).

The Federal Circuit held that § 3.156(c)(1) does not apply if VA has already reconsidered the merits of a veteran’s claim in light of the relevant service record.  In such circumstances, VA has exhausted its duty under § 3.156(c)(1).  If the reconsideration under § 3.156(c)(1) does not result in benefits, subsections (c)(3) and (c)(4) are inapplicable.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran filed his original claim for service connection for posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) in October 1988.  In response to the claim, VA obtained his service records, which showed that he served in Vietnam and received the Vietnam Service Medal.  The regional office (RO), in April 1989, denied service connection for PTSD on the basis that his VA examination did not support a PTSD diagnosis.  No appellate action was taken.

In August 1992, the veteran filed to reopen the claim.  VA associated with his claims file, a Department of the Army (DA) Form 20, which listed the specific dates he served in Vietnam.  This evidence had not been of record at the time of the 1989 decision.  The RO reopened the claim and conducted another examination, which did not support a PTSD diagnosis and noted the “absence of a definitive confirmable stressor.”  The RO denied the claim in June 1993 and the veteran did not appeal.

The veteran, on July 25, 2008, filed another request to reopen his PTSD claim and submitted a three-page statement describing his Vietnam experiences.  VA also received, for the first time, medical documentation showing a positive PTSD diagnosis.  Based on the newly submitted evidenced, the RO granted service connection for PTSD and assigned a 10 percent rating from July 25, 2008.  The veteran appealed, alleging that he should be entitled to an effective date of June 9, 1993.  The RO’s Statement of the Case indicated that he was not entitled to an earlier effective date because his earlier claims were unsupported by a PTSD diagnosis and his 1992 claim lacked a “confirmable stressor.”

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) affirmed the RO decision, concluding that the date of the veteran’s 2008 claim, not the date of the 1992 claim, was the controlling effective date under 38 C.F.R. § 3.400(r). The regulation provides that an award of compensation based on a reopened claim may be no earlier than the date of receipt of the claim, or the date entitlement arose, whichever is later.

The Veterans Court affirmed the Board’s decision, concluding that § 3.156(c) was inapplicable to the veteran’s case because the service record at issue, the DA Form 20, had been associated with the veteran’s claims file before the June 1993 decision.  The Veterans Court reasoned that if VA were under a duty to reconsider the veteran’s claim in light of his DA Form 20, that duty would have arisen at the time VA received it, not in 2008.

The veteran argued before the Federal Circuit that § 3.156(c) requires VA to consider a retroactive date of entitlement, even if VA has already examined the newly associated service record, and despite that record, denied the veteran’s claim on the merits and benefits are later granted based on those records, in whole or in part.

The Federal Circuit rejected the veteran’s argument, holding that § 3.156(c) was inapplicable to the veteran’s case and did not require VA to determine whether he was entitled to an earlier effective date for his service-connected PTSD.

The Federal Circuit stated that the veteran’s reading of §3.156(c) conflates subsections (c)(1) with subsections (c)(3) and (c)(4).  The Federal Circuit held that under the plain language of the regulation, § 3.156(c)(1) does not apply under such circumstances because VA has already reconsidered the merits of the veteran’s claim in light of the relevant service record.  In such circumstances, VA has exhausted its duty under § 3.156(c)(1).  Because the initial reconsideration in 1993 under § 3.156(c)(1) did not result in benefits, subsections (c)(3) and (c)(4) do not apply in this case.

The Federal Circuit observed that the veteran’s claims were denied in 1989 and 1993 because he had not been diagnosed with PTSD and the record also lacked evidence of a definitive confirmable stressor.  The Veteran’s DA Form 20 did not remedy these defects, as it did not show combat service or a PTSD diagnosis.  The only arguably relevant information contained in the DA Form 20 is the specific time period during which he served in Vietnam.  However, the dates of the veteran’s Vietnam service were unquestioned and did not lead to the award of benefits in 2008.  New medical records showing a PTSD diagnosis and evidence of a confirmable stressor led to VA’s decision to award benefits.

The Federal Circuit held that any obligation VA had to reconsider the veteran’s claim in light of his DA Form 20 was exhausted in 1993 when it reopened his claim and, despite possessing the newly associated service record, again denied the claim on the merits.

The Federal Circuit affirmed the Veterans Court decision.
