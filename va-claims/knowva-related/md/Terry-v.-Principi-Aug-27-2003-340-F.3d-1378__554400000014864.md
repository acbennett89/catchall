Decision Assessment Document
 Terry v. Principi, Aug 27, 2003, 340 F.3d 1378
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that [38 CFR § 3.303(c)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=1d1ff5eee1512aef8815c028e3a62c3e&node=se38.1.3_1303&rgn=div8) is valid and precludes the veteran’s claim of entitlement to service connection for presbyopia.  The Federal Circuit found the fact that presbyopia is due to developmental problems associated with aging rather than trauma incurred during military service is a reasonable basis for excluding “refractive error of the eye” from the construction of the terms “injury” and “disease” in [38 USC §§ 1110](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1110) and [1131](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1131).

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact on VBA.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The RO denied the veteran’s claim seeking service connection for presbyopia.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) affirmed the RO’s denial, concluding that 38 CFR § 3.303(c) was a bar to the veteran’s claim because the regulation provides that “refractive error of the eye” is not a disease or injury within the meaning of veterans’ benefits statutes.  The Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims affirmed the Board’s decision and upheld the validity of 38 CFR § 3.303(c).

The Federal Circuit stated [38 USC §§ 1110](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1110) and [1131](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1131) make it clear that if a disability cannot be attributed to an “injury” or a “disease” incurred or aggravated in the line of duty, the disability is not compensable.  In [38 CFR § 3.303(c)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=1d1ff5eee1512aef8815c028e3a62c3e&node=se38.1.3_1303&rgn=div8), VA excluded certain conditions, including “refractive error of the eye,” from the terms “injury” and “disease.”  According to *Dorland’s Illustrated Medical Dictionary*, “injury” is defined as harm or hurt, and is usually applied to damage inflicted on the body by an external force; “disease” is defined as any deviation from the normal function of the body.  The Federal Circuit noted that presbyopia, a refractive error of the eye, is due to developmental problems associated with aging rather than trauma incurred during military service.  Since aging is not an external force or a deviation from the normal function of the body, the Federal Circuit held that 38 CFR § 3.303(c) is a permissible construction of the statutes.

The Federal Circuit also held that 38 CFR § 3.303(c), which prohibits compensation for refractive error of the eye and, in particular, for presbyopia, is not inconsistent with 38 USC §§ 1111 and 1153, relating to the presumption of soundness and aggravation of a preexisting injury or disease, respectively.  The Federal Circuit stated that Congress could not have intended to include every defect, infirmity, and disorder within the scope of compensable disabilities.  Therefore, 38 CFR § 3.303(c) is based on a permissible construction of the governing statutes and is valid.
