Decision Assessment Document
King v. Shulkin, Dec 21, 2017, 29 Vet.App. 174 (2017)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the availability of a higher schedular rating is irrelevant in making an analysis under 38 C.F.R. 3.321(b)(1) for the purpose of determining whether to refer a case for consideration of an extra-schedular evaluation.

The Court further held that this interpretation of the law is a general principle under 3.321(b)(1) and is not dependent on the particular type of claim at issue; thus, its holding regarding the interpretation of the regulation is not exclusive to hearing loss evaluations but is applicable to all claims.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court’s holding has been incorporated into guidance contained in the Manual M21-1 at III.iv.5.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who is rated zero percent for service-connected bilateral hearing loss, underwent a VA audiological examination in 2009, which resulted in the examiner commenting that the hearing loss significantly affected the veteran’s occupation.  The examiner also noted poor social interactions and hearing difficulty.  A VA examiner in 2011 stated that the veteran’s hearing loss affected daily life and occupation due to “hearing difficulty.”

The following year, the veteran testified before the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) that his bilateral hearing loss rendered him unable to hear the telephone ring or bird sounds, makes him turn up the television volume to such levels that it drove his wife to leave the room, and required him face a speaker.  He also testified that having to ask others to repeat their words to him made him angry.  The Board remanded the case for another examination.  The subsequent examination found that the veteran’s hearing loss did not impact his ordinary conditions of daily life or ability to work.

The Board, in its June 2016 decision, denied referral for and entitlement to an extra-schedular evaluation for the veteran’s bilateral hearing loss on the basis of its finding that the rating criteria reasonably described the veteran’s disability levels and symptomatology, and provided for higher ratings for more severe symptoms.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board erred in finding that extra-schedular referral was not warranted because all of the functional effects of his bilateral hearing loss were contemplated by the rating criteria.  He also contended that the Board failed to consider the entire disability picture when deciding not to refer the claim for extra-schedular consideration.

The Court, prior to applying the legal framework for referral for extra-schedular consideration established under Thun v. Peake, 22 Vet.App. 111 (2008), addressed the Secretary’s argument that the Court’s section of Doucette v. Shulkin, 28 Vet.App. 366 (2017) stating that there is a class of functional effects outside of the rating schedule for hearing loss, which the Court held in that decision to contemplate difficulty hearing and understanding speech, as dicta.

The Court rejected the Secretary’s argument, explaining that the notion that there is a class of functional effects existing outside the rating schedule was integral to the Court’s holding in Doucette.   The Court clarified that it acknowledged in Doucette the existence of effects that would be inherently outside of the rating schedule.  In Doucette, the Court referenced ear pain, dizziness, recurrent loss of balance or social isolation due to difficulties communicating as only examples where the Board would be required to explain whether the rating criteria contemplated those functional effects.  However, as none of those effects were present in Doucette, the Court did not address them further and would not render such discussion in the present case as it would be remanding the case on other grounds.

The Court held that the availability of higher schedular ratings plays no role in an extra-schedular analysis and that it was inappropriate for the Board to deny extra-schedular referral on that basis.  The Court observed that schedular and extra-schedular ratings exist as two separate and distinct paths to disability compensation.  The Court found the Board’s reasons or bases in using the fact that the rating criteria for hearing loss provided for higher ratings for severe symptoms as a reason to deny the veteran referral for extra-schedular evaluation to be incorrect as a matter of law.

The Court also held that there was not anything special regarding hearing loss that suggested that its opinion was limited to hearing loss claims, as 3.321(b)(1) is applicable to all claims.

The Court remanded the Board decision.
