Decision Assessment Document
 Yancy v. McDonald, Feb 26, 2016, 27 Vet.App. 484 (2016)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

 The Court held that the plain meaning of the word “injury” limits the application of 38 C.F.R. 4.71a, Diagnostic Code (DC) 5284 to disabilities resulting from actual injuries to the foot, as opposed to disabilities caused by, for example, degenerative conditions.  However, unlisted conditions that are not explicitly listed for foot disabilities under § 4.71a may be rated by analogy under DC 5284.

The Court held that the first and second elements under Thun v. Peake, 22 Vet.App. 111 (2008), although interrelated, involve separate and distinct analyses.  Since the inquiries are independent, an error with respect to one element does not necessarily affect VA’s analysis of the other element.  If either element is not met, referral for extraschedular consideration is not appropriate.  (The three Thun elements are (1) inquiry to determine whether the evidence before VA presents such an exceptional disability picture that the available schedular evaluations are inadequate; (2) determination of whether the exceptional disability picture exhibits other related factors such as marked interference with employment or frequent periods of hospitalization; and (3) if the first two elements are satisfied, referral to Compensation Service for a determination of whether an extra-schedular rating is warranted.)

The Court also held that VA is only required to address whether referral for extra-schedular condition is warranted for a veteran’s disabilities on a collective basis when that issue is argued by the claimant or reasonably raised by the record through evidence of the collective impact of the service-connected disabilities.  The three-part analysis under Thun is also applicable to considering whether a referral for extra-schedular consideration is warranted based on the combined effects of a veteran’s service-connected disabilities.  (The Federal Circuit, in Johnson v. McDonald, 462 F.3d 1362 (Fed. Cir. 2014), held that § 3.321(b)(1) provides for extra-schedular consideration based on the collective impact of multiple disabilities.)

**Impact on VBA:**

The Manual M21-1 will be revised at III.iv.4.A.3 and III.iv.6.B.4 in compliance with the Court’s decision.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The regional office (RO), in April 2008, granted service connection for bilateral pes planus, with a 10 percent evaluation, and hallux valgus of the great toes, which were assigned noncompensable evaluations.  In an April 2009 Notice of Disagreement, the veteran stated that he was “unable to stand in one sport for long periods and in connection with the pain in both knees” and “due to the current condition of his feet and knees, he had been unable to remain as physically active.”

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in August 2014, increased the evaluation for the bilateral pes planus to 30 percent and denied higher evaluations for the hallux valgus conditions, finding that he did not have “severe” hallux valgus, which warrants a compensable evaluation under DC 5280.  The Board found that the veteran did not meet the requirements for a separate compensable rating for hammertoe under DC 5282, and that he had not been diagnosed with weak foot (DC 5277), claw foot (DC 5278), Morton’s disease (DC 5279), hallux rigidus (5281), malunion or nonunion of the tarsal or metatarsal bones (DC 5283), or other foot injuries (5284).  The Board also determined that referral for extraschedular consideration was unwarranted, as the various schedular ratings were adequate.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board failed to adequately explain why he was not entitled to increased or additional disability ratings under DC 5281 or DC 5284.  He also contended that the Board clearly erred when it determined that he had not been diagnosed with hallux rigidus and thus failed to discuss whether he was entitled to a compensable rating under DC 5281.

In regards to the Board’s determination that referral for extraschedular consideration was unwarranted, the veteran argued that the Board failed to properly conduct the three-part analysis required by Thun, as it failed to adequately compare all of his symptoms to those contemplated by the rating criteria required by the first Thun element, and did not discuss the second Thun element.  The veteran also asserted that the Board should have discussed whether he was entitled to extraschedular referral for his service-connected disabilities on a collective basis pursuant to Johnson.

The Court found that the Board failed to properly consider whether the veteran was entitled to disability ratings under DCs 5281 and 5284.  The record contains medical evidence of a diagnosis of bilateral hallux valgus et rigidus, which the Board failed to discuss whether it encompasses a diagnosis of hallux rigidus.  The Court rejected the Secretary’s argument that the failure to consider a disability rating under DC 5281 in view of the noncompensable ratings for under DC 5280, as the Court found the argument to presuppose that any hallux rigidus suffered by the veteran is less than severe, which is a determination that the Board must make in the first instance.

The Court, agreeing with the Secretary’s interpretation of the word “injury” under DC 5284, held that the plain meaning of the word limits the application of DC 5284 to disabilities resulting from actual injuries to the foot.  Although the Court agreed with the Secretary’s interpretation, it found the Board’s reasons or bases for determining that the veteran was not entitled to an evaluation under DC 5284 to be inadequate, as the Court held that the Board may rate closely related conditions by analogy under DC 5284.  (The Court held in Copeland v. McDonald, 27 Vet.App. 333 (2015) that DC 5284 does not apply to the eight other foot conditions specifically listed in § 4.71a, so that any listed condition cannot be rated by analogy under DC 5284.)

The Court found that the veteran had been diagnosed with conditions not explicitly listed in the rating schedule, such as calcaneus foot deformity and heel spurs, that may be rated by analogy.  The Court held that the Board erred in failing to discuss whether the veteran’s conditions that were not listed in the rating schedule could be rated by analogy pursuant to DC 5284.

In addressing the extra-schedular issue, the Court described the first Thun element as comparing a claimant’s symptoms to the rating criteria, while the second element addressed the resulting effects of those symptoms.  In citing Thun, the Court described the first inquiry to require “a comparison between the level of severity and symptomatology of the claimant’s service-connected disability with the established criteria found in the rating schedule for that disability.”

Furthermore, also referencing Thun, the Court indicated that if “the criteria reasonably describe the claimant’s disability level and symptomatology, then the claimant’s disability picture is contemplated by the rating schedule … and no referral is required.”

The Court held that the Board is required, under Johnson, to discuss the collective impact of a veteran’s service-connected disabilities; however, it does not alter the Board does not have jurisdiction to consider whether referral is warranted solely for any disability or combination of disabilities not in appellate status, just as it lacks jurisdiction to examine the proper schedular rating for a disability not on appeal.

The Court, finding that the record contained evidence of the collective impact of his service-connected disabilities, held that the Board erred in failing to address whether the veteran was entitled to referral for extraschedular consideration for his service-connected disabilities on a collective basis.  Specifically the Court found that the Board erred by failing to discuss the combined effects of the veteran’s disabilities in its analysis of the first Thun element.

The Court remanded the case with instructions that the Board (1) determine whether the record contains a diagnosis of hallux rigidus and, if so, whether the veteran is entitled to an evaluation under DC 5281; (2) whether the unlisted diagnoses entitle him to a rating by analogy under DC 5284; and (3) discuss whether the combined effects of the veteran’s service-connected disabilities warrant referral for extra-schedular consideration.
