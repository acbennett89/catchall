## 2.01 Casualty Assistance Program[Back to top](#toc) Change Date: July 1, 2025

1. ### Casualty Assistance Definition

   A survivor of a Servicemember who dies on active duty receives special assistance and priority claims processing. Veterans Benefits Administration (VBA) provides direct outreach to survivors to ensure their claims are received and processed in a timely manner.

   *Resource*: The [Veterans Affairs Office of Survivors Assistance](http://www.va.gov/survivors) (VA) (OSA) provides resources and information on benefits and services available to surviving spouses, dependents, and family members. OSA can be reached at [officeofsurvivors@va.gov](mailto:officeofsurvivors@va.gov).

   *References*: For more information on VA's mandate to conduct outreach services to eligible survivors see:

   * [Title 38 United States Code (U.S.C), Chapter 63](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title38/part4/chapter63&edition=prelim)
   * Public Law 107-14 Veterans' Survivors Benefits Improvement Act of 2001
2. ### RO Responsibilities for Casualty Outreach

   VA Regional Offices (ROs) are required to designate one Casualty Assistance Coordinator (CAC) to provide outreach services to designated survivors.

   * ROs will ensure that coordinators listed in the [Casualty Assistance Coordinator Directory](https://dvagov.sharepoint.com/sites/VBABASqualitytraining/bastrain/data/siteassets/coordinator/frame_pages/casframe.aspx) are kept up to date and any change submitted within two (2) business days.
   * Designations of alternate CAC representatives are determined at RO levels.
   * RO CACs are required to coordinate with Military Casualty Assistance Officers to ensure accurate and timely benefit resources are provided to eligible survivors.

   Note: RO CAC outreach efforts can be coordinated and reassigned between RO's for the convenience of the beneficiary and upon RO agreement.

   *References*: <https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/BAS/outreach/military/casualty-assistance/Index.asp>
3. ### Military Casualty Assistance Officers

   The position title of the military casualty representatives is determined by the respective branch of service as follows:

   * US Air Force: Casualty Assistance Representative (CAR)
   * US Army: Casualty Assistance Officer (CAO)
   * US Coast Guard: Casualty Assistance Calls Officer (CACO)
   * US Marines: Casualty Assistance Calls Officer (CACO)
   * US Navy: Casualty Assistance Calls Officer (CACO)

   Resources: Military CAO/CACO/CAR Liaison listing:

   * Army Casualty Assistance: 888-ARMYHRC (276-9472)
   * Air Force Casualty Assistance (Air and Space Force): 800-525-0102, select Option 2, then Option 1
   * Navy Casualty Assistance/Family Liaison: 800-368-3202
   * Marine Corps Casualty Assistance: 800-847-1597
   * Coast Guard Casualty Assistance: 571-266-2375
4. ### Reports of Casualty

   Casualty reports are received through the Federal Case Management Tool (FCMT) and assigned upon notification from the military branch CAO/CACO/CAR for in-service deaths.

   * The Department of Defense (DoD) DD Form 1300, *Report of Casualty*, is used to report in-service deaths.
   * The CAO/CACO/CAR contact information is included in section 8 of the DD Form 1300.
   * The DD Form 1300 is updated in FCMT by the CAO/CACO/CAR.
   * Casualty cases are assigned based on the office of jurisdiction nearest the casualty and/or survivors' residence.
   * ROs are automatically notified, via designated casualty email mailbox, when a DD Form 1300 has been entered into FCMT for their jurisdiction.
   * RO CACs must process all casualty contact cases within thirty (30) days of FCMT emailed notification.

   Note: The FCMT system will automatically send two emails for each survivor when contact is required.

   * The FCMT dashboard displays the RO's status of casualty contact as either pending, in progress, or completed.

   Note: Notification of casualty to the survivors has taken place prior to VA's receipt of the DD Form 1300. There must be no delay in providing benefit assistance to survivors upon FCMT notification.

   Released from Active Duty (REFRAD), also known as Chapter 13, refers to Veterans who have died within a 120-day period after being released from active duty.

   * REFRAD cases will be identified in block 4G, Duty Status, on the DD Form 1300.
   * REFRAD survivors may be eligible for the same benefits as the in-service death claims.

   Note: VBA is required to process separate Survivors' Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC) claims for a service-connected rating for the cause of death.

   * The RO CAC will process the First Notice of Death (FNOD) and request a Presidential Memorial Certificate.
   * The military CAO/CACO/CAR will explain Servicemembers Group Life Insurance (SGLI) death claim and Chapter 30 death benefit procedures to the survivors.

   Note: The CAO/CACO/CAR is responsible for sending the DD Form 1300 to the Office of Servicemembers' Group Life Insurance (OSGLI) for life insurance claim processing.

   References: For more information on notice of death processing procedures and memorial certificates see:

   * [M21-1, Part XI, Subpart i, Chapter 1, Section A - General Policies for Veteran First Notice of Death (FNOD) Processing](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174187/M21-1-Part-XI-Subpart-i-Chapter-1-Section-A-General-Policies-for-Veteran-First-Notice-of-Death-FNOD-Processing)
   * [M21-1, Part XI, Subpart iii, Chapter 1, Section D - Memorialization](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174203/M21-1-Part-XI-Subpart-iii-Chapter-1-Section-D-Memorialization)
   * [38.CFR §1312](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title38/part2/chapter13&edition=prelim). Benefits in certain cases of in-service or service-connected deaths
5. ### Contact with Surviving Spouses and Dependent Children

   Upon receipt of the FCMT email notification, RO CACs will contact the identified military CAO/CACO/CAR to determine specific survivor information.

   Note: The FCMT status will auto update to “in progress” when case work is started on each contact.

   * CAOs will initiate the first contact with the survivors by telephone.
   * Basic entitlements should be reviewed prior to the initial telephone call and/or visit.
   * Establish a time and/or place convenient for the survivor(s), if possible.

   Note: Telephone contacts may be the only option when providing assistance to survivor(s) in remote locations.

   * When an in-person visit is not possible, the CAC will verify the mailing address and send the survivor appropriate information via mail.
   * If agreed by the survivor, links to forms and brochures may be sent by email, in lieu of, or in addition to, mail.

   During the visit, the RO CAC will:

   * Provide information on survivor benefits to include VA Pamphlet 27-12-2 -September 2014 Revised, Summary of VA Dependents' and Survivors' Benefits.
   * RO CACs should provide the brochure to survivors if it was not previously provided. Stock replenishment requests should be sent to their station Publication Control Office or Support Services Chief.
   * Assist with completing DD Form 397, Death Gratuity, and VA Form 21P-534EZ, Application for DIC, Survivors Pension, and/or Accrued Benefits.
   * CACs will provide their contact information, the VBA National Contact Center (800) 827-1000, and provide referral information about other agencies, organizations, and support groups.
   * CACs will annotate FCMT indicating action(s) taken.
   * CACs will update FCMT to completed status.
6. ### Contact with Surviving Parents

   The CAO/CACO/CAR representatives determine surviving parent benefit eligibility. Contact with surviving parents will only be made if validation of Parents' DIC entitlement exists.

   * Parents' income and other relevant information will be obtained by the military CAO/CACO/CAR and will be discussed with the RO CAC before a visit is scheduled with surviving parents.

   If entitlement doesexist, contact must be made, and assistance provided in completing the most current version of VA Form 21P-535, *Application for Dependency and Indemnity Compensation by Parent(s).* The RO CAC will submit the application to the Philadelphia Pension Management Center (PMC) per [M21-1, Part II, Subpart ii, Chapter 3 - Claims Jurisdiction and Transfer](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174868/M21-1-Part-II-Subpart-ii-Chapter-3-Claims-Jurisdiction-and-Transfer#1).

   The RO CAC will advise parents of the availability of bereavement counseling.

   If entitlement does not exist, a visit is not necessary.

   Annotate the FCMT system if contact was or was not made to surviving parents.

   Resources:

   * [38.CFR §1315](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title38/part2/chapter13&edition=prelim). Dependency and indemnity compensation to parents
   * [Bereavement Counseling - Vet Centers (Readjustment Counseling)](https://www.vetcenter.va.gov/Bereavement_Counseling.asp)
7. ### DIC Claims for Processing

   * VA Form 21P-534a*, Application for Dependency and Indemnity Compensation by a Surviving Spouse or Child (In-Service Death Only)*
   * VA Form 21P-534, *Application for Dependency and Indemnity Compensation, Death Pension and Accrued Benefits by a Surviving Spouse or Child*,
   * VA Form 21P-534EZ,
     + The form requires the claimant's signature and additional information needed for claims processing.
     + The form will also initiate direct deposit, when requested.

   The RO CAC will submit hard copies of the DD Form 1300 to Philadelphia for DIC claims processing in accordance with the Data Sharing Agreement with the DoD.

   *Note:* The military CAO/CACO/CAR representatives have verified the Veteran's name, social security number, duty status; the name and address of the surviving spouse; the name, age (only for some branches), address of children; and in some cases, a line of duty determination (which is not used for processing claims).

   * Email the completed application to casualty.vbaphi@va.gov or faxed to (215) 381-3084.
   * Claims may be submitted by either the military CAO/CACO/CAR or RO CAC, based on who assists the survivor.
   * It also may be mailed to Department of Veterans Affairs, Centralized Mail Intake Center, Box 4444, Janesville, WI 53547-4444.

   Claims status inquiries and other questions should be emailed to the following mailbox: casualty.vbaphi@va.gov

   Parents' DIC claims must be made on VA Form 21P-535. The applications should be mailed to the Philadelphia RO.

   The address is:

   * Department of Veterans Affairs
   * Pension Intake Center
   * PO Box 5365
   * Janesville, WI 53547-5365

   * The Marine Gunnery Sergeant John David Fry Scholarship (Fry Scholarship) for children and spouses of Servicemembers who died in the line of duty after September 11, 2001.

   The RO CAC will assist in completing VA Form 22-5490, *Dependents' Application for VA Education Benefits,* and will fax the form to the Regional Processing Office (RPO) of jurisdiction, as follows:

9. ### SGLI Death Claims Processing

   The military CAO/CACO/CAR representative certifies Servicemembers' Group Life Insurance (SGLI) coverage by providing the following to Office of Servicemembers' Group Life Insurance (OSGLI):

   * DD Form 1300
   * SGLI Certificate of Coverage from SGLI Online Enrollment System (SOES) or SGLV 8286 Insurance Election and Certificate
   * SGLV 8283 Claim for Death Benefits
   * DD 93 Record of Emergency Data (optional)
   * Confirmation of last SGLI premium payment
   * Letters of Guardianship/Conservatorship for minor beneficiaries
   * When applicable, provide effective date if the member:
     + Separated/discharged
     + Reservist called to Active Duty
     + AWOL/Unauthorized Leave

   Branch of Service certifies Family SGLI coverage by providing the following to OSGLI:

   * SGLV 8700 Report of Death of a Family Member - indicates whether premiums are due for spouse
   * SGLI Certificate of Coverage from SOES showing the member had SGLI and FSGLI coverage
   * SGLV 8283A Claim for Family Coverage Death Benefits
   * Dependent's death certificate
   * Birth Certificate - dependent child only
   * Certificate of Stillbirth or Fetal Death - stillbirths only
10. ### Home Loan Guaranty: Certificate of Eligibility for Survivors

    Surviving spouses must be granted DIC to use the Home Guaranty Loan Program.

    The eligible surviving spouse should complete VA Form 26-1817, Request for Determination of Loan Guaranty Eligibility - Unmarried Surviving Spouses Part I.

    RO CAC completes Section B of Part II of the VA Form 26-1817 and will fax the completed document to the VA Home Eligibility Center. The Eligibility Center will make a formal determination of loan guaranty eligibility and notify the surviving spouse by mail.

    *Note*: Surviving spouses interested in using the VA Home Loan Guaranty Program should contact a lender who will ensure basic program requirements are met.

    *References:*

    * [M26-1 Guaranteed Loan Processing Manual - Web Automated Reference Material System](https://www.benefits.va.gov/warms/M26_1.asp)
    * [VA Home Loan Website](http://www.benefits.va.gov/homeloans)
11. ### Home Loan Guaranty: Financial Hardship

    Surviving spouses with a current VA loan who need assistance due to financial hardship are advised to contact their mortgage lending company immediately. The RO CAC must provide the survivor the VA Regional Loan Center (RLC) phone number (877) 827-3702. The VA home loan toll-free number operates Monday - Friday, from 8:00am - 6:00pm ET. Surviving spouses can also send the RLC a message online through the home loan guaranty support portal.

    [RLC Mailbox Pathfinder - VA Customer Support](https://yourit.va.gov/csm?id=rlc_pathfinder&spa=1&group_id=6c3b027b8766e51034d68737cebb354b)

    *Note:* VA does not make mortgage payments for the surviving spouse; a loan service representative may be able to suggest other remedies.
12. ### Bereavement Counseling

    Bereavement counseling is assistance and support to people with emotional and psychological stress after the death of a loved one and covers a broad range of transition services including outreach, counseling, and referral services.

    VA offers no cost bereavement counseling to the spouse, children, and parents of Armed Forces personnel who died while on active duty. Services are provided through community-based Vet Centers located nearest the survivors. Family members of reservists and national guard members who die while on duty are also eligible for counseling.

    Vet Center bereavement services are obtained by calling the Vet Center Call Center at: Phone - (877)-927-8387 e-mail - VetCenterBereavement@va.gov

    The [Location Finder](https://www.va.gov/find-locations/?facilityType=vet_center) can also be used to find the nearest Vet Center.

## 2.02 Elderly Veterans Outreach Program[Back to top](#toc) Change Date: October 1, 2024

1. ### Definition of an Elderly Veteran

   An elderly Veteran is defined as a person who served in the active military and who was discharged or released therefrom under conditions other than dishonorable, and who is 65 years of age or older.

   Priority claims processing is required for Veterans 85 years and older.

   For additional information on elderly Veterans, reference: M21-1, Part X, Subpart i, Chapter 1 - Priority Processing Programs and Special Requirements
2. ### RO Responsibility for Elderly Veteran Outreach Coordinators

   ROs are required to designate one Elderly Veteran Coordinator to provide outreach services and claim assistance for the elderly Veteran population within their jurisdiction.

   * ROs will ensure that coordinators listed in the [Elderly Veterans Coordinator Directory](https://dvagov.sharepoint.com/sites/VBABASqualitytraining/bastrain/data/siteassets/coordinator/frame_pages/evcframe.aspx) are kept up to date and any change submitted within two (2) business days.
   * Designations of alternate Elderly Veteran Coordinators are determined at RO levels.
3. ### Elderly Veteran Outreach Coordinator Duties

   The coordinators duties may include, but are not limited to, the following:

   * Maintain a broad knowledge of the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) benefit eligibility requirements.
   * Liaise with Veterans Health Administration (VHA) facilities to assist elderly Veterans with VA benefits applications and services.
   * Liaise with the local USAging representatives and other community partners.
   * Assist community partners by providing VA information and resources to include pamphlets and other information materials for their use and dissemination.

   For a complete list of coordinators, go to:

   * [Elderly Veterans Coordinator Directory](https://dvagov.sharepoint.com/sites/VBABASqualitytraining/bastrain/data/siteassets/coordinator/frame_pages/evcframe.aspx)
4. ### Elderly Outreach Hour Requirement

   RO Elderly Outreach Coordinators are required to conduct at least one (1) hour of elderly outreach per quarter.

   Examples of elderly outreach events are as follows:

   * Coordinate outreach events for aging populations sharing information on VA benefits and services, i.e., pension, Aid & Attendance, etc., that may be of interest to this population.
   * Conduct workshops on VA benefits, and other similar outreach activities.
   * Participate in town hall meetings with representatives from other community agencies for the elderly.
   * Coordinate with counterparts at VA medical centers in support of elderly Veterans.
   * Contact and visit nursing homes, senior service centers, assisted living organizations and other places with elderly Veterans.

## 2.03 Former Prisoners of War (FPOW) Outreach Program[Back to top](#toc) Change Date: October 1, 2024

### FPOW Outreach Hour Requirement

RO FPOW Outreach Coordinators are required to conduct at least one (1) hour of FPOW outreach per quarter.

1. ### Definition of a FPOW

   A FPOW is defined as a person who, while serving in the active military, naval, or air service, was forcibly detained or interned in the line of duty by

   * an enemy government or its agents, or a hostile force, during a period of war, or
   * a foreign government or its agents, or a hostile force, under circumstances which the Secretary finds to have been comparable to the circumstances under which persons have generally been forcibly detained or interned by enemy governments during periods of war.

   Resources:

   * [38 U.S.C. 101(32)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/101)
   * [M21-1, Part VIII, Subpart iv, Chapter 2, Section A - Former Prisoner of War (FPOW) Status](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000177470/M21-1-Part-VIII-Subpart-iv-Chapter-2-Section-A-Former-Prisoner-of-War-FPOW-Status#1)
2. ### Overview of FPOW Outreach Program

   The purpose of this program is to advise FPOW and their dependents of all VA benefits and services to which they may be entitled.
3. ### RO Responsibility for FPOW Outreach Coordinators

   ROs are required to designate one FPOW Outreach Coordinator to provide outreach services and claim assistance to FPOWs, their spouses and families within their jurisdiction.

   * ROs will ensure that coordinators listed in the [Former Prisoner of War Coordinator Directory](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/BAS/outreach/veterans/fpow/fpow-index.asp) are kept up to date and any change is submitted within two (2) business days.
   * Designations of alternate FPOW Outreach Coordinators are determined at RO levels.
4. ### FPOW Outreach Coordinator Duties

   Examples of FPOW Outreach Coordinator duties include:

   * meeting quarterly with FPOW groups in the RO's jurisdiction provided there are groups within that jurisdiction
   * developing a resource directory of VA and community resources who provide services aiding FPOWs
   * collaborating with VHA, NCA, Vet Centers, and other community organizations to conduct joint FPOW outreach events
   * planning activities for FPOW celebrations to include Missing in Action (MIA) family members
   * participating in community and stakeholder FPOW sponsored events
   * providing training and resources to organizations that support FPOWs
   * liaising with FPOW organizations and assist with obtaining speakers for their meetings and special events when appropriate, and
   * providing VA benefit, resources, and information in areas where FPOWs live or frequently visit to include ROs.
5. ### FPOW Outreach Hour Requirement

   RO FPOW Outreach Coordinators are required to conduct at least one (1) hour of FPOW outreach per quarter.
6. ### FPOW Claims

   FPOW Veterans receive priority claims processing. The FPOW flash will result in prioritization of the FPOW claim within the National Work Queue (NWQ) and prompt the claim to be routed to the RO nearest in geographical location to the Veteran or claimant.

   To assist the RO with timely FPOW claims determination and processing, the FPOW claims coordinator will:

   * Search the applicable series maintained on the Access to Archival Databases page of the National Archives and Records Administration website in the [Genealogy Personal History: Prisoners of War](http://aad.archives.gov/aad/series-list.jsp?cat=GP24) section.
   * Contact the FPOW or surviving spouse if information is needed to initiate or complete a claim or provide assistance when a form has not been returned, and
   * add the POW flash (if it is not already present) when a claim is received.

   *Note:* The VA Care and Benefits Teams (CBTs) for FPOWs consist of members from both VHA and VBA. The CBT provides mandatory training programs to FPOW coordinators and for liaisons between VA and FPOWs per VHA Directive 1650, dated July 31, 2018.

## 2.04 Homeless and Justice Involved Veterans (HJIV) Outreach Program[Back to top](#toc) Change Date: October 1, 2024

1. ### HJIV Program Overview

   In support of VA's goal of eliminating Veteran homelessness, it is VBA's policy to:

   * accept a Veteran's self-identification, or any other information obtained by VBA, as fact which indicates a Veteran is homeless, at imminent risk of homelessness, or formerly homeless,
   * have at least one full-time employee at each of the twenty-five (25) ROs identified with the largest homeless Veteran populations as required in [38 U.S.C. § 2003(a)](https://codes.findlaw.com/us/title-38-veterans-benefits/38-usc-sect-2003/) (a) assigned to oversee and coordinate homeless Veteran outreach and referral services,
   * conduct homeless Veteran outreach at each RO, even if an RO is not required to have a full time HVOC
   * provide priority claims processing for all claims for benefits received by a homeless Veteran or Veteran at imminent risk of homelessness,
   * coordinate outreach efforts by VA at the national and local level to Veterans who are homeless, at imminent risk of homelessness, or formerly homeless,
   * require each RO director to develop, implement, and maintain a location-specific RO HJIV program plan that identifies local resources, guidelines for referring Veterans to other agencies, outreach needs, station specific metrics, and claims prioritization and workload management procedures, and
   * maintain case management coordination and accountability mechanisms for reporting the number of currently homeless Veterans, Veterans at imminent risk of homelessness, and formerly homeless Veterans assisted through expedited claims processing, referral services, and/or outreach efforts.

3. ### Determining Veteran Homeless-Related Status

   VBA qualifies a Veteran as homeless when a Veteran self-identifies as such, to include selecting the homeless box on VA forms, or when there is an indication the Veteran is experiencing any of the following situations:

   * living in a primary nighttime residence not meant for human habitation, such as a(n):
     + car
     + park
     + abandoned building
     + train station, or
     + campground
   * living in housing designated to provide temporary living arrangements, such as a(n):
     + congregate shelter
     + transitional housing, or
     + hotel or motel paid for by charitable organizations or by federal, state, or local government programs
   * residing in an emergency shelter
   * exiting an institution without sufficient resources to prevent returning to such an environment
   * living with a family member or friend due to lack of own housing or economic hardship
   * fleeing or attempting to flee domestic violence when there is evidence the Veteran has no other residence and lacks the resources, including social support networks, to obtain permanent housing

   VBA qualifies a Veteran as at imminent risk of homelessness when a Veteran self-identifies as such, or when there is an indication, the Veteran is experiencing any of the following situations:

   * will imminently lose their primary nighttime residence, including:
     + owned, rented, or free permanent housing
     + shared housing
     + hotel or motel rooms not paid for by federal, state, or local government programs for low-income individuals or charitable organizations
   * lacks the resources or support networks needed to obtain permanent housing and no subsequent residence has been identified
   * resides in a public or privately operated institution
   * residence will be lost within 30 days of the date of notification of financial hardship to VA
   * has received notification that their right to occupy the current housing or living situation will be terminated
   * is exiting a publicly funded institution or system of care, including release from incarceration
   * is transitioning from active-duty service and has been identified as “at-risk”
   * has moved frequently due to economic reasons
   * lives in housing that has characteristics associated with instability and an increased risk of homelessness

   VBA qualifies a Veteran as formerly homeless when a Veteran self-identifies as such, or if there is an indication the Veteran is living in a fixed, regular, and adequate nighttime residence intended for human habitation and:

   * the adequate nighttime residence is considered a permanent place of residency, or
   * there is an indication the Veteran has the resources or support networks to obtain and sustain permanent housing without assistance from charitable organizations or federal, state, and local government programs, or
   * the Veteran is receiving support from VA's Homeless Program to maintain permanent housing, such as:
     + Housing and Urban Development - VA Supportive Housing (HUD-VASH) Grant and Per Diem Program
     + Support Services for Veteran Families (SSVF), or
     + other programs within VA that support Veteran housing

   *References:*

   * [42 U.S.C. § 11302 (a)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title42/chapter119&edition=prelim#:~:text=11302.,Funding%20availability%20and%20limitations.)
   * [M21-1.X.i.1.3, Claims Requiring Priority Processing Because of Extreme Financial Hardship](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000177939/M21-1-Part-X-Subpart-i-Chapter-1-Priority-Processing-Programs)
   * [VA Homeless Programs Website](https://www.va.gov/homeless/hud-vash.asp)
   * [Supportive Services for Veteran Families (SSVF) Website](https://www.va.gov/homeless/ssvf/index.html)

5. ### Jurisdiction of Claims for Benefits from Homeless Claimants

   The closest RO to the Veteran's physical address is considered the station of jurisdiction (SOJ) for claims from Veterans who are homeless or at imminent risk of homelessness.

   If the Veteran's physical address changes while the claim is being processed, the SOJ will be changed to the closest RO from the new address. The claim will be transferred to the new SOJ by the NWQ when ready for next action. As soon as a claim is noted to be in an SOJ other than the one matching the Veteran's address, the HVCC or designee will email the NWQ for action.

   Non-rating claims are not mandated to be processed by the SOJ and may be assigned by the NWQ to any appropriate station.

   *Note:* See [M21-1, X.i.1 - Priority Processing Programs](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000177939/M21-1-Part-X-Subpart-i-Chapter-1-Priority-Processing-Programs), for additional information on processing priority claims.

## 2.05 Indigent Veterans and Unclaimed Remains Outreach Program[Back to top](#toc) Change Date: July 1, 2025

1. ### Definition of Indigent Veterans and Unclaimed Remains

   Indigent refers to someone without assets sufficient to pay for the cost of funeral arrangements. Each state has an obligation to take care of the disposition of those who become the responsibility of the state as indigent Veterans, to include those who find themselves in state care such as:

   * Hospitals (to include mental health facilities)
   * Care homes
   * Penitentiaries and local jails
   * Homeless shelters

   Unclaimed Veterans remains are defined as Veterans whose:

   * remains have not been claimed by relatives and/or friends
   * estate does not have sufficient resources available to cover the burial and funeral expenses, and
   * burial is not the responsibility of any other Federal, state, or local agency.
2. ### RO Responsibility for Indigent Veterans and Unclaimed Remains Coordinators (IVURC)

   ROs are required to designate two (2) IVURCs to provide outreach services and process requests for funeral arrangements of unclaimed remains to include validation of Veteran status for a deceased person alleged to be a Veteran.

   * RO will ensure that coordinators listed in the [Indigent Veterans and Unclaimed Remains Coordinator Directory](https://vbaw.vba.va.gov/BAS/outreach/Coordinators.asp) are kept up to date and any change is submitted within two (2) business days.
   * Designation of alternate IVURCs are determined at RO levels.
3. ### Coordinator Duties for Handling Indigent Veterans and Unclaimed Remains

   IVURCs play a critical role in ensuring a dignified burial for deceased Veterans whose remains are unclaimed. Coordinator duties include, but are not limited to:

   * conduct no less than three (3) hours of outreach per quarter that is focused on disseminating information to the custodians of unclaimed remains
   * all IVURC outreach event details must be entered into eORT and events must be categorized as:
     + Event Type: Special Emphasis
     + Event Target: Indigent
   * develop and maintain a local resource directory of custodians of unclaimed remains, advocacy groups, and community organizations
   * develop and maintain partnerships with local VAMCs and National Cemetery Administration (NCA) facilities
   * develop and maintain partnerships with state and tribal cemeteries
   * provide Veteran benefit and survivor information to:
     + Local funeral directors
     + Local medical examiner's offices
     + Veterans Service Organizations (VSOs)
     + County VSOs
     + Local medical facilities and nursing homes, and
     + Local agencies of jurisdiction providing service for unclaimed remains
   * coordinate with custodians of unclaimed remains, such as local funeral homes, to provide burial services for Veterans whose remains are unclaimed, and
   * process requests for funeral arrangements for Veterans whose remains are unclaimed, to include validation of Veteran status per [M21-1, Part XI, Subpart iii, Chapter 1, Section B, 7](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174201/M21-1-Part-XI-Subpart-iii-Chapter-1-Section-B-Burial-Benefits#7).
4. ### Claims by Funeral Homes or Other Third Parties

   If a funeral home or third party who handled the arrangements for a Veteran whose remains are unclaimed files a claim for a non-service connected (NSC) burial allowance, VBA pays:

   * NSC burial allowance based on unclaimed remains
   * Reimbursement for transportation expenses if the Veteran is buried in a VA national or covered Veterans' cemetery

   The plot or interment allowance should not be paid to a funeral home or third party if the Veteran is buried in a state Veterans cemetery or another cemetery as listed in [38 CFR 3.1707](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3/subpart-B/subject-group-ECFR5b4f6562c054728/section-3.1707), as the plot or interment allowance is payable to the state, agency, or political subdivision of a state.

   *Note:* VA Form 21P-530EZ, *Application for Burial Benefits*, is used for burial allowance claims, as well as plot or internment allowance claims based on unclaimed remains.

   *References:*

   * [M21-1, Part XI, Subpart iii, Chapter 1, Section A](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174200/M21-1-Part-XI-Subpart-iii-Chapter-1-Section-A-General-Information-on-Burial-Benefits-and-Allowances?query=who%20can%20file%20a%20claim%20for%20a%20plot%20or%20interment%20allowance#3) - General Information on Burial Benefits and Allowances
   * [M21-1, Part XI, Subpart iii, Chapter 1, Section B](https://vaww.vrm.km.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_kanew/help/agent/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001034/content/554400000174201/M21-1,-Part-XI,-Subpart-iii,-Chapter-1,-Section-B----Burial-Benefits#7) - Burial Benefits

## 2.07 Military Sexual Trauma (MST) Outreach Program[Back to top](#toc) Change Date: October 1, 224

1. ### Overview and Purpose

   VBA established the MST Outreach Program to educate, empower, and spread awareness about benefits and services that may be available to this Veteran population.

   MST Coordinators assist Veterans who have experienced MST by:

   * conducting outreach and disseminating MST-related information,
   * assisting with navigating the process of filing a claim related to MST, and
   * providing trauma-sensitive customer service.
2. ### RO Responsibility for MST Outreach Coordinators

   ROs are required to designate one (1) female and one (1) male MST Outreach Coordinator to assist Veterans and Service members who may have experienced MST.

   * ROs will ensure that coordinators listed in the [MST Outreach Coordinator Directory](http://www.benefits.va.gov/benefits/mstcoordinators.asp) are kept up to date and any change is submitted within two (2) business days.
   * Designations of alternate MST Outreach Coordinators are determined at RO levels.

   *Note:* In line with trauma-sensitive practices, Veterans and Service members have the choice to correspond with either MST Outreach Coordinator based on personal preference.
3. ### MST Outreach Coordinator Duties

   MST Outreach Coordinator duties include, but are not limited to:

   * Provides outreach to MST survivors and other Veterans about MST-related resources and VA benefits and services
   * Interacts with MST Claims Processors as needed.
   * Serves as Veterans' primary VBA point of contact for filing their MST-related claim.
   * Serves as a resource for Veterans, Service members, and RO staff with questions regarding available benefits and services.
   * Connects Veterans, Service members and family members to MST-related resources and/or services available within VA and the community.
   * Provides case management by tracking claim progress to update the Veteran or POA as requested.
   * Establishes and maintains a network of community service providers and shares information about MST-related claims processing with Women Veteran Program Managers (WVPCs), MST Coordinators at VA VAMCs, Vet Center staff and community organizations.
   * Participates in and provides MST-related trainings, briefings, and support for MST-related events.
4. ### MST Outreach Coordinator Responsibilities

   Coordinators must maintain detailed knowledge about VA benefits, services, and the MST-related claims process. It is also important for coordinators to understand how their interactions impact the Veterans' experience.

   Coordinators are responsible for providing quality customer service through actions such as, but not limited to:

   * demonstrating professional, empathetic, and open-minded advocacy for Veterans and beneficiaries
   * building and maintaining a trusting relationship with Veterans
   * using a calm and composed tone when speaking with Veterans
   * using discretion to protect Veteran's privacy, and
   * educating Veterans about disability evaluation claim applications and applying for compensation benefits.

   | Often assigned as a collateral duty for Public Contact Representatives (PCR) or Legal Administrative Specialists (LAS)  *Note:* RO may also assign other positions to perform this role | Assigned to the MST Operations Center |
   | Designated to assist Veterans who have experienced MST | Designated to process MST-related claims |

   * providing updates to the Veteran and/or POA on file, and

## 2.08 Minority Veterans Outreach Program[Back to top](#toc) Change Date: October 1, 2024

1. ### Overview of the Minority Veterans Outreach Program

   The primary goal of the Minority Veteran Outreach Program is to promote the use of VA benefit programs by the following minority groups:

   * Asian American, Native Hawaiian, and Pacific Islander (AANHPI)
   * African Americans
   * Hispanic Americans, and
   * Native Americans and Alaska Natives
2. ### RO Responsibility for the Minority Veterans Program Coordinator (MVPC)

   ROs are required to designate one MVPC to ensure that information for VA benefits is provided to all Veterans, their spouses, and widows regardless of race within their jurisdiction.

   * ROs will ensure that coordinators listed in the [Minority and Tribal Veterans Coordinator Directory](https://dvagov.sharepoint.com/sites/VBABASqualitytraining/bastrain/data/SiteAssets/coordinator/frame_pages/mvcFrame.aspx) are kept up to date and any change is submitted within two (2) business days.
3. ### Critical Elements for MVPC

   To meet program objectives, MVPCs must

   * participate in any conference calls pertaining to minority Veteran outreach
   * develop a written operation plan outlining outreach and activities to be conducted targeting minority Veterans
   * conduct trend analyses of minority Veterans' concerns or issues and recommend proactive initiatives and corrective actions to resolve recurrent challenges to the RO director
   * prepare and submit activity reports highlighting outreach activities conducted and issues identified through tracking and trending analysis
   * participate in a minimum of two (2) town hall meetings annually and/or Veteran forums annually
   * meet with a minimum of three (3) different stakeholders quarterly to promote the use of VA programs and services, and
   * distribute and use culturally sensitive materials to inform minority Veterans and their families of the availability of VA services and benefits.
4. ### Duties of the MVPC

   The coordinator duties include:

   * establishing and maintaining contact with minority organizations within the local area(s) served by the RO to present information concerning VA benefits, hear possible concerns, and to answer questions
   * formulating and communicating program objectives that raise the consciousness and awareness of the Minority Veteran Program
   * supporting and initiating activities that inform and sensitize RO staff to the unique needs of minority Veterans
   * providing appropriate educational and outreach materials to targeted outreach groups
   * serving as a consultant and ombudsperson on issues pertaining to minority Veterans
   * keeping abreast of VA policies, laws, and regulations and their impact on minority Veterans
   * reviewing and evaluation of statistical and narrative information to determine the utilization of the benefits and services by minority Veterans
   * advocating on behalf of minority Veterans by making recommendations to improve service delivery with their RO
   * participating as appropriate on local advisory committees related to Veterans to ensure that minority Veterans' concerns are adequately addressed in decision-making processes
   * increasing local awareness of minority issues and developing strategies for increasing minority participation in all the VA service delivery areas
   * serving as liaison and advocate on the behalf of minority Veterans with VSOs; federal, state, and local agencies; and other local community stakeholders and serve providers to identify the unique needs of minority Veterans
   * keeping RO director informed of all related activities performed in the Ros jurisdiction, and
   * ensuring that monthly activity report is reported accurately and within established timelines.

   *Notes:*

   * The MVPC should also work with other VA staff to identify gaps in benefits delivery to minority Veterans and afford equal access to and knowledge of all benefits (disability, education and training, vocational rehabilitation and employment, insurance, survivors benefits and home loans).
   * The MVPC will also be responsible for working with tribes in providing benefits information and assistance.

   For a complete list of coordinators go to:

## 2.9 Rural Veterans Outreach Program[Back to top](#toc) Change Date: July 31, 2025

1. ### RO Responsibility for Rural Outreach Coordinators (ROC)

   ROs are required to designate one ROC to ensure that all VA benefits information is provided to Veterans, dependents, and survivors, in rural areas.

   * ROs will ensure that coordinators listed in the [Rural Outreach Coordinator Directory](https://dvagov.sharepoint.com/sites/VBABASqualitytraining/bastrain/data/SiteAssets/coordinator/frame_pages/rocFrame.aspx) are kept up to date and any change is submitted within two (2) business days.
   * Designations of alternate ROCs are determined at RO levels.

   *Note:* The coordinator must work with other VA staff to identify gaps in benefits delivery to Veterans in remote locations and afford equal access to and knowledge of all benefits (disability, education and training, vocational rehabilitation and employment, insurance, survivors benefits and home loans).
2. ### General Guidance on Rural Veterans Outreach

   The following procedures are to be followed when working with the rural Veteran population:

   * Contact the County Service Officer that serves the area. If there is not a County Service Officer, contact the Chamber of Commerce, City Council, or other civic organizations to ask if you can send pamphlets for display.
   * Coordinate to attend town hall meetings.
   * Create VA benefit information packages to leave at various locations such as colleges, post offices, libraries, and other places frequented by the general public.
   * Introduce yourself to various VA medical centers, outpatient clinics and arrange to attend any activities they have scheduled.
   * Request service organizations such as Veterans of Foreign Wars (VFW), American Legion (AL), Disabled American Veterans (DAV), etc. to sponsor events in rural areas.
   * Arrange to serve as speaker and provide information on various benefits available and how to apply for them.
   * Ensure the Mobile Vet Centers have VA benefits materials available to provide to Veterans residing in rural areas.
   * Announce upcoming events by researching free newspapers and community newspapers to place informational ads.
   * Attend additional meetings such as homeless stand downs. Coordinate these with the Homeless, Minority, and/or other outreach coordinators at the RO.
   * Coordinate with the MSC at the RO to provide information on VA benefits and services to reserve/guard units in remote areas.
3. ### Duties of the ROC

   The coordinator duties include:

   * Establish and maintain contact with rural organizations within the local area(s) served by the RO to present information concerning VA benefits, to hear their concerns, and to answer questions.
   * Formulate and communicate program objectives that raise the consciousness and awareness of the Rural Veteran Program.
   * Support and initiate activities that inform and sensitize RO staff to the unique needs of rural Veterans.
   * Provide appropriate educational and outreach materials to targeted outreach groups.
   * Serve as a consultant and ombudsperson on issues pertaining to rural Veterans.
   * Maintain awareness of VA policies, laws, and regulations and their impact on rural Veterans.
   * Review and evaluation of statistical and narrative information to determine the utilization of the benefits and services by rural Veterans.
   * Advocate on behalf of rural Veterans by making recommendations to improve service delivery with their RO.
   * Participate in local advisory committees related to Veterans to ensure that rural Veterans' concerns are adequately addressed in decision-making processes.
   * Keep RO director informed of all related activities performed in the ROs jurisdiction.
4. ### Critical Elements for the ROC

   To meet program objectives, the ROC shall:

   * participate in any conference calls pertaining to rural Veteran outreach
   * conduct trend analyses of rural Veterans' concerns or issues and recommend proactive initiatives and corrective actions to resolve recurrent challenges to the RO director, and
   * participate in a minimum of two (2) town hall meetings and/or Veteran forums annually
   * meet with a minimum of three different stakeholders quarterly to promote the use of VA programs and services.
5. ### Rural Outreach Coordinator Directory

   For a complete list of coordinators go to:

   * [Rural Outreach Coordinator Directory](https://dvagov.sharepoint.com/sites/VBABASqualitytraining/bastrain/data/SiteAssets/coordinator/frame_pages/rocFrame.aspx)

   VA recognizes the distinctive challenges and barriers facing women Service members and Veterans.
