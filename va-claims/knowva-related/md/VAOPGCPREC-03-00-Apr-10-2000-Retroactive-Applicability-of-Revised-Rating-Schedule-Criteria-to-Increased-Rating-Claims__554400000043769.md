Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 03-00, Apr 10, 2000, Retroactive Applicability of Revised Rating Schedule Criteria to Increased Rating Claims
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

When the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) issues an  amendment to a provision of its rating schedule while a claim for an increased rating is pending, what is the proper analysis for determining whether, and to what extent, the pending claim is governed by the prior rating-schedule provision or the revised rating-schedule provision?

When the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) addresses an increased-rating claim involving a disability for which the rating criteria have changed during the pendency of the appeal, should the Board make separate findings of fact and conclusions of law, and provide reasons or bases in its decision, with respect to application of both the old and the new rating criteria?

Where there has been a change in rating criteria during the pendency of an appeal, should all evidence of record be considered when determining whether an increased rating is warranted, or should only the evidence which pre-dates or post-dates the effective date of the change in law be taken into consideration when addressing the rating prior to and after the change in law, respectively?

**Impact on VBA**

This opinion confirms the holding in Karnas v. Derwinski, 1 Vet.App. 308 (1991).

**Summary on facts and findings**

This precedent opinion addresses the subject of retroactive applicability of revised rating schedule criteria to increased rating claims.  Although the opinion is written in response to a request from the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), it applies equally to regional office decision makers.  In brief, the opinion holds that when a provision of the VA rating schedule is amended while a claim for an increased rating under that provision is pending, the following analysis should be employed by VA decision makers:

(1) the decision maker should determine whether the intervening change is more favorable to the veteran;

(2) if the amendment is more favorable, the decision maker should apply that provision to rate the disability for periods from and after the effective date of the regulatory change; and

(3) the decision maker should apply the prior regulation to rate the veteran's disability for periods preceding the effective date of the regulatory change.

In addition, this opinion addresses the need to include separate findings and conclusions in decisions on determinations of which version of an amended rating-schedule provision is more favorable to a claimant, and rating of a disability using the rating criteria applicable for a particular period of time.  Finally, the opinion addresses what evidence VA decision makers are required to consider when addressing an increased rating claim in which an amendment has been made to the VA rating schedule.
