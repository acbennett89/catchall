Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 04-10, May 25, 2010, Clothing Allowance Benefit - 38 U.S.C. 1162
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

A veteran’s return to active duty does not terminate the individual’s status as a veteran under 38 U.S.C. § 101(2).

A veteran is eligible to receive a clothing allowance after returning to active duty, and may receive the clothing allowance while also receiving active service pay.

A non-veteran serving on active duty cannot receive a clothing allowance prior to discharge because the individual, who is not yet a veteran, does not meet the eligibility criteria for a clothing allowance under 38 U.S.C. § 1162, which directs the VA Secretary to pay a clothing allowance to a “veteran” because of a service-connected disability.

**Impact on VBA**

M21-1, MR, IX.i.7.1.a will be revised in accordance with the General Counsel Opinion.

**Summary on facts and findings**

These aforementioned holdings were rendered by General Counsel in response to questions posed by the National Program Manager, Prosthetics and Sensory Aids Service.  General Counsel noted that it had previously held in Precedent Opinion 10-2004 that a person who had previously attained veteran’s status does not lose such status merely because he or she reentered active duty.

General Counsel indicated that a veteran may become ineligible for certain VA benefits upon return to active duty, not because the person is no longer a veteran, but because statutes may otherwise limit eligibility.  For example, 38 U.S.C. § 5304(c) prohibits the payment of pension, compensation, or retirement pay for any period for which a person receives active service pay.  Thus, a person retains the status of veteran upon return to active duty, but may lose entitlement to certain VA benefits.

Obviously, a clothing allowance is neither retirement nor pension; however, General Counsel also determined that a clothing allowance was not compensation.  Although both clothing allowance and compensation are paid on the basis of a service-connected disability, clothing allowance is paid annually, whereas compensation means a monthly benefit as defined by Congress.  See 38 U.S.C. § 101(13).

General Counsel also had previously held in Precedent Opinion 10-2004 that VA should process the claims of veterans who have returned to active duty in the same fashion as it would had the veterans not returned to active duty.  Therefore, General Counsel concluded that a veteran’s successful annual application for the clothing allowance is not precluded by active service.
