**Decision Assessment Document**

***Bailey v. Wilkie*, 33 Vet.App. 188 (January 6, 2021)**

**U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)**

**What this case is about:** A Veteran may have only a single evaluation for prostate cancer residuals under DC 7528 based on either renal or voiding dysfunction, whichever is predominant, but may be evaluated for reasonably raised secondary residuals in other body systems. 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.155(d)(2) and 3.160 require VA to develop and adjudicate related claims for secondary service connection for disabilities that are reasonably raised during the adjudication of a formally initiated claim for the proper evaluation level for the primary service-connected disability.

**Impact on VBA:** The M21-1 Adjudication Procedures Manual will be updated at V.ii.3.A.1.a.  Recognizing Issues and Claims When Preparing a Rating Decision; V.iii.7.3.c. Rating Prostate Cancer; V.ii.2.D.1.a. Provisions for SC Under 38 CFR 3.310(a) and (b); V.ii.3.A.2.c.  Considering Complications of an Expressly Claimed Issue

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:** Veteran served in the Army from August 1954 to October 1974. He was diagnosed with prostate cancer in March 2013 and granted service connection at 100 percent disabling in August 2013. In September 2014, Veteran underwent an examination to determine the severity of the prostate cancer. He was in remission and was using five to six absorbent pads per day to control urinary symptoms.

In October 2014, the Regional Office (RO) proposed to reduce Veteran’s benefits from 100% to 60%. Veteran timely objected and submitted a private disability questionnaire (DBQ) showing, among other complaints, frequent bowel movements, diarrhea, and swelling of the ankles and feet. In November 2015, he was diagnosed with lymphedema of the lower extremities. The next month, the RO reduced benefits to 60%, and, in February 2016, Veteran timely filed a Notice of Disagreement (NOD).

Veteran underwent a VA examination in February 2016, in which the examiner noted Veteran needed to undergo a colonoscopy and provide the report to a VA examiner if he claimed a rectal condition related to his prostate-cancer radiation. The examiner also stated Veteran’s lower extremity lymphedema was caused by radiation for the prostate cancer and caused him to lose his job but did not assess the lymphedema.

The RO issued its September 2017 Statement of the Case confirming the 60 percent evaluation, and Veteran timely perfected his appeal to the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA). In January 2019, Veteran filed a claim for individual unemployability (TDIU) due to prostate cancer and lower extremity lymphedema.

In March 2019, BVA found the reduction to 60 percent to be proper and concluded the residuals of diarrhea and lymphedema were not due to the prostate cancer itself, but the radiation treatment, which was not contemplated under the prostate cancer rating schedule. Veteran appealed.

In response to the TDIU claim, the RO granted the bilateral lower extremity lymphedema secondary to the prostate cancer and TDIU, each effective January 30, 2019, the date of claim. Veteran filed a supplemental claim for an earlier effective date for both, which were denied.

The Court held that DC 7528 provided only two options once prostate cancer is in remission – rate on voiding dysfunction or renal dysfunction, whichever is predominant. Thus, non-voiding or renal residuals cannot be factored into an evaluation under DC 7528. However, when other residuals are reasonably raised as caused by or aggravated by the prostate cancer or its treatment, these must be addressed on the merits even in the absence of a formal claim under 38 C.F.R. § 3.155(d)(2), regardless of whether the Veteran files a formal claim for those residuals. The text, history, and purpose of §§ 3.155(d)(2) and 3.160 indicate that VA is required to develop and adjudicate related claims for secondary service connection for disabilities that are reasonably raised during the adjudication of a formally initiated claim for the proper evaluation level for the primary service-connected disability.
