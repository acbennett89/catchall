Decision Assessment Document
Tedesco v. Wilkie, Aug, 16, 2019, 31 Vet.App. 360 (2019)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that severe painful motion and limitation of motion under 38 C.FR. 4.71, Diagnostic Code (DC) 5055, are distinct concepts; however, limitation of motion is a factor that may be considered when evaluating painful motion.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court’s holding will be incorporated into guidance contained in the Manual M21-1 under III.iv.4.A.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran is rated 30 percent for a left knee disability under DC 5055.  He appealed an October 2010 regional office decision that continued the 30 percent evaluation.  He testified before the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) in January 2017 that he experienced knee pain six hours a day and relied upon a walker to get around.

The Board, in March 2018, determined that a higher evaluation was not warranted based on limitation of motion, as it found that the veteran’s range of motion was, at worst, 95 degrees of flexion and zero degrees extension.  The Board also found that no intermediate rating could be assigned because the left knee did not warrant more than a minimum rating for painful motion under the other diagnostic codes for limitation of motion.  The Board also observed that VA examiners found moderate weakness and normal strength.  The Board concluded that a disability in excess of 30 percent could not be granted because his disability “did not rise to the level of severe painful motion or weakness.”  The Board acknowledged the veteran’s reports of instability of the left knee; however, it noted that the joint stability testing conducted by VA examinations showed no instability and concluded that the medical findings regarding instability were more probative.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board erred by conflating severe painful motion with limitation of motion, which is not among the 60 percent rating criteria under DC 5055.  He contended that the Board did not discuss whether the evidence of record showed that he had severe painful motion to warrant a 60 percent evaluation.  The veteran also argued that the Board failed to properly consider his lay assertions of lateral knee instability in denying a separate rating under DC 5257.

The Court, analyzing the meaning of the 60 percent criteria under DC 5055, concluded that it is clear from the plain language of DC 5055 that severe painful motion and limitation of motion are distinct concepts.  The Court observed that, at the 60 percent rating level, the diagnostic code specifically refers to “severe painful motion” while it does not mention limitation of motion.  Also, at the “intermediate” level in DC 5055, it expressly differentiates between limitation of motion and pain, as it lists the two as alternative evaluation criteria.

The Court held that the Board erred in substituting a requirement of limitation of motion for the severe painful motion analysis contemplated by DC 5055.  The Court emphasized that the Board is not precluded from considering objective tests for limitation of motion in evaluating residuals of a knee replacement under DC 5055, but the Board must do more than merely cite those test results and state the corresponding evaluation under the pertinent limitation of motion diagnostic code to adequately assess the degree of painful motion under DC 5055, as limitation of motion is but one fact when assessing the disability commensurate with “severe painful motion.”

The Court found that the Board, in evaluating entitlement to a 60 percent rating, did not assess the severity of the veteran’s painful motion when it found that the veteran experienced pain in the left knee, but the “range of motion was not limited to compensable levels.”  The Court, describing the Board’s finding that severe painful motion was not present because the veteran’s range of motion was not limited by pain, concluded that this such a finding is not what DC 5055 requires for a 60 percent rating, as severe painful motion, not limitation of motion, is required to the 60 percent rating.

The Court, concluding that the Board provided inadequate reasons or bases to assess whether a 60 percent rating was warranted under DC 5055, remanded for the Board to properly apply the evaluation criteria of DC 5055.

The Court also found that the Board failed to explain why the medical findings were more probative than the veteran’s lay assertions of instability other than to assert that specific medical tests are designed to reveal instability of the  joints.  The Board’s explanation appears to rely on an understanding that medical evidence is inherently more probative under DC 5257, which the Court rejected in English v. Wilkie, 30 Vet.App. 352 (2019) (“Nothing in DC 5257 provides that objective medical evidence is required or is to be favored over lay evidence”).  The Court concluded that remand was also required for the Board to provide an adequate statement of reasons or bases for finding a separate rating under DC 5257 for instability was unwarranted.  (The Court rendered no opinion on whether a separate rating could be assigned for instability of the knee under DC 5257 apart from a rating under DC 5055).
