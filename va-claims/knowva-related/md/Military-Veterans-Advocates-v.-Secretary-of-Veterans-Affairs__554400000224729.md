**Decision Assessment Document**

**Military-Veterans Advocates v. Secretary of Veterans Affairs,**

**Case No: 2019-1600**

**United States Court of Appeals, Federal Circuit**

**7 F.4th 1110 (July 30, 2021)**

**What the case is about:**

In this consolidated appeal the Federal Circuit Court of Appeals (Federal Circuit) held: (1) 38 CFR § 14.636(c)(1)(i) is contrary to the plain and ordinary meaning of 38 USC § 5904(c)(1) and invalid; (2) 38 CFR  §3.2500(b) is invalid for contravening 38 U.S.C § 5104C’s clear statutory text, and; (3) 38 CFR § 3.155’s preamble is invalid to the extent that it excludes supplemental claims from the intent-to- file framework.

**Impact on VBA:**

VBA will issue guidance directing how the Federal Circuit’s holding will be implemented by claims adjudicators, the decision will be referenced in updated guidance contained in the Manual M21-1, and the invalidated regulations will be amended to comport with the Federal Circuit’s findings and mandates.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

In this consolidated appeal numerous petitioners collectively raised thirteen challenges regarding VA’s regulatory implementation of various components of the Appeals Modernization Act of 2017 (AMA).

The Federal Circuit devoted the first 33 pages of its 63-page decision to discussing the background and mechanisms of the AMA and resolving the issue of which petitioners in the consolidated cases had standing to prosecute specific challenges to contested regulations VA implemented concerning the AMA.

The Federal Circuit concluded that two veterans’ service organizations, Military-Veterans Advocacy (MVA) and Paralyzed Veterans of America (PVA), demonstrated sufficient standing based on claimed injuries to their members to bring three challenges relating to supplemental claims— one of the three review lanes established by the AMA.  Specifically, the Federal Circuit found MVA and PVA had standing to challenge: (1) 38 C.F.R. § 14.636(c)(1)(i), which limits when a representative may charge fees for work on supplemental claims; (2) 38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(b) which bars the filing of a supplemental claim when adjudication of the same claim is pending before a federal court; and: (3) 38 C.F.R. § 3.155, which excludes  supplemental claims from the intent-to-file framework. The Federal Circuit ultimately held that all three of those regulations are invalid for contravening the unambiguous meaning of the governing statutory provisions of the AMA.

Regarding 38 C.F.R. § 14.636(c)(1)(i) the Federal Circuit noted that Congress has thrice changed the triggering event for when attorneys’ fees may be charged, each time shifting the entry point for such fees—and thus a claimant’s ability to retain paid representation—earlier in the administrative appeals process. The Federal Circuit noted that these shifts, including the one enacted by the AMA allowing paid representation regardless of the form of review (i.e. supplemental claim, higher level review, or notice of disagreement to the Board of Veterans Appeals), could be seen as part of a continuing congressional effort to enlarge the scope of activities for which attorneys can receive compensation for assisting Veterans.

The Federal Circuit determined that § 14.636(c)(1)(i) treats supplemental claims arising pursuant to 38 USC § 5104C(b) differently from all other forms of administrative review authorized under the AMA, including supplemental claims filed under 38 USC § 5104C(a). Specifically, under § 14.636(c)(1)(i) supplemental claims filed under § 5104C(a), which are continuously pursued within one year of a prior decision, will be considered part of the earlier [initial] claim, such that attorneys may charge fees for any work performed on the supplemental claim.  But under that same regulation supplemental claims filed under § 5104C(b) (i.e. supplemental claims filed more than one year after the date of decision with respect to that claim) limits fees only to work performed after “an initial decision on [the] supplemental claim” itself. The Federal Circuit  found that § 14.636(c)(1)(i) permits claimants to receive paid representation for all work on a § 5104C(a) supplemental claim—including the preparation and filing of such a claim—but requires a § 5104C(b) supplemental claim to be first denied before paid representation is available. The Federal Circuit concluded that § 14.636(c)(1)(i) contradicts the unambiguous meaning of § 5904(c)(1), which permits paid representation for all forms of administrative review under the AMA, including § 5104C(b) supplemental claims. Ultimately, the Federal Circuit held that § 14.636(c)(1)(i) is contrary to the plain and ordinary meaning of § 5904(c)(1) and invalidated that regulatory provision.

Regarding 38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(b), the Federal Circuit acknowledged that regulation prohibits claimants from pursuing two concurrent lanes of administrative review for the same claim or issue. However, the Federal Circuit also found that § 3.2500(b) prohibits claimants from filing for administrative review of a claim “[w]hile adjudication of a specific benefit is pending on appeal before a federal court.” The Federal Circuit noted that the only form of administrative review that is potentially available for a claim already pending on appeal before a federal court is a supplemental claim. Thus, the Federal Circuit reasoned that the regulatory prohibition of § 3.2500(b) primarily affects claimants who have already appealed their claim to a federal court (from an adverse Board decision) but believe they have “new and relevant evidence” for that same claim that could entitle them to benefits. § 3.2500(b) consequently bars claimants from filing a supplemental claim with the VA based on that “new and relevant evidence” while judicial appeal of their initial claim remains pending. They must instead wait until the judicial appeal concludes to file any such supplemental claims.

The Federal Circuit noted that nothing in § 5104C nor any other provision of the AMA expressly prohibits filing a supplemental claim during a pending appeal in federal court. It consequently concluded that § 5104C unambiguously permits claimants to file supplemental claims while judicial review of the same underlying claim or issue is pending in federal court, and that no provision of the AMA requires completion of an ongoing judicial review as yet another hurdle to filing a supplemental claim. The Federal Circuit reasoned that § 5104C, read in its entirety, makes clear that a claimant whose initial claim is on appeal before a federal court does not have to wait until the completion of that appeal to file a supplemental claim. The Federal Circuit therefore held §3.2500(b) invalid for contravening § 5104C’s clear statutory text.

Regarding 38 C.F.R. § 3.155 and intents-to-file, the Federal Circuit noted that the regulation’s preamble expressly excludes supplemental claims but not initial claims from § 3.155(b)’s intent-to-file framework.  However, 38 USC § 5110(a)(3) states that the effective date of § 5104C(b) supplemental claims “shall not be earlier than the date of receipt of the supplemental claim.” 38 USC § 5110(a)(1) likewise requires that the effective date of an initial claim “shall not be earlier than the date of receipt of application therefor.” Citing the well-established canon of statutory construction that Congress is presumed to have intended for “identical words used in different parts of the same act . . . to have the same meaning,” the Federal Circuit emphasized that  VA had not offered any explanation for why it interpreted the substantively identical language in § 5110(a)(1) and § 5110(a)(3) inconsistently when it promulgated § 3.155. The Federal Circuit therefore agreed with PVA that § 3.155’s exclusion of supplemental claims from the intent-to- file framework is arbitrary and capricious and held that § 3.155’s preamble is invalid to the extent that it excludes supplemental claims from the intent-to- file framework.
