Decision Assessment Document
 Terry v. Principi, May 10, 2004, 367 F.3d 1291
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that the two-year limitation for accrued benefits in [38 U.S.C. § 5121(a)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5121) (as it existed before the passage of Public Law 108-183) means “any two years”.

**Impact on VBA**:

This case applies to the accrued benefits of payees whose deaths occurred prior to December 16, 2003, the date upon which Public Law 108-183 removed the two-year limitation.  M21-1MR, Part VIII, Chapter 3 will need to be amended to show that the two-year limitation (in effect for deaths occurring prior to December 16, 2003) for accrued benefits means ”a maximum two year period of accrued benefits regardless of when those benefits were actually accrued.”  Terry, slip op. 5.  Also, consideration should be given to amending [38 CFR 3.1000](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=03f4aefe355bf2756e5b88b1679767c8&node=se38.1.3_11000&rgn=div8) or its counterpart in new part 5.

**Summary of the facts and Court's reasons:**

The facts are as follows.  During his lifetime, the veteran was awarded a total rating based upon individual unemployability (TDIU) effective from April 14, 1986.  In June of 1995, he challenged the effective date of the total rating based upon an allegation of clear and unmistakable error (CUE).  He argued that April 1985 was the correct effective date.  While the case was on appeal to the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA), the veteran died.  In September 1997, the veteran’s surviving spouse filed a claim for unpaid accrued benefits, arguing that her husband’s CUE theory granted her an entitlement to the one year of unpaid benefits dating back to 1985.

The BVA denied the surviving spouse’s claim holding that [section 5121(a)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5121) only permitted payment of benefits accrued in the two-year period immediately prior to death.  The Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (CAVC) affirmed the BVA’s decision.  The Federal Circuit held that the clear and unambiguous terms of section 5121(a) do not so limit a survivor’s potential recovery.  As a result, the Federal Circuit reversed the decision of the CAVC and remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion.

Note:  The Veterans’ Benefits Act of 2003 amended 38 U.S.C. § 5121(a) to remove the two-year limitation period on a survivor’s recovery of accrued benefits.  P.L. 108-183 § 104(a).  Thus, the current version of section 5121(a) does not limit a survivor’s recovery of accrued benefits to a maximum time period.  The 2003 amendment to section 5121(a) was not, however, made retroactive.
