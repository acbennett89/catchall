Decision Assessment Document
Morgan v. Wilkie, May 16, 2019, 31 Vet.App. 162 (2019)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that VA’s duty to maximize benefits requires it to exhaust all schedular alternatives for rating a disability before the extra-schedular analysis is triggered.

The Court described the extra-schedular analysis as a “threshold analysis” intended to ensure that VA has satisfied its duty to maximize benefits by examining all possible rating methods in search of the highest level of established schedular compensation before resorting to the extra-schedular referral process, which is something that it is meant to be “exceptional” under 38 C.F.R. 3.321(b)(1).

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court’s holding will be incorporated into guidance contained in Manual M21-1 under III.iv.6.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran is service connected for hearing loss with a 10 percent evaluation.  He reported during an August 2014 VA audiology examination that he could not hear his preacher at church or his grandchild.  He also stated that he had to open his car windows to hear traffic.  The veteran testified before the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) that his hearing loss had worsened since the 2014 rating decision that assigned a 10 percent evaluation and he frequently had to ask others to repeat themselves.

The Board, in December 2016, denied an increased schedular evaluation for the bilateral hearing loss.  Regarding an extra-schedular rating, the Board indicated that it had considered whether referral for an extra-schedular rating was warranted.  The Board found that the hearing loss disability was manifested by signs and symptoms such as hearing loss, difficulty hearing individuals during conversations, and use of hearing aids, which are contemplated by the rating schedule as part of the evaluation for hearing impairment.  The Board stated that “neither the facts of this case nor the veteran’s allegations raise the issue of extra-schedular consideration, and a referral for an extra-schedular analysis is not necessary.”

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board erred by not referring him for extra-schedular consideration because the issue was reasonably raised by the evidence of record reflecting safety concerns from his having to roll down the car window to hear traffic while driving.  The Secretary argued that the Board did not err, as it was not obligated to consider extra-schedular referral because the functional effects of the veteran’s hearing loss did not reasonably raise the issue.

The Court, concluding that it was unable to determine whether the Board found the extra-schedular issue not reasonably raised, or in fact found it reasonably raised but then declined to refer the veteran for extra-schedular consideration, held that the Board’s statement of reasons or bases frustrated judicial review.  The Court further stated that it was unable to determine if the error was harmless because if the Board found the issue raised, that would be favorable finding that it could not overturn.  Accordingly, the Court held that remand was warranted due to the Board’s inadequate statement of reasons or bases.

The Court, having determined that remand was required, next considered the Board’s duty to maximize benefits and held that VA’s duty to maximize benefits requires it to first exhaust all schedular alternatives for a rating a disability before the extra-schedular analysis is triggered.  This is a threshold question intended to ensure that VA has satisfied its duty to maximize benefits by examining all possible rating methods in search of the highest level of established compensation as a schedular matter before resorting to the extra-schedular referral process.

The Court identified numerous schedular concepts that are critical components of the duty to maximize benefits before reaching an extra-schedular analysis, which include, but are not limited to, secondary service connection, analogous ratings, the requirement to assign a higher schedular rating if the disability more nearly approximates the higher rating, the requirement to resolve reasonable doubt in the favor of claimants, individual unemployability ratings, special monthly compensation, and the ability to rate a single disability under multiple diagnostic codes without pyramiding.

The Court emphasized that while this duty requires VA to search all avenues of schedular rating before resorting to an extra-schedular analysis, the Board is not required to discuss each of these tools in every case, but it must do so only when possible schedular alternatives for rating a disability are either raised by the claimant or reasonably raised by the record.  The Court noted that focusing on the full scope of schedular rating devices will significantly reduce the need to address extra-schedular referral, reserving it for those cases that are truly “exceptional.”
