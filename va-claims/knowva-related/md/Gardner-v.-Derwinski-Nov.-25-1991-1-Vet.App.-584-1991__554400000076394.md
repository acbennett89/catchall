Decision Assessment Document
Gardner v. Derwinski, Nov. 25, 1991, 1 Vet.App. 584 (1991)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that 38 C.F.R. 3.358(c)(3) is unlawful as exceeding the authority of the Secretary and in violation of the statutory rights granted to veterans by Congress under 38 U.S.C. 1151.  The Court noted that 38 C.F.R. 3.358(c)(3) requires either accident of fault on the part of the VA for the veteran to receive compensation for increased disability for any aggravation or injury from treatment at a VA facility; however, 38 U.S.C. 1151 has no requirement for a showing of accident or fault.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into guidance contained at Manual M21-1, IV.ii.2.G.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran underwent back surgery for a non-service connected back condition in June, 1986 at a VAMC.  He asserts that his left leg suffered severe nerve and muscle damage as a result of surgery.  His claim for compensation under 38 USC 1151 was denied on 1/26/89.  The rating board found no indication of negligence in the surgical procedure.  On 9/5/89, the rating was confirmed with the decision relying on 38 CFR 3.358(c)(3) which requires either accident or fault on the part of the VA for the veteran to receive compensation for increased disability for any aggravation or injury resulting from treatment at a VA facility.  BVA affirmed stating "we do not find evidence of fault on the part of the VA, or of the occurrence of an unforeseen, untoward event, resulting in permanent additional disability".  The Court reversed the BVA decision, holding 38 CFR 3.358(c)(3) unlawful as exceeding the authority of the Secretary and in violation of the statutory rights granted to veterans by Congress under section 1151.
