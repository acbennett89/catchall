First party individuals have an inherent right under the Privacy Act to their own information (or to the information of the person they represent). Public Contact Representatives (PCRs) must ask required ID Protocol questions to verify that they are speaking to the first party, or an authorized individual, prior to divulging protected information or making changes to a record. ID PROTOCOL GUIDELINES REPRESENTATIVE INTERACTIONS Representative Proofing Requirements Accredited representatives (i.e. Veteran Service Officers (VSO), County VSOs, private attorneys and agents), also known as Powers of Attorney (POA) for VA purposes, and VA-assigned payees (Fiduciary, Guardian, Custodian), are considered first-party inquirers and must be asked the same ID protocol questions as if they are the Veteran or beneficiary. Additionally, PCRs must:  For appointed VSOs, verify accreditation through the OGC Accreditation site and confirm the individual is accredited with the organization on record.  For VA-assigned payees, verify the full name matches the payee name on record. Note: POA access to the Veteran/beneficiary’s record is based on the restrictions of the representation form. IN-PERSON INTERACTIONS (STANDARD REQUIREMENTS) In-Person/Video Interview Proofing Requirements Verify the identity of the individual by reviewing a valid photo identification (State-issued driver’s license, Federal or State Identification card, etc.) If valid photo identification is not available, Veteran/beneficiary must confirm:  Veteran’s Social Security Number (SSN) or Claim Number  Veteran’s Full Name  Veteran’s Date of Birth  Veteran’s Branch of Service  Inquirer’s full name (if someone other than the Veteran)  Security Question (if individual has a VALID VA Form 21-0845 on record) Notes: • Further questions may be asked if you are not comfortable with the above responses. All responses must be verified in the system. • If Identity Proofing is completed without the use of a photo ID, telephone enhanced ID Protocol requirements must be completed prior to a change of address, change in direct deposit, change in telephone number, or change in email address during the interaction. TELEPHONE INTERACTIONS The ID Protocol used must depend on whether the caller successfully entered any information in the Interactive Voice Response (IVR) system with Computer Telephony Integration (CTI). PCRs must utilize the “CTI Requirements” protocol to verify any information provided by the caller via CTI. Cisco Finesse screens must be visible on PCRs’ monitors for quality review purposes. Further questions may be asked if PCRs are not comfortable with the caller’s responses. All responses must be verified in the system. PCRs may ask additional questions until the PCR determines if they are comfortable releasing/updating information. PCRs must enter a note in the system listing the additional questions asked. See the list below of additional questions that may be asked. If the update still fails, PCRs must advise the individual of alternative update methods, or the option to try a different account. • For other language indicating CADD system is unavailable, PCRs must apologize for the system being unavailable and advise the beneficiary of alternative update methods, or to call back later. ID PROTOCOL FAILURE & CHANGE OF ADDRESS/DIRECT DEPOSIT/CONTACT INFORMATION If the individual attempting the update is calling from a telephone number of record and standard or EIDP cannot be passed to process a requested change, PCRs must: • Apologize for not being able to change the address, direct deposit, and/or contact information at this time. • Provide the inquirer with the appropriate options to complete the request. o Ask if they have a VA.gov account to make the update: o If yes, advise that the change can be processed online using their VA.gov account. o If no, advise of the benefits of having an account and advise the caller of steps to obtain a VA.gov account. o Advise the caller they may call back once they have the information available to answer the ID Protocol questions. o For change of address/telephone number or cancellation of direct deposit, offer to email/mail/fax the VA Form 20-572, Request for Change of Address/Cancellation of Direct Deposit and provide the caller with information on how to access the necessary form online at the www.va.gov website. PCRs must also provide the caller with the appropriate return address and/or instructions. o For new or changing direct deposits, offer to email/mail/fax the individual the Standard Form 1199a, Direct Deposit Sign-Up Form, and advise that the SSN/Claim number must be in Box C, and Section 3 of the form must be completed by their financial institution. o The SF 1199a will be used for individuals without access to both telephone and internet (ex. some incarcerated beneficiaries, overseas beneficiaries, etc.) and for special circumstances (i.e. Fiduciary updates, files flashed for written updates only, etc). • If at any time the PCR feels that the requested change may not be authorized by the Veteran/beneficiary and may be an attempt to misroute funds, follow procedures in Potential Fraud Incidents – Suspicious Individual Attempting to Update Information. COMPLETED FORM VIEWABLE IN VBMS EFOLDER OR CENTRALIZED MAIL PORTAL (CMP) If an inquirer indicates previously submitting a VA form or written correspondence with change of contact or payment information, but the information has not been updated in the record, PCRs must review the eFolder and/or review the mail portal for the form or correspondence. If PCRs can view a correctly completed form or correspondence, they must: • Verify that new direct deposit information has been submitted on a completed VA form that has direct deposit fields (ex. 21-526ez, 21-534ez, etc.) • Verify the inquirer is the same individual who signed the form or correspondence • Ask the inquirer to verify the new address/direct deposit/contact information listed on the form or correspondence, and • Complete the required update to the record without the need for enhanced ID protocol. o If the Veteran/beneficiary’s record has a flash preventing update, PCRs must follow guidance in Potential Fraud Incidents – Information Update – “CADD – Written Request Only” Flash (02X Block). Notes: • Acceptable methods of notifying VA of contact information updates can be found at M21-1.II.iii.3.B.1.a. • If PCRs cannot view a correctly completed form or correspondence, or if the inquirer requests to update information that was not included on the previously submitted form or correspondence, enhanced ID protocol, as applicable, must be completed prior to making updates to the record. • PCRs must not process requests submitted via FaxZero. See Potential Fraud Incidents for additional procedural guidance. • PCRs must not advise Veterans/beneficiaries to submit claim forms (i.e., 21-526ez, 21-534ez, etc.) in order to update direct deposit and contact information. PCRs must advise Veterans/beneficiaries to use appropriate forms and alternative update methods (i.e., 20-572, SF 1199a, or VA.gov account) • Use of VA Form 24-0296, Direct Deposit Enrollment Form, was discontinued effective August 1, 2020. A grace period for processing receipt of the form was effective until October 13, 2020. SECURITY PERSONAL IDENTFICATION NUMBER (PIN) Veterans and survivor beneficiaries may utilize an optional Security PIN for processing changes to a payment address or direct deposit account through the National Contact Centers (NCC) and PCT. The security PIN is currently unavailable for apportionees or other dependents in receipt of benefits. Currently, the Security PIN may only be established and changed through the NCC/PCT. If a Security PIN is of record, all first party requestors authorized to make updates to a payment address or direct deposit information (i.e., authorized third parties, etc.) must provide the PIN as mandated by ID Protocol requirements. CRM/UD-O currently allows only one Security PIN to be established per record. If there is more than one survivor beneficiary, and a PIN is of record, PCRs must verify which beneficiary the PIN belongs to by reviewing the CRM/UD-O Solicitation tab and system notes. Solicitation and Establishment of Security PIN For each interaction with a competent Veteran or survivor beneficiary in receipt of benefits (Compensation/Pension, Education (Chapter 30, Chapter 33, or Chapter 35), and/or VR&E awarded), PCRs must review the record for a Security PIN: • If a Security PIN is on record, PCRs must utilize it as mandated by ID Protocol requirements. • If no Security PIN is on record, PCRs must review for a solicitation record of the option to add the Security PIN in the CRM/UD-O Solicitation tab under Vet History for the prior year (365 calendar days). o If there is a record documenting solicitation of the Security PIN within 365 calendar days, no action is needed. o If there is no record documenting solicitation of the Security PIN within 365 calendar days, PCRs must:  Provide the following information: • Option of adding a 4-digit Security PIN • If added, anyone authorized to make changes will have to provide the Security PIN when requesting changes to payment information through the NCC/PCT • The Security PIN cannot be removed, but may be changed • Would you like to add the Security PIN? o Optional script: “You have the option to add a 4-digit Security PIN to your record for added protection against unauthorized changes to your payment address/direct deposit information. If added, you, or anyone you authorize to make changes, will have to provide it when requesting changes to payment information through the National Contact Center or Public Contact Team. You cannot remove it from your record, but you can change it at any time. Would you like to add the Security PIN?”  If the Veteran’s or survivor beneficiary’s response to the solicitation is: • No, record the solicitation for “Security PIN” in CRM/UD-O. • Yes, PCRs must: o Complete basic and enhanced ID Protocol and ask two (2) additional protocol questions. The enhanced ID Protocol must correspond to the payment address type (mailing address or direct deposit account). If the Veteran or survivor beneficiary fails the enhanced ID Protocol or the two (2) additional questions, record a solicitation for “Security PIN” in CRM/UD-O and take no further action regarding the PIN. Examples of additional questions that may be asked (PCRs may select additional data points from the record, if needed):  Character of Discharge  Pay Grade at Discharge  Service number  Full name(s) of dependent(s) on award  Date(s) of Birth of dependent(s) on award  VA appointed Power of Attorney  Veteran’s date of death  One service-connected or non-service-connected disability, and percentage  Location of entry/exit from DD-214 o Ask the Veteran or survivor beneficiary to provide a 4-digit numeric PIN. The PIN may repeat numbers but cannot be four repeating numbers in a row, ex. 2233 is acceptable, 2222 is not acceptable. Remind the beneficiary they must remember this number and must safeguard it. o Enter the number into the Security PIN field in the Interaction screen and Save. o Verify a note documenting the action taken to add a Security PIN to the record was generated to include the name of the beneficiary who established the PIN and that it includes the 4-digit PIN. o Note: If the Security PIN is successfully added to the record during the interaction, a solicitation does not need to be recorded in CRM/UD-O. Exceptions: PCRs must not solicit for the Security PIN and must record a new non-solicitation exemption reason into the CRM/UD-O Solicitation tab due to one of the reasons below: • Pin-Death- Related: Used during death-related interactions (e.g., FNOD, death of dependent, etc.), • Pin- Supervisor Escalation: Used during interactions requiring transfer to the Supervisor Escalation Queue (NCC) or supervisor involvement (PCT), • Pin- Crisis: Used during crisis-related interactions (e.g., suicidal individual, physical threat, etc.), • Pin- Fraud: Used during interactions where fraud is suspected (e.g., potential incident flash is of record, 02X Error on record, or where PCR is required to document potential incident on VAF 27-0820), or • Pin- Failed ID Proof: used during interactions where ID Protocol required for PIN establishment has already failed or is known will fail, i.e., caller has already made known they do not have all required pieces of information. o This exclusion must not be used if enhanced IDP is failed during an attempt to add a Security PIN. In that instance, PCRs must record a new solicitation for “Security PIN” into CRM/UD-O even though a PIN was not successfully added. Changing the Security PIN Only the competent Veteran or survivor beneficiary may change the Security PIN number. For requests to change the Security PIN, PCRs must: • Complete basic and enhanced ID Protocol and ask two (2) additional protocol questions. The enhanced ID Protocol must correspond to the payment address type (mailing address or direct deposit account). Examples of additional questions that may be asked (PCRs may select additional data points from the record, if needed):  Character of Discharge  Pay Grade at Discharge  Service number  Name(s) of dependent(s) on award  Date(s) of Birth of dependent(s) on award  VA appointed Power of Attorney  Veteran’s date of death  One service-connected or non-service-connected disability • Ask the Veteran or survivor beneficiary to provide a 4-digit numeric PIN. The PIN may repeat numbers but cannot be four repeating numbers in a row, ex. 2233 is acceptable, 2222 is not acceptable. Remind the beneficiary they must remember this number and must safeguard it. • Overwrite the existing Security PIN field with the new number in the Interaction screen and Save. • Verify a note documenting the action taken to change the Security PIN was generated and that it includes the 4-digit PIN. Notes: • Procedures for changing a Security PIN apply to a record where a PIN was inadvertently added. Due to the inability to clear the PIN altogether, the Veteran or survivor beneficiary may provide a PIN of their choice. • After the death of Veteran with a Security PIN of record, the surviving spouse may utilize the existing PIN number, or may change it to a PIN of their choice. If the surviving spouse does not know the PIN of record, utilize Forgotten Security PIN procedures. Forgotten Security PIN If an individual authorized to make an update has forgotten the Security PIN, PCRs must: • Complete basic and enhanced ID Protocol (except for the forgotten PIN) and ask two (2) additional protocol questions. The enhanced ID Protocol must correspond to the payment address type (mailing address or direct deposit account). Examples of additional questions that may be asked (PCRs may select additional data points from the record, if needed):  Character of Discharge  Pay Grade at Discharge  Service number  Name(s) of dependent(s) on award  Date(s) of Birth of dependent(s) on award  VA appointed Power of Attorney  Veteran’s date of death  One service-connected or non-service-connected disability • Send the PIN Retrieval letter (blank letter with PIN Retrieval Quick Write) to the address of record. o Notes:  PCRs must not complete a change of mailing address prior to sending the PIN Retrieval letter.  PCRs may send the PIN Retrieval Letter to the surviving spouse’s mailing address, if different than the address of record. • Advise the individual of all options to update the payment address or direct deposit information. • Enter a note documenting who requested the PIN Retrieval letter and the action taken to send it. Do not include the 4-digit PIN. Did Not Request PIN Retrieval Letter If the individual reports receipt of a PIN Retrieval Letter they did not request, PCRs must determine if the PIN was disclosed to the proper Veteran/survivor. • If the PIN was sent to the proper Veteran/survivor, PCRs must review the record to determine who requested the PIN (i.e., authorized 3rd party, etc.) and advise the Veteran/survivor accordingly. If the Veteran/survivor indicates the PIN request/attempted update was not authorized, PCRs must send a VAF 27-0820 via encrypted email to VAVBAWAS/CO/FRAUDINCIDENTTEAM containing the following information: o Veteran/Beneficiary Name o SSN/Claim Number o Current contact information (phone number, mailing address, and/or email address) to ensure Veteran/beneficiary can be contacted immediately o Statement of alleged unauthorized request/update to the payment information o The subject line of the email must read: Unauthorized CADD/DD Attempt (include Veterans first initial of their last name and last four (4) digits of the Veteran’s social Security number in the subject line (e.g., D-2556)). The email must be marked with high importance (!). • If the PIN was sent to the incorrect Veteran/survivor, PCRs must follow procedures in Potential Incident/Privacy Violations for reporting a Privacy Violation. CREDIT REPORT/CLEAR REPORT/VET360 UPDATES If a claimant’s or beneficiary’s address is changed by VA due to a database report or VA Profile and the individual is requesting the address be reverted to the previous address of record, PCRs must: • Ask the inquirer to validate the full address they are indicating is the correct address. • If the inquirer can verify the full address for the previous address of record, the PCR must update the address and no additional ID Protocol questions are required. • If the inquirer is unable to verify the full address for the previous address of record, the PCR must attempt to process a standard change of address to include verifying all required enhanced ID Protocol requirements. Note: A review of the CADD History and/or Notes may be required to determine the source of the address change. ID PROTOCOL REMINDERS: • “Change of” contact or payment information includes the update of, addition of, or removal of an address, direct deposit, telephone number, and/or email address. • Always review flashes, flags, and notes in the system of records for pertinent information before releasing information or updating contact or payment information. • Protected information includes anything pulled from a living Veteran’s/beneficiary’s record except the current monthly benefit amount. For a deceased Veteran, protected information is anything pulled from the record except Veteran status, dates of service, branch of service, and location of gravesite in a national cemetery. Records of deceased Veterans may be released to next of kin if disclosures will not be injurious to the physical or mental health of the person on whose behalf the information is sought or cause repugnance or resentment toward the decedent. • All ID Protocol questions must be completely answered directly by the first party inquirer. If the first party provides verbal permission to speak to a third party, the first party must still answer the basic AND enhanced (when applicable) ID Protocol questions. o When information is provided via CTI, and the first party is present with a third party, PCRs may take the CTI entry as a first-party response and continue by verifying any remaining ID protocol requirements with the first party, along with obtaining verbal permission to speak to the third party. For example, CTI data is entered, but the third party is on the line. PCRs do not need to repeat the CTI-answered questions with the first party. PCRs must only complete the remaining ID protocol and obtain permission to resume speaking with the third party. • VA employees must provide a business need for the requested information and be verified in Outlook. While ID Protocol is not required, PCR must ask for enough information to be sure the correct Veteran’s record is accessed. • Responses to ID protocol requirements must be verified in the system of records. • After asking required ID protocol questions, PCRs may ask additional questions if they are not comfortable with the caller’s responses; however, PCRs may not substitute for any of the required questions if the inquirer is unable to provide the correct response. A note must be left in the system indicating why additional questions were asked. • If the inquirer is unsure of the necessary information, it is okay to provide helpful information on where to locate the necessary information – it is not acceptable to provide hints or allow multiple “guesses” to assist the caller with completing the ID Protocol requirements. o It is acceptable to advise the inquirer they can look at a bank statement, a recent letter from VA, etc. to find the monthly benefit amount or type of benefit, but telling the inquirer “higher/lower” with the payment amount, is not acceptable and will result in an ID Protocol error. o It is acceptable to ask probing questions, such as “do you have an additional period of duty” to determine if the inquirer has an additional period of service other than what is available in the system of records. It is not acceptable to allow the inquirer to guess multiple branches in order to pass enhanced ID Protocol requirements and will result in an ID Protocol error. o “Pension” is an acceptable response for Benefit Type when the beneficiary is in receipt of Survivor’s Pension. • The following actions are considered corrections and do not require enhanced ID protocol: o Misspelled words in the address/email address o Incorrect ZIP codes o Correction of address designation (ex. change from “street” to “drive”) o Transposed numbers for any direct deposit or contact information o Extra digits (only for a direct deposit account) • While ID Protocol is not required for FNOD calls, PCRs must ask for enough information to be sure the correct record is accessed. • If change of contact or payment information is of issue, enhanced identification protocol information, e.g., monthly benefit amount, date of birth, etc., required to make the change must not be divulged verbally or in writing. If answers to ID Protocol questions were provided prior to the PCR knowing CADD/DD was at issue, the CADD/DD update cannot be completed during the interaction, as ID protocol cannot be completed. • PCRs must ask the inquirer to provide the payment or mailing address, telephone number, or email address, and either confirm the information is correct or process a change if necessary (after all ID protocol is satisfied). • When verifying the account number of record, if the account number contains “leading zeros” (ex. 00012345), the caller does not need to verify the leading zeros. • When reviewing for a VA Form 21-0845 on record, PCRs may utilize a complete form that is viewable in the mail portal to complete ID Protocol. • On the VA Form 21-0845, the information provided in Item 6, "Name of Beneficiary/Claimant Who Is Not the Veteran" cannot be the same information provided in Item 10. • The VA Form 21-0845 signed by the fiduciary allows PCRs to provide information about the beneficiary, not the fiduciary. • When completing CTI ID protocol requirements, PCRs must ensure information displayed in Cisco Finesse (entered by the caller) matches the information of record. If the information does not match or is not populated, standard verbal ID protocol must be completed for the mismatched/blank items. o Cisco Finesse screens must be visible on PCRs’ monitors for quality review purposes. • Example of acceptable payment amount confirmation during enhanced IDP verification: Beneficiary is in receipt of $152.64. Acceptable responses are $152.64, $152, or $153. • If PCRs receive a call from a third party (i.e., a doctor's office or court of jurisdiction for fiduciary activity) and they have received a letter requesting evidence in support of the Veteran's claim/regarding fiduciary activity, PCRs can discuss only that letter with the third party.
