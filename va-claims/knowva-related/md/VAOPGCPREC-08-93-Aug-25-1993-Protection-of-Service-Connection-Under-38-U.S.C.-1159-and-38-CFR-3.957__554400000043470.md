Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 08-93, Aug 25, 1993, Protection of Service Connection Under 38 U.S.C. 1159 and 38 CFR 3.957
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Do the provisions of 38 U.S.C. § 1159 and 38 C.F.R. § 3.957 protect an award of dependency and indemnity compensation (DIC) under which benefits have been paid for over ten years but which was erroneously made in light of a rating-board decision that the veteran's death was not service connected?

**Impact on VBA**

The provisions of 38 U.S.C. § 1159 and 38 CFR § 3.957 do not protect an award of dependency and indemnity compensation under which benefits have been paid for over ten years but which was erroneously made in light of a rating board decision that the veteran's death was not service-connected.

**Summary on facts and findings**

In August 1982, a rating decision denied service connection for the cause of the veteran's death.  Despite this decision, award action was taken, erroneously, to award Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC) to the surviving spouse.  Two letters were sent to her advising that the claim for DIC had been approved.  DIC has been paid continuously since July 1, 1982.  An administrative decision in January 1993 concluded that the award was solely an administrative error made by VA.  An opinion was requested on the applicability of 38 U.S.C. § 1159 and 38 CFR § 3.957 to the award of DIC.

Section 1159 provides that service connection for any disability or death granted under title 38 which has been in force for ten or more years shall not be severed except upon a showing that the original grant of service connection was based on fraud or if the person did not have the requisite service or character of discharge.  In considering the plain meaning of the statute, it is clear that there be an actual determination of service connection for the protection afforded by the statute to apply.  If Congress had intended for protection to refer to the award of benefits or the period of payment, rather than the finding of service connection, it could easily have done so.  As an actual determination of service connection is lacking in this case, Section 1159 does not provide protection.  The protection extended by Section 1159 has not been extended to the erroneous payment of benefits when the requisite rating was not made.

Recommend that a copy of this assessment document and a copy of the opinion be furnished the ROs.
