Decision Assessment Document
Spencer v. Brown, Feb 5, 1994, 17 F. 3d 368 ( Fed. Cir. 1994)
U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that the Veterans' Judicial Review Act (VJRA) is not a new basis of entitlement to claimed benefits permitting de novo review of claim on the basis of the same evidence previously presented, and that a determination of the Court of Veterans Appeals (Court) that veteran had not presented new evidence was not erroneous.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Federal Circuit's holding has been incorporated into guidance contained in the Manual under III.iv.5.C.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran had active service from March 1944 to February 1946.  In November 1976, the veteran claimed service connection for multiple sclerosis (MS).  In May 1978, the Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) upheld the denial and an expanded panel of the Board in 1980 in a reconsideration decision continued the denial of service connection.  After submission of additional evidence, the denial was upheld by Boardin October 1984 and again in March 1987.  In July 1990, the Board again upheld the previous denials because the additional evidence presented by the veteran did not reasonably establish that the MS was the result of his active service.

The veteran argued that VA is required to review previously and finally denied claims de novo, regardless of whether new and material evidence has been presented or secured, when there has been an intervening liberalization of a law affecting entitlement to benefits and that the VJRA is such a law.  The veteran asserted that the multiple number of provisions contained in the VJRA require VA, upon application, to adjudicate de novo any claim that had been previously and finally denied prior to the law's enactment.

The veteran appealed the July 1990 Board decision to the Court on August 10, 1990. The veteran argued that he was entitled to de novo review of his previously and finally disallowed claim, irrespective of whether new and material evidence had been presented. Specifically, the veteran asserted that the Veterans' Judicial Review Act of 1988 (VJRA), Pub.L. No. 100–687, 102 Stat. 4105 (1988), created a new substantive right of entitlement to disability compensation and that the Board was required to review his claim de novo under 38 U.S.C. 5110(g), which, according to the veteran, provided an exception to the finality provision of 38 U.S.C. 7104(b) where there has been a statutory or regulatory change in veterans' benefits law affecting a previously and finally disallowed claim.

The Court agreed that section 5110(g) “appears to be contingent upon, and thus to presuppose, the existence” of a right to de novo adjudication of a previously and finally denied claim “where an intervening change in law or regulation has created a new basis of entitlement to a benefit.” 4 Vet.App. at 288, 289. However, the Court rejected the veteran's assertion that he was entitled to de novo review of his claim under the VJRA, because that legislation did not effect an intervening change in law that substantively affected the veteran's claim for service-connected disability compensation such that a new basis for entitlement was created. In affirming the Board's decision, the court also held that the veteran did not present new and material evidence justifying reopening of his previously and finally disallowed claim.

The Federal Circuit noted that it is clear that the VJRA instituted changes in the veterans' benefits system and expanded the rights of veterans relating to the adjudication of their claims, including the right to seek judicial review, the right to have reasonable doubt resolved in favor of the claimant, and the right to an agency decision based on the evidence of record. However, we do not agree that such changes provided the veteran with a new basis of entitlement to his claimed benefits. Although the changes occasioned by the enactment of the VJRA were indeed significant, such changes were unmistakably procedural in nature.

The Federal Circuit held that held that the VJRA is not a new basis of entitlement to claimed benefits permitting de novo review of claim on the basis of the same evidence previously presented.

The Federal Circuit also held that it perceived no error in the legal analysis used by the Court in resolving the new and material evidence issue. In the absence of such error, the Federal Circuit stated that it could not disturb the Court's holding that there was no new and material evidence presented after the Board's 1987 decision that would entitle the veteran to a reopening of his claim, as that would require us to review the application of law to the facts of this case, concerning which it had no jurisdiction.The Federal Circuit held that the determination of the Court that the veteran had not presented new evidence was not erroneous.

In conclusion, the Federal Circuit held that the Court, in affirming the decision of the Board, did not err in holding that the veteran was not entitled to de novo review of his finally disallowed claim for service-connected disability compensation for MS under the Veterans' Judicial Review Act of 1988. Additionally, the Federal Circuit held that the Court did not err in determining that the veteran failed to present new and material evidence warranting reopening of his claim.
