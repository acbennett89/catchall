Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 01-96, Feb 17, 1996, Effective Dates Where Award to a Surviving Spouse Results in Termination of an Award Based on a Deemed Valid Marriage
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Do the provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.114(b) apply to cases in which benefits are reduced or terminated as the result of a judicial precedent?  If so, when, in such cases, benefits are awarded to one individual as the surviving spouse of a veteran and discontinued to another individual previously awarded benefits based on a "deemed valid" marriage to the veteran, is the effective date of the discontinuance of the latter individual's benefits governed by 38 C.F.R. § 3.114(b) or 38 C.F.R. § 3.657(a)?

**Impact on VBA**

The provisions of 38 U.S.C. 5112(b)(6) and 38 C.F.R 3.114(b), which govern the effective date of a reduction or discontinuance of benefits by reason of a change in law or or administrative issue or a change in interpretation of a law or administrative issue, are applicable where the change in the interpretation of the law is the result of a judicial precedent.

When, as the result of such a change in interpretation, an award of benefits is established for one individual as the legal surviving spouse of a veteran and discontinued for another individual, who had previously received benefits based on a deemed valid marriage, the effective date is governed by 38 U.S.C. 5110(a), which provides for the establishment of an effective date based on the facts found, but not earlier than the date of receipt of an application for benefits.  The effective date of the discontinuance to the prior payee is governed by 38 U.S.C. 5112(b)(6) and 38 C.F.R 3.114(b).  If application of 38 C.F.R. 3.657(a) would, in a particular case, suggest that the prior payees award be terminated at a date earlier than that provided by 38 U.S.C. 5112(b)(6), the regulation is superseded by 38 U.S.C. 5112(b)(6).  If a concurrent payment required by 38 U.S.C. 5110(a) and 5112(b)(6) would conflict with 38 U.S.C. 103(a), that conflict should be resolved in favor of the beneficiary. In this type of case, it is appropriate to provide payments concurrently to the prior payee and the legal spouse as required by 38 U.S.C. 5110(a) and 5112(b)(6).

**Summary on facts and findings**

The veteran was married three times with no indication that any of the marriages were legally terminated.  In 1968, VA awarded death benefits to the veteran's third "spouse" on a deemed valid marriage.  In 1969, the veteran's first spouse claimed entitlement to benefits as the veteran's surviving spouse. VA determined that although she might not have been at fault in the initial separation, she was not faultless in the continued estrangement and was not entitled to benefits as the veteran's surviving spouse.

In a May 13, 1993, precedential decision Gregory v. Brown, the Court of Veterans Appeals held that 38 U.S.C 101(3), which defines surviving spouse for Title 38 purposes, requires that to attain that status, a purported spouse who was separated from the veteran prior to the veteran's death must have been without fault only in the initial separation, not with regard to the continued estrangement. Additionally, the Court held unlawful that portion of 38 C.F.R. 3.53(a) which did not require the separation to have been due to the misconduct of or procured by the veteran.  On March 20, 1995, the veteran's first wife filed a claim for benefits as the veteran's surviving spouse. The regional office believed that the first wife would be entitled to benefits as the surviving spouse and referred the case to Central Office for an advisory opinion as to who should be paid, when benefits to the current spouse should be terminated and what would be the effective date of the first wife's award.

Mention this opinion in the next quarterly training letter.  The procedures in M21-1, Part IV, chapter 5 already cover this situation.  Regional offices should be reminded that this type of case is a contested claim.
