Decision Assessment Document
Hayes v. Brown, Apr 10, 1996, 9 Vet.App. 73 (1996)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that when VA reduces a rating without observing the applicable laws and regulations, the rating is voided, the reduction is set aside as not being in accord with the law, and the original rating is restored.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorported into guidance contained in the Manual M21-1, III.iv.8.D.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran served on active duty from 1943 to  1945, and was held as a POW from November 1944 to May 1945.  Immediately after discharge, the veteranwas service connected for frozen feet and initially rated as 100% disabling and then gradually reduced until a 10% rating was assigned in May 1955.  In September 1979, the regional office granted service connection for a pulmonary embolism at the 10% level.  The veteran filed a NOD and VA further developed the case, during which the issue of thrombophlebitis of the right leg was raised.  In September 1981, the RO granted service connection for thrombophlebitis of the right leg at 10%.  In February 1990, the appellant sought service connection for "arthritis" and a "gastrointestinal" disorder and subsequently added claims for rating increases for all of his service-connected disabilities.  In July 1990 the RO denied service connection for arthritis and a gastrointestinal condition, continued the 10% rating for frozen feet and reduced the ratings for pulmonary embolus and thrombophlebitis from 10% to 0%.  In April 1991 he had a hearing.  In February 1992, the hearing officer reinstated the 10% rating for thrombophlebitis, confirmed the reduction to 0% for pulmonary embolus and confirmed the denial of service connection for arthritis and a stomach disorder.  On February 15, 1994, the Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) granted service connection for traumatic arthritis of the ankles, denied service connection for multiple joint degenerative arthritis and a stomach disorder and denied increased ratings for frozen feet, a pulmonary embolus (rated as 0% disabling) and right thrombophlebitis (rated as 10% disabling).

The Court found that there was a plausible basis to deny an increased rating for frozen feet and to deny service connection for a gastrointestinal disorder.  The Court found that no plausible basis existed in the record for the denial of the claim for degenerative arthritis of multiple joints and that this portion of the Board decision was clearly erroneous and remanded the issue.  Regarding the pulmonary embolism issue, the Court found that the Board omitted the reasons and bases on which the reduction was based.  Where VA reduces a rating without observing the applicable laws and regulations, the rating is void and the Court sets aside the decision as not being in accord with the law.  Since VA did not follow the requirements, the decision was reversed and VA was instructed to reinstate the 10% rating from the date of reduction.  In the claim for thrombophlebitis, the Court remanded the issue because the Board substituted its own medical judgment for independent medical evidence.  The Board must provide reasons for rejecting the medical authority supporting  a claim and must provide a medical basis, other than its own unsubstantiated conclusions to support its ultimate decision.  BVA did not do this, and the Court vacated the Board decision and remanded this claim for further action
