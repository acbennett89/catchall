Decision Assessment Document
 Read v. Shinseki, Jun 22, 2011, 651 F.3d 1296
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that service connection for a disability is not severed simply because the situs of a disability, or the diagnostic code associated with it, is corrected to more accurately determine the benefit to which a veteran may be entitled for a service connected disability.  There is no violation of [38 U.S.C. § 1159](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1159) if VA undertakes such action.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, who sustained a gunshot wound (GSW) of the right thigh during his July 1967 to July 1969 service, filed for service connection for a right thigh GSW in 1995.  The regional office (RO) granted service connection for residuals of a right thigh GSW and assigned a 10 percent evaluation under [38 C.F.R. § 4.73](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=15a6389c31eeef746d61192987a558ee&node=se38.1.4_173&rgn=div8), Diagnostic Code (DC) 5313 (Muscle Group XIII, posterior thigh group).  The veteran appealed the decision.  A December 1996 VA examination contained a diagnostic impression of “residuals of [GSW], inner medial proximal thigh right.”  In 2002, the RO maintained the 10 percent evaluation under DC 5313 and the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) later affirmed the decision.

The Veterans Court, in February 2005, remanded the case for an examination to address the nature and extent of any muscle injuries resulting from the right thigh GSW.  A VA medical examiner in December 2005 found that only Muscle Group (MG) XV (mesial thigh group) was involved with impairment of mild to moderate weakness.  The RO subsequently determined that the right thigh GSW would be properly and accurately rated under DC 5315, rather than DC 5313.  The RO also indicated that the rating criteria was the same whether DC 5313 or DC 5315 was used and no change in benefits resulted.  The Board affirmed the RO decision.

The Veterans Court also affirmed, rejecting the veteran’s argument that the change to a diagnostic code representing an injury to MG XV, rather than MG XIII, was an impermissible severance of service connection to MG XIII, which was protected under section 1159.

The Federal Circuit noted that it had previously held that one of the five common elements in a claim for benefits was “a connection between the veteran’s service and the disability (service connection).”  *See [Collaro v.](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/136/136.F3d.1304.97-7049.html)* [*West*, 136 F.3d 1304, 1307 (Fed. Cir. 1998)](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/136/136.F3d.1304.97-7049.html) (other elements consist of status as a veteran and existence, degree, and effective date of disability).  Thus, the Federal Circuit concluded that section 1159 only protects the service connection element and to sever service connection is to conclude that a disability previously determined to have been incurred in service was incurred otherwise.  Since section 1159 is not directed to the element concerning existence, or fact, of a disability, a change in the determination of applicable diagnostic code is unprotected.

The Federal Circuit agreed with the Government’s argument that because the same disability – residuals of a right thigh GSW – was involved in both the initial disability determination, without a specific diagnosis of MG XIII, and the later specific identification of MG XV, the change in diagnostic code did not sever anything.

The Federal Circuit affirmed the Veterans Court determination that the VA did not violate [section 1159](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1159) by changing the situs of the veteran’s disability from MG XIII to MG XIV.
