Decision Assessment Document
 Kowalski v. Nicholson, Jun 8, 2005, 19 Vet.App. 171
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

In connection with this veteran’s claim for service connection for hearing loss, the Court found, among other things, that the appellant had not demonstrated good cause for failing to appear for a VA examination as required by [§ 3.655(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=7e4b46615bffa9c0459882ca4daf4a45&node=se38.1.3_1655&rgn=div8).

**Impact on VBA:**

This case is important as guidance regarding how VA should proceed when a veteran refuses to report for a VA examination in connection with a claim for service connection.

****Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:****

In this case, a 1999 VA examination did not show any hearing-loss disability.  Thereafter the appellant requested that the report of the 1999 examination be expunged from the record as inadequate, and he provided a private audiologist’s opinion of dubious quality.  When VA scheduled the appellant for another examination, his counsel advised that the appellant would not submit to any further VA examinations absent compelling reasons.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals concluded that the appellant’s refusal to submit to examination constituted an abandonment of his claim.  Alternatively, it rejected the audiologist’s opinion as lacking probative value, and denied the claim based on the remaining evidence of record.

The Court noted that the Board erred in relying on the abandonment regulation—[38 C.F.R. § 3.158](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=7e4b46615bffa9c0459882ca4daf4a45&node=se38.1.3_1158&rgn=div8)—instead of on 38 C.F.R. § 3.655 pertaining to failure to report for examination.  It found the error to be harmless, however, as the Board proceeded to address the merits of the claim based on the evidence of record.  Nonetheless, the Court ultimately remanded the matter, concluding that the Board had provided an insufficient basis for rejecting the audiologist’s opinion.

Importantly, the Court found that the appellant had not demonstrated good cause for failing to appear for VA examination, as required by § 3.655(a).  Regarding the audiologist’s evidence, the Court noted that there was no indication that the audiologist was a “physician” within the meaning of [38 U.S.C. § 5125](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5125), which permits VA to accept evidence from private physicians in lieu of VA examinations, and, regardless, VA is not required to rely on the audiologist’s opinion alone, unless failure to do so would be arbitrary and capricious.  Here the Court found that VA’s attempt to obtain further evidence to support the claim was justifiable.
