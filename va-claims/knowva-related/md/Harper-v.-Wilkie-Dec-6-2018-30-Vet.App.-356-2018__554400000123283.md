Decision Assessment Document
Harper v. Wilkie, Dec 6, 2018, 30 Vet.App. 356 (2018)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that a claim for total disability evaluation due to individual unemployability (TDIU), which has been awarded, remains in appellate status if the TDIU award is not effective from the date of claim for the underlying disability rating that is on appeal.  If the TDIU award is not retroactive to the effective date of the underlying disability rating on appeal, the TDIU award is only a partial grant and remains in appellate status.  A Notice of Disagreement (NOD) is not required to place the TDIU issue in appellate status.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court’s holding will be incorporated into guidance contained in Manual M21-1, IV.ii.2.F.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran filed for service connection for PTSD in August 2008.  The RO, in December 2008, awarded service connection for PTSD with a 30 percent evaluation.  The veteran timely filed a NOD in response to the assigned evaluation.  In July 2012, the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), increased the evaluation for PTSD to 50 percent.  The Court, in February 2013, granted a joint motion for partial remand (JPMR) in which the parties agreed that the Board provided inadequate reasons or bases for its denial of a rating in excess of 50 percent.  The Board subsequently remanded the case back to the RO for additional development.

In February 2014, the veteran, asserting that he was unable to secure or follow substantially gainful employment due to PTSD, filed for a TDIU evaluation.  The RO, in December 2015, awarded a 70 percent evaluation for PTSD from December 2015.  In May 2016, the RO awarded a TDIU evaluation from February 11, 2016.  The veteran did not file a NOD in response to the TDIU award.

The Board, in July 2016, denied a disability evaluation for PTSD in excess of 50 percent prior to December 2015 and concluded that the TDIU issue was not part of the underlying appeal because the veteran did not appeal the May 2016 RO decision that awarded TDIU effective February 11, 2016.  The Board also denied entitlement to an evaluation higher than 70 percent for PTSD; however, the veteran did not challenge that decision.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board erred in concluding that the issue of entitlement to TDIU prior to February 2016 was not on appeal.  The veteran asserted that, pursuant to Rice v. Shinseki, 22 Vet.App. 447 (2009), the issue of entitlement to TDIU became “part and parcel” of his appeal for a higher initial disability rating for PTSD in February 2014 when he explicitly raised the issue while his appeal was pending.

The Court concluded that the Board had jurisdiction over the issue of entitlement to TDIU prior to February 2016, because the issue of TDIU became part and parcel of the underlying PTSD claim and the RO’s grant of TDIU did not bifurcate the appeal.  The Court explained that the veteran did not need to appeal the May 2016 RO decision awarding TDIU because the issue of entitlement to TDIU became part and parcel of the underlying PTSD claim and the RO’s grant of TDIU served only as a partial grant of his request for TDIU.  The Court determined that, under Rice, the issue of entitlement to TDIU prior to February 2016 remained part and parcel of the veteran’s underlying PTSD claim and was properly before the Board for adjudication.  Once the veteran’s PTSD claim was in appellate status by virtue of the December 2008 NOD, the issue of TDIU became part of the underlying PTSD claim when he filed an application for PTSD in February 2014.

The Court noted that the facts in the present case were similar to a 2015 nonprecedential decision of the Federal Circuit, Palmatier v. McDonald, in which the Federal Circuit held that the issue of entitlement to TDIU, which was awarded in 2011, was not bifurcated from the veteran’s low back disability evaluation appeal, as the veteran’s requests for TDIU subsequent to the initial June 2002 filing for the low back claim were part and parcel of the low back disability claim.  The 2011 TDIU award was only a partial grant.

Similar to Palmatier, in the present case the issue of entitlement to TDIU was not bifurcated from the veteran’s PTSD claim.  Once the veteran’s PTSD claim was in appellate status by virtue of the December 2008 NOD, the issue of TDIU became part and parcel of that claim when the veteran filed an application for TDIU in February 2014.  Thus, the issue of entitlement to TDIU was also in appellate status, and because the veteran did not withdraw the issue of TDIU from his pending appeal he did not bifurcate his appeal.  As in Palmatier, the RO’s grant of TDIU in May 2016 did not serve to bifurcate his appeal, but instead served simply to partially grant the request for TDIU.

The Court agreed with the veteran that the Board failed to rectify the reasons or bases errors identified in the February 2013 JPMR.  The Court found that although the Board acknowledged in its recitation of the evidence that the veteran experienced hallucinations, it failed to address this evidence in its analysis.  The Court stated that the Board cannot satisfy its duty to provide adequate reasons or bases by merely listing evidence and then reaching a conclusion.  The Court also found that the Board failed to adequately discuss any occupational impairment that the veteran may experience because of his PTSD, and may have overlooked favorable evidence.

The Court reversed the portion of the Board’s July 2016 decision that found that it lacked jurisdiction over the issue of entitlement to TDIU.  The Court set aside that portion of the Board decision that denied an initial disability rating in excess of 50 percent for PTSD prior to December 17, 2015, and remanded the matter for proceedings consistent with its decision.
