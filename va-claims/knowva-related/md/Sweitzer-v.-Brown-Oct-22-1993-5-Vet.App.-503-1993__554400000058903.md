Decision Assessment Document
 Sweitzer v. Brown, Oct 22, 1993, 5 Vet.App. 503 (1993)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that In order to be compensable under the provisions of 38 U.S.C. 1151, any injury or aggravation of an injury must have resulted from the examination itself, not from the process of reporting for the examination.

**Impact on VBA:**

Manual M21-1, IV.ii.2.G was revised in accordance with the Court's holding.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

In February 1990, appellant reported to the radiology department of a VA medical center for a scheduled upper gastrointestinal x-ray examination.  He was advised that there would be a 20 minute wait.  He decided to take a walk, went into another building where he began reading the bulletin board, and was struck by a motorized wheelchair piloted by another patient.  He was struck in the lower back and knocked to the ground.  He reported the incident and received medical attention.  He was subsequently treated over a period of months for claimed increased lower back pain.

Appellant filed a claim for compensation under the provisions of 38 U.S.C. 1151, on the grounds that his preexisting nonservice-connected back disorder had been aggravated as a result of the mishap.  The RO concluded that appellant had sustained no additional disability as a result of the accident and denied the claim.  He appealed the decision and the BVA determined that he had not submitted a well-grounded claim under section 1151 because any additional lower back disability he may have sustained was not the result of hospitalization, medical treatment, or examination by VA.

The Court noted that the issue here is one of statutory interpretation, and as such, the analysis begins with an examination of the plain language of the statute.  The Court concluded that when the statutory phrase "having submitted to an examination" is construed in connection with every other part or section so as to produce a harmonious whole, an injury or aggravation of an injury, in order to be compensable under section 1151, must have resulted from the examination itself, not from the process of reporting for the examination.  The Court further noted that the statute does not address disabilities that are merely coincidental with the receipt of VA treatment or which are the result of actions by the claimant, i.e., applying for or seeking hospitalization, treatment, or examination.

Appellant stressed the language in 38 CFR 3.800(a), rather than that in 3.358(a), and urged the Court to interpret his arrival at the VA, coupled with his signing in at the clinic where the examination was to be conducted as commensurate with his "having submitted to" an examination for section1151 purposes.  The dissenting judge agreed and spoke of appellant's having been within the "control and authority of the VA" and of the VA's "duty to insure his safety," and concluded that "the VA was responsible for taking all reasonable precautionary measures to assure the appellant's safety."  The majority noted that "Without dwelling upon the mischief that could result from such an interpretation, we note only our belief that section 1151 contemplates an increase in disability that results from an examination rather than one coincidentally subsequent to the mere signing of one's name or the checking of a block or two on a form."
