Decision Assessment Document
 Ellington v. Nicholson, Jul 25, 2007, 22 Vet.App. 141
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the effective date assigned for a secondarily service-connected disability does not relate back to the filing date of a prior claim for the antecedent disability.

The Court also held that a veteran’s listing of current symptoms in response to a VA medical examination questionnaire does not constitute a claim filed for disability benefits.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran’s claim for service connection for non-lymphocytic leukemia was denied by the regional office (RO) in 1992 and 1993.  During an October 1993 VA examination, the veteran responded to questions concerning present complaints (symptoms) on VA Form 21-2545, Report for Medical Examination for Disability Evaluation, listing his complaints/symptoms as “fatigue, skin problems from chemotherapy, ear problems, high blood pressure, borderline diabetic.”

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in September 1996, granted the veteran’s claim for entitlement to service connection for leukemia, which was the only issue presented to the Board for review.  The RO assigned an effective date of October 31, 1990 for leukemia.

In September 1998, the RO received a letter from the veteran’s representative that was characterized as an informal claim for service connection for diabetes mellitus and hypertension.  The RO received subsequent letters from the representative asserting that the veteran’s diabetes and hypertension were caused by his service-connected leukemia.  In March 2002, the Board granted secondary service connection for the veteran’s diabetes and hypertension.  The RO assigned an effective date for each disability of September 10, 1998, the date the RO received the first letter from the veteran’s representative.  The Board, in March 2004, upheld the RO’s assignment of effective date, finding that the veteran had not filed a claim for either of the secondary disabilities prior to the date in question.

The veteran argued before the Court for an effective date of October 31, 1990, the date that VA received his original claim for service connection for leukemia, October 6, 1993, the date he responded to the medical questionnaire, or the date his diabetes and hypertension became capable of diagnosis, whichever is later.

The Court indicated that it recently rejected an analogous interpretation of [38 C.F.R. § 3.310(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=287737a22d722bfea23ee07172a073c4&node=se38.1.3_1310&rgn=div8) in [*Roper v. Nicholson*, 20 Vet. App. 173 (2006)](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014875/), which is controlling in this case and held that § 3.310(a) governs only entitlement to secondary service-connection claims and the other elements of a secondary service-connection claim, such as the effective date of the award, are outside of the reach of the regulation.  The Court held that the effective date assigned for secondarily service-connection claims are governed by [38 C.F.R. § 3.400](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=287737a22d722bfea23ee07172a073c4&node=se38.1.3_1400&rgn=div8), as with any claim.

The Court rejected the veteran’s argument that he filed an informal claim for hypertension and diabetes when he submitted the VA Form 21-2545.  The Court explained that the informal claim must be in writing, request a determination of entitlement or evidence a belief in entitlement to a benefit, and adequately identify the benefit sought.

The Court characterized the VA Form 21-2545 as designed to elicit information from a claimant for diagnostic purposes only.  An application for benefits was not filed each time a question was answered on the form by providing current medical information.

The Court concluded that requiring VA to open an informal claim whenever a veteran provides medical information in a VA questionnaire defies common sense.  VA must have some means of distinguishing between legitimate claims and ordinary medical paperwork.  The Court indicated that the procedural requirements under [38 C.F.R. § 3.155](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=287737a22d722bfea23ee07172a073c4&node=se38.1.3_1155&rgn=div8) serve this purpose.  The Court noted that it does not hold that the submission of a document with a VA examination could never constitute an informal claim if the veteran sufficiently manifested an intent to apply for benefits for a particular disease or injury in a VA form or questionnaire.  However, the Court found that such facts were clearly not presented in this case.

The Court affirmed the Board’s March 2004 decision.
