Decision Assessment Document
Ardison v. Brown, Apr 4, 1994, 6 Vet.App. 405 (1994)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that a VA examination must be undertaken during active stage of the veteran's service-connected tinea pedis.

A medically established history of recurrence and remission of the disability was shown.  The tinea pedis was a seasonal or intermittent disorder.  The period of recurrence was seasonal and over along period of time.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.3.B.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran was granted a noncompensable (0%) evaluation for tinea pedis following discharge from service.  He submitted private medical statements in 1973 which showed that his condition was characterized by intermittent flareups with purulent vesicles, macerated skin, and involvement of the hands and possibly lips.  On October 3, 1975, the Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA) found the condition to be compensable and assigned a 10% evaluation.  In January 1990, the veteran filed a claim for an increase.  Treatment records from a VA Medical Center were reviewed, and a VA examination was conducted in September 1991.  The claim was denied and the denial was upheld by the BVA on June 24, 1992.

The Court stated that the veteran's claim for an increase was a new claim.  The record, however, was inadequate because the BVA failed to fulfill its duty to assist in the development of the claim.  The September 1991 VA examination was inadequate, given appellant's prior history of remission and recurrence of tinea pedis.  Citing Bowers v. Derwinski, 2 Vet. App. 675, 676 (1992), the Court noted that it is the frequency and duration of outbreaks in skin disease cases, and the appearance and virulence of the disease during the outbreaks that must be addressed.  Nevertheless, the VA examination was not performed during the active stage of the condition.  It was also not clear whether the examiner evaluated appellant for the id reaction which is part of his disability.  On remand, the BVA was to provide for an adequate examination during the active stage of the veteran's tinea pedis.
