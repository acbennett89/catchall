Decision Assessment Document
Emerson v. McDonald, Aug 10, 2016, 28 Vet.App. 200 (2016)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that 38 C.F.R. 3.156(c)(1) requires that VA, upon receiving official service department records, must reconsider a claim for service connection for a disability that was denied when the claim was first decided, even though a subsequent decision awarded service connection for the disability, if the relevant service records had existed and had not been associated with the claims file when VA first decided the claim.

**Impact on VBA:**

Manual M21-1, III.iv.2.B will be revised to implement the Court’s opinion.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran’s service in the Army from September 1967 to September 1969, included Vietnam service.  His awards included the Aircraft Crewman Badge and Air Medal.  He filed a claim for service connection for posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) in September 2000 and provided a statement describing his service as a helicopter door gunner in Vietnam that included missions transporting wounded service personnel.  He later submitted a private medical opinion that contained a PTSD diagnosis based on the veteran’s reports of seeing wounded and dead service members and incoming enemy fire during missions.

The regional office (RO), in August 2002, sent the veteran a statement requesting stressor information; however, no response was provided.  The RO, in February 2003, denied service connection for PTSD based on the lack of a verified stressor, noting that the veteran failed to return the stressor form.

VA, effective July 12, 2010, amended 38 C.F.R. 3.304(f) to eliminate the requirement for corroborative evidence of a stressor where a VA mental health expert has diagnosed PTSD and the stressor is related to the veteran’s fear of hostile military or terrorist activity.

The RO, in August 2010, interpreted a statement of the veteran as a request to reopen the claim for service connection for PTSD and afforded him a VA examination in January 2011, which resulted in a PTSD diagnosis and the examiner linking it to the veteran’s fear of hostile military or terrorist activity in Vietnam.  The RO, in June 2011, awarded service connection for PTSD with a 30 percent evaluation from August 23, 2010.  The veteran appealed the evaluation and effective date.

In July 2012, the veteran submitted service department records that included an Air Medal worksheet that listed the date and duration of completed helicopter missions with the letter “C” written next to each “Mission Type.”  Other service department records included personnel information that showed assignments, duties, awards, campaigns, and showed participation in the Tet Counter Offensive.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in May 2014, awarded a higher evaluation of 50 percent for PTSD, but denied entitlement to an effective date earlier than August 23, 2010.

The veteran argued before the Court that the plain language of 3.156(c)(1) mandates that VA reconsider his claim “at any time” upon the receipt of official service department records that were not previously associated with the claims file.  He contended that the regulation contained no limiting language or ambiguity pertaining to timing that would permit VA to not reconsider by granting service connection on a different, less favorable theory.  The veteran also argued that the Board provided inadequate reasons or bases in not discussing the full range and severity of his symptoms in denying a PTSD evaluation in excess of 50 percent.

The Secretary maintained that VA was not required to “reconsider the claim” for service connection for PTSD in this case.  The Secretary argued that 3.156(c)(1) was inapplicable because the veteran was granted service connection in June 2011, before service department records were sent to VA by the veteran and associated with the claims file.  The Secretary contended that 3.156(c)(1) applies only when VA has denied a claim but later receives relevant service department records, as the regulation presupposes that a claim has been denied.

The Court, in examining the plain language of the regulation and the 2005 notice of proposed rulemaking, rejected the Secretary’s argument and held that the plain language of 3.156(c)(1) required VA to reconsider the 2003 claim.

The Court, applying its holding to the case, held that based upon the plain language of § 3.156(c)(1), upon receiving official service department records in 2012, VA was required to “reconsider the claim” for service connection for PTSD that was denied in February 2003, notwithstanding the fact that service connection was granted in 2011.

The Court, finding that the Board erred in failing to “reconsider the claim” for service connection for PTSD that was denied in February 2003, remanded the issue of entitlement to an effective date earlier than August 23, 2010, for service connection for PTSD with instructions to the Board to include determining whether current law or law in effect at the time of the 2003 decision was to be applied, and if the newly associated service department records are at least partially decisive as to an award made under 3.156(c)(1).

The Court also, finding that the Board failed to discuss several purportedly favorable pieces of evidence that relate to the 70 percent criteria for PTSD, also remanded the issue of entitlement to an initial evaluation in excess of 50 percent for PTSD.
