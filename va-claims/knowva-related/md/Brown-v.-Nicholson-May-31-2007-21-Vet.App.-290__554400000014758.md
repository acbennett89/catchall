Decision Assessment Document
 Brown v. Nicholson, May 31, 2007, 21 Vet.App. 290
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that [38 U.S.C. § 5110(g)](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5110) and [38 C.F.R. § 3.114(a)(3)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=48bc6abc8a6d0a53a9724e802d044c27&node=se38.1.3_1114&rgn=div8), which allow an effective date up to year prior to the date of claim if the claim is granted on the basis of liberalizing law or administrative issue, are not applicable where there is no change in law following a final decision *that considered the liberalizing law or regulation*, because an award has not been made “pursuant to” an act or administrative issue.

The Court’s analysis concluded that the statute and its implementing regulation require two specific findings:  A finding that a liberalizing law or administrative issue was implemented *and* a determination that the ultimate grant of benefits was “pursuant to” such a favorable change in law.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, a POW during the Korean War, died in November 1978 as a result of atherosclerotic heart disease.  The appellant’s initial claim for service connection for cause of death was denied in 1981.  Her claim to reopen was denied in 1994.  While her appeal was pending before the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), [38 C.F.R. § 3.309(c)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=48bc6abc8a6d0a53a9724e802d044c27&node=se38.1.3_1309&rgn=div8) was amended effective August 24, 1993, which extended presumptive service connection for beriberi heart disease to include ischemic heart disease (synonymous with arteriosclerotic heart disease) in a former POW who had experienced localized edema during captivity.  The Board, in denying the surviving spouse’s appeal in February 1997, referenced § 3.309(c) in its conclusion that the veteran did not have beriberi heart disease.

The appellant sought to reopen her claim on April 19, 2001, with a statement from an individual who was a POW with the veteran, which reported observing the veteran’s lower extremities swelling and cracking open on several occasions while in captivity.  The regional office (RO) granted the surviving spouse’s claim because the new evidence showed localized edema in service and established a presumption of ischemic heart disease.  In assigning an effective date for the DIC benefits of April 19, 2001, the RO indicated that consideration for an effective date under § 3.114(a) has been given; however, the change in the decision is based upon new evidence received after the liberalizing changes in the law and regulations.

In its November 2003 decision, the Board upheld the April 19, 2001, effective date, noting that the effective date cannot be earlier than the date of the claim to reopen under section 5110(a) and [§ 3.400(q)(1)(ii)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=48bc6abc8a6d0a53a9724e802d044c27&node=se38.1.3_1400&rgn=div8).  In addressing the liberalizing law argument, the Board indicated that the liberalizing amendment was considered in the Board’s 1997 decision.

The Court determined that the intervening 1997 Board decision, which rejected the appellant’s claim to reopen despite promulgation of the liberalizing amendment to § 3.309(c), rendered § 3.114(a)(3) and the retroactive assignment of an effective date inapplicable as there was no additional change in law following the 1997 decision.  The Court indicated that it found no evidence that the Board did not consider the liberalizing changes to § 3.309(c) in its 1997 decision.

The Court observed that the only difference between the Board’s 1997 decision and decision on appeal is the consideration of new evidence, the 2001 lay statement, provided by the appellant.  Thus, while the appellant derived some benefits from the liberalizing amendment, the *ultimate award* of benefits was not “pursuant to” the change in law, but was made pursuant to the submission of the new evidence – that was not before the Board in 1997 – that the veteran suffered from localized edema while a POW.  The Court concluded that the Board did not err in not discussing § 3.114(a)(3) as it was inapplicable in determining the effective date of the award of DIC benefits because of the intervening 1997 Board decision.

The Court affirmed the Board decision.
