Decision Assessment Document
 Andrews v. Nicholson, Aug 17, 2005, 421 F.3d 1278
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

In this case, the Federal Circuit explained its earlier precedent in [*Roberson v. Principi*, 251 F.3d 1378 (Fed. Cir. 2001)](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/251/251.F3d.1378.00-7009.html), which held that VA must sympathetically read a veteran's filings to identify all claims that they raise.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

In *Andrews*, the court held that (1) *Roberson* requires VA to sympathetically read all pleadings filed pro se, including claims of clear and unmistakable error (CUE); (2) *Roberson* does not require sympathetic reading of pleadings filed by counsel; (3) VA’s duty to sympathetically read a pro se claimant’s CUE motion is antecedent to a determination as to whether CUE has been plead with specificity; (4) when VA violates *Roberson* by failing to adjudicate a raised claim, the claim is not considered unadjudicated, but the error is instead properly corrected through a CUE motion; and (5) the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Veterans Court) lacks jurisdiction over a CUE issue not raised before VA by a represented claimant.  Because the appellant in this case was represented, the Federal Circuit affirmed the Veteran’s Court’s decision declining to address a CUE challenge that had not been specifically raised to the Board.
