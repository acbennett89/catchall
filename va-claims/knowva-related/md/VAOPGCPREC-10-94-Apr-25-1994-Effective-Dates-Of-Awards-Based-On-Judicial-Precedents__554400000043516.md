Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 10-94, Apr 25, 1994, Effective Dates Of Awards Based On Judicial Precedents
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Effective Dates Of Awards Based On Judicial Precedents.

**Impact on VBA**

The effective dates of awards of compensation or pension based upon judicial precedents alone are governed by 38 U.S.C. § 5110(a) and not 38 U.S.C. § 5110(g), i.e., the effective dates may generally be no earlier than dates of claims.  However, if an award may be predicated upon an administrative issue, such as an amendment to a regulation, prompted by a judicial precedent, 38 U.S.C. § 5110(g) should be applied in assigning the effective date if to do so would be to the claimant's benefit.

**Summary on facts and findings**

As a general rule, the effective dates of awards of compensation or pension benefits, based upon either original claims, claims reopened after final adjudication, or claims for increased benefits, are to be determined in accordance with the facts found but in no event may they be earlier than the date of receipt of claim.  38 U.S.C. § 5110(a).  Section 5110(g) (implemented by 38 C.F.R. § 3.114(a)) authorizes awards retroactive for as much as one year from date of receipt of claims when awards are made "pursuant to any Act or administrative issue," although in no event may they be made effective earlier than the effective date of the liberalizing act or administrative issue.

While court precedents may effect changes in operative rules of law no less than do acts of Congress or amendments to regulations, section 5110(g) by its terms does not apply to awards grounded solely in the former.  General Counsel concluded that 38 U.S.C. §5110(a) controls the effective dates of awards based solely upon precedential decisions of courts of law.  Whether a claim is pending at the time an entitlement-conferring judicial precedent is issued, or is filed after the precedent's issuance, the effective date should be fixed in accordance with the facts found but may not be earlier than the date of receipt of the claim.  If VA implements a liberalizing regulation based upon a court case, then 38 U.S.C. § 5110(g) should be used to determine the effective date of the award if to do so would be to the claimant's advantage.

In O.G.C. Precedent 09-94, the General Counsel held that decisions of the CVA invalidating VA regulations or statutory or regulatory interpretations do not have retroactive effect in relation to prior "final" adjudications of claims, but should be given retroactive effect as they relate to claims still open on direct review.

In this opinion, O.G.C. Prec. 10-94, the General Counsel addresses the question concerning applicability of 38 U.S.C. § 5110(g) (codified by 38 C.F.R. 3.114(a)) to cases favorably impacted by Court decisions.  If a Court's decision invalidates all or part of a VA regulation and that regulation is subsequently amended, the provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.114(a) may be for application.  If the Court's decision invalidates VA's interpretation of a regulation, BUT no amendment to the regulation is promulgated, then the provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.114(a) are not for application.

The Court's decision in Gregory v. Brown, 5 Vet. App. 108 (1993) illustrates a situation where the provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.114(a) may be for application.  In Gregory issued on May 23, 1993, the Court invalidated portions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.53(a).  VA has published an amendment to 38 C.F.R. § 3.53(a) which implements the Court's decision retroactive to May 23, 1993.  Any claim received after May 23, 1993 which involves a favorable continuous cohabitation determination based on the Gregory decision is subject to the provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.114(a).

The Court's decision in Esteban v. Brown, 6 Vet. App. 259 (1994) illustrates a case where the provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.114(a) are not for application.  In the Esteban case, issued on February 25, 1994, the Court reviewed the rule against pyramiding.  The Court stated that there were three separate residuals from the veteran's facial injury; i.e. disfigurement, pain and muscle damage.  Because none of the symptomatology for any one of these three conditions is duplicative of or overlapping with the symptomatology of the other two conditions, as a matter of law, the veteran is entitled to combine his 10% rating for disfigurement under Diagnostic Code (DC) 7800 with an additional 10% rating for tender and painful scars under DC 7804 and a third 10% rating for facial injury interfering with mastication under DC 5325.  The decision liberally interpreted VA's rule against pyramiding in a manner favorable to the claimant.  However, it has been determined that no regulatory changes are required as a result of the Esteban decision.  Since there have not been any regulatory changes issued by VA, the provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.114(a) are not for application in any case where a claimant receives an evaluation for residuals of a facial injury consistent with the Court's decision in Esteban, the effective date of payment will always be the date of receipt of the claim.

M21-1, Part VI, para 3.45 deals with assignment of an effective date in a rating decision.  It would be helpful if this citation included information concerning the assignment of effective dates when a favorable decision is based upon a Court of Veteran's Appeals ruling.  The recommendation in our assessment of O.G.C. Precedent 09-94 was to revise M21-1, Part IV, Chapter 38 (or other chapters) to provide examples of effective dates in claims allowed based on new CVA precedents.  The application of 38 C.F.R. § 3.114(a) should also be covered in any revision to Part IV.

Amend M21-1, Part VI, para 3.45 and/or Part IV, Chapter 38 to include information concerning assignment of effective dates when a favorable decision is based upon a Court of Veterans Appeals ruling.  Include examples which show when 38 C.F.R. § 3.114(a) should be applied and when it should not be applied.  The need for a separate chapter on effective dates should be examined.
