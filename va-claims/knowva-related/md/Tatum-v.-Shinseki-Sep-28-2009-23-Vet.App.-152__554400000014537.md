Decision Assessment Document
 Tatum v. Shinseki, Sep 28, 2009, 23 Vet.App. 152
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) erred when it concluded that all three symptoms – fatigability, constipation, and mental sluggishness – are required for a 30 percent disability rating for hypothyroidism under [38 C.F.R. § 4.119](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9da0ab6f5d526a37179b38023fa5b9ee&node=se38.1.4_1119&rgn=div8), Diagnostic Code (DC) 7903.  Because the Board found that the veteran exhibited two of the symptoms required for a 30 percent evaluation, the Court held that the Board failed to consider whether a 30 percent rating was warranted under [38 C.F.R. § 4.7](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=2ae0fe3c4cf619f4b2c436ae2aa96991&mc=true&node=se38.1.4_17&rgn=div8).

The Court also held that the Board failed to consider or adequately discuss applicable regulations in reducing the 10 percent evaluation for peptic ulcer disease.  Although the clinical evidence clearly showed that the veteran no longer had an ulcer, she was diagnosed with several other gastrointestinal conditions.  The Court noted that the rating schedule explicitly recognizes the unique characteristics of diseases of the digestive system and that they typically produce a common disability picture.  See [38 C.F.R. § 4.113](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9da0ab6f5d526a37179b38023fa5b9ee&node=se38.1.4_1113&rgn=div8).

The Court noted other regulations applicable to the rating reduction not considered by the Board to include [38 C.F.R. § 3.344](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9da0ab6f5d526a37179b38023fa5b9ee&node=se38.1.3_1344&rgn=div8), which directs rating officials in cases affected by changes in medical findings or diagnoses to effect the “greatest degree of stability of disability evaluations,” and to retain the original diagnosis with the new diagnosis in parentheses when there is doubt as to a change in diagnosis.  The Court also found that the Board failed to apply [38 C.F.R. § 4.13](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9da0ab6f5d526a37179b38023fa5b9ee&node=se38.1.4_113&rgn=div8) by not adequately discussing the presence of other diagnoses in the record as they related to the veteran’s current and active gastrointestinal problems, nor considering whether the veteran should now be rated under a new DC associated with a changed diagnosis.

Note: 38 C.F.R. § 4.13 provides that when a previously assigned diagnosis had changed, “the aim should be the reconciliation and continuance of the diagnosis or etiology upon which service connection for the disability had been granted.”

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.  However, the Court’s holding should be followed if applying DC 7903 in assigning an evaluation for hypothyroidism or related disability.  In evaluating any condition without successive criteria, [38 C.F.R. § 4.7](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9da0ab6f5d526a37179b38023fa5b9ee&node=se38.1.4_170&rgn=div8) should always be applied to determine if the evidence warrants a higher evaluation consistent with the rating schedule criteria.  Also, 38 C.F.R. § 4.13 should be utilized if warranted by the evidentiary picture, such as separate, but related, diagnoses for a gastrointestinal disability characterized by common symptomatology.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, after discharge from service in 1991, was granted service connection for mild peptic ulcer disease and hyperthyroidism/Graves’ disease with a compensable evaluation for each disability.  The disability picture changed over time, showing that the veteran suffered from a digestive system disorder and developed hypothyroidism due to treatment for hyperthyroidism.  As a result, the 10 percent evaluation for peptic ulcer disease was reduced and the veteran’s thyroid condition was reclassified from *hyper*thyroidism to *hypo*thyroidism.

The veteran appealed to the Board for restoration of the compensable evaluation for peptic ulcer disease and an increased rating for her thyroid condition.  The Board, in 2007, found that a noncompensable evaluation was warranted for the peptic ulcer disease in the absence of an active ulcer disease and maintained the 10 percent evaluation for hypothyroidism because all the criteria for a higher evaluation were not met.

The Court found that it was indisputable that the veteran suffered from fatigability and mental sluggishness, two of three criteria required for a 30 percent rating under DC 7903, and there was evidence that she experienced weight gain, one of the three criteria for a 60 percent rating under DC 7903.

The Court found the Board’s reasons or bases to be inadequate as it failed to discuss the credibility of the veteran’s statements that she suffered from constipation.  The Court stated that the credibility of an assertion of an affliction of such a personal nature is a key consideration as to whether the veteran does or does not suffer from such affliction.

In contending that the Board correctly determined that all three symptoms were required for a higher evaluation under DC 7903 and that [§ 4.7](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9da0ab6f5d526a37179b38023fa5b9ee&node=se38.1.4_170&rgn=div8) was inapplicable, the Secretary relied upon [*Camacho v. Nicholson*, 21 Vet.App. 360 (2007)](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014801/) in arguing that the presence of the conjunctive “and” joining particular rating criteria within a diagnostic code requires that all of those rating criteria must be met to establish entitlement to the corresponding disability rating.

The Court rejected this argument, holding that *Camacho* did not render such an expansive holding and was limited to the successive rating criteria for diabetes mellitus under 38 [C.F.R. § 4.119](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9da0ab6f5d526a37179b38023fa5b9ee&node=se38.1.4_1119&rgn=div8), DC 7913.  The Court explained that the evaluation for each higher disability rating under DC 7913 included the criteria of each lower disability rating, such that if a component was not met at any one level, the veteran could only be rated at the level that did not require the missing component.  Under the specific circumstances of *Camacho*, § 4.7 was inapplicable.  However, DC 7903 does not involve successive rating criteria.  Contrary to the rating criteria under DC 7913, a veteran could potentially establish all of the criteria required for either a 30 percent or 60 percent disability rating for hypothyroidism, without establishing any of the criteria for a lesser disability rating.

The Court stated that the Board’s conclusion that § 4.7 was inapplicable would render the regulation meaningless as well as eviscerate the meaning of [38 C.F.R. § 4.21](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9da0ab6f5d526a37179b38023fa5b9ee&node=se38.1.4_121&rgn=div8) (“it is not expected…that all cases will show all the findings specified [in an applicable DC].”  Because the veteran has both fatigability and mental sluggishness, which are two of the three criteria for a 30 percent rating, the question arose as to whether a 30 percent rating is more appropriate than a 10 percent rating, which only requires fatigability.  Thus, the Court concluded that § 4.7 was clearly implicated.

The Court also found, in addition to the Board’s finding of weight gain, evidence that the veteran suffered from both muscular weakness and mental disturbance, (despite the fact that a 60 percent rating is warranted upon a showing of these symptoms under DC 7903) the Board failed to explain why a 60 percent evaluation was not warranted.  In finding Board error for this failure, the Court stressed its own precedents presuming a claimant is seeking the maximum benefits allowed by law and regulation, and cited [38 C.F.R. § 3.103(a)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9da0ab6f5d526a37179b38023fa5b9ee&node=se38.1.3_1103&rgn=div8), which obliges VA to render a decision which grants every benefit that can be supported by law.

Because the Board failed to consider whether the effects of the veteran’s hypothyroidism warranted a 30 percent rating under [§ 4.7](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9da0ab6f5d526a37179b38023fa5b9ee&node=se38.1.4_170&rgn=div8) and to weigh the evidence of muscular weakness, mental disturbance, and weight gain, or explain why a 60 percent rating was not warranted, the Court remanded the issue of entitlement to a increased evaluation for hypothyroidism.

Regarding the issue of restoration of a compensable evaluation for peptic ulcer disease, the Court observed that the veteran originally sought disability compensation for duodenitis with duodenal erosion and mild esophagitis, which the Court characterized as essentially problems of the gastrointestinal system.  The Court held that the Board erred by reducing the veteran’s rating to noncompensable, in spite of its recognition that she continued to have gastrointestinal problems, solely because she no longer had an active ulcer.  The Court indicated that such regulations as [38 C.F.R. § 3.344](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9da0ab6f5d526a37179b38023fa5b9ee&node=se38.1.3_1344&rgn=div8), [4.13](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9da0ab6f5d526a37179b38023fa5b9ee&node=se38.1.4_113&rgn=div8), and [4.113](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=9da0ab6f5d526a37179b38023fa5b9ee&node=se38.1.4_1113&rgn=div8) were applicable to the rating reduction issue and were not considered or adequately discussed by the Board.

The Court held that because the issue involved a rating reduction and the Board failed to consider the applicable laws and regulations before finding that the veteran was no longer entitled to a compensable disability the rating, the Board’s finding is rendered *void ab initio* and not in accordance with the law.  The Court reversed the Board’s decision and directed it to reinstate the compensable evaluation for peptic ulcer disease.
