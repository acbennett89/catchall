Decision Assessment Document
Rose v. West, Apr 20, 1998, 11 Vet.App. 169 (1998)
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court, finding that the Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) had relied upon its own unsubstantiated medical opinion to improperly refute the veteran's favorable evidence, concluded that the Board had articulated no valid basis for denying service connection, its decision was not plausible, and is, therefore, clearly erroneous.

The Court held that in view of the uncontested nature of the evidence of service connection, the Board's decision denying service connection must be reversed.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into guidance contained in the Manual M21-1 under III.iv.5.A.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran averred that he injured his back while lifting a hatch on-board ship while serving the U.S. Coast Guard in March 1952.  Service medical records revealed treatment for back pain from March 1952 until at least August 1953.  He was discharged from active duty in May 1954.  Evidence submitted in connection with his claim for service connection for residuals of the back injury included two medical opinions that directly related the veteran’s current disability to the original injury in March 1952.  In upholding the denial of service connection for degenerative arthritis of the lumbosacral spine with disc disease and nerve root impairment, the Board stated, in essence, that the appellant’s evidence did not meet the requirements of 38 C.F.R. 3.303(b).

The Court stated that section 3.303(b) provides an alternative method of demonstrating entitlement to service connection which the veteran may utilize because it provides a substitute way of proving service connection.  It is clear that service connection may be established by medical evidence of an in-service injury or aggravation, medical evidence of a current condition, and medical evidence of a nexus between the two (see Caluza v. Brown, 7 Vet.App. 498 (1995), aff’d per curiam, 78 F.3d 604 (Fed. Cir. 1996)(table).  Medical evidence of nexus can be rebutted by medical evidence that demonstrates the significance of a lack of continuity of symptomatology.  In this case no such evidence existed and the Court stated that it was not the function of judicial review simply to accord the government a remand to obtain such evidence.

In this case, each of the three requirements for the establishment of service connection under Caluza was unequivocally supported by the medical evidence submitted by the veteran.  There was no medical evidence in the record that was contrary to the appellant.  The Court stated that the Board improperly refuted this evidence with its own unsubstantiated medical opinion.  The Court held that because the BVA had articulated no valid basis for denying service connection, its decision was not plausible and was, accordingly, clearly erroneous.

The Court had previously reversed several decisions as arbitrary because there was NO evidence against the claim, much less a fair preponderance.  The Court has consistently held that there is no legal basis for a denial when all of the credible evidence is favorable.  In this decision, the Court has made it clear that once a BVA decision has been issued, the Court will not remand a case because the VA failed to undertake appropriate development for evidence that may be unfavorable to the claimant.  If all the evidence is favorable and the VA has failed to grant the claimed benefit, the Court will reverse and order that the benefit be granted.
