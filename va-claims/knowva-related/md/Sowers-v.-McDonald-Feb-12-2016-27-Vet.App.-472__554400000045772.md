Decision Assessment Document
Sowers v. McDonald, Feb 12, 2016, 27 Vet.App. 472
U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that 38 C.F.R. § 4.59 is inapplicable to a diagnostic code that does not provide a compensable evaluation.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court’s holding has been implemented by a Manual revision contained in III.iv.4.A.  The Manual directs that when applying § 4.59 to assign at least the minimal compensable rating under the appropriate diagnostic code for the joint involved.

An example is provided indicating that painful motion of a right ring finger fracture that is rated under 38 C.F.R. § 4.71a, Diagnostic Code (DC) 5230, would not receive a compensable evaluation under § 4.59, as DC 5230 does not contain a compensable evaluation. Another example is provided in III.iv.4.A. directing that 20 percent is the minimal compensable evaluation based for painful shoulder motion.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran is service connected for a right ring finger disability that resulted from a fracture of the distal interphalangeal joint (DIP) joint in service.  The regional office (RO), in May 2006, assigned a zero percent evaluation under DC 5010-5230.  The veteran appealed the RO decision, asserting that a higher evaluation was warranted because he had severe limited range of motion with pain.

Following a Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board) remand in April 2012, the veteran underwent a VA examination in June 2013.  The examiner found occasional pain and stiffness with decreased range of motion of the right ring finger.  The veteran was able to perform repetitive use testing without any additional loss of motion.

The Board, in its December 2013 decision, conceded that there was evidence of painful motion; however, the Board denied a compensable evaluation for the right ring finger disability.  The Board reasoned that the since the Rating Schedule does not provide for a 10 percent evaluation for any amount of limitation of motion of the ring finger, there is a no basis for a minimum compensable rating for limitation of motion of a ring finger under § 4.59. The Board also determined that DC 5010 was inappropriate for rating the ring finger disability as arthritis had not been substantiated by X-ray findings.  In the absence of a finding of arthritis, the Board concluded that the right ring finger was most appropriately rated under DC 5230.

The veteran argued before the Court that the plain meaning of “entitled to at least the minimum compensable rating of the joint” under § 4.59 directs VA to apply the minimum compensable rating across all diagnostic codes pertaining to the affected joint.  Acknowledging that his assigned diagnostic code, DC 5230, does not allow a compensable evaluation, the veteran asserted that other diagnostic codes affecting the same joint provide a guideline for the minimum compensable evaluation of the right ring finger.  He specifically argued that since DC 5227 allows for a compensable evaluation when ankylosis of the right ring finger equates to amputation, VA must use it to build up a rating that recognizes his disability, which entitles him to a compensable rating under § 4.59.

The Court observing that DC 5230, unlike other diagnostic codes, does not instruct that other diagnostic should be considered, stated that the inclusion of criteria in one diagnostic code indicates that VA’s exclusion of that criteria elsewhere was purposeful.  The Court concluded that VA clearly expressed its determination that DC 5230 is the only diagnostic code applicable to a right ring finger disability based on limitation of motion and that limitation of motion of the right ring finger does not warrant a compensable disability rating.

The Court held that, reading § 4.59 in conjunction with DC 5230, the veteran is not entitled to a compensable rating under this diagnostic code.  The Court noted that § 4.59 intends to recognize actually painful joints and provide at least the minimum compensable rating for the joint; however, there is no minimum compensable rating available under DC 5230, that is, any level of disability warrants a zero percent rating.  Thus, the Court concluded that DC 5230’s specific finding that there is no impairment in earning capacity from any limitation of motion of the ring finger trumps the general intent in § 4.59 to compensate painful motion with at least the minimum compensable rating.

The Court held that if the Rating Schedule does not provide a compensable rating, a veteran “may not shop around among diagnostic codes to find a better deal.”  The Court held that consistent with its decision in Copeland v. McDonald, 27 Vet.App. 333 (2015), there is no support for the veteran’s assertion that he is entitled to consideration of every potentially applicable diagnostic code in order to ensure he receives a compensable rating.  Note: In Copeland, the veteran argued that instead of being rated under the listed diagnostic codes for his foot disability, pes planus and hallux valgus, he should be able to avail himself of other “potentially appropriate” diagnostic codes that provided a higher evaluation.  The Court rejected the argument, finding that a “listed condition should be rated under the diagnostic code that specifically pertains to it.”  The Court held that an analogous rating is inappropriate where a condition has a listed diagnostic code.

The Court, finding further flaw in the veteran’s argument, expressed concern that while the approach would be advantageous to veterans rated under DC 5230, it would not always be beneficial.  The Court provided an example, noting that while the minimum compensable evaluation for shoulder limitation of motion is 20 percent under DC 5201, the minimum compensable rating available across all diagnostic codes for the shoulder is a 10 percent evaluation under DC 5203.  Thus, in applying the veteran’s interpretation, a veteran would not be entitled to the 20 percent evaluation under the applicable diagnostic code for painful shoulder motion, but instead would receive a 10 percent rating because that is the minimum compensable rating available for the joint.

The Court affirmed the Board’s decision denying a compensable rating under § 4.59 for painful motion of the right ring finger.  However, the Court also vacated and remanded the Board’s decision denying an extra-schedular evaluation for the Board to address the veteran’s arguments regarding the applicability of a collective extra-schedular under Johnson v. McDonald, 762 F.3d 1362 (Fed. Cir. 2014) in the first instance.
