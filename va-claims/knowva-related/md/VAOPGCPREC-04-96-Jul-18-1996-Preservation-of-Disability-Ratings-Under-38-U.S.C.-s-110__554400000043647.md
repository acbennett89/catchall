Decision Assessment Document
 VAOPGCPREC 04-96, Jul 18, 1996, Preservation of Disability Ratings Under 38 U.S.C. s 110
 Office of General Counsel Precedent Opinion

**What the case is about**

Are the provisions of 38 U.S.C. s 110 violated when two service-connected disabilities, which have been erroneously rated as one disability at or above a specific evaluation for 20 or more years, are rerated as two separate disabilities such that the combination of their evaluations equals or exceeds the prior specific evaluation?

**Impact on VBA**

The provisions of 38 U.S.C. § 110, which prohibit a disability that has been continuously rated at or above any evaluation for 20 or more years for compensation purposes from thereafter being rated at less than such evaluation, are not violated when two or more service-connected disabilities, which have been erroneously rated as one disability (but not as the result of the combination of known or determinable separate disability evaluations under 38 C.F.R. § 4.25), at or above a specific evaluation for at least 20 years, are rerated as separate disabilities such that the combination of their evaluations equals or exceeds the prior specific evaluation.

**Summary on facts and findings**

The question presented to the General Counsel arose in a case where a veteran had been awarded service connection at the 30% level for psoriasis under diagnostic code 7816.  Service connection for psoriatic rheumatoid arthritis was subsequently established and a single 50% evaluation was assigned under diagnostic code 5002-7816.  Thirty-six years later, he underwent a left total knee replacement, and a rating of at least 50% under diagnostic code 5002-5055 remained in effect until September 1986, when a rating board determined there had been clear and unmistakable error (CUE) in previous rating decisions for not providing a separate evaluation for psoriasis, assigned a 30% evaluation under diagnostic code 5002-5055 and another 30% evaluation under diagnostic code 7816, with a combined evaluation of 50%.  Assuming that the two disabilities rated as one in this case are a disability within the meaning of section 110 with respect to preservation of disability evaluations which have been in effect for 20 years or more, then section 110 prohibits those two disabilities rated as one from being rated as less than 50%.  Because the two disabilities together were not rated at less than 50% upon discovery of CUE in the September 1986 rating decision, section 110's provisions were not violated.  Having established that, the rating decision finding CUE in prior decisions was, itself, incomplete.  Under the provisions of 38 C.F.R. § 3.105(a), a rating which constitutes a reversal of prior decisions on the basis of CUE, has the same effect as if the correct decision had been made on the date of the reversed decision.  In this case, the separate evaluations were assigned prospectively.  To properly correct the error, VA must assign separate evaluations for each disability retroactively, as if the separate evaluations had been assigned in the June 1949 rating decision.  While this might not result in the payment of additional compensation, section 110 will preserve those retroactive separate evaluations, for those disabilities will have been so rated for at least 20 years.  (See VAOPGCPREC 68-91, which also held that section 110 preserves a long-standing evaluation for a disability, not the evaluation assigned under any particular diagnosis).
