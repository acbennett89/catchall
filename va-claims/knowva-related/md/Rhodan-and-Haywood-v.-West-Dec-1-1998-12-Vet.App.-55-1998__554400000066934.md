Decision Assessment Document
 Rhodan and Haywood v. West, Dec 1, 1998, 12 Vet.App. 55 (1998)
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

When amended regulations expressly state an effective date and do not include any provision for retroactive applicability, application of the revised regulations prior to the stated effective date is precluded, notwithstanding Karnas v. Derwinski, 1 Vet.App. 308 (1991).

**Impact on VBA:**

The Court's holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, III.iv.5.c.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The Court denied Mr. Rhodan’s and Mr. Haywood’s applications for Equal Access to Justice (EAJA) fees, finding that VA’s position was substantially justified.  The analysis used by the Court in this decision was of interest to rating specialists, since it has a discussion about assigning effective dates in light of changes to the rating schedule.  The facts are as follows:  In August 1996, the Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) denied an increased rating for Mr. Rhodan’s service connected PTSD.  Mr. Haywood’s claim for increased rating for PTSD was denied on October 31, 1996.  On October 8, 1996, the final version of the revised mental disorder rating schedule was published with an effective date of November 7, 1996.  In August 1997, Mr. Rhodan’s case was remanded by the Court on the sole ground that under Karnas he was entitled to an adjudication of his claim using the revised regulations.  In September 1997, Mr. Haywood and the Secretary filed a joint motion for remand because the change in regulations warranted a remand under Karnas.  The Court granted the motion.  Both Mr. Haywood and Mr. Rhodan filed applications for EAJA fees.

The Court stated that the determinative issue was whether VA’s position, at the administrative level, was substantially justified.  The Board decisions in each case were rendered when the old version of the rating schedule was in effect.  The Court noted that “[w]hen the Secretary adopted the revised mental disorder rating schedule and published it in the Federal Register, the publication clearly stated an effective date of November 7, 1996.  Because the revised regulations expressly stated an effective date and contained no provision for retroactive applicability, it is evident that the Secretary intended to apply those regulations only as of the effective date.  See Allin v. Brown, 6 Vet. App. 207, 211 (1994).”  The Court stated that it “holds that VA’s legal obligation to apply the November 7, 1996, effective date “prevents the application, prior to that effective date, of the liberalizing law rule stated in Karnas.”

The Court then cited 38 USC 5110(g) which states that “where compensation . . . is awarded or increased pursuant to any Act or administrative issue, the effective ate of such an award or increase . . . shall not be earlier than the effective date of the Act or administrative issue.”  The court pointed out that this effective date rule prevents the application of a later, liberalizing law to a claim prior to the effective date of the liberalizing law.  The Court stated that it “holds that for any date prior to November 7, 1996, the Board could not apply the revised mental disorder rating schedule to a claim.”  Based upon this reasoning, the Court concluded that VA’s position at the administrative level was substantially justified and denied EAJA fees.
