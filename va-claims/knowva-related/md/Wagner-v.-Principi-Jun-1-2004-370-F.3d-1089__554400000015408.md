Decision Assessment Document
 Wagner v. Principi, Jun 1, 2004, 370 F.3d 1089
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (CAVC) applied the incorrect legal standard to rebut the presumption of soundness under [38 USC § 1111](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1111).   When no pre existing condition is noted upon entry into service, the veteran is presumed to have been sound upon entry.  The burden then falls on the government to rebut the presumption by clear and unmistakable evidence that the veteran’s disability was both preexisting and not aggravated by service.

**Impact on VBA**:

This opinion is consistent with [VA General Counsel Opinion 3-2003](http://www.va.gov/ogc/docs/2003/PREC3-2003.DOC), which held that in order to rebut the presumption of sound condition under 38 U.S.C. § 1111, VA must show by clear and unmistakable evidence both that the disease or injury existed prior to service and that the disease or injury was not aggravated by service.  The opinion clarified that the claimant is not required to show that the disease or injury increased in severity during service before VA’s duty under the second prong of this rebuttal standard attaches.  The opinion also held that the provisions of [38 C.F.R. § 3.304(b)](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=03f4aefe355bf2756e5b88b1679767c8&node=se38.1.3_1304&rgn=div8) which were inconsistent with 38 U.S.C. § 1111 were invalid.  The manual, M21-1, Part VI, par. 7.05 should be updated to reflect the holding in this case.

**Summary of the facts and Court's reasons****:**

In this case, no disabilities were noted upon the veteran’s entry into service.  Service medical records (SMRs) showed the veteran complained of problems with the right knee.  Several SMRs showed the veteran stated he had sustained an injury to the right knee prior to service.  The Federal Circuit makes clear that despite the veteran’s admission that he sustained an injury prior to service, section 1111 presumption of sound condition applies to this case.

According to the Federal Circuit, the effect of [section 1111](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1111) on claims for service-connected disability thus may be summarized as follows.  When no preexisting condition is noted upon entry into service, the veteran is presumed to have been sound upon entry.  The burden then falls on the government to rebut the presumption of soundness by clear and unmistakable evidence that the veteran’s disability was both preexisting and not aggravated by service.  The government may show a lack of aggravation by establishing that there was no increase in disability during service or that any “increase in disability [was] due to the natural progress of the” preexisting condition.  [38 U.S.C. § 1153](http://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/1153).  If this burden is met, then the veteran is not entitled to service-connected benefits.  However, if the government fails to rebut the presumption of soundness under section 1111, the veteran’s claim is one for service incurrence.  This means that no deduction for the degree of disability existing at the time of entrance will be made if a rating is awarded.  See [38 C.F.R. § 3.322](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=03f4aefe355bf2756e5b88b1679767c8&node=se38.1.3_1322&rgn=div8).

On the other hand, if a preexisting disorder is noted upon entry into service, the veteran cannot bring a claim for service incurrence for that disorder, but the veteran may bring a claim for service-connected aggravation of that disorder.  In that case section 1153 applies and the burden falls on the veteran to establish aggravation.
