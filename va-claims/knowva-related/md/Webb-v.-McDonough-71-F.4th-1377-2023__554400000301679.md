**Decision Assessment Document**

***Webb v. McDonough*, 71 F.4th 1377 (June 29, 2023)**

**U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (Federal Circuit)**

**What the case is about:**

The Board of Veterans' Appeals (Board) affirmed a decision of a Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) regional office (RO), where the RO assigned a noncompensable rating for the Veteran’s unlisted service-connected condition of erectile dysfunction. The Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court) affirmed the Board’s decision, and the Veteran appealed. The Federal Circuit concluded the Veteran was not required to show that his unlisted condition identically matched the criteria of the listed condition to which his condition was rated by analogy.

**Impact on VBA:**

The Federal Circuit’s holding has been incorporated into the guidance contained in Manual M21-1, V.ii.3.D.

**Summary of the facts and Federal Circuit’s reasons:**

The Veteran received a noncompensable rating for service-connected erectile dysfunction in a 2015 rating decision. At that time, VA’s Schedule for Rating Disabilities did not include a diagnostic code for erectile dysfunction. As a result, the RO rated the Veteran’s disability by analogy to diagnostic code (DC) 7522, which provided a 20 percent disability rating for “[p]enis, deformity, with loss of erectile power.” The RO determined the Veteran’s disability entitled him to a noncompensable rating. The Veteran appealed to the Board.

The Board affirmed the RO’s determination. The Board acknowledged the Veteran’s disability was rated by analogy as provided for by 38 C.F.R. § 4.20, but explained that DC 7522 required the Veteran to show “deformity of the penis with loss of erectile power.” Because the Veteran did not have such a deformity, the Board determined he was not entitled to a compensable rating. On appeal, the Court agreed and affirmed the Board’s decision. The Veteran appealed to the Federal Circuit.

The Federal Circuit noted that neither the Court, the Board, nor the RO addressed § 4.20 or whether the functions affected, anatomical location, and symptomatology of the Veteran’s condition were closely analogous to the functions affected, anatomical location, and symptomatology of the condition listed under DC 7522. Instead, VA and the Court required the Veteran to meet the requirements of DC 7522 as if he were being directly rated under the diagnostic code. The Federal Circuit concluded the Court erred in doing so because § 4.20 does not require a Veteran to show that his unlisted condition identically matches the criteria of the listed condition to which he is being rated by analogy.

The Federal Circuit held that, when rating by analogy under §4.20, VA must adhere to the requirements of the regulation. This means the listed disease or injury to which a Veteran’s unlisted condition is being rated by analogy must be only “closely related” to, not identical to, the listed condition. The regulation provides guidance for determining whether a listed condition is “closely related” to the unlisted condition; under § 4.20, a “closely related” condition is one “in which not only the functions affected, but the anatomical localization and symptomatology are closely analogous.”

The Federal Circuit also stated that, based on the plain language of § 4.20, it would be “nonsensical” to require a Veteran’s unlisted disability to precisely meet the criteria for a listed disease or injury’s diagnostic code. If the Veteran’s condition did precisely meet the requirements of a listed condition, there would be no need to rate by analogy.

In this case, the Veteran sought to have his unlisted condition rated by analogy under § 4.20, which does not require that he demonstrate that his condition is identical to a listed condition. Because the Court misinterpreted the requirements of § 4.20, the Federal Circuit vacated the Court’s decision and remanded the case for further proceedings.
