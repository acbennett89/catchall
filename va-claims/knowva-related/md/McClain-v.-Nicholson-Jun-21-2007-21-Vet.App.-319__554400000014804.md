Decision Assessment Document
 McClain v. Nicholson, Jun 21, 2007, 21 Vet.App. 319
 U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (Court)

**What the case is about:**

The Court held that the requirement that a veteran have a current disability before service connection may be awarded is satisfied when the veteran has a disability at the time a claim for VA disability compensation is filed or during the pendency of that claim even though the disability resolves prior to the claim’s adjudication.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran filed a claim in September 1994.  A VA examination report from October 1995 contained a diagnosis of depression due to multiple somatic complaints as a result of Gulf War Syndrome and an August 1997 VA examination diagnosed possible Gulf War Syndrome manifested by memory loss, joint pain, and headaches.  A VA examination report from 2003 contained the examiner’s assessment that the depression had resolved.

The Board of Veterans’ Appeals (Board), in November 2004, conceded that the veteran had been diagnosed with major depression, which two physicians associated with service in the Persian Gulf War; however, the Board determined that the veteran’s claim for service connection for a psychiatric disability, to include major depression, could not be granted because the veteran was no longer currently diagnosed with major depression.

The veteran argued before the Court that the Board should have granted service connection because he suffered with a service-connected disability at the time of his claim and during its adjudication.

The Court held that because the Board had *implicitly* found that the veteran had depression *at some point* during the processing of his claim, he had a current psychiatric disability that may be service connected.  Accordingly, it was determined that the Board erred in its decision that service connection must be denied because the veteran no longer manifested a psychiatric disability at the time the Board rendered its decision.

The Court reversed the Board’s decision and remanded the case for further adjudication and assignment of an effective date and staged ratings, if so deemed appropriate.
