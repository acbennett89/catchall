Decision Assessment Document
 Yonek v. Shinseki, Jul 8, 2013, 722 F.3d 1355
 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

**What the case is about:**

The Federal Circuit held that Diagnostic Code (DC) 5201 only allows a single disability evaluation for the shoulder, even if the shoulder manifests both limitation of motion with respect to both flexion and abduction.  The Federal Circuit ruled that the plain meaning of DC 5201 is that any “limitation of motion” of a single arm at the shoulder joint constitutes a single disability, regardless of the number of planes in which the arm’s motion is limited.

**Impact on VBA:**

No new impact warranting regulatory revision or Manual change.

**Summary of the facts and Court’s reasons:**

The veteran, following discharge from service in 1992, was granted service connection for a right shoulder disability with an evaluation of 10 percent.  The evaluation was increased to 20 percent in 1999.  Examinations of record showed divergent results with flexion ranging between 80 and 180 degrees and abduction of 60 to 180 degrees.  The Board of Veterans’ Appeals, in June 2010, denied entitlement to a rating in excess of 20 percent for the right shoulder disability.

The Veterans Court held that the veteran was not entitled to separate ratings under DC 5201 for limitation of motion of the right arm in flexion and abduction, concluding that DC 5201 only allows a single rating for limitation of motion, and that the plane in which the limitation of motion manifests itself is irrelevant.

The veteran argued before the Federal Circuit that DC 5201, when viewed within the context of the regulatory schedule, shows intent to provide for separate ratings of these movements.  However, the Federal Circuit found nothing in [38 C.F.R. § 4.71a](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=f77b6fe59c4300f8e3ff663c0639111f&node=se38.1.4_171a&rgn=div8) to suggest that DC 5201 does not mean what it says.  The Federal Circuit noted that it was significant that parts of § 4.71a addressing joints other than the shoulder assign different codes to limitation of motion in different planes, or in different directions within a single plane, such as Diagnostic Codes 5260 and 5261, which provide for ratings based on limitation of flexion and extension of the knee, or Diagnostic Codes 5206 and 5207, which provide for ratings based on limitation of flexion and extension of the elbow.  Thus, the Federal Circuit concluded that the plain meaning of DC 5201 is that any limitation of motion of a single arm at the shoulder joint constitutes a single disability, regardless of the number of planes in which the arm’s motion is limited.

The Federal Circuit, in addressing the flexion and abduction movements of the shoulder in Plate I of § 4.71a, concluded that it only demonstrates how to measure motion of the shoulder in the flexion and abduction planes and that limitation of motion in either plan may establish a compensable disability under DC 5201, and not that limitation of motion in both planes constitute two separate disabilities.

The Federal Circuit rejected the veteran’s reliance on [38 C.F.R. § 4.25(b](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=f77b6fe59c4300f8e3ff663c0639111f&node=se38.1.4_125&rgn=div8)) and [§ 4.45](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=f77b6fe59c4300f8e3ff663c0639111f&node=se38.1.4_145&rgn=div8) in support of his interpretation of DC 5201, finding that DC 5201 indicates only a single disability; thereby, precluding application of § 4.25(b)’s instruction that “disabilities arising from a single disease entity … are to be rated separately” and that § 4.45’s general directive to take into account limitation of motion “in different planes” does not require VA to create separate diagnostic codes.

The Federal Circuit also rejected the veteran’s argument that VA Office of General Counsel Prec. Op. [9-2004](http://www.va.gov/ogc/docs/2004/PREC92004.doc) required separate ratings, finding that the opinion was limited to the question of “whether rating under more than one diagnostic code is in order when a veteran manifests various limitation of motion in the same knee” and did not suggest that two ratings are permissible under a single diagnostic code.

The Federal Circuit affirmed the Veterans Court holding that DC 5201 only allows a single disability rating for each affected arm.
