# Mental health ratings: what has changed and what is proposed

*Researched 2026-10-09. Every claim below cites a primary document saved in this library.*

## Current vs. proposed, by area of life

**Current system:** one ladder of symptom lists at 0 / 10 / 30 / 50 / 70 / 100%. The adjudicator picks the level whose overall "occupational and social impairment" best fits the evidence. The symptoms are examples ("such symptoms as"), not checklists.

**Proposed system:** five **domains**. Each domain gets a score from 0 to 4 based on **intensity** (none, mild, moderate, severe or total) and **frequency** (less than 25% of the time, or 25% or more, over the past month). The rating comes from those scores, as set out in proposed § 4.130 (87 FR 8504-06).

### 1. Hygiene, dressing and eating ("Self-care")

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Current**, 100% | "intermittent inability to perform activities of daily living (including maintenance of minimal personal hygiene)" |
| **Current**, 70% | "neglect of personal appearance and hygiene" |
| **Current**, 30% | "generally functioning satisfactorily, with routine behavior, **self-care**, and conversation normal" |
| **Proposed** domain 5, Self-care | "May include, but is not limited to … Hygiene, dressing appropriately, or taking nourishment." |
| Proposed level 2 (Moderate) | "Clinically significant difficulties in one or more aspects of self-care that interfere with tasks, activities, or relationships **without accommodations or assistance**." |
| Proposed level 3 (Severe) | "Serious difficulties … **even with** accommodations or assistance." |
| Proposed level 4 (Total) | "Profound difficulties … that cannot be managed or remediated; difficulties that completely interfere … even with accommodations or assistance." |

**What changes:** self-care becomes a stand-alone domain. Under the proposed table, a level 4 in self-care alone would support 100%, and a level 3 alone would support 70%. The proposal also adds eating and nourishment to self-care. Today, eating disorders have a separate weight-based formula, which the proposal would eliminate.

### 2. Work, school, chores and caregiving ("Task completion and life activities")

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Current**, 100% | "Total occupational and social impairment" |
| **Current**, 70% | "deficiencies in most areas, such as work, school…"; "difficulty in adapting to stressful circumstances (including work or a worklike setting)" |
| **Current**, 50% | "reduced reliability and productivity"; "forgetting to complete tasks" |
| **Current**, 30% | "occasional decrease in work efficiency and intermittent periods of inability to perform occupational tasks" |
| **Current**, 10% | "decrease work efficiency … only during periods of significant stress, or symptoms controlled by continuous medication" |
| **Proposed** domain 3 | "Vocational, educational, domestic, social, or caregiving" activities |
| Proposed level 1 | tasks "completed with minor stress or minor accommodations" |
| Proposed level 2 | tasks "completed with significant stress **or** accommodations" |
| Proposed level 3 | serious difficulties in **two or more** aspects, "completed with significant stress **and** accommodations" |
| Proposed level 4 | profound difficulties in two or more aspects, "**one of which must be vocational**," not completed even with considerable accommodations |

**What changes:** job performance is no longer the backbone of every rating level. It becomes one domain among five. The proposed "Total" level in this domain specifically requires that **work (vocational)** be one of the affected areas. This domain also brings in what accommodations the veteran needs to get things done.

### 3. Relationships, family, coworkers and social life ("Interpersonal interactions and relationships")

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Current**, 70% | "inability to establish and maintain effective relationships"; "deficiencies in … family relations"; "impaired impulse control (such as unprovoked irritability with periods of violence)" |
| **Current**, 50% | "difficulty in establishing and maintaining effective work and social relationships" |
| **Current** § 4.126(b) | VA "shall not assign an evaluation **solely on the basis of social impairment**" |
| **Proposed** domain 2 | "Includes both informal (social, associational, etc.) and formal (coworkers, supervisors, etc.)" relationships |
| Proposed level 3 | serious difficulties "even with accommodations or assistance" |
| Proposed § 4.126 | **deletes paragraph (b)**. VA calls it "obsolete," because social functioning would now be its own domain (87 FR 8501). |

**What changes:** today, social impairment alone cannot drive a rating. Under the proposal it is a full domain, so a level 4 in relationships alone would support 100%, and a level 3 alone would support 70%.

### 4. Thinking, memory and concentration ("Cognition")

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Current**, 100% | "gross impairment in thought processes or communication"; "memory loss for names of close relatives, own occupation, or own name" |
| **Current**, 50% | "difficulty in understanding complex commands"; "impairment of short- and long-term memory"; "impaired judgment; impaired abstract thinking" |
| **Current**, 30% | "mild memory loss (such as forgetting names, directions, recent events)" |
| **Proposed** domain 1 | "memory, concentration, attention, goal setting, speed of processing information, planning, organizing, prioritizing, problem solving, judgment, making decisions, or flexibility in adapting" |

### 5. Leaving home, crowds, driving and travel ("Navigating environments")

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Current** | No separate category. Closest items: "spatial disorientation" (70%) and "disorientation to time or place" (100%). Agoraphobia-type limits are rated only through overall occupational and social impairment. |
| **Proposed** domain 4 | "Leaving the home, being in confined or crowded spaces, independently moving in surroundings, navigating new environments, driving, or using public transportation" |

**What changes:** this domain is entirely new. Avoiding crowds, being unable to drive, or being unable to leave the house would be scored directly.

## How the proposed scores become a rating

From the proposed § 4.130 table (87 FR 8504):

| Rating | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **100%** | Level 4 in one or more domains, **or** Level 3 in two or more domains |
| **70%** | Level 3 in one domain, **or** Level 2 in two or more domains |
| **50%** | Level 2 in one domain |
| **30%** | Level 1 in two or more domains |
| **10%** | Minimum rating for any service-connected mental disorder |

Level definitions, which are the same for every domain (87 FR 8502):

- **0**: no difficulties
- **1**: mild at any frequency, or moderate less than 25% of the time
- **2**: moderate 25% or more of the time, or severe less than 25% of the time
- **3**: severe 25% or more of the time, or total less than 25% of the time
- **4**: total 25% or more of the time

## Other proposed changes that cut both ways

1. **No 0% rating.** The minimum would be 10% for any service-connected diagnosed mental disorder (87 FR 8502).
2. **Medication (proposed Note 2).** "Include any ameliorating effects of medications when evaluating the extent of disability." In other words, the veteran would be rated as they function *on* medication. VA wrote this note specifically to override *Jones v. Shinseki*, 26 Vet. App. 56 (2012) (87 FR 8503).
3. **One rating for all co-existing mental disorders (proposed Note 1).** All co-existing mental disorders would get a single evaluation, under the rule against pyramiding.
4. **Proposed § 4.126(a) is rewritten.** The current text tells VA to consider "the length of remissions" and to rate on all the evidence "rather than solely on the examiner's assessment of the level of disability at the moment of the examination." That language does not appear in the proposed paragraph (a). Instead, frequency is measured over "the past month" (Note 3).
5. **VA's own estimate.** VA's 100-veteran proof-of-concept study found the proposed formula "would increase the average disability evaluation": fewer veterans at or below 50%, and more at 100% (87 FR 8503).

## The medication question in 2025-2026

- ***Jones v. Shinseki*** (2012), in `../case-law/`: when the rating criteria don't specifically mention medication, the Board may not discount the beneficial effects of medication.
- ***Ingram v. Collins***, 38 Vet. App. 130 (CAVC, decided 2025-03-12), in `../case-law/`: applied *Jones* to musculoskeletal ratings (back and ankle). VA's 2026 rule says the decision "could be applied broadly to over 500 separate diagnostic codes" and affect "over 350,000 currently pending claims" (91 FR 7118). Secondary reporting says VA has appealed *Ingram* to the Federal Circuit. I did not confirm that against the docket.
- **2026-02-17, interim final rule (91 FR 7118)**: amended 38 CFR § 4.10 to require rating the veteran's actual functioning, including the benefit of medication.
- **2026-02-27, rescission (91 FR 9712)**: VA withdrew that rule, citing commenters' concern about "adverse consequences." It says the rescission "does not resolve the legal questions now before the courts."

**Why this matters for mental health:** the current 10% level explicitly mentions "symptoms controlled by continuous medication," so for that level the criteria do contemplate medication. Proposed Note 2 would make on-medication functioning the rule for every level. How *Jones* and *Ingram* apply to each current mental health level is a legal question for an accredited representative or attorney.
